Can you drink young Kadarka?

Short Answer

Depends entirely on how it is made. Kadarka is a thin-skinned Hungarian grape that, left to its own devices, tends toward something light, aromatic, and high in acid, closer in spirit to a fresh Pinot Noir than to anything built for the cellar, and plenty of producers now bottle it exactly that way for early drinking. The tannic, age-worthy version comes from a more traditional style, longer skin contact and extended maceration, a technique that was common when Kadarka was made for structure and export rather than immediacy. That style really is aggressive young, with tannins that clamp down and fruit that stays closed, and it genuinely rewards ten years or more in bottle before it loosens up and shows its perfume. So check the label or ask before assuming: a modern, lighter Kadarka is meant to be drunk within a couple of years while it is still vibrant, while an old-style, structured bottling needs the patience. Serve the young style cool, around 14 to 16 degrees, and the aged style closer to 16 to 18.
