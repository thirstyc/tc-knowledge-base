Why is Xinomavro so tannic?

Short Answer

Xinomavro's tannin comes from genetics and terroir together. The grape has notably thick skins, and tannin lives mostly in skins and seeds, so extended skin contact during fermentation pulls more structure into the wine. Add the cooler, higher-altitude vineyards of Naoussa in northern Greece, where slow ripening builds phenolic complexity and acidity without the fruit ever going plush. It's one of Greece's oldest cultivated grapes, and it's often compared to Nebbiolo for good reason: both are pale-skinned varieties that pack far more structure than their light colour suggests, both need bottle age to become approachable, and both can age into serious, savoury complexity once that tannin resolves. Some modern winemakers use shorter maceration or partial destemming to soften the grip without losing the grape's identity, but traditional Naoussa Xinomavro is still built to last, not to be casual about. If aggressive tannin scares you, this isn't a grape to open on a whim - it rewards people willing to wait for it.

What pairs with Xinomavro?

Xinomavro's tannin and acid are aggressive enough that they need food with real fat and protein to grip onto - on their own they just feel harsh and drying.
