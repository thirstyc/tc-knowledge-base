Why does Dornfelder fly under the radar?

Short Answer

Germany sold the world on Riesling so thoroughly, sweet and dry alike, that most drinkers never think to look for a German red at all, let alone a good one. Dornfelder has the added problem of a reputation earned in the 1970s and 80s, when it was often churned out as a cheap, semi-sweet bulk wine, the kind sold in bulk to supermarkets rather than to anyone paying attention. That history stuck, even as growers quietly got better at it. Dornfelder is now Germany's most widely planted red grape, and serious producers make dry, structured versions with real depth of color and fruit, often from lower-yielding old vines. But because it never had an export campaign behind it the way Riesling did, and because its early reputation was mediocre at best, it just never built an international audience. The wine deserves a second look; it's simply never been given the marketing to earn one.
