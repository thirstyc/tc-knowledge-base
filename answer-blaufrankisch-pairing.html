What pairs with Blaufränkisch?

Short Answer

Blaufränkisch's calling card is a black-pepper spiciness that comes from rotundone, the same aromatic compound found in Syrah and, oddly, in green bell peppers and Grüner Veltliner. That spice note makes it a natural match for food that's peppery or lightly smoked itself — grilled meats with a cracked-pepper crust, paprika-forward Central European dishes like goulash, or game birds. The grape's firm tannin and high acidity also want fat to work against, so richer cuts of pork or beef, and dishes with a bit of char from the grill, do well. Mushrooms are a good match too, especially with more savoury, earthier examples from cooler sites, where the wine's dried-herb and forest-floor notes echo what's on the plate. It's a poor match for anything delicate or sweet-fruited — the tannin and pepper will overwhelm a simple roast chicken or a fruit-driven sauce. Think Austrian and Hungarian home cooking rather than French bistro fare, and you're in the right territory.

Blaufränkisch gets treated as Austria's answer to a serious Bordeaux-style red because it genuinely has the structural components for it: thick skins that yield real tannin, and a late ripening cycle in cool sites like Mittelburgenland and Leithaberg that lets the grape build sugar and flavour while holding onto sharp, refreshing acidity instead of flabbing out.
