Why does Okanagan Valley wine taste so bright and ripe at once?

Short Answer

The valley runs through desert, so days get properly hot, but a long, deep lake running its length acts like thermal ballast, absorbing heat and releasing it slowly overnight so vines never shut down from cold shock. That day-night swing is what keeps sugar and acid both climbing through the season instead of one racing ahead of the other, giving wines that taste ripe and fresh in the same sip. The valley also splits climate-wise from south to north: the southern end, drier and hotter, grows bold, structured reds like Syrah and Merlot; the cooler north favors aromatic whites like Riesling and Pinot Gris. So one appellation actually behaves like two, and it's worth checking which end of the valley a bottle comes from before assuming what's in it.
