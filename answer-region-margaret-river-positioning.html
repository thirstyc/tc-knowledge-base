Why do critics compare Margaret River to Bordeaux?

Short Answer

It's not a stretch of the imagination, it's geology and weather doing the same job twice. Margaret River sits on a narrow peninsula between the Indian and Southern Oceans, so the Cabernet grows under a maritime influence that flattens out temperature swings the way the Gironde estuary does for Bordeaux, no brutal heat spikes, long slow ripening, tannins that finish fine rather than harsh. The gravelly, well-drained soils over ancient granite push vines to struggle a bit, which is exactly what you want for concentration without heaviness. Add a Cabernet-Merlot-based blending culture that producers borrowed directly from Bordeaux, right down to barrel regimes, and you get wines with that same cedar-and-cassis restraint instead of the jammy fruit bomb style common elsewhere in Australia. The region only got planted seriously in the 1960s and 70s, so it's Bordeaux's structure and seriousness without the centuries of baggage, or the price tag that comes with a first-growth label.
