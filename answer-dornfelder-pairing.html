What pairs with Dornfelder?

Short Answer

Dornfelder sits in an odd, useful middle ground: too much fruit and tannin for delicate Pinot Noir pairings, too light and juicy for anything that wants a big Cabernet. That makes it the wine to reach for with grilled chicken, roast turkey, pork chops, or a mushroom risotto, dishes with enough weight to need a red but not enough fat or char to demand serious tannin. Its moderate acidity also handles slightly sweet glazes and barbecue sauce better than a bone-dry Bordeaux blend would. Mushrooms in particular play well against Dornfelder's dark fruit and faint earthiness, echoing rather than fighting it. Serve it lightly chilled, around 12 to 14 degrees, which keeps the fruit lively instead of jammy. Avoid pairing it with anything very rich or heavily marbled, like a ribeye, since the tannins simply aren't built to cut through that much fat, and the wine will taste thin by comparison. Think casual, weeknight food, not steakhouse.
