What wine goes with steak?

Short Answer

A bold red with some tannin: Cabernet Sauvignon, Malbec or Syrah. The fat in a good steak softens tannin, and the tannin makes the steak taste less heavy, so the two make each other better.

The cut changes the answer. A marbled ribeye can take the biggest wines: Napa Cabernet, a Left Bank Bordeaux, or a Barossa Shiraz. A lean filet mignon has less fat to tame tannin, so go softer: Merlot, a Rioja Reserva or even a fuller Pinot Noir.

Flank, skirt and hanger steaks, especially with chimichurri, are made for Argentine Malbec. Its plush fruit and fresh acidity handle the herbs and garlic.

Then look at what's on top. Peppercorn sauce loves Syrah, which has its own peppery streak; a northern Rhône bottle like Crozes-Hermitage is perfect. Blue cheese butter can take a big Cabernet. Béarnaise is rich and buttery, so a red with good acidity, like a Bordeaux or a Chianti Classico, keeps things fresh.

Cooked over fire? Smoke and char pull toward riper, spicier reds like Zinfandel or Grenache blends.

What to avoid: very light, delicate reds with a fatty cut, which taste thin next to it, and oaky whites, which fight the meat.

Not in a red mood? A rich, dry rosé or a skin-contact white can surprise you.
