Wine and allergies?

Topic

Sulphites

Difficulty

Beginner

Short Answer

True allergy to wine is rare, but many people react to something in it. The usual suspects are sulfites, histamine, grape proteins, or residues from fining agents.

Possible causes of reactions:

  • Sulfites: most people tolerate them, but a small number, particularly some people with asthma, are sensitive. That's why labels in many countries say "contains sulfites".
  • Histamine: produced during fermentation, and higher in some red wines. People who break down histamine poorly may get flushing, a stuffy nose, headaches or hives.
  • Grape proteins: rarely, people are genuinely allergic to proteins in grapes.
  • Fining agents: egg, milk and fish products are sometimes used to clarify wine. Tiny traces may remain, so some regions require them on labels.
  • Alcohol flush: facial redness caused by a genetic difference in breaking down alcohol, which isn't an allergy but is often mistaken for one.

What can help:

  • Note which wines cause problems. Many people react to red wine but not white, or the reverse.
  • Try vegan wines, made without animal-derived fining agents, if you have egg, milk or fish allergies.
  • See an allergist, who can test for specific sensitivities.

Seek emergency help for severe symptoms such as swelling of the lips or throat, difficulty breathing or dizziness.
