What is orange wine?

Short Answer

Orange wine is white wine made like red wine: the juice stays in contact with the grape skins during fermentation, which gives it colour, tannin and texture. It's also called skin-contact or amber wine.

Normally white grapes are pressed and the juice is separated from the skins immediately. Leave them together for days, weeks or months, and the wine picks up colour ranging from pale gold to deep amber, plus grippy tannins and flavours of dried apricot, orange peel, tea and nuts.

The style is ancient, not new. It has been made in Georgia for thousands of years, traditionally in buried clay vessels called qvevri, and it survived in Friuli in northeastern Italy and Slovenia, where it was revived in the 1990s.

Because it has tannin, orange wine behaves more like a red at the table. Serve it cool but not cold, and pair it with bold food: curry, roast vegetables, aged cheese, charcuterie or anything with strong spices.

It's often, but not always, made with minimal intervention, so it appears frequently on natural wine lists.

If you're trying it for the first time, start with a short-maceration version, which is fresher and less tannic.
