How Wine Works

Sparkling Wine

Bubbles come from carbon dioxide trapped during a second fermentation. How that fermentation happens — slowly inside each bottle, or quickly in a big pressurised tank — decides whether a wine tastes of toast and biscuit or of fresh pear and flowers.

Traditional Method
Second fermentation in the bottle, then ageing on the spent yeast (Champagne, Cava, Crémant, English sparkling)
Tank Method
Second fermentation in a pressurised tank, bottled under pressure (Prosecco, most Lambrusco)
Ancestral Method
One unfinished fermentation, bottled before it ends (pét-nat)
Sweetness Scale
Brut Nature and Extra Brut are driest, then Brut, Extra Dry, Sec, Demi-Sec, Doux
