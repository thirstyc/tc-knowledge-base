Why does Douro Red taste so dark and structured?

Short Answer

Blame the schist. The Douro's terraced vineyards are cut into fractured slate rock, and vines have to fight their roots down through it to find water, which naturally caps yields and concentrates whatever fruit does make it into the glass. The grapes themselves, Touriga Nacional, Touriga Franca, Tinta Roriz, Tinta Barroca, were essentially bred for Port: thick skins, dense pigment, high natural sugar, built to survive fortification and decades in a barrel or bottle. Take that same genetic toolkit and ferment it dry instead of fortifying it, and you get wines loaded with tannin and colour compounds from those skins, ripened fully under intense sun on steep, reflective terraces. That's where the darkness and grip come from, not winemaking tricks. Give these wines air. Decant an hour before serving, pour around 16-18°C, and expect the tannins to soften into something closer to dark chocolate and graphite than to raw grip.
