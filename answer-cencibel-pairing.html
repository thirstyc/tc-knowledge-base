What pairs with Cencibel?

Short Answer

Cencibel is just Tempranillo's alias in La Mancha and the surrounding plateau, and the name change matters because it points to where the grape grows: flat, brutally hot land where relentless sun pushes sugars, and therefore alcohol, way up while acidity drops fast. That combination, high alcohol, low acid, ripe jammy fruit, is why the wine needs food with real fat and char to stand up to it, otherwise it just tastes hot and shapeless. Grilled lamb, chorizo, morcilla, anything cooked over live fire: the smoke and char echo the wine's own baked-fruit, slightly roasted character instead of fighting it. Manchego works for the same reason, its salt and nuttiness giving the wine something to lean against. What kills it is anything acidic or delicate, a squeeze of lemon on fish, a vinaigrette-heavy salad, because there isn't enough acid in the wine to answer back, and it just collapses into flab. Serve at 16 to 18°C, not full room temperature; push it warmer and the alcohol takes over before the fruit gets a word in.
