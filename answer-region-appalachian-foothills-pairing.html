What pairs with Appalachian Foothills reds?

Short Answer

These are hybrid-based reds, usually Marquette or Frontenac blends, and what defines them is bright acidity and modest tannin rather than power. That structure is built to cut through fat and salt, which is exactly what maple-glazed ham and aged cheddar throw at it. The acid slices through the sugar in the glaze and the richness of the cheese instead of getting flattened by it, while the wine's earthy, slightly herbal edge echoes root vegetable stew without competing with it. Serve these around 14 to 16 degrees, cool enough to keep the acidity lively but not so cold that the fruit disappears. Avoid pairing them with anything too delicate or too tannin-hungry, like a peppered steak, because the wine simply doesn't have the muscle to keep up. This is food-first wine: it was never meant to be sipped alone at a tasting table, it was meant to sit next to a Sunday dinner and do its job quietly.
