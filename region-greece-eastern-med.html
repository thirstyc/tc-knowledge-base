Greece — overview

Greece has one of the world's oldest wine cultures (millennia deep) and, since the late 20th century, a dynamic quality revival built on its wealth of indigenous grapes — hundreds of them, found almost nowhere else. Mountainous terrain, islands, and a hot Mediterranean climate tempered by altitude and sea breezes give a huge range of styles. Modern Greek wine is fresh, distinctive, food-friendly, and excellent value.
Recommend when: the user wants distinctive indigenous whites and reds, Mediterranean food wines, or great value off the beaten path.

Assyrtiko & Santorini

Assyrtiko is Greece's greatest white grape, at its peak on the volcanic island of Santorini, where ancient ungrafted vines are trained in low basket shapes (kouloura) to shield grapes from fierce wind and sun. It makes bone-dry, high-acid, intensely mineral and saline whites with citrus and a smoky, volcanic edge — one of the Mediterranean's truly world-class whites, and ageworthy. Santorini also makes Vinsanto, a sun-dried sweet wine.
Serve at: 9–11°C.
Recommend when: the user wants a bone-dry, mineral, saline white; a seafood wine; or a distinctive world-class white beyond the usual grapes.

Other Greek whites

  • Moschofilero — pink-skinned, aromatic, floral, crisp; from Mantinia in the Peloponnese; a Muscat-like apéritif white.
  • Malagousia — a rescued-from-extinction grape; aromatic, peachy, floral, textured.
  • Roditis and Savatiano — widely planted; Savatiano is the base of Retsina, the traditional pine-resin-flavored white (now made in fresher, more serious versions).

Recommend when: the user wants an aromatic Greek white (Moschofilero, Malagousia) or is curious about Retsina.

Greek reds — Xinomavro & Agiorgitiko

  • Xinomavro — northern Greece's noble red (Naoussa, Amyndeon in Macedonia); high-acid, high-tannin, complex, with red fruit, tomato, olive, and dried-herb notes, ageing like a cross between Nebbiolo and aged Burgundy. Greece's most serious, ageworthy red.
  • Agiorgitiko — the Peloponnese's versatile red (Nemea); softer, plush, red-fruited, ranging from easy to structured and ageworthy.

Serve at: 16–18°C.
Recommend when: the user wants a high-acid, savory, ageworthy red (Xinomavro — great for Nebbiolo lovers) or a smooth, plush Mediterranean red (Agiorgitiko).

Cyprus

Cyprus has an ancient wine history and never suffered phylloxera (its ungrafted vines are protected by quarantine), preserving old indigenous varieties. The signature is Commandaria — a historic, luscious sun-dried sweet wine from Mavro and Xynisteri grapes, aged in oak; often called one of the world's oldest named wines still in production. Modern dry wines are improving, made from local Xynisteri (crisp white), Maratheftiko (characterful red), and international grapes at altitude on the Troodos Mountains.
Recommend when: the user wants a historic sweet wine (Commandaria) or a distinctive Mediterranean-island wine.

Turkey

Turkey has a vast vineyard area but most grapes are eaten or dried rather than vinified (for cultural and tax reasons). A quality wine scene has grown around indigenous grapes: Öküzgözü and Boğazkere (characterful eastern reds, often blended — one juicy, one tannic), Kalecik Karası (softer red), and whites like Narince and Emir. International varieties (especially Syrah) also do well in the hot, dry terrain.
Recommend when: the user wants an unusual indigenous red (Öküzgözü/Boğazkere) or is exploring Anatolian wine.

Lebanon

Lebanon, centered on the high Bekaa Valley, has a French-influenced wine tradition (a legacy of the colonial era). Its reds are typically warm, ripe, structured blends of Bordeaux and Rhône grapes (Cabernet, Merlot, Cinsault, Syrah, Grenache/Carignan), sometimes with indigenous whites (Obeideh, Merwah). The wines are sun-warmed, spicy, and ageworthy; the region is famous for perseverance through decades of conflict.
Serve at: 16–18°C.
Recommend when: the user wants a warm, spicy, structured Mediterranean/Middle Eastern red blend.

Israel

Israel has a modern, quality-focused industry, strongest in cooler, higher sites like the Galilee and Golan Heights. Mostly international grapes (Cabernet, Syrah, Chardonnay), increasingly well-made, with growing interest in Mediterranean varieties suited to the hot climate and in reviving indigenous grapes. Much (not all) production is kosher, which affects some winemaking practices but not necessarily quality.
Recommend when: the user wants an Eastern Mediterranean international-style wine, or specifically a kosher wine.

Quick regional summary greece

  • Greece: indigenous-grape revival; Assyrtiko (Santorini — mineral, saline, world-class white); aromatic Moschofilero/Malagousia; Retsina; reds Xinomavro (savory, ageworthy, Nebbiolo-like) and Agiorgitiko (plush).
  • Cyprus: ancient, phylloxera-free; historic sweet Commandaria; local Xynisteri/Maratheftiko.
  • Turkey: indigenous Öküzgözü/Boğazkere reds; huge but mostly-table vineyard.
  • Lebanon: Bekaa Valley; warm, structured French-grape blends.
  • Israel: cool-site Galilee/Golan; international grapes; much kosher.
