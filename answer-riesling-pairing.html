What pairs with Riesling?

Short Answer

Riesling's food reputation isn't hype - it comes down to three real mechanisms. First, acidity: it routinely hits 7-9 g/L total acidity, higher than most whites, which cuts through fat and richness like a squeeze of lemon on schnitzel. Second, alcohol: many styles sit between 8-11%, so the wine never fights food for attention the way a 14.5% oaked Chardonnay can. Third, residual sugar: off-dry styles like Kabinett or Spätlese use a bit of sweetness specifically to tame chili heat and salt in Thai or Szechuan food - the sugar doesn't fight the spice, it balances it. Dry (trocken) Riesling works beautifully with fish, pork, and sauerkraut. Serve dry styles at 8-10°C, off-dry and sweeter styles slightly warmer, 10-12°C, so the sugar doesn't feel cloying. The common mistake is assuming all Riesling is sweet - most quality Riesling today, especially from Alsace or Austria, is bone dry. That spectrum of sweetness, built on the same acid spine, is exactly what makes it the most flexible wine at the table.
