Barbera vs Sangiovese?

Short Answer

Both are high-acid Italian reds made for food, but Barbera is juicy and soft while Sangiovese is savoury and firm.

Barbera comes from Piedmont, in the northwest. It has deep colour, dark cherry and plum fruit, very high acidity and surprisingly low tannin. That combination makes it smooth and refreshing, easy to drink young, and one of the best everyday reds in Italy. Look for Barbera d'Alba and Barbera d'Asti.

Sangiovese comes from Tuscany, in central Italy. It's paler, with red cherry, dried herbs, tea leaf and a savoury, tomato-leaf edge. It has high acidity like Barbera, but much firmer tannins, which give it more structure and let the best versions, like Brunello di Montalcino, age for decades. Chianti is the best-known example.

At the table, both are natural partners for tomato sauce, pizza and pasta, since their acidity matches the tomato. Barbera is the easier match for simple weeknight food. Sangiovese stands up better to grilled meat and aged cheese.

Which to choose? Barbera for juicy, low-tannin fruit; Sangiovese for savoury structure.
