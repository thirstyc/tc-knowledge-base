Best Argentine wine?

Topic

Tannins

Difficulty

Beginner

Short Answer

Malbec from Mendoza is Argentina's calling card: plush, dark and velvety, with plum and violet notes and soft tannins.

Mendoza sits in the foothills of the Andes, and altitude is what makes the wines work. Vineyards are planted high, where strong sunshine ripens the fruit and cold nights keep the acidity fresh.

Within Mendoza, the Uco Valley is the region to know. Its high, cool vineyards make more structured, fresher Malbec than the warmer, riper style from lower sites.

Salta, in the far north, has some of the highest vineyards in the world and is home to Torrontés, a floral, aromatic white that smells sweet but tastes dry.

Patagonia, in the south, is cooler and windier, making more delicate Malbec and Pinot Noir.

Argentine Cabernet Franc has quietly become excellent, and is worth trying if you like herbal, savoury reds.

Where to start: an everyday Mendoza Malbec with steak, then an Uco Valley bottle to taste what altitude does.

Argentina is also good value, though prices for top wines have risen as its reputation has grown.
