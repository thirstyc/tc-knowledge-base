What pairs with New Zealand Gewürztraminer?

Short Answer

The reason this grape works so well with spice is chemistry, not just tradition. Gewürztraminer is often made slightly off-dry, and that residual sugar physically buffers capsaicin, the compound that makes chili burn, so heat feels rounder instead of sharper. Low tannin helps too, since tannin and chili heat amplify each other unpleasantly, which is why big reds fight with Thai or Sichuan food. The wine's own aromatics, lychee, ginger, rose, echo lemongrass, galangal and five-spice rather than competing with them. That makes it a natural match for Thai curries, Vietnamese salads, and Indian dishes with real heat. It also handles pungent washed-rind cheeses like Munster or aged Époisses, where a neutral wine gets steamrolled but Gewürztraminer's intensity and touch of sweetness can stand up to the funk and salt. Serve around 8 to 10°C. Avoid pairing it with anything delicate or subtle, it will flatten quieter dishes rather than complement them.

Why does New Zealand Gewürztraminer taste so aromatic and floral?

Gewürztraminer's perfume comes from terpenes in the grape itself, mainly linalool and geraniol, the same aromatic compounds found in rose petals and lychee.
