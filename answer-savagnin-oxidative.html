Why does Savagnin taste so dry and oxidative?

Short Answer

Savagnin's dryness and nutty, curry-like edge come from how it's raised, not just where it's grown. In the Jura, it's often aged under voile, a film of yeast that forms on top of the wine in barrels left deliberately unfilled and untopped for years, the same mechanism sherry uses under flor. Oxygen seeps in slowly, acetaldehyde builds up, and you get that walnut, apple-skin, curry-spice signature instead of fresh fruit. The extreme version is Vin Jaune, aged a minimum of six years and three days this way. What keeps it from tasting like flat, tired wine is the grape's naturally high acidity, which survives the oxidative aging and holds everything in tension, so it reads as intense and precise rather than oxidized in the bad sense. Serve it a touch warmer than a typical white, around 12 to 14°C, to let the aromatics open up.
