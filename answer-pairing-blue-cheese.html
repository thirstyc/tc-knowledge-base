Wine with blue cheese?

Short Answer

A sweet wine. Blue cheese is salty, pungent and creamy, and a sweet wine creates a sweet-and-salty contrast that works beautifully, while most dry wines taste bitter or thin next to it.

The two most famous pairings come with their own tradition.

Roquefort and Sauternes: the honeyed, apricot richness of Sauternes against the salty, tangy sheep's milk blue is a classic of French cuisine.

Stilton and Port: a British Christmas tradition. Ruby, Late Bottled Vintage or Vintage Port all work, and Tawny Port suits a milder Stilton.

Other good options include late-harvest Riesling, Tokaji Aszú from Hungary, Recioto della Valpolicella or Moscato.

Gorgonzola dolce, the creamy, milder style, can pair with sweet wines as well as a rich red like Amarone.

Prefer dry? Pick a big, fruity red with soft tannins, like Zinfandel or Amarone. The ripe fruit helps balance the salt.

Blue cheese as part of a dish, like a sauce on steak or crumbled on a salad, changes the pairing. A steak with blue cheese butter can take a Cabernet Sauvignon, because the meat does most of the work.

What to avoid: light, delicate reds and crisp, lean whites. The cheese overwhelms them.
