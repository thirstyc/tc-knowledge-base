What pairs with Yarra Valley Chardonnay?

Short Answer

The acid-to-texture ratio is what makes this wine work at the table. It's got enough grip to cut through fat but enough weight from lees ageing to not feel thin next to something substantial. Rich fish like salmon or trout, especially with a butter or cream-based sauce, is the classic move: the acid slices the richness while the wine's own texture matches the dish instead of getting steamrolled. Roasted chicken or turkey works the same way, the wine's structure holds up next to the browned skin and pan juices. Soft, bloomy-rind cheeses (think brie or a young Camembert) are good too, since the wine's acidity resets your palate against the fat instead of getting flattened by it. What to avoid: anything very spicy or aggressively sweet, which will make the wine taste sharper and thinner than it is. Serve at 10-12°C, and give it a real glass, this one has more going on aromatically than warm-climate Chardonnay and a narrow glass mutes it.
