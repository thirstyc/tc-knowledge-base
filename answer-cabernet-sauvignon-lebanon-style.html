What does Lebanese Cabernet Sauvignon actually taste like?

Short Answer

Forget the idea that Middle Eastern wine has to be some novelty bottle you buy once for the story. The Bekaa Valley sits at real altitude, often 900 to 1200 metres, so despite the punishing daytime sun, nights drop hard and cold. That swing is the whole mechanism: sugars ripen fast under the sun, but acidity survives because the vine essentially shuts down and rests overnight instead of slowly cooking. The result is Cabernet that's riper and darker than Bordeaux, with baked plum, tobacco leaf and often a warm spice note from the heat, but it doesn't flop into jammy soup because that acid backbone is still there holding the shape. Tannins tend to be firm, sometimes a touch rustic if the winemaking is old-school, which just means give it more air or a few extra years. It's closer in spirit to a warm-climate Cabernet from somewhere like Chile than to anything European, just with its own dusty, herbal fingerprint from the terrain.
