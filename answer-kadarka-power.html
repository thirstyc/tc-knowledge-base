Why does Kadarka taste so tannic?

Short Answer

Kadarka's tannin is a bit of a magic trick, because the grape itself has thin skins and would normally make something pale and soft, closer to a rustic Pinot than a brawny red. The structure people notice comes largely from winemaking choices in Szekszárd, where Kadarka has long been the backbone of the Bikavér, or Bull's Blood, blend. Producers there often ferment with whole clusters or a portion of stems included, which pulls grippy, savory tannin into the wine that the skins alone wouldn't provide. Loose, elongated bunches and a long, late ripening season in a warm but not scorching continental climate also let the grape build real acidity alongside that stem-derived structure. The result is a wine that's pale in colour but far from light in the mouth: peppery, sinewy, a little rustic, with tannin that grips more than the colour suggests. It's proof that tannin comes from winemaking decisions as much as grape genetics, and it's exactly why Kadarka needs food, not solo sipping, to show its best side.
