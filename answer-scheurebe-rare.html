Why is Scheurebe hard to find outside Germany?

Short Answer

It's a Riesling cross bred in Germany in the early twentieth century by Georg Scheu, and it never got the marketing push or the vineyard real estate that Riesling did. Part of that is practical: the grape ripens early but has thin skin and rots easily, so it's fussier to farm than growers want to deal with at scale. Part of it is flavor. Scheurebe's grapefruit-and-cassis-leaf character is distinctive, sometimes to the point of being a little polarizing, and unripe fruit can tip into an unpleasant catty note that scares off casual buyers. So it stayed concentrated in a handful of German regions, mainly Pfalz and Rheinhessen, where growers who like the challenge keep making it and locals keep drinking most of what's produced. There was never a coordinated push to build it an export identity the way Riesling had decades of investment behind it. It's not a conspiracy, just a grape that stayed a regional specialty instead of becoming a global calling card.
