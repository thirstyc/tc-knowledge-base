What makes Provence wine unique?

Short Answer

Provence is the world's benchmark for dry rosé. Most of what the region produces is pink, pale and built for the Mediterranean table.

The style is distinctive: very pale, almost onion-skin colour, with red berry, citrus and herbal notes, bone-dry and refreshing. It comes mostly from Grenache, Cinsault, Syrah and Mourvèdre, with the local Tibouren adding character. The colour is deliberate, from very brief contact with the grape skins.

The region isn't only rosé, though. Bandol, on the coast, makes structured Mourvèdre-based reds that age for years, and excellent rosé too. Cassis makes textured whites, and Palette is a tiny appellation with a cult following.

The climate does much of the work: hot, dry summers, cooling sea air, and the mistral wind that keeps vineyards healthy. The scrubland herbs known as garrigue, rosemary, thyme and juniper, are often used to describe the wines' aromas.

Provence rosé is made to be drunk young and cold, with grilled fish, salade niçoise, tapenade or anything eaten outdoors.

One caution: pale colour is a style, not a quality guarantee. Look for producer and appellation rather than the lightest bottle on the shelf.

What makes Piedmont wine special?
