Why does German Riesling taste so elegant-mineral?

Short Answer

German Riesling comes mostly from steep slate and slate-derived soils along rivers like the Mosel and Rhine, where the vines are planted on inclines steep enough to make a mountain goat nervous, purely to catch enough sun this far north. Those cool, marginal sites mean grapes ripen slowly and hang onto tartaric and malic acid that would burn off in a warmer climate, which is the actual source of that electric, almost saline intensity people call minerality, not literal rock flavor dissolving into the wine. Many styles keep some residual sugar specifically to balance that acid, so an off-dry Kabinett doesn't taste sweet so much as it tastes vibrant, the sugar is there to keep the acid from feeling austere. Alcohol often sits low, 8 to 11 percent, because fermentation is stopped before all the sugar converts. That acid backbone is also why these wines age for decades. Serve at 8 to 10°C, and don't assume off-dry means dessert wine, it's built for the dinner table.
