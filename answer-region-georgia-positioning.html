Why is Georgia suddenly trendy in wine circles?

Short Answer

Georgia's claim to being wine's birthplace rests on actual archaeology - grape pips and residue in clay vessels from around 6000 BC found in the South Caucasus, predating Mediterranean winemaking by millennia. That's not marketing copy, it's carbon dating. What makes it trendy now is qvevri: large egg-shaped clay vessels buried in the ground, used to ferment and age wine on skins, stems and seeds for weeks or months. The natural wine movement adopted this as its founding technique because it needs no additives, no mechanical temperature control beyond the earth itself, and produces amber 'orange' wines with real structure. So a country that never stopped doing this is suddenly being read as the avant-garde, when really the avant-garde caught up to a practice older than written language. Try a qvevri Rkatsiteli served around 12-14°C to taste skin contact done at its origin rather than as a stylistic flourish.
