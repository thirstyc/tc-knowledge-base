Wine with pulled pork?

Topic

Grenache

Difficulty

Beginner

Short Answer

A juicy, ripe red with soft tannins, like Zinfandel, Shiraz or Grenache, or an off-dry Riesling if the sauce is sweet and spicy. Pulled pork is rich, smoky and usually sweet, so it needs fruit more than structure.

Zinfandel is the classic. Its jammy fruit and peppery spice match smoky, sweet barbecue sauce, and its soft tannins won't fight the meat.

Australian Shiraz, Côtes du Rhône and Spanish Garnacha work for the same reason: ripe fruit, a little spice, nothing too austere.

The sauce decides a lot. Sweet, tomato-based sauces want fruity reds. Tangy, vinegar-based Carolina sauces are lighter and sharper, and a chilled Beaujolais or a dry rosé handles them better than a big red.

If there's real heat, switch to an off-dry Riesling or a fruity rosé. Lower alcohol and a touch of sweetness cool the spice rather than amplifying it.

Pulled pork sandwiches with slaw are a great match for sparkling wine or a crisp rosé, especially outdoors.

What to avoid: highly tannic, oaky reds like young Cabernet Sauvignon. The sweet sauce makes them taste harsh and bitter.
