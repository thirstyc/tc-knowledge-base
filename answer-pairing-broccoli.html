Wine with broccoli?

Short Answer

A crisp, green-edged white: Sauvignon Blanc, Grüner Veltliner or Verdejo. Broccoli's bitter, sulfurous notes can make oaky or tannic wines taste harsh, so freshness is the goal.

Like asparagus and Brussels sprouts, broccoli belongs to a family of vegetables that clash with many wines. The trick is to echo its green flavour rather than fight it.

What works:

  • Steamed or boiled broccoli: Sauvignon Blanc, Grüner Veltliner or a dry Riesling.
  • Roasted or charred broccoli: the sweetness and char suit a fuller white like Chenin Blanc, or a light red like Pinot Noir or Gamay.
  • Broccoli with cheese sauce: an unoaked or lightly oaked Chardonnay.
  • Broccoli with garlic and chili, or in a stir-fry: off-dry Riesling or Grüner Veltliner.
  • Broccoli pasta with anchovy and Parmesan: Vermentino or Verdicchio.

What to avoid: big, tannic reds and heavily oaked whites, which the vegetable's bitterness makes taste metallic.

Most of the time, the sauce and cooking method matter more than the broccoli itself. Butter, cheese, lemon or roasting all soften its edges and open up more options.
