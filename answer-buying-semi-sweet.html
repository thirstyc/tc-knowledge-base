Best semi-sweet wine?

Short Answer

Off-dry Riesling is the classic: a touch of sweetness balanced by bright acidity, so it tastes juicy rather than sugary.

Good semi-sweet wines:

  • German Riesling labelled Kabinett or Spätlese, or "feinherb", which is off-dry.
  • Vouvray labelled "demi-sec", from Chenin Blanc in the Loire.
  • Alsace Gewürztraminer and Pinot Gris, which are often off-dry, sometimes without saying so.
  • Moscato d'Asti: lightly sparkling, sweet, fragrant and low in alcohol.
  • Lambrusco labelled "amabile": a gently sweet, fizzy red.
  • Brachetto d'Acqui: sweet, sparkling and rose-scented.
  • Demi-sec Champagne or other sparkling wine.

Check labels for clues. "Off-dry", "medium-sweet", "demi-sec", "amabile" and "feinherb" all suggest some sweetness. Lower alcohol, around 8–11%, often means some sugar was left unfermented.

Semi-sweet wines are fantastic with food, especially spicy dishes like Thai, Indian and Sichuan cooking, where a touch of sweetness calms the heat. They also suit salty cheese, cured ham and fruit-based desserts that aren't too sweet.

Serve them well chilled.
