How to age wine at home?

Short Answer

Keep bottles cool, dark, still and at a steady temperature, lying on their sides if they have corks, and choose wines that are actually built to age.

The ideal conditions:

  • Temperature: around 12–14°C. Consistency matters more than the exact number; frequent swings do more damage than a steady 16°C.
  • Darkness: light, especially sunlight, can damage wine quickly.
  • Humidity: around 60–70% stops corks from drying out.
  • Stillness: keep bottles away from vibration, such as washing machines.
  • Position: store cork-sealed bottles on their side to keep the cork moist.

Where to do it:

  • A wine fridge is the easiest reliable option.
  • A cool basement or interior closet can work if the temperature stays steady.
  • Avoid the kitchen, the top of the fridge, garages and anywhere near heating.

Choose wines that improve with age: structured reds like Bordeaux, Barolo, northern Rhône Syrah and good Rioja, plus Riesling, Chenin Blanc, white Burgundy, vintage Champagne and vintage Port. Most everyday wine is made to drink young.

Buy several bottles of the same wine and open one every few years. It's the best way to learn when it tastes best to you.

Keep a simple record of what you have and when to drink it.
