Wine with Asian cuisine?

Short Answer

Off-dry Riesling is the most versatile choice, but "Asian food" covers so many cuisines that the best wine depends on the dish: sweetness, heat, umami and freshness all matter.

Some reliable starting points:

  • Chinese: Cantonese dishes suit Riesling, Champagne or a light Pinot Noir; Sichuan heat wants off-dry Riesling or Gewürztraminer; Peking duck loves Pinot Noir.
  • Japanese: sushi and sashimi pair with Champagne, Grüner Veltliner or Chablis; teriyaki and yakitori with Pinot Noir or Gamay.
  • Thai: off-dry Riesling or Gewürztraminer balances sweet, sour, salty and spicy flavours at once.
  • Vietnamese: fresh herbs and lime work with Sauvignon Blanc, Grüner Veltliner or dry rosé.
  • Korean: barbecue and gochujang-based dishes suit a juicy red like Zinfandel or Grenache, or an off-dry Riesling for spicier dishes.

A few general rules help. Umami from soy sauce and fish sauce makes tannic reds taste bitter, so keep reds light. Chile heat is amplified by alcohol, so choose lower-alcohol wines for spicy food. Sweet sauces call for a wine with at least a little sweetness.

Sparkling wine is a safe choice for a shared meal with lots of different dishes.
