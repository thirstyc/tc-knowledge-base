What pairs with English sparkling wine?

Short Answer

English sparkling wine is made the same way as Champagne, same grapes too, mostly Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Pinot Meunier, secondary fermentation in the bottle to build those fine, persistent bubbles, and it comes from a similarly cool, marginal climate that keeps acidity high. Southern England's chalk soils are even a geological continuation of the same seam that runs under Champagne itself. That shared backbone of high acid and fine mousse is exactly why the food pairings overlap so closely: fish and chips work because the acid and carbonation cut through the fried batter and oil the same way Champagne cuts through richness. Oysters play off the wine's briny, saline edge. Soft, bloomy-rind cheese gets a lift from the bubbles scrubbing your palate clean between bites. Serve it properly cold, around 8-10°C, colder than you'd pour a still white, to keep the bubbles tight and the acid feeling crisp rather than sharp. Treat it like Champagne because, mechanically, it basically is.
