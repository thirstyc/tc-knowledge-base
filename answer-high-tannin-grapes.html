Which grapes have high tannins?

Short Answer

Nebbiolo, Tannat, Cabernet Sauvignon, Sagrantino, Syrah, Malbec, Petite Sirah, Aglianico and Mourvèdre are among the most tannic grapes.

Tannin comes mostly from grape skins, seeds and stems, so thick-skinned varieties with small berries give the most. Oak barrels add more.

Nebbiolo is the famous case: pale in colour but ferociously tannic, which is why Barolo needs years to soften. Tannat, from Madiran in France and Uruguay, is so tannic that producers often soften it deliberately. Sagrantino, from Umbria, is among the most tannic of all.

Cabernet Sauvignon, Syrah, Malbec, Petite Sirah, Aglianico and Mourvèdre are all firmly structured, though ripeness and winemaking change how harsh that feels.

At the other end, Pinot Noir, Gamay, Grenache, Barbera, Dolcetto, Zinfandel and most lighter reds have gentle tannins.

Tannin isn't a flaw. It gives a wine its shape and lets it age, and it's what makes big reds work so well with fatty, protein-rich food.

If a wine feels too grippy, try it with a steak, decant it, or give it a few more years. All three soften the sensation.

Which grapes are high in acidity?

Among whites, Riesling, Chenin Blanc, Sauvignon Blanc, Albariño, Assyrtiko, Furmint and Grüner Veltliner are among the most acidic.
