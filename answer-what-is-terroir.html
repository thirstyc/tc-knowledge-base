What is terroir?

Short Answer

Terroir is the idea that a wine tastes like where it comes from. It covers soil, climate, topography and the local traditions of the people who farm there.

The four parts, roughly:

  • Soil: drainage, heat retention and nutrients, which shape how the vine grows.
  • Climate: temperature, rainfall and sunshine over the season.
  • Topography: slope, altitude and which way the vineyard faces, which change how much sun and air it gets.
  • Human practice: which grapes are planted, how vines are pruned, when the grapes are picked.

Burgundy is the classic demonstration. One grape, one winemaker, two neighbouring plots, and two noticeably different wines.

One common misunderstanding: vines don't absorb flavour from rocks. A wine grown on slate doesn't taste of slate because it drank slate. Soil affects wine mostly through drainage, temperature and vine vigour, and "minerality" is a description of a sensation, not a chemical transfer.

Terroir is also why European labels name places rather than grapes: the place is understood to be the more important information.

It's a real, observable effect, and also a marketing term. Both things are true.

What is a sommelier?

A sommelier is a wine professional, usually working in a restaurant, who builds the wine list, manages the cellar, and helps guests choose and enjoy wine.
