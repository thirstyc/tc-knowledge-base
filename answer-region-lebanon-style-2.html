Why does Bekaa Valley Cabernet taste so elegant?

Short Answer

The Bekaa Valley sits around 1,000 metres up, sandwiched between Mount Lebanon and the Anti-Lebanon range, which blocks coastal humidity and leaves the vines to bake in Mediterranean sun by day and drop ten-plus degrees by night. That diurnal swing is the real engine here: grapes build sugar and phenolic ripeness during the heat, then hang onto acidity and aromatic freshness overnight instead of turning flabby. Add limestone and clay soils that drain fast and force roots deep, and you get Cabernet Sauvignon with real backbone rather than sweetness for its own sake. Winemaking technique imported from Bordeaux over the past century - barrel aging, blending with Cabernet Franc or Syrah, careful extraction - shapes that fruit into something savory and structured rather than jammy. This is also one of the oldest continuously cultivated wine regions on the planet, so none of this is borrowed prestige; it is just old land finally paired with modern control in the cellar. Decant an hour before drinking, serve around 16-18°C, and expect tannins that want food - lamb, not popcorn.
