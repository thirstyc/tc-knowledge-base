Why does Australian Semillon taste so citrusy and mineral?

Short Answer

It comes down to where it's grown and how it's made. Hunter Valley in New South Wales is warm, but Semillon there is picked early and quickly, before the sugars build up and while acidity is still high, specifically to preserve that lemon-and-lime freshness rather than let the grape ripen into something broader. It's then fermented in stainless steel with essentially no oak contact, so there's no vanilla or toast to soften the citrus edges, and no malolactic fermentation either, which keeps the naturally sharp malic acid intact rather than converting it to the creamier lactic acid you'd get in a buttery chardonnay. The mineral, almost flinty character people pick up isn't literally minerals from the soil dissolving into the wine, that's a myth, it's more a combination of that high acidity, low fruit sweetness, and subtle sulphur-derived characters from winemaking that read as stony or chalky on the palate. The result is a wine that's lean and taut young, built to either drink fresh or age for a decade-plus into something richer.
