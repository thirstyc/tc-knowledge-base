What pairs with Etna Rosso?

Short Answer

Etna Rosso runs on high acid, fine grainy tannin and a savory, faintly smoky volcanic minerality, closer in structure to Nebbiolo or Pinot Noir than to a typical Sicilian red, so it wants food with its own char, umami or fat to meet it halfway. Grilled octopus works because the wine's smokiness echoes what the char already gave the fish, while the acid cuts the richness. Wild mushroom pasta leans into the earthy, savory side of Nerello Mascalese, and aged pecorino gives the tannin something salty and fatty to grip onto so it stops feeling austere. Serve it a touch cool, around 14 to 16°C, not full room temperature, since the volcanic soils and altitude already give it plenty of freshness that warmth will just blur. Avoid heavy tomato-based or sweet sauces, which fight the smoke instead of complementing it.
