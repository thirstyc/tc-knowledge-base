How to become a sommelier?

Short Answer

Work in restaurants and study at the same time. Most sommeliers combine hands-on experience on the floor with structured qualifications and years of tasting.

Experience matters most. Many start as servers, bartenders or cellar assistants in restaurants with serious wine programmes, learning service, storage and how guests actually order.

Qualifications give structure and credibility. The main paths:

  • Court of Master Sommeliers: service-focused, with four levels from Introductory to Master Sommelier.
  • WSET: knowledge-focused, from Level 1 through Level 3 to the Level 4 Diploma.
  • Master of Wine: a demanding, research-oriented qualification from the Institute of Masters of Wine.

Many countries also have their own national sommelier associations and courses.

What the work actually requires:

  • Tasting constantly, ideally blind, and in groups.
  • Learning regions, grapes, producers, laws and vintages.
  • Service skills: decanting, opening sparkling wine safely, handling a busy floor.
  • Business skills: buying, pricing and managing stock.

It's demanding: long evening hours, physical work and constant study. Tasting groups, mentors and working under an experienced sommelier are the fastest ways to learn.

What is a sommelier?

A sommelier is a wine professional, usually working in a restaurant, who builds the wine list, manages the cellar, and helps guests choose and enjoy wine.
