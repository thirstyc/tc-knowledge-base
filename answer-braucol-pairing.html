What pairs with Braucol?

Short Answer

Braucol (the same grape as Fer Servadou, mostly seen around Gaillac and Marcillac) has a rustic, almost peppery tannin structure with a savage streak of iron and dark berry. That tannin is grippy and dry, built to cut through fat rather than sit next to something delicate. Pair it with grilled or braised meat: lamb, duck, a good daube, or the kind of cassoulet that could stop a clock. Charcuterie works too, especially anything smoked or cured, since the fat coats your palate and the tannin scrubs it clean. Avoid fish, light salads, or anything subtle, the wine will flatten them. Serve it around 16 to 17 degrees, slightly cooler than room temperature, so the fruit stays lifted and the tannin doesn't turn harsh. If it feels aggressive on its own, that's not a flaw, it just means you haven't put food in front of it yet. This is a wine built for a table, not a couch.

Why does Braucol taste so dark and earthy?

Braucol is the local Southwest France name for Fer Servadou, and that name is a clue - "fer" means iron, a nod to both the grape's naturally high tannin (rigid, almost metallic in youth) and the iron-rich clay and limestone soils around Gaillac and Marcillac where it's most planted.
