Why orange wine is trendy?

Short Answer

Because it's new to most drinkers but thousands of years old, it pairs brilliantly with food, and it grew up alongside the natural wine movement and a new generation of wine bars.

Orange wine is white wine made like red: the juice ferments with the grape skins, giving an amber colour, grippy texture and flavours of dried fruit, tea, nuts and spice.

Why it caught on:

  • An ancient story: it began in Georgia, where amber wines have been fermented in clay qvevri for about 8,000 years. UNESCO recognised the qvevri method as intangible cultural heritage in 2013.
  • A modern revival: winemakers in Friuli and Slovenia brought the style back from the late 1990s.
  • The natural wine scene: many orange wines are made with minimal intervention, and natural wine bars made them a staple.
  • Food versatility: their texture and tannin handle dishes that defeat many wines, like spiced food, fermented flavours, and Middle Eastern and Asian cooking.
  • Something different: adventurous drinkers enjoy a style that doesn't fit the usual red, white or rosé.
  • Social media: the colour is striking.

It's not a passing fad. Orange wine is now an established category, often called "skin-contact" or "amber" wine, made by serious producers around the world.
