Why does Loire wine taste so fresh and mineral?

Short Answer

The Loire is a long, cool river valley running west to east, and that cool climate is the whole story: grapes ripen slowly, holding onto malic acid instead of burning it off the way they would in a hotter region, so nearly everything made here comes out fresh rather than heavy. Chenin Blanc is the chameleon grape, capable of bone-dry (Savennières), off-dry, sweet and botrytized (Coteaux du Layon), or sparkling depending on how it's picked and vinified, and its natural acidity is high enough to support all of those styles without collapsing into flab. Sauvignon Blanc around Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé gets its citrus and flint character partly from acidity and partly from limestone and flint soils that add a smoky, mineral edge. Cabernet Franc reds from Chinon and Bourgueil stay light-bodied and peppery rather than jammy because the grape simply doesn't ripen into heavy tannin or high sugar this far north. None of it is the most famous wine in France, but it might be the most useful - there's a Loire wine for almost any meal, and almost none of them need decades in a cellar to taste right.
