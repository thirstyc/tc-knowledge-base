What pairs with Schiava?

Short Answer

Because Schiava has almost no tannin and a bright acidic spine, it does best with food that's equally light on its feet. Grilled chicken works because there's enough acid to cut through without any tannin to clash with the char. Charcuterie is a natural match in its home region, where alpine cheeses and cured meats are the everyday table. Fresh pasta with a simple butter or herb sauce lets the wine's floral, faintly almond-tinged finish come through instead of getting buried. What you want to steer clear of is heavy red meat or anything in a rich, reduced sauce, since Schiava simply doesn't have the structure to stand up to that kind of weight, and it'll taste thin and overwhelmed by comparison. Serve it lightly chilled, around 12 to 13 degrees, which sharpens the fruit and makes it feel more refreshing. Treat it as a lunch wine, not a wine for anything that needs a serious backbone.
