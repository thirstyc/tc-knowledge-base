What pairs with Central Coast wine?

Short Answer

The Central Coast isn't one climate, so it isn't one pairing strategy. Regions like Santa Rita Hills and parts of Monterey sit close enough to the Pacific that fog rolls in most mornings, keeping Chardonnay and Pinot Noir cool and acid-driven — those wines want grilled fish, roast chicken, anything that needs a bright wine to cut through fat without overpowering it. Move inland to Paso Robles, where the fog burns off by mid-morning and afternoons run hot, and you get riper, bigger reds — Zinfandel, Syrah, Cabernet blends — built for barbecue, smoked meat, anything with char and spice that a delicate wine would just disappear next to. So the honest answer is: find out which valley the bottle is from before you plan the meal, because a coastal Pinot and an inland Zinfandel are barely playing the same sport. Serve the cool-climate whites and Pinots around 10-12°C, and give the bigger reds more like 16-18°C to let them open up.
