Why is Okanagan Valley worth knowing?

Short Answer

Okanagan is Canada's fastest-growing fine wine region, and it earned that growth rather than hyping it. It built its reputation on ice wine, the intensely sweet stuff made from grapes frozen solid on the vine, harvested at night at minus 8 Celsius or colder so the water stays as ice crystals and only concentrated sugar and acid press out. That novelty got the valley noticed decades ago. What is happening now is more interesting: serious dry table wine, Bordeaux reds and cool-climate whites, grown on a narrow strip of desert benchland around a long lake. The scene is still small by world standards, a fraction of what Niagara or the Napa Valley produce, but quality is climbing fast and prices haven't caught up yet with what's in the glass. If you want to see where Canadian wine is actually heading rather than where it has been, this is the valley to watch.

What pairs with Okanagan Merlot or Riesling?

The Okanagan Valley is a semi-arid desert climate wedged between mountains, with hot days and cool nights that let grapes ripen fully while still holding onto acidity, so both the Merlot and the Riesling come out riper and rounder than their cooler-climate counterparts but not flabby.
