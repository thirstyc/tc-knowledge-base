Petite Sirah vs Syrah?

Short Answer

They're related but not the same grape. Petite Sirah is a cross of Syrah and an obscure French grape called Peloursin, created in France in the nineteenth century and known there as Durif.

Despite the "petite" in its name, which refers to its small berries, Petite Sirah makes huge wines: inky, almost black, with blueberry, blackberry, black pepper and chocolate, and very high tannin.

Syrah is more varied and usually more elegant. In the northern Rhône and other cool places, it's peppery, smoky and savoury; in warm regions, where it's often called Shiraz, it's riper and fuller, but rarely as tannic as Petite Sirah.

Petite Sirah is almost entirely a California story today, where it was long used to add colour and structure to blends, especially Zinfandel. Varietal bottlings have become more common.

Ageing: both can age, and Petite Sirah's tannins often need several years to soften.

At the table, Petite Sirah wants hearty food: barbecue, braised short ribs, smoked meats. Syrah is more flexible, good with grilled lamb, peppercorn steak and stews.

Which to choose? Syrah for pepper and balance; Petite Sirah for maximum depth and power.
