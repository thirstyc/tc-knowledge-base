What pairs with Lambrusco?

Short Answer

Lambrusco was built, whether anyone planned it that way or not, to survive Emilia-Romagna's dinner table, which is one of the fattiest, saltiest cuisines in Italy. Mortadella, prosciutto, culatello, parmigiano reggiano, fried gnocco fritto, ragù, this is rich, dense food, and Lambrusco's job is to cut through it. The mechanism is straightforward: its natural acidity and light carbonation act like a scrub brush on the palate, slicing through fat and resetting your taste buds between bites, while a touch of fruit or residual sugar (in amabile styles) balances the saltiness of cured meats. It's the same logic as squeezing lemon on fried food, just delivered in wine form. Serve it properly chilled, around 10 to 12 degrees, so the acid and bubbles stay sharp rather than flabby. Avoid pairing it with anything delicate or subtle, it will flatten a light fish dish. Save it for the charcuterie board, the fried snacks, and anything swimming in pork fat.
