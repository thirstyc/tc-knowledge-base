Why does Rossese taste so elegant and mineral?

Short Answer

Rossese di Dolceacqua comes from Liguria, a narrow strip of Italian coastline squeezed between mountains and the Mediterranean, and that geography does most of the work. The vines grow on steep, well-draining slopes with sandy and calcareous soils, and the sea moderates temperatures so the grapes ripen slowly without piling on sugar or losing acidity - unlike a lot of hot coastal regions where ripening runs fast. That slow ripening keeps natural acidity high even at full maturity, which is what gives Rossese its bright, mineral edge rather than the softer, fruit-forward profile you'd expect from an Italian coastal red. Tannins stay light and fine because the grape is thin-skinned, so the wines end up structured by acid and minerality rather than grip - closer in feel to a good Pinot Noir than to typical Italian reds. Production is tiny because the terraced, hand-worked vineyards don't scale, which is the real reason bottles are hard to find. Serve it lightly chilled, around 14-16°C, to keep that acidity and minerality sharp rather than let warmth soften it into something duller.
