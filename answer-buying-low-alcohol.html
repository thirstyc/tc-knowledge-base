Best low-alcohol wine?

Short Answer

German Riesling Kabinett, Moscato d'Asti, Vinho Verde and Lambrusco are some of the best naturally low-alcohol wines, often between 5% and 11%.

Alcohol comes from sugar, so wines from cool climates, or wines that keep some sweetness, usually have less of it.

Good low-alcohol choices:

  • Moscato d'Asti: around 5–6%, sweet and lightly sparkling.
  • German Riesling Kabinett: often 7.5–10%, with a touch of sweetness and bright acidity.
  • Vinho Verde: usually 9–11.5%, light and fresh.
  • Lambrusco: often 8–11%, a lightly sparkling red.
  • Txakoli from the Spanish Basque Country: around 10.5–11.5%, crisp and slightly spritzy.
  • Muscadet: usually around 12% or less.
  • Light reds like Beaujolais and some Loire reds: often 12–13%.

For comparison, many full-bodied reds are 14–15% or more.

Always check the ABV on the label, since it can vary a lot between producers and vintages, even for the same style.

If you want to avoid alcohol entirely, look for dealcoholised wine instead. It's made like regular wine, then the alcohol is removed.

Lower alcohol doesn't mean less flavour. Many of these wines are full of aroma and freshness, and they're great for long lunches or warm evenings.
