What pairs with Kékfrankos?

Short Answer

Kékfrankos, which Austrians call Blaufränkisch, carries enough acidity and tannin to stand up to genuinely hearty food, and it's wasted on anything timid. The acidity comes from grapes that ripen slowly in cooler pockets of Hungary like Eger and Sopron, holding onto malic acid even as sugars climb, while the tannin is moderate but firm, giving the wine a peppery, structured backbone. That combination wants fat, char and spice to soften it. Grilled meats with a good sear, venison, wild boar or duck, and Hungarian standbys like paprikás or goulash all work because the wine's acid cuts the richness while the tannin meshes with the meat's char and the dish's paprika heat. Spicy sausage or a peppered steak plays to the same strengths. Where it falls short is with anything too light or sweet, since the wine's structure will simply overwhelm delicate flavours. Serve it around 15 to 17°C, cool enough to keep the acidity crisp but warm enough to let the peppery fruit open up.
