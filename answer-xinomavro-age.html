Can you drink young Xinomavro?

Short Answer

You can drink young Xinomavro, but it's not much of an argument for doing so. This is Greece's answer to Nebbiolo - pale in colour but loaded with fierce, grippy tannin and racy acidity, especially from Naoussa's cooler, higher-altitude sites where the grape ripens slowly and holds onto structure. In youth those tannins are dry and astringent, and the fruit hasn't had time to fill the gaps, so the wine can taste more like chewing tea leaves than drinking. Bottle age resolves that: over 10 years or more, tannins polymerize and soften, acidity integrates, and the wine opens into dried cherry, tomato leaf, olive, and a savoury, almost Barolo-like complexity. If you must drink it young, decant hard for a few hours and serve with fatty, protein-rich food that can absorb the tannin. But if you have the patience, let it sit. This is a grape built for the cellar, not the Tuesday-night table, and rushing it wastes what makes it interesting.
