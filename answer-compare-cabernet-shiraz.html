Cabernet vs Shiraz?

Short Answer

Both are full-bodied reds, but Cabernet Sauvignon is structured and savoury while Shiraz is richer, rounder and spicier.

Cabernet tastes of blackcurrant, cedar and graphite, with firm, grippy tannins and a tighter, more linear shape.

Shiraz, the Australian name for Syrah, gives blackberry, plum, chocolate and pepper, with softer tannins and a plusher, more generous feel. Cooler-climate Syrah is more peppery and restrained; warm-climate Shiraz is fuller and riper.

Australia is the easiest place to compare them: Coonawarra and Margaret River for Cabernet, Barossa Valley and McLaren Vale for Shiraz. The two are often blended there, a classic Australian combination that balances Cabernet's structure with Shiraz's flesh.

With food, Cabernet suits steak, lamb and aged cheese, where fat softens tannin. Shiraz handles barbecue, sausages, braised beef and smoky, spiced dishes.

Both can age, though Cabernet usually rewards patience more.

Which to choose? If you like a firm, dry finish, Cabernet. If you prefer something rounder and more fruit-forward, Shiraz.
