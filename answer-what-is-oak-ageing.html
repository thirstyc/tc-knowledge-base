What is oak aging?

Short Answer

Oak ageing means keeping wine in oak barrels, which adds flavour and texture and lets in a tiny amount of oxygen that softens the wine over time.

Oak gives vanilla, baking spice, toast, smoke and sometimes coconut or dill. It also adds a little tannin, and the slow, controlled oxygen exchange through the wood rounds the wine's edges.

The main variables:

  • New versus used: new barrels give the most flavour; older ones mostly provide gentle oxygen exchange.
  • French versus American: French oak tends toward subtle spice and fine tannin; American oak gives sweeter vanilla, coconut and dill.
  • Size: small barrels, like the 225-litre barrique, give more oak character than large casks, which is why traditional Barolo and Rioja producers favour big old ones.
  • Toast level: barrels are charred to different degrees, which changes how smoky or sweet they taste.

Cheaper alternatives exist. Oak chips, staves or powder give flavour without the cost of barrels, and many inexpensive wines use them.

Oak is seasoning, not an ingredient. Used well you barely notice it; used heavily it buries the fruit, which is why "unoaked" became a selling point.
