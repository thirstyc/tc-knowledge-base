Why does Agiorgitiko fly under the radar outside Greece?

Short Answer

Greek wine as a whole still carries an old, unfair reputation from decades of cheap retsina exports that flooded international markets in the mid-20th century. That resinous, pine-flavored wine became shorthand for 'Greek wine' abroad, even though it represents a tiny sliver of what the country actually produces. Agiorgitiko never had that problem stylistically, it's a soft, dark-fruited red closer in spirit to Merlot, but it got swept up in the same reputational undertow simply by being Greek. There's also a language barrier: unfamiliar names and labels written in Greek script make shoppers hesitate at the shelf. Add limited export volume and you get a grape that's beloved domestically, especially around Nemea, but barely known elsewhere. That's changing slowly as Greek producers modernize winemaking and push into export markets, but Agiorgitiko still trades well below its quality level. For now, that means genuinely excellent, food-friendly red wine at prices that would be unthinkable for a comparable Bordeaux or Rioja.
