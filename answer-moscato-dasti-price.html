Why is Moscato d'Asti so affordable?

Short Answer

Moscato d'Asti stays cheap because almost nothing about how it's made costs much time or money. It's produced using the tank method, also called Charmat or Martinotti, where the second fermentation happens in a large pressurized steel tank rather than in individual bottles, so there's no lengthy hand riddling or aging on lees the way there is with Champagne. The fermentation itself is stopped early by chilling and filtering out the yeast, which is what leaves the wine low in alcohol, around 5-6%, and naturally sweet from unfermented grape sugar. No oak, no extended aging, no complicated blending, just fresh Muscat grapes turned around quickly. Piedmont also grows a lot of Moscato Bianco across the hills around Asti, so volume is high and competition keeps prices honest. It's meant to be consumed within a year of release, so producers aren't tying up capital in inventory either. Chill it to 6-8°C and drink it soon after buying.

What pairs with Moscato d'Asti?

Moscato d'Asti's low alcohol, usually around 5-6%, and gentle sweetness make it one of the few wines that actually cools down spicy food instead of fighting it, so it's a natural with Thai, Sichuan, or Indian dishes that would clash with a bigger, more tannic red.
