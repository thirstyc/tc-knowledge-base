How to pair wine with a healthy diet?

Short Answer

If you drink wine, keep it moderate, have it with meals, and match it to fresh, whole foods, much like the Mediterranean way of eating.

Health guidance has become more cautious in recent years. The World Health Organization says no level of alcohol is completely risk-free, and Canada's 2023 guidance considers two drinks or fewer per week low risk. So wine is best seen as a pleasure to enjoy in small amounts, not a health food. Nobody should start drinking for health reasons.

Practical habits:

  • Drink with food, never on an empty stomach.
  • Keep portions small: a standard glass is about 150 ml, less than many people pour.
  • Have several alcohol-free days each week.
  • Choose lower-alcohol wines, around 11–12.5%.
  • Drink water alongside.

Wine-friendly healthy pairings:

  • Grilled fish and vegetables with crisp whites like Albariño or Sauvignon Blanc.
  • Legume dishes, like lentil stews, with light reds such as Pinot Noir or Gamay.
  • Salads with goat cheese and Sancerre.
  • Roast chicken and vegetables with Chardonnay or a light red.
  • Tomato-based dishes with Chianti or Barbera.

Savouring one good glass slowly with a meal is more rewarding, and healthier, than several quick ones.

If you have health conditions or take medication, ask your doctor what's right for you.
