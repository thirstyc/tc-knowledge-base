Why is Veneto Italy's most commercially important wine region?

Short Answer

Veneto produces more wine than any other Italian region by volume, largely on the back of Prosecco, which turned an entire province into a household name almost overnight through cheap, food-friendly, easy-drinking bubbles made via tank method rather than the slower, pricier traditional method used in Champagne. That alone would make it commercially dominant. But the region doesn't stop at volume — Valpolicella and its appassimento technique, drying Corvina grapes on racks before fermentation to concentrate sugar and flavour, produces Amarone, one of Italy's most serious, age-worthy, expensive reds. So you get mass-market Prosecco funding infrastructure and reputation, while Amarone and structured Valpolicella Ripasso keep the region credible with collectors and critics. Few wine regions manage both ends of the market successfully; most either chase volume and lose prestige, or chase prestige and stay small. Veneto's split identity, industrial-scale sparkling wine next to painstaking dried-grape reds, is exactly why it's impossible to ignore.
