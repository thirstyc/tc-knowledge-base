What food actually works with Tocai/Friulano, and why does it pair so well with prosciutto?

Short Answer

The classic pairing is San Daniele prosciutto, and it's not just a regional coincidence, it's a real mechanism. Friulano has a bitter, almost almond-like finish and a texture with some grip to it, which cuts through the fat of cured pork the same way a squeeze of lemon would, but without the sharpness that a high-acid wine like Sauvignon Blanc would bring. The wine's savory quality actually echoes the salt-cured, slightly funky notes in the prosciutto instead of fighting them. Beyond that, think anything with a fatty or oily component that needs a counterpoint rather than a contrast: frittata, seafood risotto, grilled sardines, or a simple plate of soft cheese and cured meats. Avoid pairing it with anything too delicate or sweet, since that bitter edge will just read as harsh next to shy flavors. It's also not a wine for heavy tomato sauces or red meat, it doesn't have the structure or fruit weight to stand up to those. Keep it in its lane: charcuterie, seafood, simple preparations.
