What pairs with Loire Cabernet Franc?

Short Answer

The trick is matching the wine's leafy, peppery edge and its fairly light tannin rather than fighting it with anything too rich or fatty. Grilled fish, especially something with a bit of char like mackerel or sardines, works because the wine's acidity and herbal notes echo smoke and citrus without overwhelming the fish. Mushrooms, sautéed in butter or in a tart, pick up the earthy, forest-floor undertone that shows up in older bottles. Goat cheese is the classic regional pairing, and not by accident, Sancerre and Chinon share the same limestone soils that produce Crottin de Chavignol, so the wine's chalky minerality and the cheese's tang were built for each other. Roast chicken with tarragon or thyme also plays nicely with the herbal profile. Avoid heavy cream sauces or anything too sweet, they flatten the wine's brightness. Serve around 14 to 16 degrees so the tannins stay in check and the fruit doesn't get swallowed by warmth.

Why does Loire Valley Cabernet Franc taste so herbaceous?

That green pepper, tomato leaf, graphite note comes from methoxypyrazines, aromatic compounds that Cabernet Franc produces naturally and that break down as grapes ripen.
