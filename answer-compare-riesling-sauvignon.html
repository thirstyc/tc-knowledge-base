Riesling vs Sauvignon Blanc?

Short Answer

Both are high-acid, aromatic whites, but Riesling smells of flowers and stone fruit and can be sweet or dry, while Sauvignon Blanc is herbaceous, citrusy and almost always dry.

Riesling gives lime, green apple, peach and floral notes, plus a distinctive petrol-like aroma as it ages that fans love. It's usually unoaked, often lower in alcohol, and made in every style from bone-dry to lusciously sweet.

Sauvignon Blanc is greener and zestier: grass, gooseberry, green pepper and grapefruit. Those notes come from compounds called pyrazines, which are stronger in cooler climates.

Ageing is the big divide. Great Riesling can improve for decades, developing honey and toast. Most Sauvignon Blanc is meant for drinking young, though oaked white Bordeaux is an exception.

With food, Riesling, especially off-dry, is the better choice for spicy food, pork and Asian dishes. Sauvignon Blanc shines with goat cheese, salads, herbs and seafood.

One label tip: German Riesling isn't always dry. Look for "trocken" if that's what you want.

Which to choose? Riesling for versatility and ageing; Sauvignon Blanc for immediate, zesty refreshment.
