Why does White Burgundy (Chardonnay) taste so tense-mineral?

Short Answer

The tension in White Burgundy comes from cool Burgundian nights that let Chardonnay hang on the vine without losing acidity, and limestone-clay soils - Kimmeridgian marl in Chablis, oolitic limestone further south - that drain well but hold just enough water to stress the vine gently, concentrating flavour without dehydrating it. That stress reads as a chalky, saline grip rather than fruit sweetness. One correction: most White Burgundy isn't actually unoaked - premier and grand cru wines see barrel ageing routinely - but the barrels are old and large enough to frame the wine rather than flavour it, giving texture without vanilla or toast. Winemakers also lean on lees stirring for weight instead of new wood or sugar, keeping the wine dry and structured rather than round. The result is built on structure, not fruit: green apple, wet stone, occasionally a struck-match note from careful, low-oxygen handling. Serve at 12°C, not fridge-cold, or the minerality just reads as thin acid with nothing behind it.
