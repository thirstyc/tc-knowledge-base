What pairs with Bobal?

Short Answer

Bobal's tannin is assertive and a little rustic, and tannin needs fat and protein to soften into something pleasant. On its own, a young Bobal can taste gripping and dry; put it next to a grilled ribeye or lamb chops with charred fat, and the fat coats your palate while the tannin binds to the proteins, so both sides feel smoother. That's the actual chemistry, not just tradition. Spanish tapas built around this instinct: chorizo, morcilla, grilled chicken with pimentón, slow-braised stews with paprika and garlic all work because they're rich and often a bit spicy, which Bobal's fruit can stand up to without getting harsh. Avoid delicate white fish or anything cream-based and light; the tannin will flatten it. Serve around 16 to 18°C, slightly cooler than room temperature, so the tannin doesn't feel hot or aggressive. If you find a bottle too tannic to enjoy on its own, that's not a flaw to fix with more wine, it's a cue to put more food on the table.

Why is Blaufränkisch Austria's serious red?

Blaufränkisch gets treated as Austria's answer to a serious Bordeaux-style red because it genuinely has the structural components for it: thick skins that yield real tannin, and a late ripening cycle in cool sites like Mittelburgenland and Leithaberg that lets the grape build sugar and flavour while holding onto sharp, refreshing acidity instead of flabbing out.
