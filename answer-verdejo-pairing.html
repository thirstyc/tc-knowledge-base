What pairs with Verdejo?

Short Answer

Verdejo, mostly grown in Rueda in northwest-central Spain, has thick skins that carry natural bitter and herbal compounds, giving the wine its signature fennel, hay and bitter-almond streak alongside citrus and stone fruit. That herbal edge plus a slightly waxy, textured mouthfeel is exactly why it works so well with small plates: it can stand up to garlic, olive oil and herbs without getting steamrolled the way a plainer white might. Grilled shrimp benefit from the wine's acidity cutting through any char or oil, while fresh goat cheese's tang plays nicely against Verdejo's own bitterness rather than clashing with it. Tapas built around olives, almonds, jamón or fried fish all sit comfortably here too, since the wine has enough weight to not disappear next to bolder flavors. Serve it cold, around 8 to 10 degrees, and drink it young: Verdejo doesn't gain much from age, its whole appeal is that fresh, slightly bitter snap.
