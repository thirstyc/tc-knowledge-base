How to host a wine tasting?

Short Answer

Pick a theme, choose four to six wines, pour small tastes, and give people a reason to compare. That's all a great tasting needs.

Themes make tastings memorable:

  • One grape, several places: Chardonnay from Burgundy, California and Australia.
  • One region, several styles: whites, reds and sweet wines from the Loire.
  • Price test: a cheap and an expensive bottle of the same style, tasted blind.
  • Old World versus New World.

Quantity: pour small tastes, around 30–60 ml. A bottle then serves 12–25 tastes, so four to six bottles covers a group of eight to twelve.

Order matters: whites before reds, light before heavy, dry before sweet.

What you'll need:

  • One glass per person, rinsed between wines, or more if you have them.
  • Water, plain crackers or bread, and a spit bucket.
  • Simple note cards.

Taste blind if you can: wrap bottles in paper bags or foil and number them. It removes label bias and makes the reveal fun.

Serve food afterwards, or alongside for a pairing tasting.

Keep it relaxed. Ask people what they like and why, not what they're "supposed" to taste.
