What pairs with Sauvignon Blanc?

Short Answer

Sauvignon Blanc's whole personality comes from pyrazines (the green, herbaceous, cut-grass and green-pepper notes) and thiols (grapefruit, passionfruit, sometimes a whiff of cat's pee, which sounds worse than it tastes). Layer on searing acidity from grapes usually picked early to preserve it, and you've got a wine built to cut, not cushion. That's why it loves seafood and goat cheese: both have a natural brininess or tang that the acid meets head-on instead of fighting. Fresh herbs, dill, cilantro, basil, mirror the herbaceous side directly. Asparagus, famously hard to pair, works here because the vegetal notes on both sides agree with each other rather than clash. What it hates is butter, oak, and cream sauces, anything rich and round smothers the wine's tension, which is the entire point of drinking it. Serve it cold, 8 to 10°C, and drink it young. This is not a wine that improves with bottle age; the freshness is the product, and it fades.
