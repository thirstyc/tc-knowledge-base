Alsace — overview

Alsace sits in northeastern France, along the German border, sheltered from Atlantic rain by the Vosges mountains — which makes it one of France's driest, sunniest wine regions despite its northern latitude. Its history has shifted between France and Germany, and its wine culture reflects both: German grape varieties and a focus on aromatic whites, but a French dry style. Alsace is unusual in France for labeling wines by grape variety, and for its tall, flute-shaped bottles. It is overwhelmingly a white-wine region.
Recommend when: the user wants aromatic, expressive dry whites; a match for spicy or aromatic cuisine; or varietally labeled French wine.

The noble grapes of Alsace

Four grapes are designated "noble" in Alsace and may be used for its top wines:

  • Riesling — the most prized; dry, racy, mineral, citrus and stone-fruit, highly ageworthy. Alsace Riesling is typically drier and fuller than German.
  • Gewurztraminer (spelled without the umlaut in Alsace) — intensely aromatic; lychee, rose, ginger, spice; full-bodied, low-acid, often faintly off-dry.
  • Pinot Gris — rich, full, smoky, honeyed; more powerful than Italian Pinot Grigio.
  • Muscat — dry, grapey, floral; a distinctive aromatic apéritif style.

Recommend when: matching aromatics and food — Riesling for minerality, Gewurztraminer for perfume and spice-food, Pinot Gris for richness, Muscat as a dry aromatic apéritif.

Other Alsace grapes

  • Pinot Blanc (and Auxerrois) — soft, gentle, easy dry whites; also the base of much Crémant.
  • Sylvaner — light, crisp, earthy; a traditional everyday white.
  • Pinot Noir — the only red permitted; historically light, but increasingly serious and structured as the climate warms.

Recommend when: the user wants an easy, gentle white (Pinot Blanc), a crisp everyday white (Sylvaner), or a cool-climate Pinot Noir.

Dryness in Alsace — a labeling note

Alsace can be confusing on sweetness: most basic wines are dry, but Pinot Gris and Gewurztraminer especially can carry noticeable residual sugar without saying so on the label. Producers increasingly indicate a dryness scale. The key point for the sommelier: Riesling and Muscat are usually dry; Pinot Gris and Gewurztraminer may be off-dry depending on producer and ripeness.
Recommend when: the user is unsure whether an Alsace wine will be sweet, or wants a guaranteed-dry aromatic white.

Alsace Grand Cru

Alsace has 51 Grand Cru vineyards — specific, delimited sites recognized for superior terroir, generally planted to the four noble grapes. A Grand Cru on the label signals a single top vineyard and usually a more concentrated, mineral, ageworthy wine. The rest of the region's wine is labeled simply "Alsace AOC," often with the grape variety.
Recommend when: the user wants a step up in concentration and terroir expression, or an ageworthy Alsace white.

Sweet Alsace — Vendanges Tardives & SGN

Alsace makes two levels of naturally sweet wine from ripe or botrytis-affected noble grapes:

  • Vendanges Tardives (VT) — "late harvest"; rich, concentrated, off-dry to sweet.
  • Sélection de Grains Nobles (SGN) — "selection of noble berries"; intensely sweet, botrytized, rare and long-lived.

Both are made from Riesling, Gewurztraminer, Pinot Gris, or Muscat.
Serve at: 8–10°C.
Recommend when: the user wants a rich late-harvest or botrytized dessert wine, or a match for foie gras and rich desserts.

Crémant dAlsace

Alsace is one of France's largest producers of Crémant — traditional-method sparkling wine, most often based on Pinot Blanc and Auxerrois, sometimes Pinot Noir (for rosé), Chardonnay, or Riesling. Crisp, fresh, and excellent value, it's a serious Champagne alternative.
Serve at: 8°C.
Recommend when: the user wants an affordable, high-quality sparkling wine.

Food pairing — Alsaces strength

Alsace's aromatic, often dry-but-fruity whites are among the most food-versatile wines in the world, and a classic answer to cuisines that trouble other wines: Gewurztraminer with Munster cheese, Thai, Indian, or Sichuan food; Riesling with choucroute (sauerkraut with sausages), trout, or pork; Pinot Gris with smoked fish or richer poultry.
Recommend when: the user is eating spicy, aromatic, or Alsatian food and needs a versatile wine.

Quick regional summary

  • What: dry, sunny northeast France; aromatic white-wine region, varietally labeled, tall flute bottles.
  • Noble grapes: Riesling (mineral, dry, ageworthy), Gewurztraminer (perfumed, spice-food), Pinot Gris (rich, smoky), Muscat (dry, grapey).
  • Also: Pinot Blanc/Sylvaner (easy whites), Pinot Noir (the lone red), Crémant dAlsace (sparkling).
  • Sweet: Vendanges Tardives and Sélection de Grains Nobles.
  • Strength: unbeatable with spicy and aromatic cuisines.
