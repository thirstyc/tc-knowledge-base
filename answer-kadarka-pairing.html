What pairs with Kadarka?

Short Answer

Kadarka looks deceptively light in the glass, almost like a rustic Pinot, but don't be fooled into pouring it with delicate food. The tannin is real, and it's often sharpened by winemakers who ferment with some stems included, since the grape's own skins are thin and don't give up much structure on their own. That grip needs fat and spice to soften it. Think Hungarian classics: paprikás, goulash, stuffed cabbage, anything built on lard, paprika and slow-cooked meat. The fat coats the tannin, the paprika's warmth matches the wine's own peppery streak, and the wine's acidity cuts back through the richness so the dish doesn't sit heavy. Grilled sausage or a smoky lamb chop works too. Avoid pairing it with anything too clean or delicate, like white fish or fresh salads, because the tannin will just read as harsh with nothing to push against. Serve it a touch cool, around 14 to 16°C, so the fruit stays lifted and the tannin doesn't turn coarse.
