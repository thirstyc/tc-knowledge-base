Why does Chilean Sauvignon Blanc fly under the radar?

Short Answer

Chile built its export identity on big reds, Cabernet and Carménère, because that's what the US and UK markets wanted to buy in bulk through the 1990s and 2000s, and the marketing budgets followed the money. Coastal whites like Casablanca Sauvignon Blanc came later, once producers realized the cooling fog off the Humboldt Current could make something genuinely distinctive, but by then Chile's reputation was already locked in as a red wine country. There's also a branding problem: New Zealand's Marlborough region had already claimed the global Sauvignon Blanc conversation with its loud, tropical, unmistakable style, leaving little oxygen for a quieter, more citrus-driven version from Chile to get noticed. None of this reflects the wine's quality, it's just a function of timing and where the marketing dollars went. If anything it works in your favor, since these wines are still priced like an afterthought while overdelivering for what you pay.
