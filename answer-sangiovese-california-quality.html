Is Sangiovese from California actually any good, or is it just a novelty?

Short Answer

Depends entirely on the site and the ego behind it. Sangiovese is thin-skinned, prone to uneven ripening, and unforgiving of lazy winemaking, so it exposes mediocrity fast. Planted in a spot that's too warm, it turns into a jammy, shapeless red with none of the personality that makes the grape worth growing in the first place. But a handful of producers in cooler pockets, places with real diurnal temperature swings, have figured out that restraint is the whole game here: less new oak, earlier picking, lower extraction. Done that way, you get something with genuine acidity, a savoury edge, and tannins that don't beat you up. It's not going to fool anyone into thinking it's Brunello, and it shouldn't try to. The honest answer is that most bottles are fine but forgettable, and a small number are quietly excellent. It's a grape worth trying once from a producer who clearly respects it, not something to buy blind off a supermarket shelf.
