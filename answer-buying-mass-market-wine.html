Are big-brand supermarket wines good?

Topic

Buying Wine

Difficulty

Beginner

Short Answer

They're consistent, easy to drink and cheap, which is exactly what they're designed to be. But they're rarely the best value, even at the bottom of the price range.

Big-brand wines are made in huge volumes, often from grapes grown across large areas and blended for a reliable house style. Many are soft, fruity and slightly sweet, sometimes with a few grams of residual sugar and oak flavouring from chips or staves rather than barrels. That makes them pleasant and approachable, especially for new wine drinkers.

The trade-offs:

  • A large share of the price goes to marketing, packaging, distribution and retailer margins, not the wine.
  • The same taste every year means little sense of place or vintage.
  • Residual sugar can make them feel heavy with food.

For a similar price, you can often get more character from a smaller producer in a value region, like Côtes du Rhône, the Douro, Spanish Garnacha, Chilean Carmenère or Picpoul de Pinet.

That said, there's nothing wrong with enjoying a wine you like. If a familiar bottle makes you happy, drink it. Just try something new alongside it now and then.
