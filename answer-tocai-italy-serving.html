What temperature should I serve Tocai/Friulano at, and does it need to breathe?

Short Answer

Serve it cooler than you'd think is generous, around 10 to 12 degrees Celsius, straight from the fridge with maybe ten minutes to come off the deep chill. Too cold and you mute the almond-skin bitterness and waxy texture that make the grape interesting; too warm and the alcohol and that bitter edge start to feel aggressive instead of savory. It doesn't need decanting. This isn't a wine built on primary fruit aromatics that need coaxing out, it's built on texture and a slightly phenolic grip, so pouring straight from bottle to glass works fine. If anything, give it a few minutes in the glass rather than a decanter, just to let it lose the initial chill without exposing it to too much air. Buy it young. Most Friulano is meant to be drunk within two to three years of the vintage, while that fresh bitterness and acidity are still bright. Let it sit too long and it can turn flat and lose the exact quality that makes it worth seeking out.
