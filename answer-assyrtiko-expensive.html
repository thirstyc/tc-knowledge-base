Why is Assyrtiko expensive?

Short Answer

Partly prestige, but mostly brutal, unavoidable labor economics. Santorini's vines are ungrafted, some over a century old, because the island's sandy volcanic soil has kept phylloxera out entirely, which is rare and valuable in itself. But those old vines are trained into low, coiled baskets called kouloura, nested close to the ground to survive constant wind, and every single one has to be pruned, managed and harvested by hand since no machine can work that shape. Yields are naturally tiny because the soil is poor and the climate dry, so there's just not much wine to go around. Add in that vineyard land on Santorini is squeezed hard by tourism, which pushes real estate prices well past normal agricultural land, and the cost per bottle climbs before a single sommelier gets involved. So yes, there's a marketing halo around 'Santorini,' but the underlying scarcity and hand labor are real, not manufactured, which is why quality generally does track with price here more reliably than in most wine regions.
