What pairs with Caladoc?

Short Answer

This is built for the grill, not the raw bar. Caladoc's low acidity and soft, ripe tannins mean it doesn't need a squeeze of lemon-juice-style contrast the way a Sancerre would with fish; instead it wants fat and char to lean against. Think lamb chops, merguez, a daube or cassoulet, anything slow-cooked or smoked where the meat's richness matches the wine's own density rather than fighting it. The dark fruit and slightly baked character also play nicely with barbecue sauce, cumin, and grilled peppers, so don't be shy with spice rubs. Where it struggles is with anything delicate or acidic, a bright salad or a citrus-forward dish will make the wine taste flat and heavy by comparison. Serve around 16-17°C, a touch cooler than room temperature, so the alcohol doesn't dominate. If you've got leftover barbecue the next day, this is also a solid bottle to pour with sandwiches made from the same meat, since it was basically built for exactly that kind of food.
