Can you age Vino Nobile?

Short Answer

Yes, but calibrate your expectations. Vino Nobile is built on Sangiovese, specifically the Prugnolo Gentile clone, which brings enough tannin and acid to hold together over time. DOCG rules require a minimum of two years of aging before release, with riserva bottlings needing three, so these wines already arrive with some development baked in. Give a good bottle another 5-10 years in a cool, dark cellar and the tannins soften, the fruit shifts from cherry toward dried fig and leather, and the whole thing gets more savory and less aggressive. That said, don't confuse this with Brunello or top Bordeaux as a long-haul investment wine; Vino Nobile lives in a more mid-term window. Past 12-15 years, most bottles start drying out rather than gaining complexity. Serve at 16-18°C, and decant an hour ahead if it's young, both to soften the tannin and let the aromatics open up.
