Is Trebbiano actually one grape?

Short Answer

No, and this trips up a lot of people. Trebbiano is really a family name slapped onto a bunch of genetically distinct varieties across Italy and France, the way 'Muscat' covers a dozen cousins. Trebbiano Toscano, the one behind most basic Italian whites and the base of a lot of Cognac distillation as Ugni Blanc, is a high-yielding, fairly neutral grape that ripens late and holds onto acid. Trebbiano Abruzzese, grown in Abruzzo, is a different plant altogether, capable of more texture and depth, especially in the hands of producers who treat it seriously rather than just farming volume. Trebbiano di Soave and Trebbiano di Lugana are different again, sometimes not even true Trebbiano genetically. So when someone says a wine tastes like 'typical Trebbiano,' ask which one, because the family resemblance stops at the name. It is less a grape than a bureaucratic category that outlived its usefulness.
