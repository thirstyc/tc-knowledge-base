Why does Beaujolais taste so light and fruity?

Short Answer

Two reasons: the grape, and what gets done to it.

Gamay is naturally low in tannin and high in acid, a thin-skinned grape that was never going to produce anything brooding. Burgundy tried to banish it in 1395 for being too easy to like.

Then there's carbonic maceration, which is the part that makes Beaujolais taste like Beaujolais. Rather than crushing the fruit, whole bunches go into a sealed tank flooded with CO2. Fermentation starts inside each intact berry instead of in the juice around it. That pulls out colour and fruit but almost no tannin, and it throws off aromatic compounds you don't get any other way — banana, bubblegum, kirsch. The smell people associate with Beaujolais Nouveau.

The ten crus, Morgon and Fleurie and Moulin-à-Vent among them, use less of it or none at all, and taste considerably more serious for it. Those age properly, which tends to surprise anyone who wrote the region off after one bad November.
