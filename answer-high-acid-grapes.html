Which grapes are high in acidity?

Short Answer

Among whites, Riesling, Chenin Blanc, Sauvignon Blanc, Albariño, Assyrtiko, Furmint and Grüner Veltliner are among the most acidic. Among reds, Nebbiolo, Sangiovese, Barbera, Pinot Noir and Gamay stand out.

High-acid white grapes:

  • Riesling: famously tangy, from dry to sweet.
  • Chenin Blanc: bright acidity that lets it age for decades.
  • Sauvignon Blanc: zesty and green.
  • Albariño: fresh and saline.
  • Assyrtiko: bracing acidity even in hot Santorini.
  • Furmint: high acid behind Tokaji's sweetness.
  • Picpoul: its name means "lip stinger".
  • Muscadet (Melon de Bourgogne) and Chablis (Chardonnay from a cool climate).

High-acid red grapes:

  • Barbera: very high acid, low tannin.
  • Sangiovese: tart cherry and savoury.
  • Nebbiolo: high acid and high tannin.
  • Pinot Noir and Gamay: fresh and bright.
  • Blaufränkisch and Aglianico.

Climate matters as much as the grape. Cool climates, high altitude and early picking preserve acidity; heat lowers it. The same grape can taste much more tart from a cool region.

High-acid wines are the most food-friendly, since acidity refreshes the palate and cuts through fat and salt. They also tend to age better.

How to age wine at home?

Keep bottles cool, dark, still and at a steady temperature, lying on their sides if they have corks, and choose wines that are actually built to age.
