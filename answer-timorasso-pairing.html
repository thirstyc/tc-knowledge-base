What pairs with Timorasso?

Short Answer

Timorasso's high acidity and saline, chalky texture exist to cut through fat, so pair it with things that have fat to cut. Seafood, especially anything with a bit of richness like scallops or a butter-based sauce, gets balanced rather than overwhelmed because the acid resets your palate after every bite. Fresh pasta with cream or butter works the same way, the wine's tension keeps the dish from feeling heavy. Soft, bloomy-rind cheeses are the classic move too, since the acid slices through the fat in the cheese the way a squeeze of lemon would, while the wine's texture is substantial enough not to get bullied by the cheese's richness. Avoid delicate, barely-dressed raw fish, where Timorasso's structure can overpower rather than complement. Serve it around 10-12°C, cold enough to keep the acidity bright but not so cold that you mute the saline, waxy texture that makes this grape interesting in the first place.

Why does Timorasso taste so mineral and crisp?

Timorasso is grown in the Colli Tortonesi, in hilly Piedmont on soils that are heavy in limestone and clay marl, and it is a genetically late-ripening grape with thick skin, so it hangs on the vine well into autumn.
