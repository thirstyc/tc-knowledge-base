What is Barolo?

Short Answer

Barolo is a red wine from Piedmont in northwestern Italy, made entirely from Nebbiolo, and it's one of the most structured, age-worthy wines in the world.

The wine is famously pale, with a brick-orange rim even when young, which fools people into expecting something light. It isn't: Barolo has very high tannin and high acidity, with aromas often described as tar and roses, plus sour cherry, dried herbs and truffle as it ages.

The rules require at least 38 months of ageing before release, 18 of them in wood. Riserva bottles age at least 62 months. Even so, most Barolo needs another decade to soften.

The wine comes from a small group of villages, including Barolo itself, La Morra, Serralunga d'Alba, Castiglione Falletto and Monforte d'Alba, and single-vineyard bottlings are common.

Styles vary. Traditional producers use long macerations and large old casks; modern ones use shorter macerations and smaller barrels for a rounder, earlier-drinking wine.

Barolo wants rich food: braised beef, game, mushroom or truffle dishes, aged cheese.

Not ready to wait or spend? Langhe Nebbiolo comes from the same area and grape, and drinks well young.
