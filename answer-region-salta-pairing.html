What pairs with Salta Torrontés?

Short Answer

Torrontés is aromatic, high acid, and dry despite smelling like a fruit basket. That combo needs food with heat or acid of its own, otherwise the wine's perfume just sits there looking pretty and slightly at odds with the plate. Chili in Thai food doesn't fight the wine's aromatics, it rides them - the floral lift of jasmine and rose petal on the nose cools the spice while the acidity resets your palate between bites. Ceviche works for the same reason: citrus mirrors the wine's own backbone rather than competing with it. Empanadas with chimichurri are the local answer, and honestly the right one - the herbaceous, garlicky punch needs a wine with real acid and a bit of grip in the finish, not a soft neutral white that disappears under it. Serve it cold, around 8-9°C, colder than you'd expect for something this aromatic - warmer and the perfume tips into soapy. Avoid heavy cream sauces or anything too delicate; Torrontés isn't a wallflower, it wants something with a bit of fight in it.
