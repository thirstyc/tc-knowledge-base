What pairs with South African Chenin Blanc?

Short Answer

Chenin's whole appeal on the table is that bracing acidity, and it works because acid cuts fat and resets your palate between bites, which is exactly what oily fish and shellfish need. A grilled snoek or simple prawns get lifted rather than weighed down, since the wine's acid slices through the richness instead of competing with it. Fresh, tangy cheeses like chèvre or a young feta work on a matching principle, their own lactic sharpness meets the wine's acidity rather than clashing with it. Light pasta in butter or olive oil, especially with lemon or herbs, plays the same cutting role, the wine trims the richness of the sauce so it doesn't feel heavy. Where it struggles is with anything too sweet or too tannic-heavy in the food itself, that flattens Chenin's natural zip. Keep it cold, around 8-10°C, and let the acid do the actual work rather than the fruit.
