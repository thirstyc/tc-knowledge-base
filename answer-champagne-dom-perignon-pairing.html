What pairs with Dom Pérignon?

Short Answer

Its pairing range comes down to structure: high acidity, fine persistent bubbles and real depth from extended lees aging give it enough backbone to stand up to richer food than most Champagne, not just canapés. Think roasted or poached lobster, langoustine, scallops seared in butter, or a proper caviar service, the wine's acid cuts the richness while its toasted, nutty lees character echoes the buttery or briny notes in the food rather than clashing with it. It also works well with richer poultry like guinea fowl or roast chicken in cream sauce, and with aged, nutty cheeses like Comté, where the wine's own brioche notes find an echo. Where it falls short: very sweet desserts, which make the wine taste thin and sharp, and anything heavily spiced or smoked, which overwhelms its subtlety. This isn't a wine that needs an occasion to justify it, but it does need food with enough weight to meet it halfway. Serve at 10-11°C, not ice cold, so the aromatics and texture actually show.
