What are Sonoma wines like?

Short Answer

Sonoma is bigger and more varied than neighbouring Napa, and generally better value. Because it stretches from the cool Pacific coast to warm inland valleys, it makes a much wider range of styles.

The Russian River Valley, cooled by fog that pushes in through a gap in the coastal hills, is known for Pinot Noir and Chardonnay with bright acidity. The Sonoma Coast, closer to the ocean, is cooler still and makes leaner, more mineral versions.

Dry Creek Valley is Zinfandel country, including old vineyards over a century old, giving spicy, brambly reds.

Alexander Valley, warmer and inland, makes softer, riper Cabernet Sauvignon than Napa's.

Carneros, shared with Napa at the southern end, is cool and windy, ideal for Pinot Noir, Chardonnay and traditional-method sparkling wine.

Sonoma's style is usually a little fresher and less polished than Napa's, and its wine country feels more agricultural than luxurious.

Where to start: a Russian River Pinot Noir or Chardonnay, or a Dry Creek Zinfandel with barbecue.
