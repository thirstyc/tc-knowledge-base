What should I actually eat with California Sangiovese?

Short Answer

Treat it the way you'd treat any medium-bodied red with decent acid and moderate tannin: food that has some fat or richness to push against. Sangiovese's whole job at the table is cutting through, so tomato-based dishes work shockingly well even though tomatoes are murder on most reds, because the wine's acidity matches the sauce's acidity instead of getting steamrolled by it. Grilled sausages, a roast chicken with crispy skin, mushroom risotto, or a hard aged cheese all give it something to work with. Where it struggles is delicate or very lean food, fish, plain salads, that sort of thing, because there's nothing for the acidity and tannin to grip onto and the wine just feels aggressive. Avoid pairing it with anything too sweet or heavily spiced, since California versions often run riper and higher in alcohol than Italian Sangiovese, and that heat gets more obvious next to chili or sugar. Pour it at 16°C, not room temperature, and it'll behave itself.
