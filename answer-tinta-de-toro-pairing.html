What pairs with Tinta de Toro?

Short Answer

Tinta de Toro, grown in the Toro region of Castilla y León, is basically Tempranillo pushed to an extreme by heat and altitude: thick skins, high sugar, high alcohol, and a wall of tannin. That tannin is the whole reason for the pairing rule. Tannin binds to protein and fat, softening on the palate when it meets them, so a rich grilled steak, lamb, or a slow-braised stew gives the wine something to grab onto and round off. Without that fat, the tannin can feel drying and aggressive, chewing on your gums rather than the meat. Aged, hard cheeses work for the same reason, the fat content tempers the grip. Skip anything delicate, light salads, white fish, cream sauces, the wine will flatten them and taste harsher than it actually is. Serve around 17-18°C, slightly below typical red wine service, since the alcohol can feel hot if poured too warm, and let it breathe for half an hour before drinking.
