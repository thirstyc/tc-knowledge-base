Why is Piccolit a gem?

Short Answer

Picolit is rare almost by biological accident. The vine is prone to floral abortion, meaning a large percentage of its flower clusters never develop into fruit, so yields are naturally tiny compared to almost any other variety grown commercially. That scarcity made it a prized, even aristocratic wine in 18th-century Friuli, served at European courts before phylloxera and changing tastes nearly wiped it out. It survived through a handful of dedicated growers in the Colli Orientali del Friuli who kept propagating it despite the low returns, and modern clonal selection has improved fruit set without erasing the grape's stubborn tendencies. What you get is a small-production, single-region dessert wine with real historical pedigree rather than a manufactured luxury story. It's a gem because it shouldn't really exist commercially at all—the economics only work because people are willing to pay for honeyed apricot and acacia in a tiny glass instead of a big one.
