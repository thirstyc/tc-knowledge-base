Why is Banyuls hard to find outside France?

Banyuls comes from a small, specific patch of coastline near the Spanish border, where Grenache is grown on steep schist terraces too tight and steep for machines — everything is hand-harvested, which caps production before you even get to the cellar. Fortified sweet wine is also a shrinking category globally; demand isn't what it was a century ago, so there's less incentive to scale up or push hard into export markets. Add to that a strong local market — the French drink a fair share of what's made close to home — and you're left with very little heading overseas. It's less a case of scarcity for scarcity's sake and more a small, labor-intensive style from a small appellation serving a market that's mostly domestic and no longer huge to begin with. If you find a bottle outside France, it's usually because an importer specifically sought it out.
