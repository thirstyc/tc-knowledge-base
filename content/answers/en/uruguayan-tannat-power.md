Why does Uruguayan Tannat taste so powerful and tannic?

The tannin comes from the grape itself, not just the climate. Tannat originated in southwest France, around Madiran, where it was already known as one of the most tannic red varieties grown anywhere, thanks to thick skins and a high ratio of skin to juice. Uruguay's main wine regions, concentrated around Canelones near Montevideo rather than the beach towns further east, have a mild, maritime-influenced climate that lets the grape ripen fully without turning jammy, so you get ripe tannin and structure rather than harsh, green tannin. Extended maceration during fermentation pulls even more tannin and colour out of those skins, which is why the wines often feel dense and chewy young. That structure is exactly why Tannat pairs so well with grilled red meat, the fat and protein soften the perceived tannin on the palate. Serve it around 16-17°C, and don't be afraid to decant a younger bottle before drinking.
