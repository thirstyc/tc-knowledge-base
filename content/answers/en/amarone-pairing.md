What pairs with Amarone?

Amarone's high alcohol, dried-fruit concentration and soft but present tannins mean it needs food with real fat and weight to stand up to it, or it just overwhelms the plate. Think braised short rib, osso buco, venison or other game, anything slow-cooked with a rich sauce that can meet the wine's intensity. Aged hard cheeses like a well-matured Parmigiano Reggiano or aged pecorino work too, since their concentrated, salty savoriness balances the wine's raisiny sweetness without getting lost. A mushroom or truffle risotto is a classic regional match for the same reason, earthy richness against dried-fruit depth. What doesn't work is anything delicate, raw, or acid-driven like salads or ceviche, the wine will flatten it completely. Serve Amarone closer to 18°C, not fridge-cold, and decant it for at least an hour; the alcohol and tannin need air to settle before the fruit and savory notes come forward properly.
