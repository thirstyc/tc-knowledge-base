What pairs with Amarone?

Amarone is made from grapes dried for months after harvest, which concentrates sugar, glycerol and flavour before fermentation even starts; the result is a wine that ferments fully dry but tastes almost sweet, with alcohol often pushing past 15% and tannins softened by that extra ripeness and time. Food needs real fat and weight to meet that intensity or it just gets steamrolled. Braised short rib, osso buco, venison or anything slow-cooked in a rich sauce works because the dish's fat and the wine's glycerol are operating on the same scale. Aged hard cheeses like well-matured Parmigiano or aged pecorino match for the same reason, their concentrated saltiness cutting the raisiny depth. A mushroom or truffle risotto does the regional version of this, earthy richness against dried-fruit weight. Skip anything raw, delicate or acid-driven, salads and ceviche just vanish next to it. Serve around 18°C, not fridge-cold, and decant for at least an hour so the alcohol and tannin settle before the fruit shows properly.
