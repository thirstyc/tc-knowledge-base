What pairs with Campania wine?

This is a rare case where the pairing logic writes itself, because the food and the wine grew up on the same hillside. Fiano and Greco, with their bright acidity and bitter almond snap, cut straight through buffalo mozzarella's fat and cream, and do the same job for oily seafood pasta like linguine alle vongole. Aglianico is a different animal — tannic, dark-fruited, structured almost like a young Nebbiolo — so it wants something with fat and char to soften it, like grilled lamb or a ragù that's simmered for hours. Neapolitan pizza actually splits the difference: the acid in a good Fiano handles the tomato, while a lighter, chilled Aglianico can handle a meatier pie. Serve the whites around 10-12°C, cool enough to keep that almond bitterness crisp without numbing it, and give Aglianico a real 16-18°C so its tannins soften and the fruit shows through instead of just gripping your teeth.
