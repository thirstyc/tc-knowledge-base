What pairs with Zweigelt?

Zweigelt's whole appeal is that it drinks like a red but behaves like a white at the table -- bright cherry fruit, low tannin, snappy acidity -- which makes it unusually flexible with food that would clash with a heavier red. Its acidity cuts through the richness of roasted or grilled poultry without needing tannin to do the work, and because there's so little tannin to begin with, it won't turn bitter against delicate proteins like grilled fish, something a Cabernet or Syrah would butcher. Light salads work for the same reason white wine usually gets the nod -- there's no harsh tannin-vinaigrette clash to worry about. It's also a good call for charcuterie boards, mild cheeses, or anything with a slightly smoky char, since the fruit has enough presence to stand up without overpowering. Serve it lightly chilled, around 13-15°C, closer to a rosé than a typical red, which keeps the fruit lively and makes it genuinely refreshing rather than just light for light's sake.
