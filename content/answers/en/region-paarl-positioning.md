Why does Paarl matter beyond Stellenbosch?

Paarl is one of South Africa's original wine regions, older in vine age than much of Stellenbosch, but it's spent decades in that neighbor's shadow simply because Stellenbosch built the stronger brand and tourism infrastructure. What Paarl actually has is old bush-vine Chenin Blanc, some of it dry-farmed and decades old, planted before anyone thought to market South African wine internationally. Chenin is the grape South Africa has genuinely made its own: it arrived with Dutch settlers centuries ago and adapted so well it now covers more land there than anywhere else in the world, including its native Loire. Old, unirrigated vines naturally yield less fruit, but that fruit is more concentrated, giving Paarl Chenin real depth without needing new oak or manipulation to get there. Because the region's reputation hasn't caught up to Stellenbosch's, prices stay honest. It's arguably the best-value serious white wine in the Cape right now.
