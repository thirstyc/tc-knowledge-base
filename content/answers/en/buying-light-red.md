Best light red wine?

Pinot Noir and Beaujolais are the go-to light reds. They're low in tannin, bright in acidity and full of red fruit, and both taste great slightly chilled.

Pinot Noir is the benchmark. Look to Burgundy for elegance, or to Oregon, New Zealand and Germany, where it's called Spätburgunder.

Beaujolais, made from Gamay, is juicy and fun. The crus, like Fleurie and Morgon, add more depth without losing freshness.

A few lighter reds worth discovering:

- Frappato from Sicily: floral and strawberry-scented.
- Valpolicella from northern Italy: cherry and almond, easy-drinking.
- Schiava from Alto Adige: pale, delicate and aromatic.
- Poulsard from the Jura: so light it can pass for rosé.
- Zweigelt from Austria: juicy and peppery.
- Cinsault: soft and perfumed, especially from South Africa and southern France.
- Lambrusco: a lightly sparkling red from Emilia-Romagna, great with food.

Light reds are especially good with salmon, roast chicken, charcuterie and pizza, and they're a smart choice for warm weather.

Serve them cooler than bigger reds, around 12–14°C. Twenty minutes in the fridge is usually enough.
