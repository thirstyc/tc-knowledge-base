Can you age Australian Cabernet?

Good Australian Cabernet ages well because it starts with the raw materials for it: firm tannin, real acidity, and enough fruit concentration to outlast both. Regions like Coonawarra, with its terra rossa soil over limestone, and cooler pockets of Margaret River and Yarra Valley produce Cabernet with structure rather than just ripeness. Over 10 to 20 years, tannins that felt gripping in youth soften and knit into the fruit, savoury notes emerge (dried herb, tobacco, cedar from oak), and the wine shifts from primary fruit to something more textured and layered. That's the actual mechanism, not magic, just slow chemical polymerisation of tannin and pigment. Not every bottle is built for this. Cheaper, fruit-forward styles are made to drink young and won't improve, they'll just fade. If you're cellaring, look for wines with genuine tannic grip and acidity on release, store around 12–14°C, and don't assume price or region alone guarantees longevity. Taste one every few years to track where it's at.
