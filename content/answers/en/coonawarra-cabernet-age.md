Can you age Coonawarra Cabernet?

Yes, and it's one of the more reliable ageing bets in the Southern Hemisphere. The terra rossa soil over limestone gives Coonawarra Cabernet firm, ripe tannins alongside real acidity, and that combination is what actually lets a wine improve rather than just survive in the cellar. Tannin and acid both act as natural preservatives, slowing oxidation and giving the fruit time to soften into secondary flavours: cedar, dried herbs, tobacco, a graphite edge. A well-made bottle can easily hold for 15 to 20 years, sometimes longer from a strong vintage. Young, it can be a bit closed and austere, all structure and not much charm, which is exactly why patience pays off here. If you're drinking one with some age, pull the cork an hour or two ahead, or decant it, and serve around 16-18°C rather than fridge-adjacent cellar temperature. Don't assume every bottle is built for the long haul, though; entry-level Coonawarra Cabernet is made to drink now, not to outlive you.
