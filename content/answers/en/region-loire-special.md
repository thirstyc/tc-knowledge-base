What's special about Loire Valley wines?

The Loire makes more different styles than any other French region, and it does it with high acidity and modest prices. Follow the river and the wine changes with it.

Near the Atlantic, Muscadet is made from Melon de Bourgogne: light, lemony and saline, often aged on its lees, which the label calls "sur lie". It's the classic with oysters.

In the middle, Anjou, Saumur and Touraine are Chenin Blanc country. The same grape makes bone-dry Savennières, off-dry and sparkling Vouvray, and sweet Coteaux du Layon, so reading the label matters.

The same stretch grows Cabernet Franc for Chinon, Bourgueil and Saumur-Champigny: light, savoury reds with red fruit, graphite and a leafy edge, best served slightly cool.

Upriver, Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé are Sauvignon Blanc at its most mineral and restrained.

The region is also a centre of low-intervention winemaking, so natural wine lists lean heavily on Loire bottles.

The common thread is freshness. These are food wines, rarely heavy or high in alcohol, and they remain some of the best value in France.
