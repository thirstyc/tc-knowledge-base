Why does Israeli Cabernet taste so ripe and bold?

The heat is real, that's not marketing. Israel's Mediterranean climate delivers long, intense sunshine that packs Cabernet grapes with sugar and dark fruit, which is where the ripeness and boldness come from in the first place. What keeps it from turning into jam is elevation. Vineyards in the Golan Heights and Judean Hills sit at meaningful altitude, sometimes over 700 or 800 metres, and that height brings a real drop in temperature after sunset. That diurnal swing slows sugar accumulation at night while the daytime heat still builds flavour and colour, so the grapes end up ripe and concentrated but with acidity and tannin intact rather than cooked out. The result is Cabernet with real structure underneath the fruit, not just a heavy, flat wine. Decant it if it's young, since the tannins can be firm, and serve around 16 to 18°C rather than room temperature in a warm house. It's a serious, modern expression of a very old wine-growing country, not a novelty bottle.
