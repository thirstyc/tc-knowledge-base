Why does Rebula taste so mineral and crisp?

Rebula (the same grape as Italy's Ribolla Gialla) is grown mainly in Slovenia's Brda hills, right across the border from Friuli, on marl and sandstone soils locally called ponca. That soil, combined with the region's cool nights even in summer, is what drives the acidity — grapes ripen sugar during warm days but hold onto malic acid overnight, so you get real freshness even at full ripeness. The chalky, mineral-tinged soils also show up as a saline, stony edge on the palate rather than fruit-forward richness. Some producers ferment it with extended skin contact, orange-wine style, which adds texture and grip without killing the acid backbone. Serve it around 10-12°C — cold enough to keep the acidity crisp, but not so cold you mute the chamomile and citrus-pith aromatics that skin contact and cool-climate ripening bring out. It's a wine built for structure and cut, not up-front fruit.
