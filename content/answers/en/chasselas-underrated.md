Why does Chasselas fly under the radar outside the Alps?

Chasselas has almost no aromatic signature of its own. It's a grape that tastes mostly of where it's grown and how it's handled, which made it a favourite table grape for eating fresh but a hard sell as a serious wine grape anywhere without the patience to coax character out of it. Outside Switzerland and Savoie, most winemakers gave up on it, since neutral grapes don't market themselves. But in Vaud and Valais, generations of vignerons figured out how to get real depth from it: old vines, careful lees aging, sites with the right mix of lake reflection and mineral soil. The result is wine with a saline, faintly nutty texture that rewards attention even though it never announces itself. The catch is that almost none of it leaves Switzerland, since the Swiss drink most of what they make. So the world's impression of Chasselas is stuck at grocery-store table grape, while the people actually growing it know it can be something quietly serious.
