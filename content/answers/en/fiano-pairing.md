What pairs with Fiano?

Fiano has more phenolic texture and body than most Italian whites, which is what lets it stand up to richer food without acidity alone doing all the work. That weight comes partly from thicker skins and the volcanic soils around Avellino, which push mineral density and glycerol-like mouthfeel rather than pure citrus zing. Seafood risotto works because the wine's texture matches the dish's creaminess instead of getting steamrolled by it. Roast chicken benefits from Fiano's low-key nutty, honeyed notes, which read like a savory echo of the skin and fat. Aged cheese is the more interesting call: firmer, nuttier cheeses like aged pecorino pick up on Fiano's own hazelnut character rather than clashing with it. Serve around 10-12°C, a touch warmer than a basic Pinot Grigio, so the texture and aromatics actually show. Avoid pairing it with anything too delicate; it will overpower a plain green salad or light fish.
