Why is Montepulciano d'Abruzzo a value?

Montepulciano d'Abruzzo punches above its price because the region's economics haven't caught up to the wine's quality. Abruzzo sits between the Apennines and the Adriatic, giving the Montepulciano grape (no relation to the Tuscan town or Sangiovese-based Vino Nobile di Montepulciano, a constant source of confusion) cool nights that preserve acidity even as the fruit ripens fully in the daytime heat. The grape itself gives deep color, soft tannins, and dark plum and cherry fruit with a rustic, savory edge, so it drinks like something twice its price. Land and labor in Abruzzo are cheaper than in Tuscany or Piedmont, and the region never built the marketing machine those areas did, so prices stayed grounded while the wine improved. Look for producers who keep yields down; high-volume versions can taste thin and jammy. Serve around 16-18°C, slightly cooler than a big Tuscan red, and it'll handle a Tuesday-night pasta with tomato and sausage without complaint.
