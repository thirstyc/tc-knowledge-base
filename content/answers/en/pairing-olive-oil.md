Wine with olive oil?

A crisp Mediterranean white, like Vermentino, Verdicchio, Assyrtiko or Albariño, or a light, savoury red from the same places the olive oil comes from.

Olive oil is rarely eaten alone, but a dish dressed generously with it shares its flavours with the wine. The fruitiness, bitterness and peppery bite of good extra virgin oil all matter.

Matching the oil's character:

- Delicate, buttery oils: softer whites like Pinot Grigio, Soave or Pinot Blanc.
- Grassy, green oils: Sauvignon Blanc, Vermentino or Verdejo, which echo the herbal notes.
- Peppery, bitter oils, like early-harvest Tuscan oil: savoury reds with good acidity, like Chianti or other Sangiovese.

Regional pairings work beautifully: a Tuscan oil with Chianti, a Greek oil with Assyrtiko, a Spanish oil with Albariño or Rioja, a Provençal oil with dry rosé.

For bread dipped in olive oil, try a dry rosé or a crisp sparkling wine, which refresh the palate between bites.

Avoid heavily oaked whites and big, tannic reds, which can make fresh olive oil taste bitter and flat.
