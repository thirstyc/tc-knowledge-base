Why does Fetească Albă fly under the radar?

Romania barely registers on most wine drinkers' radar at all, let alone its native white grapes, and there's a real historical reason for that. Under communist-era central planning, Romanian wine production was geared toward bulk output and export quotas rather than quality or identity, so investment in native varieties like Fetească Albă stalled for decades while the rest of the wine world was building reputations. Since 1989, producers have been slowly replanting, modernizing, and trying to convince international drinkers that Romania makes more than cheap bulk wine, but that kind of reputation shift takes generations, not vintages. Fetească Albă never got the marketing push that grapes like Chardonnay or Sauvignon Blanc did, despite offering a genuinely distinct, acid-driven, aromatic white at a fraction of the price. It's one of Eastern Europe's best-kept secrets mostly because nobody's finished telling the story yet.
