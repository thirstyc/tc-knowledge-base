Can you age White Burgundy?

Yes, and well-made White Burgundy from top villages rewards real patience, though most bottles you'll find are meant to drink within a few years. The premier cru and grand cru wines from places like Meursault, Puligny-Montrachet, and Chassagne-Montrachet have the acidity and phenolic structure from barrel aging to hold and evolve for 10-15 years, sometimes longer in cold cellars. Young, they can taste tight, appley, even a bit austere; with a decade they shift into notes of hazelnut, honey, brioche, and beeswax as the wine's reductive elements soften. The catch is premature oxidation, a real problem that hit a chunk of vintages from the late 1990s through the 2000s, so provenance and storage matter enormously; a wine that's traveled badly or sat warm will die early instead of gracefully. Store it on its side, cool and dark, and don't assume every bottle is a candidate: village-level wines are usually best within 5-8 years, save the long cellar for premier and grand cru.
