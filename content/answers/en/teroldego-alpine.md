Why does Teroldego taste so Alpine-structured?

Teroldego grows almost exclusively on the Campo Rotaliano, a flat gravel plain in Trentino ringed by mountains, and that setting is the whole story. The alluvial gravel drains fast and reflects heat during the day, then the surrounding peaks send cold air rolling down at night, so the swing between day and night temperatures is dramatic. That swing is what builds structure: the vine photosynthesizes hard in the heat, then the cold nights shut down respiration and lock in acidity and colour instead of burning it off. Add thick skins bred for a short growing season, and you get wine loaded with anthocyanins and tannin, dark as ink, with a grippy, almost stony backbone. It is not rustic for the sake of it, it is a direct readout of a specific mountain microclimate. Give it air, decant an hour if it is young, and serve around 16-18°C. Too cold and the tannin turns hard and closed instead of structured.
