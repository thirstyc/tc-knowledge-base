Is wine healthy?

Current health guidance is more cautious than it used to be. Older studies suggesting moderate wine drinking protects the heart have been challenged, and major health bodies now emphasise that alcohol carries risks at any level.

The change came from better research methods. Many earlier studies compared drinkers with non-drinkers without accounting for the fact that the non-drinking group often included people who had stopped for health reasons, which made moderate drinkers look healthier than they were.

The World Health Organization has stated that no level of alcohol consumption is safe for health, and several countries have lowered their recommended limits in recent years.

Wine does contain antioxidants such as resveratrol, which is where much of the "red wine is good for you" story began, but the amounts in a glass are small, and no health authority recommends drinking wine to obtain them.

None of this means a glass of wine is dangerous. It means the honest answer is that wine is a pleasure with risks attached, not a health food.

For anything personal, especially if you're pregnant, taking medication or managing a health condition, ask a doctor rather than the internet.
