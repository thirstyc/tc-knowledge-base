Is Chablis Chardonnay?

Yes, Chablis is 100% Chardonnay, but the name refers to the place, not the grape, which is exactly why people get confused when it tastes nothing like the buttery, vanilla-laced Chardonnay they know from warmer regions. Chablis sits at the northern edge of Burgundy, cooler than the Côte d'Or, planted on Kimmeridgian limestone full of ancient marine fossils. Most producers skip new oak entirely, fermenting and aging in stainless steel or neutral vessels, which means none of the buttery malolactic richness or vanilla toast you'd get from a heavily oaked New World Chardonnay. What you get instead is pure fruit shaped by soil and cool climate: green apple, citrus, a flinty or oyster-shell edge, and racy acidity. Grand cru and premier cru sites sometimes see a little older oak for texture, but the wood is never the point. So yes, it's Chardonnay, but treat any assumption about Chardonnay's flavour as void the moment the label says Chablis. Serve it cold, around 10-12°C, with oysters or something equally briny.
