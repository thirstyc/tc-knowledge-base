Why does Finger Lakes Riesling taste so crisp and precise?

The lakes themselves are doing the work. Seneca and Cayuga are deep enough that they don't freeze, so they release stored summer heat through autumn and buffer the vineyards against the brutal winters that would otherwise kill Riesling vines outright. That moderation stretches the ripening window, letting grapes hang into October while acid stays high and sugars climb slowly rather than spiking. The result is wine with a taut, linear structure, bright green-apple and citrus fruit, and a mineral snap that never gets buried under residual sugar, even in the region's off-dry bottlings. Serve it cold, around 8 to 10°C, and it holds up to being poured well before dinner and still tasting sharp an hour later. This is one of the coldest serious wine regions in the US, which sounds like a liability until you taste what that cold actually preserves. It's not trying to be a warm-climate Riesling with sugar bolted on; the acidity is structural, not stylistic.
