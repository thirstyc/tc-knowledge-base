What pairs with Lanaudière reds?

These are cold-climate hybrid reds, usually made from Marquette or Frontenac, with bright acidity and modest tannin rather than the dense, ripe structure you'd get from a warm-region cabernet. That acidity is exactly what cuts through fatty, rich Quebec cooking: tourtière, grilled sausage, anything with a pastry crust or rendered pork fat benefits from a wine that resets your palate between bites instead of piling more weight on top of it. Farmhouse cheeses work for the same reason, the wine's acid balances the cheese's fat without needing heavy tannin to do the job. Serve these reds slightly cooler than you would a Bordeaux, around 14 to 16°C, which keeps the fruit lively and stops the wine from tasting flabby. This isn't food for a wine built to age gracefully in a cellar for a decade, it's food for a wine meant to be poured young, at a table, with people who grew the ingredients twenty minutes down the road.
