Why does Sciaccarello taste so elegant and mineral?

Sciaccarello grows almost exclusively on the granite slopes around Ajaccio and Sartène, and that rock is doing most of the work. Granite drains fast and holds almost no nutrients, so the vine struggles a little, yields stay low, and the grape concentrates flavour without piling on sugar or tannin. The thin skins mean this is a pale, light-bodied red, closer in structure to Pinot Noir than to anything Mediterranean you'd expect from an island this far south. What keeps the acidity honest is altitude: many of these vineyards sit at real elevation in Corsica's interior, so hot days are followed by cold nights, and that swing preserves the acid the coastal heat would otherwise burn off. The 'mineral' note people pick up isn't metaphorical marketing-speak, it's the saline, stony finish that comes from that granite and the sea air moving through the vines. Serve it a touch cool, around 14-16C, and the acidity and freshness both show properly instead of getting muddled by warmth.
