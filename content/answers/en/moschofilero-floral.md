Why does Moschofilero taste so floral and citrusy?

Moschofilero comes from Mantinia in the Peloponnese, grown at fairly high altitude, often above 600 metres, where cool nights slow ripening and preserve the acidity that gives it its lift. The grape itself has a distinctively thick, pinkish-grey skin, but it's pressed gently and made into a white (or occasionally light rosé), and it happens to share the highly aromatic terpene profile found in the Muscat family, even though botanically it isn't related. That's the source of the rose petal, citrus blossom and spice notes people pick up, it's not a stylistic choice by the winemaker, it's baked into the grape's chemistry. The altitude keeps sugar levels and alcohol moderate while acidity stays bright, which is why the wine reads as fresh and citrusy rather than heavy or fruit-forward. Serve it cold, around 8-10C, with seafood, Greek mezze or anything with lemon and herbs, it's built for that kind of table, not for sipping in isolation.
