Why does Marzemino taste so bright?

The brightness comes down to where it grows. Marzemino is planted on the slopes of Trentino-Alto Adige, where big swings between warm days and cold nights slow down ripening and let the grape hang onto its malic acid instead of burning it off, the way it would in a hotter, flatter region. That acid is what gives the wine its lively, almost tart edge rather than a soft, jammy character. Altitude plays a role too: thinner air and stronger UV at height thicken the skins slightly, which contributes a faint mineral, almost stony note without adding much tannin, since the grape itself is naturally low in it. The result is a red that's more about freshness and lift than power or weight. It's built to be drunk young, generally within a few years of the vintage, because that acid and fruit brightness are the whole point, and they fade quickly with age rather than developing into something richer.
