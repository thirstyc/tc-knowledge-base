Why does Wachau Grüner Veltliner taste so peppery and mineral?

The Wachau's steep terraces climb straight up from the Danube, and that river does real work — it moderates temperature and reflects light onto the vines, while the surrounding forested hills push cool air down at night. That day-night swing is what preserves acidity even as sugars ripen fully. The soils split roughly into mineral-rich loess on the lower slopes and harder gneiss and schist higher up, and the wines from each read differently — loess gives richer, rounder fruit, gneiss gives more tension and stone. The white pepper character isn't a metaphor: it comes from rotundone, a real aromatic compound also found in cool-climate Syrah. Add citrus, some weight from extended lees contact, and you get a dry white with genuine structure underneath the aromatics. Serve Federspiel around 8-10°C; give Smaragd a bit more warmth, closer to 12°C, so the body and spice show properly.
