Why does Cremant taste so elegant and complex?

It's made the same way as Champagne, full stop. Crémant undergoes traditional method production: a second fermentation happens inside the bottle, trapping carbon dioxide as fine bubbles, and the wine then rests on its spent yeast cells, the lees, for months. That lees contact is where the elegance comes from, autolysis releases compounds that give brioche, biscuit and creamy texture instead of just fruit and fizz. Regions like the Loire, Alsace, Burgundy and Limoux each bring their own base grapes and terroir, chenin blanc, pinot blanc, chardonnay, so the character shifts from place to place, but the technique and the minimum aging requirements are the same discipline Champagne uses. What you're missing isn't quality, it's brand geography. Serve it cold, around 8 to 10°C, in a proper glass rather than a flute so you can actually smell it. Given a couple of extra years in bottle, a good Crémant develops real complexity, toasted notes, tension, length, that has nothing to do with novelty and everything to do with method.
