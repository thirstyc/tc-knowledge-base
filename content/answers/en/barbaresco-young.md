Can you drink young Barbaresco?

Yes, more comfortably than Barolo. Barbaresco comes from the same Nebbiolo grape but ripens in a warmer, sandier corner of Piedmont, so the tannins are naturally softer and the wine needs less time to unwind. Young Barbaresco, even at three or four years old, is drinkable, though it will still show a firm, grippy edge and those high-toned rose and tar aromas that haven't fully integrated yet. Give it five to ten years and the tannin resolves into something silkier, with the fruit turning toward dried cherry and leather. If you're opening one young, decant it for an hour and pair it with something fatty, grilled meat or mushrooms, so the tannin has something to grip onto besides your palate. Serve at 16-18°C. It's not a wine you need to cellar for a decade to enjoy, but it does reward the wait if you have it.
