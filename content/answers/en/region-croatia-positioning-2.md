Why does Croatia matter?

Vines have grown along this coast since Greek colonists planted them millennia before Rome existed, so what looks like a young wine industry is actually one of Europe's oldest sitting under new labels. Istria, the peninsula in the north, shares the same limestone and flysch soils as Slovenia's Vipava and Brda regions just across a border that means nothing to geology, which is why its whites, especially Malvazija Istarska, have that same mineral snap. The real gap is time lost: war in the 1990s gutted the industry just as the rest of the world was modernizing, so Croatia is effectively catching up two decades late. Winemakers have spent that time replanting old indigenous varieties and dialing in quality, and it's working. Prices, though, are still set by reputation rather than what's in the glass, which means there's real value here for anyone willing to look past the unfamiliar names.
