Why does Napa Cabernet taste so ripe and powerful?

Napa's warmth is the whole story here. Long, sunny growing seasons push grapes to full ripeness reliably, which means more sugar at harvest, and since sugar converts to alcohol during fermentation, Napa Cabernet routinely lands at 14.5% or higher, sometimes north of 15%. That ripeness also means darker fruit flavours — think blackcurrant and blackberry rather than the tart red cherry you'd get from a cooler region — and softer, riper tannins, since tannins mature and smooth out the longer grapes hang on the vine in the sun. Mountain vineyards like those on Howell Mountain or Diamond Mountain sit above the fog line, giving more intense sun exposure and thicker grape skins, which translates to even bigger, more structured wines. Producers typically lean into this with generous new oak aging, adding vanilla, baking spice, and a rounder texture on top of the fruit. The whole style is a deliberate showcase of ripeness and power rather than restraint. Decant it or let it breathe, and serve at 16-18°C, never fridge-cold.
