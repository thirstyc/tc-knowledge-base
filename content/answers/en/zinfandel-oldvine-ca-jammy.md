Why does Zinfandel (Old-Vine California) taste so jammy and powerful?

Old vines, often 50 to over 100 years old in parts of California like Lodi and Sonoma, naturally yield less fruit as they age, and lower yield means the vine pours more concentrated flavour into fewer berries. Combine that with Zinfandel's habit of ripening unevenly, which pushes growers to leave fruit hanging longer so stragglers catch up, and you get very high sugar levels by harvest. Ferment that fully and alcohol climbs, often past 14.5%, sometimes well beyond, and that alcohol is what gives the wine its sense of weight and warmth, amplifying the jammy, baked-fruit character rather than fresh red-berry brightness. Many of these old vineyards are also dry-farmed, no irrigation, which stresses the vine further and concentrates things even more. The result is a big, spicy, full-throttle wine, closer to blackberry preserve and pepper than to anything restrained. Serve it a touch cooler than typical red, around 16-17°C, so the alcohol doesn't dominate, and pair it with something equally assertive, it's not a food-shy wine.
