Why does Georgia matter?

Georgia isn't claiming the oldest winemaking tradition as a marketing line, archaeologists have found grape residue in clay vessels there dating back roughly 8,000 years, which makes it the earliest confirmed wine production anywhere. What matters more than the age claim is continuity: the qvevri method, fermenting and aging wine in buried clay vessels lined with beeswax, never disappeared the way ancient techniques elsewhere did. It survived empires, Soviet collectivization and decades of industrial wine policy, and small producers kept making it the old way regardless. Saperavi, a teinturier grape with colour and tannin all the way through the flesh, and Rkatsiteli, an amber-skin white that can go fully skin-contact, don't really exist at scale anywhere else, so Georgia is the only place you can taste these grapes made in the method that shaped them. This isn't a trend riding the natural wine wave, it's the origin point that trend keeps referencing without fully crediting.
