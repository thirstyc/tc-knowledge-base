What pairs with Xinomavro?

Xinomavro's tannin and acid are aggressive enough that they need food with real fat and protein to grip onto - on their own they just feel harsh and drying. Lamb is the classic match, especially slow-roasted or grilled with its own fat rendering down, because tannin binds to fat and protein, softening on the palate while cutting through richness. Stews and braises work for the same reason: long cooking builds savoury depth and umami that echoes the wine's own dried-tomato, olive, and herb notes, while the fat blunts the tannic edge. Aged sheep's-milk cheeses, moussaka, or grilled octopus with olive oil are also good bets - anything with enough salt, fat, or char to answer the wine's structure. Avoid delicate or lean dishes; they'll just taste like they're under attack. If you're working with a younger, still-tannic bottle, lean even harder into fattier cuts and let the food do some of the softening the cellar hasn't finished yet.
