Why does Schiava fly under the radar outside South Tyrol?

Schiava never fit the story the wine market has told for decades, which rewarded darkness, tannin, and oak as proxies for seriousness. Schiava is pale, low in tannin, and finishes fast, all things that used to read as "simple" rather than "deliberate." It also comes from South Tyrol, an Italian region that speaks German, sits closer to Innsbruck than Rome in style, and has never had much interest in marketing itself to international critics the way Tuscany or Piedmont did. So the grape stayed local, drunk by people who already knew it, while export attention went to bigger reds that photographed better in a glass. The irony is that Schiava was chillable, food-friendly, and low in alcohol before any of those became selling points. It's now getting a second look precisely because those qualities are back in fashion, but the wine itself hasn't changed at all. It was always this good for a Tuesday; nobody outside the Alps was paying attention.
