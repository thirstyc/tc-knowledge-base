What pairs with Washington Cabernet?

Washington Cabernet builds its structure from a huge day-to-night temperature swing in the Columbia Valley: hot, sun-drenched afternoons ripen the fruit fully while cold desert nights lock in acidity and keep tannins firm rather than flabby. That combination of ripe fruit, real acid, and grippy tannin needs food that has fat and char to meet it halfway. Grilled lamb is the classic match, the tannin cutting through the richness while the smoke echoes the wine's own dark, savoury edge. A well-seared steak works the same way, especially anything with a hard crust from a hot pan or open flame, since the Maillard char picks up the wine's roasted, spicy notes. Avoid delicate or lean dishes here, fish, light salads, anything without real fat or protein will get flattened by the tannin. Serve it around 16 to 18°C, a touch cooler than room temperature, so the fruit stays lifted and the alcohol doesn't dominate. This is a wine built for the grill, not the picnic table.
