What pairs with Veneto wine?

Veneto isn't one wine, so it isn't one pairing — the trick is matching the specific style, not the postcode. Soave, made mostly from Garganega, has enough weight and a faint almond bitterness to carry risotto, especially one built on mushrooms or shellfish stock. Prosecco's low alcohol and persistent bubble exist to cut through fried food and salty snacks, which is why it works better with pizza or fritto misto than with anything delicate or dessert-sweet. Amarone is the outlier: made from partially dried Corvina grapes, it's dense, high in alcohol, often north of 15%, with dried-fruit and bitter-chocolate notes that need something equally stubborn — braised short rib, aged hard cheese, wild boar ragù. Serve Soave around 10-12°C, Prosecco colder at 6-8°C, and Amarone closer to 18°C like a serious red. Treat the region as three separate wine categories that happen to share a border, and the pairings sort themselves out.
