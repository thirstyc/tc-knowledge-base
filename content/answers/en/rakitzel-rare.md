Why is Rakitzel hard to find outside Czechia?

Rakitzel is a niche Moravian variety with tiny total plantings, grown almost exclusively within Czech borders, so there's simply never been enough volume to justify building export channels. Czech wine culture is also largely domestic in orientation, most of what's made gets drunk locally, at wine bars and family cellars, long before anyone thinks about shipping pallets abroad. There's also no big marketing push behind it internationally, unlike say Grüner Veltliner, which Austria spent real money promoting as its calling-card grape. Rakitzel doesn't have that champion or that budget. Add in the practical reality that Czech Republic isn't typically viewed by importers as a serious source of white wine, despite genuinely good quality in cooler-climate styles, and you get a grape that's essentially invisible outside its home region. If you ever spot a bottle outside Czechia, it's worth grabbing, not because it's some legendary rarity, but because the odds of finding it again anytime soon are genuinely low.
