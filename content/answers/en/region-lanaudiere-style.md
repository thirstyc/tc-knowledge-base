Why does Lanaudière wine taste so fruit-forward for a cold climate?

The fruit-forward character comes down to where the vines actually sit. Lanaudière's vineyards are planted on the fertile plains along the St. Lawrence, lower and slightly warmer than the hillier terrain further from the river, which means longer effective ripening time and better heat accumulation over the growing season. That extra warmth matters enormously for cold-hardy hybrid varieties like Frontenac and Marquette, which need every available degree-day to convert acid into sugar and develop real fruit ripeness rather than staying green and tart. The result is reds and whites with genuine fruit character instead of the thin, underripe profile people assume all Quebec wine has to have. It's still a cool climate by any international standard, so acidity stays high and body stays moderate, but the fruit itself tastes like it got where it needed to go. Serve the reds around 15°C, cool enough to keep them fresh, warm enough to let the fruit show.
