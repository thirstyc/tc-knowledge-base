Why Is Merlot Considered the Easy Cabernet?

Because Merlot gives you most of what people love about Cabernet Sauvignon without making you work for it.

The difference starts in the grape. Merlot has thinner skins and ripens earlier, which means less tannin and a rounder, plusher feel in the mouth. Cabernet Sauvignon has thick skins, lots of tannin and a firmer grip. Flavour-wise, Merlot leans toward plum, black cherry and chocolate, while Cabernet tends toward blackcurrant, cedar and graphite.

That softness is why Merlot drinks well younger, works with a wider range of food and rarely needs decanting. Cabernet usually wants time, air and something fatty on the plate, which is why it keeps getting paired with a ribeye.

Easy isn't the same as lesser, though. In Bordeaux the two grapes are partners. On the Left Bank, in places like Pauillac, Cabernet leads. On the Right Bank, in Pomerol and Saint-Émilion, Merlot leads, and Pomerol makes some of the most expensive wines on earth from it.

Merlot's reputation took a hit in the 2000s, partly because so much cheap, flabby Merlot was flooding shelves. Good Merlot never went anywhere.

If Cabernet feels like a lot, start with Merlot. If you love Merlot, try a Right Bank Bordeaux next.
