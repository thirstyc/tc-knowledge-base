Wine with ham?

An off-dry white, a fruity light red or a dry rosé: Riesling, Beaujolais, Pinot Noir or Provence rosé. Ham is salty and often glazed with something sweet, so the wine needs fruit and freshness rather than tannin.

Glazed ham, with honey, brown sugar or pineapple, pairs best with an off-dry Riesling or a Gewürztraminer. Their touch of sweetness matches the glaze, and their acidity handles the salt.

Beaujolais and Pinot Noir are the best reds for ham. They're light and fruity, with low tannin that won't clash with the salt.

Dry rosé is a crowd-pleaser, especially for a big spread with lots of sides.

Cured hams, like prosciutto, jamón serrano or jamón ibérico, want something dry and fresh. Fino or Manzanilla Sherry is the classic Spanish match, and a crisp sparkling wine like Cava or Prosecco works too.

Smoked ham can take a slightly fuller red, like Zinfandel or a Grenache blend.

Ham and cheese, whether in a sandwich or a croque monsieur, is great with Chardonnay or a crisp white Burgundy.

What to avoid: big, tannic reds. Salt makes tannin taste harsher and more bitter.
