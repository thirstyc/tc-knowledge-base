Why are natural wines so expensive?

Mostly because they're made in small quantities by people farming labour-intensively, with more risk of losing part of the crop or the batch.

The main cost drivers:

- Farming: organic and biodynamic viticulture needs more passes through the vineyard, more hand work and more labour, and usually yields less per hectare.
- Hand harvesting: natural winemaking almost always means picking by hand, which costs far more than machines.
- Scale: most natural producers are small, so they can't spread costs the way large wineries do.
- Risk: with little or no sulfur and no filtration, more batches fail or have to be sold off cheaply.
- Demand: fashionable producers sell out, and scarcity pushes prices up, sometimes well past what the wine costs to make.

It's worth separating those last two. Some natural wine is expensive because it's genuinely costly to produce; some is expensive because it's hard to get.

Where to find value: less fashionable regions like the Languedoc, southern Italy, Portugal and parts of Spain, where good low-intervention wine can still be found at everyday prices.

And plenty of conventional producers farm carefully without the premium, so the label isn't the only route to honest wine.
