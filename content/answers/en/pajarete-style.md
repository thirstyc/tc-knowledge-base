What exactly is Pajarete?

It's a sweet, fortified wine from around Jerez in Andalusia, made mostly from Pedro Ximénez and sometimes Moscatel grapes that get sun-dried on esparto mats before fermentation. That drying step is the whole trick: it concentrates the sugars so the must starts out syrupy, and fermentation stalls naturally or gets stopped early with added grape spirit, leaving plenty of residual sugar behind. It then usually ages in American oak butts under a solera system, the same fractional blending method used for sherry, so a bottle can carry a bit of very old wine mixed with younger stock. People confuse it with Pedro Ximénez sherry, and they're cousins rather than twins: Pajarete traditionally uses less alcohol fortification and can include a bit more blending latitude in grape or must sources historically. Expect deep amber to mahogany colour, raisin and fig, molasses, sometimes coffee or walnut. It's not trying to be elegant or restrained. It's dessert in a glass, built for slow sipping, not gulping.
