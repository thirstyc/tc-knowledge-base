Why does Côtes du Rhône taste so fruit-forward and accessible?

Grenache is the reason, and geography is the reason Grenache does what it does here. Southern Rhône is hot, sun-drenched, and in many vineyards the ground is covered in those round stones, galets roulés, that soak up heat during the day and radiate it back at night. Grenache ripens easily in that environment, piling on sugar and soft, round fruit while its naturally low acidity and moderate tannin stay in check. Blend that with a bit of Syrah for spice and structure and Mourvèdre for backbone, the classic GSM formula, and you get something generous and immediately likeable rather than austere. The appellation is also large and yields are relatively generous compared to its northern Rhône cousins, which keeps prices honest. None of this is a shortcut wine though, it's just built for early drinking rather than the cellar. Serve it around 15-16°C, slightly cool, and don't overthink it, this is a wine designed to be poured generously and finished the same night.
