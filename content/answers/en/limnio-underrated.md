Why does Limnio fly under the radar outside Greece?

Limnio has been growing on Limnos and the surrounding northern Aegean for over two thousand years, Aristotle wrote about it, which makes it one of the very few grapes with a documented ancient pedigree still being vinified today. And yet almost nobody outside Greece knows it exists. Part of that is simple geography: Limnos is a small island with limited plantable land, so total production is tiny compared to any grape with real international reach. Part of it is that Greek wine as a whole spent decades getting exported mostly as bulk or resinated Retsina, which did nothing for the country's reputation for serious varietal wine. Most Limnio produced is consumed domestically or regionally, never making it into export containers or import portfolios abroad. It is a case of a genuinely historic, distinctive grape simply not having the volume or the marketing infrastructure to get discovered, rather than any flaw in the wine itself.
