Why is Müller-Thurgau casual?

Müller-Thurgau is a cross bred in the 1880s by a Swiss scientist named, appropriately, Hermann Müller, from Thurgau. It ripens early and yields generously, which is the whole story right there: early ripening means the grape never builds up much sugar or acid before it's ready to pick, so you end up with a wine that's naturally low in alcohol, usually somewhere around 9-10%, with soft, unaggressive acidity. There's no tannin to speak of, no oak, no lees work, nothing standing between you and simple fruit and blossom aromas. Producers aren't trying to make a statement with it; they're picking early, fermenting cool, bottling young, and moving on. That's not a knock, it's the design brief. It was bred to grow easily in marginal German sites and taste pleasant without demanding anything of the drinker. Serve it cold, around 8°C, drink it within a year or two of the vintage, and don't overthink it. It was never built to age or to impress a sommelier.
