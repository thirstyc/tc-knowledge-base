What pairs with Serbian wine?

Serbian reds, particularly those made from Prokupac - the country's main indigenous red grape - tend toward moderate-to-high acidity and firm tannin without excessive weight, which is exactly what you want next to grilled and smoked meat. Ćevapi, those little grilled sausages, are fatty and intensely savory; the wine's acid cuts through the fat while the tannin grips the char. Roast pork works for the same reason - fat needs acid to keep a dish from feeling heavy, and the wine's structure holds up rather than getting flattened by the meat. Smoked cheese leans into the wine's earthier, sometimes rustic character, since a cleaner, more polished wine would feel out of place next to that much smoke. Paprika-heavy stews, built on peppers and slow-cooked meat, need backbone rather than fruit-forward softness, and that's what Serbian reds generally deliver. Serve them a touch cooler than you would a big Bordeaux, around 15-16°C, since many carry a rustic edge that turns harsh if served too warm. This is food-and-wine matching built on necessity, not fashion - the wines evolved alongside the cooking.
