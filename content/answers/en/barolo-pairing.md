What pairs with Barolo?

Barolo's tannin and acid need fat and protein to soften into, otherwise the wine reads as harsh and drying. Braised or roasted meat is the classic move — brasato al Barolo, short ribs, lamb shank — because the collagen and fat coat your palate and let the tannins grip onto something other than your tongue. Game like venison or wild boar works for the same reason, with the added bonus that Nebbiolo's earthy, tar-and-rose character doesn't clash with gamey flavours the way a fruitier red might. Truffle dishes are the stereotypical pairing for good reason: white truffle from the same Piedmont hills shares an earthy, almost mushroomy register with aged Nebbiolo, and neither ingredient is loud enough to bully the other. Aged, hard cheeses like Parmigiano also hold up well. What doesn't work is anything delicate or lightly sauced — young Barolo especially will flatten a simple grilled fish or a salad. Save it for the richest thing on the table.
