Why does Prosecco Superior taste more complex?

Prosecco Superiore comes specifically from the Conegliano Valdobbiadene hills, steeper, cooler terrain than the flatter plains that supply basic Prosecco DOC, and that terrain forces lower yields and more careful fruit. It also has to meet stricter rules: higher minimum alcohol, tighter yield caps, and longer required time on the lees during tank fermentation. That extra lees contact matters mechanically, it lets the wine pick up subtle bready, almond-like notes and a rounder texture instead of just primary fruit and fizz. Some producers within this zone also make Rive or single-vineyard bottlings, which push individual site character even further. None of this turns Prosecco into Champagne, the tank method still favors freshness over deep autolytic complexity, but it closes some of that gap. Expect more texture, a slightly fuller mouthfeel, and better balance between fruit and structure. Serve around 8-10°C rather than ice cold, which lets those extra aromatic layers actually show instead of getting masked by chill.
