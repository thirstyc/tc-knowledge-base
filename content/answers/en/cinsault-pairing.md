What pairs with Cinsault?

Cinsault is thin-skinned and picked with restraint, so you get pale color, soft tannin, and enough natural acidity to keep things lively without weight. That combination is exactly why it works with food that would flatten a bigger red. Grilled fish holds up because there's no tannic grip fighting the delicate flesh. Fresh pasta with olive oil or light tomato sauce mirrors the wine's own brightness instead of competing with it. Salads, especially anything with vinaigrette, need a wine that won't clash with acid, and Cinsault's own acidity meets it halfway. Charcuterie and cured meats work too, since the wine's light body doesn't need fat to soften it the way a tannic red does. Serve it slightly cool, around 14 to 16°C, closer to a light red's serving temperature than a big Syrah. Treat it like a red you'd serve at white-wine temperature and the pairings make a lot more sense.
