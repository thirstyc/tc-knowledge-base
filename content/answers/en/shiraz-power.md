Why does Shiraz taste so powerful and peppery?

The power and the pepper come from two different places. Heat during ripening pushes sugar levels up, which means more alcohol after fermentation, often 14% or higher, and that alcohol reads on the palate as weight, richness, and a warming finish - that's where 'powerful' comes from. The peppery note is more specific: Shiraz carries unusually high levels of a compound called rotundone, the same one responsible for black pepper's smell, and it shows up more in cooler sites or cooler vintages where the grape doesn't fully ripen past it. Worth correcting a common assumption here - people picture all Shiraz as jammy and hot, but cool-climate Shiraz, often sold under the name Syrah, can be lean, peppery, and downright savory rather than a fruit bomb. Warm-climate Australian Shiraz from places like Barossa leans into the ripe, jammy, high-alcohol style; cooler regions hold onto more pepper and less sweetness. Same grape, very different wine depending on how much sun it saw.
