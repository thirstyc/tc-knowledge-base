Why is Prosecco Superiore more expensive than regular Prosecco?

The name change isn't marketing fluff, it's geography. Regular Prosecco DOC comes largely from flat or gently rolling vineyards across the Veneto and Friuli plains, where machines can do most of the work. Prosecco Superiore DOCG is restricted to a smaller hillside zone around Conegliano-Valdobbiadene, where slopes are steep enough that harvesting has to be done by hand, often on terraces that make tractors useless. That labor cost alone pushes prices up. The DOCG rules also cap yields lower than the DOC, meaning fewer bottles per hectare, and require the grapes to hit higher ripeness and quality thresholds before they even reach the winery. Add stricter tasting panels before approval, and you get a wine with more concentration and finer bubbles, made in smaller volume, by people who can't cut corners on a hillside. You're paying for topography and human labor, not a marketing tier. Serve it around 6-8°C, and you'll taste the difference in texture, not just the price tag.
