What pairs with Sonoma Pinot?

The acidity is doing the pairing work. Sonoma Pinot Noir comes from a cooler climate than most reds, meaning lower alcohol, higher natural acid, and red fruit - cherry, cranberry, red plum - instead of jammy black fruit. That acid cuts through the fat in roast chicken skin the same way a squeeze of lemon would, and it doesn't fight with salmon's oiliness the way a heavier, tannic red does. Mushrooms bring umami and earthy depth that Pinot's own forest-floor notes actually amplify rather than clash with - it's one of the few red-and-fish pairings that genuinely works, because there's no aggressive tannin gripping the fish's delicate protein. Serve it slightly cool, around 14-16°C, cooler than you'd pour a Cabernet, so the fruit stays bright and the acid stays crisp instead of the alcohol turning soft and hot. Skip anything too rich or cream-sauced - this wine is built for lift, not weight, and a heavy sauce will bury it instead of complementing it.
