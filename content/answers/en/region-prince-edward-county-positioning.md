Why do people call Prince Edward County "Ontario's Burgundy"?

The comparison comes from geology and grape choice, not from any real resemblance in the glass. PEC sits on ancient limestone bedrock, the same type of soil that underlies much of Burgundy, and it's a genuinely cold, marginal climate where growers have deliberately planted Chardonnay and Pinot Noir instead of hardier hybrids. That's a real gamble: vines here often need to be buried under soil every winter just to survive temperatures that would otherwise kill them. The wines that result are lean, high-acid, and mineral-driven rather than fruit-forward, which echoes cool-climate Burgundy in style even if the scale and history don't come close. It's a tiny region, maybe a few dozen serious producers, all working by hand because mechanization doesn't suit the terrain or the volumes. So the nickname is aspirational marketing more than fact, but it does correctly signal what to expect in the glass: precision over power.
