Best red wine for beginners?

Start with a fruity, soft red with gentle tannins: Pinot Noir, Beaujolais, Merlot or a Côtes du Rhône. These wines are easy to enjoy without food and don't dry out your mouth the way bolder reds can.

Pinot Noir is light, silky and full of red fruit like cherry and raspberry. Affordable bottles from Chile, New Zealand and California are a friendly introduction.

Beaujolais, made from Gamay, is juicy and fresh, and it tastes great slightly chilled.

Merlot is soft and plummy, with more body than Pinot Noir but still smooth tannins.

Côtes du Rhône and other Grenache-based wines are warm, fruity and generous, and often great value.

Once you're comfortable, move up to fuller wines like Malbec or Zinfandel, which are rich and ripe but still approachable. Save big, tannic reds like young Cabernet Sauvignon, Nebbiolo or Tannat for later.

A few tips:

- Serve reds slightly cool, around 15–16°C. Warm red wine tastes more alcoholic.
- Try wines with food. Tannin feels much softer alongside a meal.
- Ask at the shop for "fruity and smooth". It's a perfectly good description.

The best way to learn is to taste a few side by side and notice what you like.
