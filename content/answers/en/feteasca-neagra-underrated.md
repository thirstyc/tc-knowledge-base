Why does Fetească Neagră fly under the radar?

Under communist-era collectivization, Romanian vineyards were farmed for volume, not character, and Fetească Neagră got lumped in with everything else destined for bulk export to Soviet markets. That history stuck even after the vines and the winemaking caught up. What's changed is basic: temperature-controlled fermentation to preserve the grape's dark-fruit aromatics instead of cooking them off, selective yields instead of stripping every vine bare, and judicious oak instead of drowning the wine in it. Old vines that survived the collective-farm era are now being vinified with actual care rather than treated as anonymous blending material. The grape itself has real bones, good acidity, firm tannin, enough structure to age, so the raw material was never the problem. It's a reputation lag more than a quality gap, and it's closing fast as small Romanian producers start bottling site-specific wines instead of generic reds.
