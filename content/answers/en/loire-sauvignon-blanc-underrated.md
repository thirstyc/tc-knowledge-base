Why does Loire Sauvignon Blanc fly under the radar?

New Zealand Sauvignon Blanc from Marlborough is loud - passionfruit, cut grass, an almost aggressive thiol-driven aroma that comes from clonal selection, cool nights, and yeast strains chosen to amplify that character. It set the modern template for what the grape should taste like, so when people meet Sancerre or Pouilly-Fumé, they expect the same thing and get something quieter instead. The Loire's Kimmeridgian limestone and flint soils, combined with a genuinely marginal climate, produce wines with less overt fruit and more saline, chalky tension - gunflint, citrus pith, sometimes a smoky reduction note from the soil rather than the yeast. It's built around acidity and texture rather than aromatics, so on a first sip it can taste like less when it's actually more precise. Serve it cold, around 8-10°C, and let it sit a few minutes - the mineral character opens up as it warms slightly. It's the reference point the New World was reacting to, not a paler copy of it.
