What pairs with Loire Sauvignon Blanc?

High acidity is a cutting tool, it slices through fat and salt, which is why this wine works so well with seafood: oysters, shrimp, a simple grilled fish with lemon. The wine's citrus and green herb notes echo what's already on the plate instead of fighting it. Goat cheese is the textbook pairing for a reason that isn't just tradition, Sancerre and the Loire's Crottin de Chavignol come from the same limestone belt, so the wine's chalky edge and the cheese's tang share a mineral backbone. Bright, acidic salads with vinaigrette are one of the few dressings that won't clash with a wine this sharp, since a lower-acid wine would just taste flat next to the vinegar. It also handles herbs well, tarragon, dill, chives, echoing its own grassy character. Avoid heavy cream sauces or anything smoked and fatty without acid to balance it, the wine will taste thin by comparison. Serve at 8 to 10 degrees, well chilled, straight from the fridge is fine here.
