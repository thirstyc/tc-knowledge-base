What pairs with Vinsanto?

Vinsanto is made by drying grapes, usually Malvasia and Trebbiano, on straw mats or hanging racks for months before pressing, which concentrates sugar dramatically before fermentation even starts. It then ages oxidatively in small barrels called caratelli, often tucked away for years, picking up notes of caramel, dried apricot, and toasted nuts along the way. That intense sweetness is the whole point, so the pairing logic is about matching or contrasting it rather than fighting it. The classic move is dunking cantucci, Tuscany's almond biscotti, straight into the glass; the wine's sweetness softens the biscuit and the almond's dry crunch balances the sugar. Aged, salty hard cheeses like a mature pecorino work the same way, salt cutting sweetness and sweetness softening salt. Toasted or candied nuts echo the wine's own oxidative character. Skip anything sweeter than the wine itself, or you'll flatten it. Serve slightly cool, around 12-14°C, in a small glass, since a little goes a long way.
