What pairs with Chasselas?

Chasselas is one of the few grapes where low acidity is a feature, not a flaw. Grown mostly around Lake Geneva and in Savoie, it produces pale, delicate wines with almost no aromatic shout and a soft, sometimes faintly spritzy texture from a touch of retained CO2. That gentleness is exactly why it works so well with fondue and raclette: a high-acid wine would clash with melted cheese, but Chasselas's roundness just sits alongside the fat and lets it be. Same logic with perch or féra fillets pulled straight from the lake, or fresh goat cheese, or a plain butter-and-herb pasta. It's not a wine that fights for attention on the plate, it's one that gets out of the way. Serve it cold, around 8 to 10°C, so the subtle mineral snap it does have stays crisp. Anything richer or more assertive than light fish, cheese or veal will simply flatten it.
