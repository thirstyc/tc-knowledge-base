Why does Stellenbosch Cabernet taste so structured and elegant?

Stellenbosch sits close enough to False Bay that cool ocean air pushes inland most afternoons, slowing ripening and keeping natural acidity intact even as sugars build during the day. That day-night temperature swing is what gives the wine its backbone rather than the soft, sweet fruit you'd get from a purely hot climate. The granite and decomposed sandstone soils on the slopes are low in nutrients and drain fast, so vines struggle a little, sending roots deep and producing smaller, more concentrated berries with thicker skins, which means more tannin and colour. Producers also tend to pick with restraint rather than chasing maximum ripeness, preserving the wine's structure over sheer power. Mineral notes come less from the wine literally tasting like rock and more from that combination of low fruit sweetness and firm acid-tannin backbone reading as savoury and precise. Serve at 16-18°C and give it time in the glass, the structure needs air to unwind.
