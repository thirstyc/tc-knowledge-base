What is Meritage?

Meritage is a term used mainly in the United States for wines made from a blend of the traditional Bordeaux grapes. It rhymes with "heritage", not with the French-sounding "garage".

The term was created in the late 1980s by a group of California wineries. US labelling rules require at least 75% of a single grape for a wine to be named after it, so many high-quality Bordeaux-style blends could only be called "red table wine". Meritage gave them a quality name of their own.

To use it, a wine must be made from at least two of the approved Bordeaux varieties:

- Red Meritage: Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Cabernet Franc, Petit Verdot, Malbec, and the rarer Carmenère, St. Macaire and Gros Verdot. No single grape may make up more than 90%.
- White Meritage: Sauvignon Blanc, Sémillon and Sauvignon Vert (Muscadelle).

Wineries must be licensed members of the association that owns the term.

In practice, many producers of Bordeaux-style blends never use the word, preferring a proprietary name. Meritage is common in California, Washington, Virginia and Canada.

Expect the same style as a Bordeaux blend: structured, with blackcurrant, plum and cedar.
