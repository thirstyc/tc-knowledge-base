Why is Corvina so elegant and light?

Corvina's lightness isn't an accident of style, it's genetics. The grape has naturally thin, pale skins that don't give up much tannin or color no matter how long you leave the juice in contact with them, which is why straight Corvina wines are almost translucent ruby rather than opaque purple. It also holds onto acidity well even when fully ripe, especially up in the Valpolicella hills where cooler nights slow things down. That combination, low tannin plus bright acid, is what gives you the cherry-and-almond profile instead of something jammy or structured. In Amarone and Ripasso, producers dry the grapes or reintroduce dried-grape skins specifically to build back the weight and tannin Corvina lacks on its own. So the same grape can read as a light summer red or a dense, brooding wine depending entirely on how much water gets pulled out of it before fermentation. Serve it around 14-16°C, cool enough to keep the fruit fresh but not so cold you mute the almond note.
