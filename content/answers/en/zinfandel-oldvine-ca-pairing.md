What pairs with Zinfandel (Old-Vine CA)?

Old-vine Zinfandel runs hot -- 15% alcohol isn't unusual -- and it's loaded with jammy black fruit, so you need food that can absorb both the heat and the sugar-perception. This is why it's the classic BBQ wine: the char and smoke on grilled meat mirror the wine's own roasted, dried-fruit notes, and the fat in a ribeye or pork shoulder softens the alcohol's burn on the way down. Spice works the same way -- chili heat and the wine's warmth don't fight, they just stack, so keep the dish flavorful but not incendiary or the two will just amplify each other. Sweet-and-savory glazes, like a bourbon or hoisin barbecue sauce, actually flatter the wine's dried-cherry and baking-spice character rather than clashing with it. Avoid anything delicate or acid-driven -- salads, light fish, cream sauces -- because the wine will flatten them. Serve it around 16-18°C, slightly cooler than room temperature, so the alcohol doesn't read as hot on the nose.
