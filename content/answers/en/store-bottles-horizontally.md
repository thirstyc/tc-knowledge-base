Does wine need to be stored horizontally?

Only if it has a natural cork and you're keeping it for more than a few months. Lying the bottle down keeps the cork moist, so it doesn't dry out, shrink and let air in.

For screwcapped bottles, and for glass or synthetic stoppers, it makes no difference. Store them any way you like.

For short-term storage, a few weeks or months, upright is also perfectly fine, even with a cork.

There's a second argument for lying bottles down: humidity. In a dry room, a cork in an upright bottle dries out faster. If your storage area is humid enough, around 60–70%, the risk is lower.

Practical reasons also favour it: bottles on their side stack neatly and take up less space, which is why wine racks are built that way.

One exception: Port and other bottles with a stopper-style cork are traditionally stored upright, because those corks aren't made for long contact with wine. Vintage Port, which uses a driven cork, is stored on its side.

If a bottle has stood upright for years, stand it up before serving anyway, so any sediment settles.
