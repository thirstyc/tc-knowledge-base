Why does Etna wine taste so smoky and volcanic?

The vines are planted directly in black volcanic ash and pumice on the slopes of an active volcano, soil that drains fast, holds heat unevenly and carries real mineral character straight into the fruit. Altitude does the rest: vineyards sit anywhere from a few hundred meters up to nearly a thousand, and the big swing between hot days and cold nights keeps acidity sharp even though Sicily itself is baking hot at sea level. Nerello Mascalese, the main red grape, is thin-skinned and naturally high-acid, low-tannin, which is why comparisons to Pinot Noir come up constantly, but it also has a structured, savory backbone that nods toward Nebbiolo. The smokiness people taste isn't a metaphor, volcanic soils genuinely carry sulfurous, mineral notes that show up in the glass. It is one of the few wines where the geology is still active underneath the vineyard, not just historical.
