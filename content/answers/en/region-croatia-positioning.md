Why does Croatia matter for wine history?

For decades, Zinfandel's origins were a mystery, then DNA testing in the early 2000s traced it back to Crljenak Kaštelanski, a nearly forgotten grape still growing in small plots along the Dalmatian coast. It turned out California's signature grape was Croatian all along, just renamed and replanted an ocean away while the original stayed home, unglamorous and nearly extinct. That's the pattern here: Croatia has been making wine continuously for thousands of years, with over a hundred indigenous varieties scattered across its islands and coastline, but isolation and war kept most of it invisible to outside markets. Only in the last couple of decades has that changed, as international attention and DNA science pulled these grapes out of obscurity. What's left is a coastline of genuinely old, distinctive viticulture that the wider wine world is only now getting around to noticing, and pricing accordingly.
