Tempranillo vs Garnacha?

Spain's two great red grapes. Tempranillo is savoury, structured and often oak-aged; Garnacha is fruity, warm and softer.

Tempranillo tastes of red cherry, dried fig, leather and tobacco. It has moderate acidity and firm tannins, and it takes oak well, which is why Rioja and Ribera del Duero age their wines for years in barrel. The name comes from "temprano", meaning early, because it ripens ahead of other grapes.

Garnacha, the same grape as French Grenache, is riper and rounder, with strawberry and raspberry fruit, soft tannins and higher alcohol. It shows best in old vineyards in places like Priorat, Calatayud and Campo de Borja.

In Rioja the two are often blended, with Tempranillo leading and Garnacha adding flesh and fruit.

At the table, Tempranillo suits roast lamb, grilled meat and hard cheeses like Manchego. Garnacha is more flexible, good with roast vegetables, charcuterie and spiced dishes.

Value note: old-vine Garnacha from lesser-known regions is some of the best-value red wine in the world.

Which to choose? Tempranillo for savoury, oak-aged classics; Garnacha for generous fruit.
