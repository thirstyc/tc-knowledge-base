What pairs with Zinfandel?

Zinfandel's calling card is a one-two punch of high alcohol -- often 14.5% or more -- and firm tannin, both of which need food with weight and fat to balance them out. That's why it's built for the grill: charred, smoky meats echo the wine's own roasted fruit and pepper notes, while the fat in a burger, sausage or ribs coats your palate and tames the tannin's grip. Spicy food works for the same reason barbecue does -- the wine's ripe fruit and warmth ride alongside chili heat instead of getting steamrolled by it, as a lighter, more acidic red would. Tomato-based sauces and smoky baked beans are classic matches too, since the wine's dark fruit meets them halfway. What it doesn't do well is delicate food -- raw fish, light salads, anything where subtlety is the point, because Zinfandel will simply flatten it. Pour it slightly below room temperature, around 16-18°C, to keep the alcohol from feeling hot.
