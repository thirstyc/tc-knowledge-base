Why does South African Chenin Blanc from Paarl taste so complex?

Paarl sits in a warm bowl inland from the cooling Cape doctor's reach, so the fruit ripens fully and gives you the mid-palate weight and stone fruit richness people notice first. But the complexity actually comes from the vines themselves: much of Paarl's Chenin is old, dry-farmed bush vine planted decades ago on decomposed granite and sandstone. No irrigation forces roots deep, yields drop naturally, and old wood concentrates flavour without ballooning sugar. Winemakers then layer technique on top of that raw material, some parcels fermented in old oak, others left on lees for months to build texture and a faint bready, nutty edge alongside the peach and quince. The granite soils also hold onto acidity even as sugars climb, so you get a wine that's simultaneously round and structured. Serve it a touch warmer than a basic white, around 10-12°C, so the lanolin and honeyed notes actually show instead of hiding behind chill.
