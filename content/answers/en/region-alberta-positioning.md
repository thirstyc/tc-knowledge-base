Why mention Alberta wine at all?

Because it exists at all, and that's the interesting part. Alberta sits at latitudes and elevations where grapevines have no business surviving winter, let alone ripening fruit. Growers there lean on cold-hardy hybrid varieties bred for exactly this kind of punishment, plus techniques like burying vines under soil for insulation, borrowed from places like Quebec and Minnesota. The wines themselves are rarely sophisticated in a Burgundy sense, and nobody's claiming they should be compared to one. What they show is the outer edge of where wine can be made on this continent, and how much ingenuity gets spent chasing that edge. It's less about what's in the glass and more about the fact that someone grew a wine grape in a place that regularly hits minus 30. Worth trying once, mostly for the story you'll tell after. If you're building a serious cellar, this isn't where the money goes. If you're curious how wine geography keeps expanding, it's a fun footnote.
