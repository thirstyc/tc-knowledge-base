What pairs with Hungarian wine?

Hungary's wines were built around specific dishes long before anyone called it pairing, so the matches tend to be almost too obvious. Sweet Tokaji, made from botrytis-affected Furmint grapes concentrated by noble rot, has enough residual sugar and searing acidity to cut straight through the richness of foie gras without collapsing into syrup, which is why the two have shared a table for centuries. Kékfrankos, a peppery, medium-bodied red with real acid backbone, was made for paprika-forward dishes like paprikás csirke, where its brightness cuts the cream and fat in the sauce rather than getting buried by the spice. Dry Furmint, unrelated in style to its sweet cousin, has a waxy texture and citrus-and-flint edge that plays beautifully against tangy goat cheese. The throughline across all three is acidity and a country's cooking that leans rich, smoky, and spiced. These are not shy, fruit-forward wines; they were made to work alongside food, not compete with it.
