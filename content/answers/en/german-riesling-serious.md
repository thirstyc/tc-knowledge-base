Why is German Riesling underrated?

The reputation problem is almost entirely a labeling and export accident. Through the 1970s and 80s, oceans of cheap, sugary, mass-produced Riesling, the Blue Nun and Black Tower era, got shipped abroad as the face of German wine. That's what a generation of drinkers associates with the word Riesling, even though it had almost nothing to do with what serious German producers were doing at home. Meanwhile in the Mosel, growers were farming absurdly steep slate slopes, some over 60 degrees, by hand because machinery can't operate there, to make wines with searing acidity, low alcohol and extraordinary precision. The slate retains heat and drains fast, forcing vine roots deep, and the wines that result can age for decades, gaining honey and petrol notes while staying vertical and taut. Rheingau does something similar with more structure. None of this matches the sweet, cheap image still stuck in most people's heads, which is exactly the disconnect.
