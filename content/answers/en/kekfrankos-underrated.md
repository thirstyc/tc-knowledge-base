Why does Kékfrankos fly under the radar?

Hungary's entire wine identity abroad has been built almost single-handedly on Tokaji, its sweet botrytized wine, which leaves very little room in most drinkers' minds for the idea that Hungary also makes serious dry reds. Kékfrankos gets caught in that blind spot even though it's the most widely planted red grape in the country and the backbone of respected regions like Eger and Sopron. It doesn't help that the same grape has a higher profile under its Austrian name, Blaufränkisch, where producers in Mittelburgenland have spent decades building its reputation as an age-worthy, terroir-driven red. So the grape's best press goes to a different country under a different name, while the Hungarian version, often just as structured and peppery, sits quietly underpriced and overlooked. If you want proof Hungary is more than a dessert-wine country, Kékfrankos is the wine that makes the case, and it's still cheap enough that trying a few bottles from different producers won't cost you much to find out.
