What pairs with Chilean Cabernet?

Chilean Cabernet's firm tannin and bright acid want fat and protein to push against, which is why grilled red meat is the obvious move - the char and fat coat the palate and soften the tannin's grip, while the acidity cuts back through and cleanses between bites. Lamb works for the same reason, and its slight gaminess plays well with the wine's darker fruit and herbal edge. Spicy food is trickier than people assume: capsaicin heat can make tannin taste harsher and more alcoholic, so if you're pairing with chili, look for a bottle with softer tannin, or add something fatty like cheese, coconut milk or avocado to buffer the burn.

Avoid delicate fish or anything built around a light vinaigrette - the tannin will flatten it. A grilling steak with a decent fat cap, or a hard aged cheese, is really the sweet spot, because the tannin needs something to grab onto. Without that, it just reads as dry and astringent instead of structured.
