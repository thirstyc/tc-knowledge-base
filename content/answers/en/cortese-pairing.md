What pairs with Cortese?

Cortese's whole appeal is cut and lift, so it works best with food that's delicate, briny or a little fatty rather than heavily sauced or spiced. Raw and lightly cooked seafood are the classic match: oysters, clams, anchovies, a simple grilled fish, since the acidity cleans the palate between bites the way a squeeze of lemon would. It's also the traditional pour with vitello tonnato and fritto misto in its home region, where the wine's brightness cuts through the richness of the tuna sauce or the fried batter without fighting it. Fresh pasta in a light butter or olive oil sauce works too, as does a young, soft cheese like a fresh goat cheese, where the wine's acid balances the cheese's creaminess instead of getting bulldozed by it. What you want to avoid is anything too rich, smoky or intensely spiced, since Cortese's quiet, mineral-driven character will simply disappear next to bolder flavours. Chilled to around 8-10°C, it's a food wine first and a sipping wine a distant second.
