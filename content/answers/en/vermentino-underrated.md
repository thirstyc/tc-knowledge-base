Why does Vermentino fly under the radar?

Vermentino has an identity crisis by geography, not by quality. It's Sardinia's signature white, but it's also grown in Liguria (where it's sometimes called Pigato), across Corsica, and increasingly in Provence and Languedoc as Rolle. Same grape, four names, four countries, no single region has ever built the kind of marketing machine Sancerre built around Sauvignon Blanc. That's the whole story. Mechanistically, it's a coastal grape through and through: thick skins that shrug off Mediterranean heat and hang onto acidity, grown on sandy or granitic soils near the sea that push it toward a saline, almost briny edge alongside citrus and bitter almond. Serve it cold, 8 to 10°C, with anything that comes out of the water: grilled fish, oysters, aioli-drenched vegetables. It's not a wine people order by name because they were never taught one name to order. That's an branding failure, not a flavour one.
