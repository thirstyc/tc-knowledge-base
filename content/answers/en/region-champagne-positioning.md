Why is Champagne the only sparkling wine allowed to use the name?

The name Champagne is legally protected, tied to a specific region in northeastern France and a specific method — secondary fermentation in the bottle, known as méthode champenoise — that creates those fine bubbles through a controlled, months-long process rather than just pumping in CO2. Anywhere else making the exact same wine the exact same way has to call it something else: sparkling wine, Cava, Crémant, or whatever the local term is. This isn't arbitrary bureaucracy — the rule exists because Champagne built its reputation over centuries before anyone thought to protect the name, and other regions eventually built their own laws copying that model to protect their own names too. It's a reminder that the word on the label is about origin and method, not necessarily about quality — a well-made Crémant can rival a mediocre Champagne. Serve Champagne cold, around 6-8°C, and remember that the region's prestige, not just its bubbles, is what you're actually paying for.
