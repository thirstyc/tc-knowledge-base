Why does Vidal Blanc taste so citrusy and fresh?

Vidal Blanc is a hybrid, bred by crossing Ugni Blanc with a French-American variety called Rayon d'Or, specifically to survive cold climates that would kill most European vines outright. That hardiness is the whole reason it took off in Ontario, Quebec, and other cold-winter regions where classic Vitis vinifera struggles. Its thick skin resists rot and frost damage, and that same skin holds onto acidity even as the grape ripens, which is what gives the wine its bright citrus and tropical fruit character rather than anything flabby or dull. That toughness also makes it the go-to grape for icewine: it can hang on the vine deep into winter, through repeated freeze-thaw cycles, without falling apart, concentrating sugar and acid together. Dry versions should be served cold, around 8 to 10°C, to keep that citrus snap front and centre. It's a practical grape before it's a glamorous one, and the flavour is a direct result of that practicality.
