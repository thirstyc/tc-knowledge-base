Why is Napa Cabernet so expensive?

Napa Valley is small, maybe 45 kilometres end to end, and only a fraction of that is prime Cabernet land on the valley floor and surrounding hillsides. That scarcity collides with decades of reputation-building, dating back to the 1976 Judgment of Paris, which put Napa Cabernet on the map as America's answer to Bordeaux. Land prices reflect that: an acre of top Napa vineyard can cost more than most houses, and that cost gets baked into every bottle before a single grape is picked. Labour is expensive, oak barrels aren't cheap, and many producers deliberately keep yields low to concentrate flavour, meaning fewer bottles to spread costs across. Add a collector culture that treats certain producers like trophies, with mailing lists and secondary markets driving prices further from anything tied to production cost, and you get a region where quality plays a role but scarcity and status do a lot of the heavy lifting. It's genuinely excellent wine, but you're also paying for the address.
