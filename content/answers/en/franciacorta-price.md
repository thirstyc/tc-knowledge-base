Why is Franciacorta expensive?

The cost comes down to method and time, not marketing. Franciacorta uses the same labor-intensive process as Champagne: a second fermentation in the bottle, hand or machine riddling to collect the sediment, disgorgement to remove it, all done wine by wine rather than in bulk tanks like Prosecco, which is made via the much cheaper Charmat method. Italian regulations also require longer minimum lees aging than Champagne's baseline, which ties up capital and cellar space for years before a bottle ever sells. Add in Lombardy vineyard land that isn't cheap, low yields required by the appellation rules, and a production zone small enough that there's no economy of scale to lean on, and the price reflects real fixed costs rather than pure prestige. It still usually lands a notch below Champagne, since the region hasn't built the same brand cachet or scarcity premium yet, but it's priced like a serious traditional-method wine because that's exactly what it is. If you see Franciacorta priced like Prosecco, be suspicious of it.
