Why is Côtes du Rhône so affordable?

Scale is the whole story. Côtes du Rhône covers a vast stretch of the southern Rhône valley, spanning both banks and thousands of hectares, while Châteauneuf-du-Pape is a small, specific patch of galet-covered ground with strict yield limits and a name that sells itself. Grenache, syrah and mourvèdre go into both, but Côtes du Rhône fruit often comes from flatter, more fertile plains that produce generously, and negociant houses blend across many growers to keep volumes high and costs down. There's no requirement to farm a single prestigious vineyard or age the wine for years before release. You're getting the same grape family and the same warm Mediterranean climate, just without the scarcity, the reputation tax or the decades of critical hype. Drink it young, slightly below room temperature around 15 to 16°C, with roast chicken, charcuterie or anything grilled. It won't have Châteauneuf's depth or aging potential, but for weeknight drinking it's honest, generous and correctly priced for what it is.
