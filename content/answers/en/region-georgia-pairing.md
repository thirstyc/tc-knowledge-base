What pairs with Georgian qvevri wine?

Qvevri wine, made by fermenting and often aging on skins and stems in buried clay vessels for months, comes out with more tannin, more texture and more savoury depth than most people expect from a white grape, let alone a red. That structure needs food with equal weight. Grilled meats, especially anything charred, meet the wine's smoky, earthy edge halfway. Walnut sauces, a staple across Georgian cooking, pick up on the same tannic grip and nutty oxidative character the qvevri process creates. Aged cheese works for the same reason cheese works with any high-tannin wine: fat and salt soften the grip and let the fruit underneath show through. This isn't a wine to serve too cold, since chilling mutes the tannin and aromatics that make it distinctive; aim for 14 to 16°C even on the amber styles. It's tannic, earthy wine that was never meant to be sipped alone, it was built for a hearty, communal table.
