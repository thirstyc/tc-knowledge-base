Why does Ontario Cabernet Franc fly under the radar?

Canada's wine reputation was built almost entirely on ice wine, a sweet, expensive novelty that made for a great export story but did nothing to signal that the same regions could also make serious dry reds. Niagara growers have actually been planting and refining Cabernet Franc since the 1970s and 80s, working out which sites near the Escarpment and the lake ripen reliably, but that work happened quietly while the marketing budget went toward dessert wine. VQA rules also keep production relatively small and mostly sold within Canada, so very little of it ever reaches export markets or international critics who shape global reputations. Add to that a general assumption that anywhere this far north can't ripen a serious red grape, which simply isn't true given how much the lake moderates the climate. The result is a genuinely good, structured red that most wine drinkers outside Canada, and plenty inside it, have never had a reason to seek out.
