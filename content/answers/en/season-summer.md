Best wine for summer?

Light, fresh and chillable: crisp whites, dry rosé, sparkling wine and light reds served cool. Heat makes heavy, high-alcohol wines feel sluggish, so summer favours freshness.

Crisp whites are the backbone: Sauvignon Blanc, Albariño, Vinho Verde, Picpoul de Pinet, Muscadet and dry Riesling. They're refreshing on their own and great with salads, seafood and grilled fish.

Dry rosé, especially from Provence, is the classic summer wine. It works with almost anything eaten outdoors.

Sparkling wine is never wrong in summer, from Prosecco and Cava to pét-nat.

Red wine still has a place, as long as it's light and cool. Beaujolais, Gamay, Frappato, Pinot Noir and Lambrusco are all great after 20–30 minutes in the fridge.

For barbecues, reach for a juicy red like Zinfandel or Grenache, served slightly chilled, or a fuller rosé.

Practical summer tips:

- Keep everything colder than you think. Warm wine tastes flabby and alcoholic.
- Use an ice bucket with water, not just ice.
- Screwcaps and cans are handy outdoors.
- Keep an eye on alcohol levels on hot days, and drink plenty of water.
