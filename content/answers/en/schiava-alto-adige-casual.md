Why is Schiava casual?

Schiava, known locally as Vernatsch, is grown high in Alto Adige where the alpine air keeps things fresh, but the wine itself is built for immediate, uncomplicated drinking rather than contemplation. The skins are thin, so almost nothing gets extracted in the way of tannin or deep color, which gives you a pale, light-bodied red with floral and bramble notes and a faint almond finish. There's no oak aging weighing it down, no attempt at structure for the cellar. It's bottled young and meant to be drunk young, chilled, often at long lunches in the mountains where nobody's treating wine like a performance. Compare it to the region's Lagrein or Pinot Noir, which are built with real ambition and tannic backbone, and Schiava's role becomes obvious: it's the wine you reach for when the goal is refreshment, not seriousness. That's not a knock against it, it's just doing a different job, and it does that job well.
