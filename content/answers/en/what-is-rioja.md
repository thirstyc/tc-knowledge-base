What is Rioja wine?

Rioja is Spain's most famous wine region, in the north of the country, making mostly red wine from Tempranillo, usually aged in oak barrels before release.

Its signature comes from that ageing. Traditional Rioja spends years in American oak, which gives vanilla, coconut and dill notes alongside red fruit, leather and tobacco.

The labels tell you how long the wine aged, and the terms are worth learning:

- Genérico or Joven: little or no oak ageing, fresh and fruity.
- Crianza: at least two years, including a year in oak.
- Reserva: at least three years, including a year in oak.
- Gran Reserva: at least five years, including two in oak.

Because the producer does the ageing for you, a Gran Reserva released with a decade of age is one of the best-value mature wines in the world.

Tempranillo leads most blends, often with Garnacha, Graciano or Mazuelo. The region also makes white Rioja, mainly from Viura, in styles from crisp to richly oaked.

A newer generation makes fresher, fruit-forward wines with less oak, so the region now offers both styles.

Try Rioja with roast lamb, grilled meat or Manchego.
