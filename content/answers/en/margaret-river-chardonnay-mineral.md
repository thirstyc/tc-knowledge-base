Why does Margaret River Chardonnay taste so elegant and mineral?

The mineral edge comes from a genuine maritime climate, not marketing copy. Margaret River sits between two oceans, and the cooling influence keeps grapes hanging longer without losing acidity, so sugars and flavour compounds develop slowly and evenly. Most producers pick earlier than you'd expect for Chardonnay, then use a fair amount of wild-ferment and lees-aging in older barrels rather than heavy new oak, which lets the fruit's natural texture and stony backbone show instead of vanilla and butter. The gravelly, ancient soils around the Wilyabrup and Karridale areas also drain fast, stressing the vines just enough to concentrate flavour without brute ripeness. What you end up with is a Chardonnay that reads closer to a serious white Burgundy than to the broader, riper style Australia is often stereotyped for: taut, saline, with citrus and white stone fruit rather than tropical richness. Serve it around 10 to 12 degrees, not fridge-cold, so the texture and minerality actually show.
