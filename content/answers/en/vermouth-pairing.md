What pairs with Vermouth?

Salt and fat, mostly. Vermouth is built to be drunk before dinner rather than with it.

In Spain and Italy the question is long settled: olives, almonds, anchovies, cured ham, crisps. The bitterness and the botanicals reset your palate between bites, which is the entire job of an aperitivo.

Dry vermouth — French in style, pale, bracing — goes where you'd otherwise pour Muscadet. Oysters, white fish, anything cold and briny. Sweet red vermouth carries more: charcuterie, aged cheese, dark chocolate, or nothing whatsoever over ice with a slice of orange.

Serve it cold and serve it fresh, and this is the part nearly everyone gets wrong. Vermouth is wine, not spirits. Once it's open it oxidises exactly like wine does. That dusty bottle at the back of the bar has been quietly dying for eight months. Keep it in the fridge, finish it inside a month, and it tastes like a different drink entirely.
