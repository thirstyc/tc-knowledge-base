Why is Waiheke Island Cabernet expensive?

Waiheke is small — you could walk across the vineyard-planted parts in an afternoon — and only a fraction of the island suits fine wine, so total production is tiny next to almost any other serious region. That scarcity alone would push prices up, but it's compounded by the island's other identity as a wine-tourism and lifestyle destination a short ferry ride from Auckland: land gets bought as much for the view and the cellar-door experience as for vineyard economics, and that shows up in the bottle price. Yields are also kept deliberately low to get the ripeness and concentration the island is known for, meaning less wine to spread costs across. Add boutique-scale production — many wineries here make a few thousand cases a year, not the tens of thousands a Marlborough Sauvignon Blanc house might turn out — and you get some of the highest per-bottle prices in New Zealand, for one of its smallest and most geographically constrained regions. It's expensive because there's very little of it, not because it's dressed up to look expensive.
