What pairs with Victoria Pinot Noir?

This is a wine built around acidity and restraint, so it wants food that's delicate rather than dominant. Grilled salmon or trout works because the wine's acid cuts through the fat without a heavy tannic backbone crushing the fish. Roasted chicken or duck is the classic move, the Pinot's savoury, earthy notes echo the browned skin and the fine tannin doesn't fight the meat. Mushroom risotto or anything with an umami, forest-floor quality is close to a natural match since good cool-climate Pinot already carries that character itself. Where it struggles is heavy red meat, thick sauces, or anything smoked and aggressive, the wine simply doesn't have the weight to stand up to it and gets steamrolled. Serve around 14 to 16°C, slightly below typical red-wine temperature, because warmer masks the freshness that makes it work at the table in the first place. Think delicate proteins and earthy sides, not steak night.
