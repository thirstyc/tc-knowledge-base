What pairs with Montepulciano d'Abruzzo?

The tannin and acid in Montepulciano d'Abruzzo are both high, and they need food that can meet them halfway. Fat softens tannin - it binds to the proteins and fat on your palate and smooths out that gripping, drying sensation - so grilled or braised meat with some real marbling works better than anything lean. Lamb, sausage, and slow-cooked beef stew all do the job. The acidity is just as important: it's sharp enough to stand up to tomato-based sauces without getting flattened, which is why a rustic ragù or a tomato-and-meat pasta is close to the textbook pairing. Try it with something delicate like plain grilled fish and the tannin will just feel harsh and out of place - this isn't a subtle wine, it wants dishes with weight and a bit of char or slow-cooked depth. Serve it a touch below room temperature, around 16-17°C, so the tannin doesn't feel even more aggressive than it already is. It's built for the table, not for sipping on its own.
