Why does Eastern Townships Frontenac taste so dark and cherry-forward?

Frontenac is a hybrid grape, part vinifera, part native North American vine, bred specifically to survive winters that would kill classic European varieties outright. That cold-hardiness comes with a flavour signature: naturally high acidity and a fruit profile that leans dark cherry and cranberry rather than the plush black fruit you'd get from a warm-climate red. The acid isn't a stylistic choice, it's baked into the grape's genetics as part of what lets it survive the freeze-thaw cycles of a Quebec winter. Winemakers here work with that rather than against it, picking to preserve freshness instead of chasing heavy ripeness the grape was never built to reach anyway. The result is a light-to-medium-bodied red that drinks best young, served around 14-15°C, slightly cool so the acidity feels bright rather than tart. It's less a stylistic cousin of Bordeaux or Rhone reds and more its own thing entirely, Quebec's answer to the simple fact that vinifera mostly can't survive here.
