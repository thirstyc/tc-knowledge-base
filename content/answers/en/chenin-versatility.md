Why is Chenin Blanc so versatile?

Chenin Blanc keeps very high acidity even when fully ripe, which means it can be made bone-dry, off-dry, sweet or sparkling and still taste balanced.

That acidity is the grape's superpower. In sweet wines it stops the sugar from cloying; in dry wines it gives tension; in sparkling wine it provides the backbone.

It also ripens unevenly, which is a problem for some grapes and an opportunity here: growers can pick several times, taking riper bunches for sweet wines and fresher ones for dry.

The Loire Valley shows the full range. Savennières is dry and mineral, Vouvray can be dry, off-dry, sweet or sparkling, and Coteaux du Layon is a rich sweet wine made with noble rot.

South Africa, where it's called Steen, is the other heartland, with old bush vines making everything from simple, fresh whites to serious, textured, age-worthy bottles.

Flavours run from quince, apple and chamomile when young to honey, marzipan and beeswax with age. Good Chenin ages for decades.

The one catch is that labels rarely state sweetness, so a Vouvray can surprise you. If you're unsure, ask.
