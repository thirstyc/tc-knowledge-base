What pairs with Outaouais whites and light reds?

Outaouais wines are young and unshowy, which makes them easy at the table rather than something to fuss over. The whites, mostly cool-climate aromatics with good acid, cut through the fat of grilled trout or a maple-glazed pork chop without fighting either the smoke or the sweetness. Light reds here rarely have the tannin or ripeness to stand up to red meat, so keep them with charcuterie or a washed-rind farmhouse cheese instead, where their brightness resets your palate rather than getting steamrolled. Serve the whites cold, around 8 to 10 Celsius, and the reds on the cool side too, closer to 13 to 15, since most of them lack the structure to carry room temperature well. Nothing here demands decanting or ceremony. This is weekday food-and-wine territory, built for cabane à sucre season and lake-house dinners, not for impressing anyone.
