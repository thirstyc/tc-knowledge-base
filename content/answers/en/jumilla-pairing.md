What pairs with Jumilla?

Jumilla is built on Monastrell grown in a hot, dry corner of Murcia, which means thick skins, ripe tannins, and alcohol that regularly pushes past 14.5 percent. That kind of weight needs food with equivalent weight and fat to push back against it, otherwise the wine reads as hot and heavy on its own. Grilled and charred meat is the obvious answer: lamb, chorizo, anything off a live fire, because the char echoes the wine's smoky, savory edge and the fat coats your palate enough that the tannin and alcohol stop feeling aggressive. Spicy food works for the same reason, the richness and slight sweetness of the fruit buffers the heat instead of amplifying it, which a high-acid, low-alcohol white would not do. What it does not want is anything delicate: light fish, salads, subtle sauces just get flattened. Serve it around 16 to 18 degrees, not warmer, since heat makes the alcohol more obvious. A short decant helps if it feels tight or hot straight out of the bottle.
