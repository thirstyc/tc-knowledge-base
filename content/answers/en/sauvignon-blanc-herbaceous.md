Why is Sauvignon Blanc so crisp and herbaceous?

That green, grassy character isn't a flaw or a style choice, it's chemistry specific to the grape. Sauvignon Blanc is naturally high in methoxypyrazines, the same compounds found in green bell peppers, which read as grass, herbs or even jalapeño depending on ripeness at harvest. Pick earlier and cooler and those compounds dominate; let the grapes hang longer and thiol compounds take over instead, pushing toward grapefruit, passionfruit and gooseberry. The acidity comes from the grape retaining malic acid well in cooler sites, and winemakers usually skip malolactic fermentation on purpose to preserve that sharpness rather than soften it into something creamy. Fermentation and aging almost always happen in stainless steel, not oak, because the whole point of the variety is freshness, not richness. Serve it cold, around 8 to 10°C, to keep that acidity feeling bright rather than sour. It's meant to be tense and aromatic, not smooth.
