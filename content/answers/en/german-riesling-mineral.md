Why does German Riesling taste so elegant and mineral?

The elegance comes from restraint, not richness: German Riesling vineyards sit on steep, often slate-heavy slopes far enough north that ripening is slow and marginal, which lets the grape hold onto acidity that would collapse in a hotter climate. That acid is the backbone of everything people describe as mineral or precise, it's less about the soil transmitting flavor and more about the wine having a taut, electric structure that reads as stony rather than fruity. Sugar and acid work as a pair here, a Kabinett or Spätlese with noticeable residual sweetness isn't a sweet wine so much as an acid wine with sugar bolted on to keep it from feeling harsh, the two balance each other rather than one dominating. Alcohol stays low, often under 11 percent, because fermentation is deliberately halted early. That same acid is also what lets these wines age for twenty or thirty years without falling apart. Serve cold, 8 to 10°C, and treat it as a food wine, it was built to sit next to something salty or spicy, not dessert.
