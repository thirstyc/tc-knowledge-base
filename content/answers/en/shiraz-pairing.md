What pairs with Shiraz?

Shiraz brings ripe black fruit, soft tannin, and often a solid 14-15% alcohol, which means it needs food with weight and some char to match it rather than get flattened by it. Grilled or smoked red meat is the classic pairing because the fat and caramelized crust stand up to the fruit and tannin, and the alcohol helps cut through richness. It also has a genuine affinity for spice - Shiraz naturally carries a compound called rotundone, which smells and tastes like black pepper, so dishes with actual pepper, chili, or smoky barbecue rub echo something already in the glass instead of clashing with it. Cooler-climate versions, often labeled Syrah, lean more toward violet and black pepper than jammy fruit, and pair beautifully with duck or lamb. Serve around 16-17°C, not warmer - Shiraz's alcohol turns hot and blowsy if it's too close to room temperature in a warm room.
