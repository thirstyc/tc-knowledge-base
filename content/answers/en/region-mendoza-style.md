Why does Mendoza Malbec taste so plush and violet-scented?

It's the combination of brutal sun and cold nights doing two different jobs at once. The high desert altitude means intense UV exposure during the day, which thickens grape skins and pumps up anthocyanins — the pigments responsible for that deep purple colour and violet aroma. But the same altitude brings a huge diurnal swing, and temperatures drop sharply after dark, which slows sugar accumulation and preserves acidity. So you get grapes that reach full phenolic ripeness — soft, round tannins, no green or bitter edge — while still holding onto the acid that keeps the wine from turning flabby or jammy. Low humidity also means growers aren't rushing to pick early to dodge rot, so fruit hangs longer and develops flavour without losing structure. Serve it around 16-18°C, not cellar-cold — colder mutes the violet and dark-fruit aromatics. Young bottles benefit from a quick 30-minute decant to soften the tannin edges further.
