What pairs with Hárslevelü?

Hárslevelű's name means "linden leaf" in Hungarian, and that's the tip-off: it's an aromatic, waxy-textured white grown mostly in Tokaj, often blended with or made alongside Furmint. In its dry form it has honeyed, linden-blossom aromatics with a broad palate and enough acid to keep it from feeling flabby -- that combination handles spicy food well because the slight waxiness and low bitterness cushion chili heat rather than fighting it. Where it gets serious is in sweet Tokaji Aszú, where botrytis concentrates sugar and glycerol; that's the version people mean when they say "dessert" or "foie gras." The logic there: foie gras is pure fat, and a sweet, acid-balanced wine cuts through it the way a fruit chutney would, without needing tannin. Serve dry Hárslevelű around 10-12°C, sweet Aszú closer to 8-10°C, and pour small -- this is a wine you sip slowly, not glug.
