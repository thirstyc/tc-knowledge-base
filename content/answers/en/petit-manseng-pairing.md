What pairs with Petit Manseng?

Petit Manseng usually shows up in two very different styles, so the pairing depends on which bottle you've got. The classic version, left on the vine into autumn until the berries shrivel like raisins, concentrates sugar and acid together, giving a wine that's sweet but never flabby. That combination is what makes it work with foie gras: the acid cuts the fat, the sugar matches the richness. It's also a natural with blue cheese, tarte tatin, or anything with caramelized or roasted fruit. Because the acidity stays so high even at high sugar levels, it also handles spicy food better than most dessert wines, the heat doesn't fight the wine, the wine just cools it down. Serve it cold, around 8 to 10°C, cold enough to keep it from tasting cloying. Drier versions of Petit Manseng exist too, more citrus and honeyed than sweet, and those go well with roast chicken or creamy seafood. Check the label before assuming dessert.
