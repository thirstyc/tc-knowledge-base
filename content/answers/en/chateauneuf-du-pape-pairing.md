What pairs with Châteauneuf-du-Pape?

This is a Grenache-dominant blend from a hot, stony corner of the southern Rhône, and everything about the wine follows from that heat. Grenache ripens to high sugar levels, which means high alcohol, often 14 to 15 percent, plus ripe, jammy fruit and soft tannins rather than a sharp acid backbone. That combination needs food with real fat and protein to balance it, otherwise the alcohol reads as hot and the wine feels flabby. Lamb, especially roasted with garlic and herbes de Provence, is the classic match because the fat coats the palate and the wine's fruit and spice play off the herbs. Daube, braised game, and mushroom-heavy dishes work for the same reason. Avoid light, delicate food entirely, it'll get steamrolled. Serve it a touch cooler than room temperature, around 16 to 17°C, which keeps the alcohol from feeling like it's leading the charge and lets the actual fruit and spice show through.
