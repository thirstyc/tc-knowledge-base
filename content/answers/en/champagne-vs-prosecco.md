Why does Champagne taste so different from Prosecco?

The difference is entirely in the fermentation, not just the marketing. Champagne undergoes its second fermentation inside the bottle, then sits on its dead yeast cells (lees) for years. That yeast slowly autolyzes, breaking down and releasing compounds that give you brioche, toast, and a creamy, almost chalky texture alongside fine, persistent bubbles. Prosecco is made from Glera grapes and fermented a second time in large pressurized tanks, the Charmat method, for a few weeks at most, which locks in fresh fruit aromas like pear and green apple but skips all that yeasty complexity. Champagne's chalk soil also retains water and reflects light back onto the vines while keeping roots cool, which helps grapes hang onto acidity even as they ripen. Prosecco vineyards don't have that. The result: Champagne is structured, savory, and built to age; Prosecco is a fruity, easy aperitif meant to be drunk within a year or two, cold, around 6-8°C. Neither is trying to be the other.
