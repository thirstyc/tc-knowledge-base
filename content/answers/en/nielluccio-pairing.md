What pairs with Nielluccio?

Nielluccio is Corsica's clone of Sangiovese, and it carries the same backbone: bright acidity, moderate tannin, red cherry and sour plum fruit, with a herbal lift that comes straight from the maquis, the wild scrubland of rosemary, thyme and myrtle that grows around the vines in Patrimonio. That acidity is the whole point at the table. It cuts through olive oil and fat without bulldozing delicate flavours, which is why it works with grilled fish and fresh pasta in a way that a bigger, oakier red never could. The herbal note also does something specific: it echoes whatever wild herbs are already seasoning the dish, so a salad dressed with thyme or a fish rubbed with rosemary feels like it was built for the wine rather than just tolerating it. Serve it around 14-16°C, cool enough to keep the acid crisp but not so cold that you mute the fruit. Avoid heavy cream sauces or aged cheese, both will flatten it.
