Why does Hawke's Bay Merlot taste so ripe yet structured?

The ripeness comes from heat, the structure comes from stress, and both come from the same gravel. Hawke's Bay is New Zealand's warmest major wine region, giving Merlot more sun exposure and riper tannins than it would ever see in Bordeaux. But the real trick is underfoot: the Gimblett Gravels site is essentially an old riverbed, stony and nearly barren of topsoil, so water drains through almost immediately and the vines have to fight for everything they get. Stressed vines make smaller berries with a higher skin-to-juice ratio, which concentrates both flavour and tannin instead of just sugar. The result is fruit that reads as generous and plummy up front, Bordeaux-style in shape but with none of the leanness, while still carrying real grip and structure underneath. It is often described, fairly, as New Zealand's answer to a Bordeaux blend, riper in body but not softer in bones.
