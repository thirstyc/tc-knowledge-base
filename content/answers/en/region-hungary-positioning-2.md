Why does Hungary matter?

Tokaji was the wine European royalty fought wars and signed treaties over before Bordeaux had any international reputation to speak of. Louis XIV's court prized it, Russian tsars kept private allocations, and it became one of the first wine regions anywhere to get a formal quality classification, in 1730, decades before anyone in France bothered. That history got buried under communism, when Tokaj's vineyards were collectivized and quality collapsed into bulk sweet wine with none of the old precision. What's happening now is a genuine recovery: producers are back to making Aszú the traditional way, but they're also proving Furmint works as a serious dry white, with the volcanic-soil tension and acidity to stand next to Chablis or Riesling. Kékfrankos, the country's main red grape, is going through the same rehabilitation, peppery, structured, food-friendly wines from producers finally being taken seriously outside Hungary's borders. It's one of the more interesting rediscovery stories in wine right now, not a nostalgia act.
