Can you age Marsanne?

Yes, and it's one of the more underrated white grapes for it. Marsanne isn't an acid bomb the way Riesling or Chenin Blanc is; its ageability comes instead from phenolic texture and body, waxy, lanolin-like weight that a lot of tannic structure and low natural acidity would otherwise leave feeling flat when young. Give it five to ten years and that waxiness turns into honeyed, beeswax, almond-paste complexity, often with a nutty edge, especially from northern Rhône appellations like Hermitage Blanc and Crozes-Hermitage, where it's frequently blended with Roussanne for added lift and aromatics. Young Marsanne can actually be a bit dull and blowsy on release, which is exactly why people underestimate it, it needs the bottle time to knit together. Drink it too early and you'll wonder what the fuss is about. Serve young bottles around 10 to 12°C to keep them from feeling flabby, and bump aged examples slightly warmer, 12 to 14°C, to let the developed aromatics show.
