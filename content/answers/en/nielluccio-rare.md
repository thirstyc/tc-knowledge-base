Why is Nielluccio hard to find outside Corsica?

Corsica only has about 8,500 hectares under vine, and a huge share of what's made never leaves the island because it's drunk by locals and by the roughly three million tourists who show up every summer wanting a glass with lunch. Nielluccio specifically is tied to Patrimonio, a small appellation on the north of the island where it has to make up the majority of the blend, so volumes are naturally capped by geography rather than demand. There's also no real tradition of large-scale export infrastructure the way Languedoc or Rhône producers built over decades, so what little surplus exists tends to get picked up by importers who already have a relationship with a specific domaine. The result is a wine that's genuinely everywhere on Corsica and genuinely scarce fifteen minutes off the island by ferry.
