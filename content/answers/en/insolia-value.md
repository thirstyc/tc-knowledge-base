Is Insolia worth buying, or is it just a cheap Sicilian filler wine?

It's underrated, not cheap filler. Insolia gets overshadowed because Sicily's reputation abroad rests mostly on Nero d'Avola and, more recently, Etna's reds and whites, so a modest, food-friendly white from the west of the island doesn't get much marketing muscle behind it. But that's exactly its appeal: producers aren't dressing it up with oak or forcing extraction to make a statement, because nobody's watching closely enough to demand it. What you get instead is a fairly transparent, inexpensive white that actually tastes like where it's from, sun-baked but not flabby, with real food-pairing chops. It won't blow you away with complexity and it's not a wine to cellar or show off at a dinner party. But for a Tuesday night with grilled fish and no pretense, it does exactly what it needs to do, and it costs a fraction of what a similarly satisfying white from more fashionable regions would run you.
