Why is Gavi sometimes expensive?

Basic Gavi is made to be cheap and cheerful: young vines or high yields, steel tank, bottled early, and meant to be drunk within a year or two while it's still zesty. The pricier bottlings come from specific single vineyards or the historic Gavi di Gavi zone around the town itself, where older vines, lower yields, and more selective sites concentrate flavor rather than just volume. Some producers also make a riserva style, often with extended lees contact or a stint in larger oak, which adds texture and ageing potential that basic Gavi was never built for. You're paying for site specificity and the labor of lower yields, not marketing gloss, the same economics that separate a village Chablis from a premier cru. If you're drinking it young and cold as an aperitif, the cheap version does the job fine. If you want something that can sit in a cellar for five or ten years and evolve, that's when the higher price actually buys you something real.
