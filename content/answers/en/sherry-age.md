Can you age Sherry?

Yes, but not the way you'd age a Bordeaux, where one vintage sits quietly in a bottle. Sherry is aged through the solera system, a stack of barrels where younger wine is periodically blended into progressively older barrels, and a portion of the oldest is drawn off to bottle. That constant blending means a bottle never represents a single vintage, it's an average of many years, some soleras running a decade or more, some considerably longer. Styles aged under a living layer of flor yeast, like Fino and Manzanilla, stay pale and fresh because the flor protects the wine from oxygen. Styles aged oxidatively, like Oloroso, are left more exposed to air on purpose, which deepens colour and builds nutty, dried-fruit, caramelized notes over time. Once you open a bottle, treat the two differently: Fino and Manzanilla fade fast and should be drunk within days, while Oloroso and Amontillado, already partly oxidized, can hold for weeks in the fridge.
