Why does Kerner taste so floral and fruity?

Kerner is a cross between Riesling and Trollinger (Schiava), made in 1929 in Württemberg by a breeder named August Herold and named after a local poet. It inherited Riesling's aromatic terpene genes, which is where the perfume comes from: linden blossom, elderflower, sometimes a whiff of jasmine, layered over peach and apricot. What it didn't inherit was Riesling's fussiness. Kerner buds late, so it dodges spring frost, and ripens relatively early, which suits Germany's marginal, cool sites where Riesling can struggle to fully mature some years. That early ripening at moderate sugar levels, combined with the cool nights typical of German vineyards, keeps acidity present but not aggressive - medium rather than searing. The fruit and florals read as generous because the wine is usually made slightly off-dry, which softens the acid and pushes the aromatics forward. Serve it cold, around 8-10°C, and don't expect Riesling's tension - it's rounder and friendlier by design.
