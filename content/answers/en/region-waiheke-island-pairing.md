What pairs with Waiheke Cabernet?

Waiheke reds, usually Bordeaux-style blends built around Cabernet Sauvignon with Merlot and sometimes Cabernet Franc or Malbec, get real structure from the island's clay-heavy soils and long, warm, maritime-moderated growing season — good ripeness, firm tannin, real concentration. That tannin and weight need protein and fat to soften against, which is why grilled steak and lamb rack work so well: the char and fat coat the palate and blunt the grip of the tannins, while the meat's richness matches the wine's density instead of getting steamrolled by it. A hard, aged cheese does the same job differently — the salt and fat cut through the tannin while the cheese's intensity holds its own against the wine. Skip anything delicate or acidic; these wines will flatten a light dish. Serve at 16-18°C, and give a young bottle real time in a decanter — an hour isn't excessive — since the tannins are often still tight and need air to unwind before they show their better side.
