Why does Vintage Port taste so dark and powerful?

Vintage Port gets fortified partway through fermentation: grape spirit is added while there's still plenty of sugar left, which kills off the yeast and locks in both high alcohol, around 20 percent, and a hefty dose of residual sugar. Because fermentation stops early, the wine also retains more color and tannin extracted from the skins of Douro grapes like Touriga Nacional, which is why it's so dense and structured compared to table wine. It's only declared a handful of times per decade, in the best vintages, and bottled young after roughly two years in barrel, then left to do the real aging in bottle. That's where the power turns into something drinkable: decades of slow change soften the tannin and knit the fruit together. Young, it's genuinely too much; that's not a flaw, it's the wine doing exactly what it's built to do.
