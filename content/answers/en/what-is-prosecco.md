What is Prosecco?

Prosecco is Italian sparkling wine from the northeast of the country, made from the Glera grape, with fresh, fruity flavours and soft bubbles.

Its bubbles come from a second fermentation in a large pressurised tank rather than in the bottle. That method is quicker and cheaper, and it preserves the grape's fresh character: pear, green apple, melon and white flowers.

Quality levels are on the label. Prosecco DOC covers a large area and most everyday bottles. Prosecco Superiore DOCG, from the hills of Conegliano Valdobbiadene or Asolo, comes from steeper, better sites and has more depth. Within that, Rive wines come from a single hillside, and Cartizze from a small, prized area.

Sweetness levels can confuse: Brut is driest, Extra Dry is slightly sweeter, and Dry is sweeter still.

You'll also find Prosecco Col Fondo, an old-style, cloudy version bottled with its yeast and left undisgorged.

Prosecco is best young and cold. It's perfect as an aperitif, with fried food or in a spritz, and it costs far less than traditional-method sparkling wine.

Don't expect Champagne's bready complexity: it's a different wine, made for freshness.
