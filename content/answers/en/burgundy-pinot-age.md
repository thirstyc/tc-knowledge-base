Can you age Burgundy Pinot Noir?

Good red Burgundy can age, but it's not a given the way it is with Bordeaux or Barolo. Pinot Noir has lower tannin than those grapes, so its longevity depends more on acidity and concentration than on tannic grip. Village-level wines are usually best within 5-8 years. Premier Cru and Grand Cru wines, from lower-yielding vines and often riper fruit, can hold and improve for 10-20 years, sometimes longer in strong vintages. What changes: the bright red-fruit character (cherry, raspberry) fades into something more savoury -- forest floor, dried herbs, mushroom, a silkier texture as the modest tannins resolve. This isn't automatic improvement; a lot of Burgundy is made to be enjoyed on the fruitier side, and cellaring won't rescue a wine that lacked structure to begin with. Storage matters more here than with tougher reds, since Pinot Noir is fragile -- keep it around 12-14C, away from light and vibration. If you're aging Burgundy as an investment, know your producer and vintage; if you're aging it to drink, taste one bottle every couple of years so you actually catch it at its peak instead of guessing.
