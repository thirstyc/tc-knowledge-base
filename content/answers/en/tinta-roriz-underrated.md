Why does Tinta Roriz fly under the radar?

It's Tempranillo under a different name, but in the Douro it spent most of its history as Port grape number two or three, not the star of a dry red. The region built its entire reputation on fortified wine, so the best fruit, the best sites, the best attention from growers went toward Port production for generations. Serious dry table wines from Tinta Roriz and its blending partners - Touriga Nacional, Touriga Franca - are a relatively recent development, really picking up momentum from the 1990s onward as producers realized the same steep schist terraces that make great Port fruit could make great still wine too. That's a short runway compared to Rioja or Ribera del Duero, and Port's shadow is long: mention Douro to most drinkers and they think fortified, sweet, after-dinner. The dry reds are still building their own identity in the market, which means good ones are often priced well below what the quality in the glass would suggest.
