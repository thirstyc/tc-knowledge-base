Why does Salta Malbec fly under the radar?

Mendoza owns the Malbec conversation, so Salta gets treated as a footnote when it's actually the more extreme expression of the grape. We're talking vineyards up around 1,700 to 3,100 metres in Cafayate and the Calchaquí Valley, some of the highest commercial viticulture on earth. Up there the sun is brutal but the nights drop hard, so grapes build thick skins and deep colour for UV protection while still holding onto acidity that would shrivel away at lower altitude. The result is Malbec with more lift, more violet and black pepper aromatics, and firmer tannin than the plush, chocolatey Mendoza style most people know. Production is tiny compared to Mendoza's scale, the region is harder to farm, and distributors default to the name people already recognize. None of that reflects quality, just market inertia. If you find a bottle, serve it a touch cooler than a typical red, around 16 to 17°C, to keep that altitude-driven freshness intact.
