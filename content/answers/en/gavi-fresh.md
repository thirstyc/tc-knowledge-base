Why does Gavi taste so crisp and fresh?

Gavi is Cortese, grown in the far south of Piedmont near the Ligurian border, close enough to the sea that summer nights cool down fast even though the days can be warm. That day-night swing is the real mechanism: grapes build sugar in the heat but keep their malic acid because the vine essentially stops respiring it away once temperatures drop. Cortese itself is also a genetically low-phenolic, high-acid grape, so even before you factor in climate it leans lean and citric rather than round. Most producers ferment in stainless steel and skip malolactic conversion, which would soften that crisp malic bite into creamier lactic acid. The result is a wine built around tension rather than fruit weight: green apple, lemon pith, a faint almond note, and a saline thread from the limestone-clay soils. Serve it cold, around 8 to 10°C, and drink most bottlings within two or three years, while that acid is still singing.
