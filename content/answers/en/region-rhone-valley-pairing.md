What pairs with Rhône reds?

Rhône reds, especially Southern blends built on Grenache, Syrah and Mourvèdre, bring ripe dark fruit, savory garrigue herb notes, grippy tannin and often noticeable alcohol, all of which need fat and protein on the plate to balance rather than fight. Braised short rib works because slow cooking breaks down collagen into gelatin, which coats the palate the same way the wine's tannin does, so the two meet in the middle instead of clashing. Herb-crusted lamb plays off the wine's own thyme-and-rosemary character, since Grenache grown among wild scrub genuinely picks up those aromatics. Peppered steak leans on the wine's spice and structure to match the char and cracked pepper without either one overwhelming the other. These are wines built for slow-cooked, fatty, herb-heavy food, not anything delicate or acid-driven. Pour them at 16 to 18°C, slightly below room temperature, so the alcohol doesn't read as hot and the fruit stays in focus.
