Best organic wine?

Organic wine is made from grapes grown without synthetic pesticides, herbicides or fertilisers. The best way to find good ones is to look for certification and for regions where organic farming is common.

Certification matters because it's regulated. Look for the EU organic logo, a green leaf of stars, or national certifiers, and in the United States, the USDA Organic seal.

A US labelling quirk worth knowing: "Organic wine" there must be made without added sulfites, while "made with organic grapes" allows some. Many good American organic producers use the second label.

Regions where organic is common include the Languedoc, Provence, Sicily, parts of Spain, and much of the Loire and Alsace, where dry, windy or sunny climates make it easier to farm without chemicals.

Two useful caveats:

- Many excellent producers farm organically without certifying, because it's expensive and bureaucratic, especially for small estates.
- Organic covers farming, not the winery. An organic wine can still be heavily processed; for minimal intervention, look at natural wine.

Quality varies like any category. Treat organic as information about how the grapes were grown, then judge the wine on how it tastes.
