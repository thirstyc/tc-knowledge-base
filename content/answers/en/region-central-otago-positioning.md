Why is Central Otago considered New Zealand's cult Pinot region?

Central Otago produces a fraction of what Marlborough does, and that scarcity is baked into its identity. Where Marlborough built an empire on affordable, high-volume Sauvignon Blanc, Central Otago's steep, marginal vineyard sites and small family-run wineries mean tiny lots of Pinot Noir made with far more hands-on attention. Less wine means higher prices, and higher prices combined with genuine quality have made it a target for collectors and Pinot obsessives who want something harder to find. The cellar-door culture reinforces this: many producers sell a meaningful chunk of their wine directly to visitors or a small mailing list rather than through wide distribution, so getting a bottle sometimes means seeking it out rather than grabbing it off a supermarket shelf. That gap between scale and reputation is exactly what cult status is built on — not marketing, but genuine limited supply meeting genuine demand from people who have tasted what the region can do.
