What pairs with Australian Semillon?

Young Hunter Valley Semillon is lean, citrusy, and bone dry, with barely any oak or fruit sweetness to get in the way, so it works best with food that's delicate rather than rich or heavily sauced. Fresh oysters, grilled white fish, or a simple lemon-dressed pasta let that lime-and-mineral edge do its job without competing for attention. Soft, young cheeses, think goat's curd or a mild fresh chevre, are a classic match, since the wine's acidity cuts through the creaminess the same way it would with a beurre blanc. It's also genuinely good with Vietnamese and Thai dishes that lean on lime and herbs rather than heavy chilli, because the wine's own citrus character just doubles down on those flavours. If you've got an aged bottle, ten years or more, it shifts into honeyed, toasty territory and can handle richer things: roast chicken, mushroom dishes, even a mild curry. Either way, serve it properly cold, and don't waste it on anything smothered in cream or heavy oak-driven sauces, that's not the register it plays in.
