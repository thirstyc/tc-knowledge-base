Why is Rebula hard to find outside Slovenia?

Rebula and Ribolla Gialla are the same grape wearing two passports. The border between Friuli-Venezia Giulia and Slovenia's Goriška Brda region cuts straight through what used to be one continuous vineyard, split up by twentieth-century politics rather than terroir. On the Italian side, producers in Collio and Friuli Colli Orientali built export machinery and international buzz around Ribolla Gialla, especially once the orange-wine movement made its skin-contact versions fashionable. Slovenia's side is smaller, family-run, and historically oriented toward domestic drinking and cross-border sales into Italy and Austria rather than shipping bottles to Montreal. There's also a naming problem: wine sold as Rebula doesn't get to ride the Ribolla Gialla name recognition abroad, so even good bottles struggle for shelf space. None of this is about quality — some of Slovenia's skin-contact Rebula, from producers working the same limestone slopes as their Italian neighbours, is genuinely excellent. It's a supply and marketing issue, not a flavour one. If you find a bottle, buy two.
