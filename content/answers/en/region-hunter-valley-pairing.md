What pairs with aged Hunter Valley Semillon?

Young Hunter Semillon is lemony, austere, barely 11% alcohol, and made for oysters or something equally raw and briny. Aged Semillon is a different animal. Ten or fifteen years in bottle turns that lean acid backbone into something with toast, honey, beeswax and dried straw notes, even though the wine never saw oak. That richness of flavour without richness of alcohol is the trick: it can stand up to roast chicken, Cantonese roast duck, or a cream-based curry without the dish tipping the wine over, because the acid is still doing its job of cutting fat. Try it with semi-hard washed-rind cheese too. Serve it a touch cooler than a red, around 10 to 12°C, so you don't fog up all that toasty complexity with warmth. The mistake people make is drinking it young and calling it boring, or drinking it old and pairing it like a rich Chardonnay. It behaves like neither.
