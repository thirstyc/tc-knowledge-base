Why is Poulsard hard to find outside Jura?

Poulsard is a nightmare in the vineyard outside its home turf. Its skins are thin and delicate, which makes it prone to rot and coulure, and it wants the specific combination of cool continental climate and clay-limestone soils that the Jura happens to have in abundance. Yields are naturally low and inconsistent, which is fine if you're a small Jura producer built around low-intervention, low-yield farming, but commercially unappealing almost everywhere else, since it's a lot of risk for not much juice. The Jura's main appellations, Arbois and Côtes du Jura among them, have also protected and championed it for generations, so there's simply no competing pool of expertise or demand pushing growers elsewhere to bother. A handful of curious producers outside France have started experimenting with it, but don't expect it to show up as a mainstream planting anytime soon; it's a grape that rewards obsession, not scale.
