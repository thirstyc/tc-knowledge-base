How long does Pinot Noir age?

Most Pinot Noir is best within two to five years. Good-quality examples age well for five to fifteen years, and the best Burgundy can improve for twenty years or more.

Pinot Noir has thin skins and relatively low tannin, so it relies on acidity and fruit concentration to age. That's why its ageing potential varies so much by origin and quality.

A rough guide:

- Everyday Pinot Noir from warm regions: drink within two to four years, while the fruit is fresh.
- Good New World Pinot from Oregon, New Zealand, California or Australia: five to ten years, sometimes more.
- Village Burgundy: five to ten years.
- Premier Cru and Grand Cru Burgundy: ten to twenty-plus years from a good vintage.

How it changes: young Pinot tastes of fresh red cherry, raspberry and flowers. With age, it develops earthy, savoury notes of mushroom, forest floor, leather and dried roses, while the colour turns from ruby to brick-orange. Many collectors think this "sous-bois" character is Pinot at its most magical.

Signs to drink up: fading fruit, a brown rim and a dry, thin finish.

Storage matters more than anything. Keep Pinot cool, dark and steady, around 12–14°C.
