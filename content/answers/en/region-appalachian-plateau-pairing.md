What pairs with Plateaux des Appalaches whites?

These whites, typically Vidal or Seyval Blanc based, are built almost entirely around acidity because that's what survives a short, cold growing season at altitude. Sugars barely ripen fully, so the wine leans lean, high-strung, and citrusy rather than round. That acidity is precisely what makes it work with smoked trout, since the wine cuts through the oil and smoke instead of being smothered by them, and with sharp cheddar, where the cut of the acid balances the cheese's saltiness. Root vegetable dishes, especially anything roasted with a little char, pick up on the wine's slightly mineral, austere edge. Serve it cold, around 8 to 10 degrees, cold enough to keep the acid feeling crisp rather than sharp-edged. This isn't a wine to drink on its own for pleasure so much as one designed to make rustic, fatty, smoky food taste cleaner. Judge it by what it does at the table, not by how it tastes alone.
