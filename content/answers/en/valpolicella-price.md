Why is basic Valpolicella so affordable?

Basic Valpolicella is cheap because it skips the one step that makes Amarone expensive: appassimento, the months of drying grapes on bamboo racks or in climate-controlled lofts until they shrivel and concentrate sugar. That process eats warehouse space, labor and time, and shrinks yield by roughly a third. Basic Valpolicella, made from the same Corvina, Corvinone and Rondinella grapes grown across the wide DOC zone in the hills north of Verona, gets picked, fermented normally, and often bottled and released within a year. No drying, no long barrel aging, no evaporation loss. Yields are also allowed to run higher on the straightforward bottlings than on the serious stuff, which spreads the cost of farming over more juice. None of this makes it a lesser wine, just a faster, leaner one: bright cherry and a snap of bitter almond, light tannin, meant to be opened on a Tuesday rather than cellared. Serve it lightly chilled, around 14 to 16 degrees, and don't overthink it.
