Why does Australian Riesling taste so citrusy and mineral?

It sounds backwards, but Australia's best Riesling comes from some of its coolest, highest-altitude sites - the Clare Valley and Eden Valley in South Australia, both sitting well above the surrounding warm plains and getting a real day-to-night temperature drop even in summer. That diurnal swing lets grapes ripen slowly, building citrus and floral aromatics while holding onto the racy natural acidity that gives the wine its backbone. The mineral impression is often credited to the region's ancient, rocky soils - slate and quartz in parts of Clare - though the more grounded explanation is that low fruit intensity paired with high acid reads to our palates as stony, rather than any literal mineral flavour transferring into the glass. These wines are almost always fermented fully dry, in stainless steel, with no oak and no malolactic softening, which is exactly why they stay so taut and age so well - good examples develop a toasty, petrol-like complexity after five to ten years in bottle. They're also chronically underpriced for the quality on offer, so don't judge one by the label's modest cost.
