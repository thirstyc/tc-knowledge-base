What pairs with Barbera?

Barbera's entire identity is acidity, often higher than Nebbiolo's, paired with very little tannin. That's the opposite structure of most serious reds, and it changes the pairing logic completely. Instead of needing fat to soften tannin, Barbera works on acid-matching-acid: tomato-based pasta sauces, which clash badly with tannic reds because tannin and tomato acid fight each other, actually harmonize with Barbera since the wine's acidity lines up with the sauce's rather than competing. The same principle carries into risotto, where butter and cheese need something to cut through the richness, and into grilled chicken or pork, where Barbera's acid does the cleansing work that tannin usually handles in a bigger red. Because there's so little tannin to manage, Barbera doesn't need rich, fatty food to tame it the way Barolo or Barbaresco do, it just wants acid or richness to play off. Serve it slightly cool, around 14-16°C, which sharpens the acidity further and makes it one of the few reds that actually improves alongside tomato-heavy cooking.
