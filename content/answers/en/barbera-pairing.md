What pairs with Barbera?

Barbera's whole personality is its acidity, often higher than Nebbiolo's, with relatively little tannin to back it up. That combination makes it a natural acid-on-acid match: tomato-based pasta sauces, which would clash with a tannic red, actually harmonize with Barbera because the wine's acid matches the sauce's rather than fighting it. The same logic works with risotto, where butter and cheese need something to cut through the richness, and grilled chicken or pork, where the acid does the job that tannin usually would in a bigger red. Because there's so little tannin, Barbera doesn't need fat to soften it the way Barolo or Barbaresco do, it just needs acid or richness to play against. Serve it slightly cool, around 14-16°C, which sharpens the acidity and makes it even more refreshing at the table. It's the rare red that works well with dishes most reds struggle against.
