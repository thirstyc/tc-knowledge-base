What pairs with Argentinean Malbec?

This is a wine built for fire and fat. Malbec's tannin and acidity are moderate but present, exactly enough to cut through the richness of grilled beef without fighting it, which is why it became the default pour for asado. Char and smoke on grilled meat actually echo the wine's own toasty, dark-fruit notes from oak aging, so the pairing reinforces itself rather than clashing. Chimichurri's vinegar and herb kick works with the wine's acidity instead of against it, brightening both sides. Spicy food works for similar reasons: Malbec's fruit-forward sweetness and soft tannins cushion chili heat rather than amplifying it, unlike a high-tannin, high-alcohol wine that can turn harsh next to spice. It's also forgiving with fattier cuts, sausage, short rib, lamb, because the wine's structure is built to stand up to protein and fat rather than delicate or acidic dishes. Serve at 16 to 17 degrees; too warm and the alcohol starts to dominate the fruit instead of framing it.
