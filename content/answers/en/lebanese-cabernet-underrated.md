Why does Lebanese wine fly under the radar?

It has nothing to do with the wine and everything to do with logistics. Building export markets requires decades of consistent shipping, stable distribution deals, and importers willing to bet on a region long-term. Lebanon spent much of the last fifty years fighting a civil war and absorbing regional conflict, which is a terrible environment for building any of that. Château Musar became the exception that proves the rule: Serge Hochar kept making wine straight through the war, sometimes literally under shellfire, and the mystique of those vintages got the label onto sommelier lists worldwide almost by accident. But one famous château does not build a country's reputation. Dozens of serious Bekaa Valley producers making excellent Cabernet, Cinsault, and indigenous blends never got the same shelf space, simply because nobody could guarantee the next container would arrive on schedule. The quality has been there the whole time. The supply chain just wasn't.
