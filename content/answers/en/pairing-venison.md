Wine with venison?

A savoury, medium to full-bodied red: northern Rhône Syrah, an aged Pinot Noir, Nebbiolo or a Rioja Gran Reserva. Venison is lean and gamey, so it pairs best with wines that share its earthy, wild flavours.

Unlike beef, venison has very little fat, so it doesn't need a huge, tannic wine to cut through it. What it wants is flavour: pepper, dark fruit, earth and a touch of age.

Syrah is a natural match. Northern Rhône wines like Crozes-Hermitage, Saint-Joseph and Hermitage have smoky, peppery notes that echo the meat.

Aged Pinot Noir, especially red Burgundy, develops forest-floor and mushroom notes that suit venison beautifully, particularly roasted loin.

Nebbiolo from Barolo or Barbaresco brings tar, roses and firm structure, great with slow-cooked venison or venison with truffles.

Venison stews and casseroles suit rich, warming reds like Châteauneuf-du-Pape or a Rioja Gran Reserva.

Venison with fruit sauces, like blackberry, cherry or juniper, pairs well with fruit-forward Pinot Noir or Syrah.

What to avoid: light, fruity reds and most whites. They get lost next to the rich, gamey flavour. Very oaky, jammy reds can also overwhelm the meat.
