What pairs with Picpoul Blanc?

Picpoul de Pinet is grown on the flats around the Bassin de Thau, a lagoon on the Languedoc coast that also happens to be one of France's biggest oyster-farming regions, so the pairing isn't marketing, it's geography. The grape's name literally means 'lip-stinger,' a nod to its naturally high acidity even in a hot southern climate where most whites go flabby. That acid does the actual work with oysters: it cuts through the brininess and fat of the shellfish liquor instead of fighting it, and it resets your palate for the next one. The same mechanism makes it work with raw seafood generally, ceviche, ortolan-thin carpaccio, and simple lemony pastas, anything where you need a wine that disappears into the food rather than competing with it. Serve it properly cold, around 8-10°C, colder than you'd think for a still wine, because warmth flattens that acidity and the wine turns dull fast. Do not bother pairing it with anything rich or oaked, it has neither the body nor the interest to stand up to it.
