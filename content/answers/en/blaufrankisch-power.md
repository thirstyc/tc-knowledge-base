Why is Blaufränkisch Austria's serious red?

Blaufränkisch gets treated as Austria's answer to a serious Bordeaux-style red because it genuinely has the structural components for it: thick skins that yield real tannin, and a late ripening cycle in cool sites like Mittelburgenland and Leithaberg that lets the grape build sugar and flavour while holding onto sharp, refreshing acidity instead of flabbing out. That acid-tannin combination is what gives the wine its backbone and its ability to age, rather than any winemaking trick. The signature black-pepper spice comes from rotundone, a compound the grape produces more of in cooler growing conditions, which is part of why the best examples come from Austria's coolest Blaufränkisch regions rather than warmer sites where the spice fades and the wine turns jammier and less distinctive. It's a grape that rewards patience, both in the vineyard, where yields need to be kept in check to avoid diluting all this structure, and in the glass, where a few hours of air or a few years in bottle will do it a lot of good.
