Wine with Mexican food?

Match the wine to the dish: crisp whites for fresh, zesty food, off-dry whites for heat, and juicy reds for grilled meat and mole. Mexican cooking is built on acidity, spice and smoke, so wines with bright acidity and ripe fruit tend to work best.

Guacamole, ceviche and fish tacos want something crisp and citrusy: Sauvignon Blanc, Albariño or Verdejo.

Tacos al pastor, with pork, pineapple and chile, suit an off-dry Riesling or a fruity dry rosé.

Carne asada and grilled steak tacos can take a juicy red like Malbec, Zinfandel or Tempranillo.

Mole, rich with chocolate, dried chiles and spices, is a great match for ripe, spicy reds like Zinfandel, Grenache or Primitivo.

Enchiladas verdes and other dishes with tomatillo and green chile work well with Sauvignon Blanc or Grüner Veltliner.

Very spicy salsas and dishes call for lower alcohol and a touch of sweetness. Off-dry Riesling is the safest choice.

Worth knowing: Mexico makes wine too, mostly in the Valle de Guadalupe in Baja California. Its reds and whites are an easy, fun match for the food.

What to avoid: oaky whites and heavy, tannic reds, which clash with chile and lime.
