Why is Burgundy so expensive?

Burgundy's price tag comes down to fragmentation and scarcity, not marketing. Centuries ago, monks mapped out which specific parcels of land produced better wine year after year, based on soil and drainage differences that can change block to block rather than village to village. That mapping became the climat system: Grand Cru, Premier Cru, village, and regional tiers, sometimes just meters apart on the same slope. Then inheritance laws required vineyards to be split equally among heirs generation after generation, so a single Grand Cru today is often owned by a dozen or more different growers, each making a few hundred cases at most. Because Burgundy is bottled as a single variety, Pinot Noir for red, Chardonnay for white, there's no blending partner to smooth over a difficult year or a mediocre plot; the wine is a direct, unfiltered report of that specific patch of ground. Combine that transparency with genuinely tiny production and a demand curve that's only grown globally, and you get an auction market where a few rows of vines can outprice an entire neighboring appellation.
