Why is the Finger Lakes considered America's Riesling frontier?

Riesling doesn't care about your marketing budget, it cares about slow ripening and cold nights that lock in acid while flavour compounds develop underneath. That's what the Finger Lakes offers: a growing season pushed to its limit, which forces grapes to hang longer and build the kind of tension you get in the Mosel or Rheingau. Winemakers here spent decades figuring out canopy management and site selection to get there, since a single wrong frost date can wipe out a vintage. The payoff is Riesling with real cut, tight fruit and a saline, stony finish, made dry more often than the region's sweet reputation suggests. Prices haven't caught up to the quality, so you can buy a bottle that drinks like a serious Kabinett or Grosses Gewächs competitor for a third of the cost. It's the most respected cool-climate region east of the Rockies for a reason, and one of the only places in America where Riesling is treated as the main event rather than an afterthought next to Cabernet.
