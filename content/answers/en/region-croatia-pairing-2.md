What pairs with Croatian wine?

Croatia's wine map splits roughly into coastal whites and the reds grown on the same sun-baked hillsides, and the pairing logic follows the coastline itself. Whites like Pošip and Grk grow on limestone islands in the Adriatic, giving them a saline, high-acid edge that's built to cut through raw or lightly cooked seafood — grilled octopus, sea bass, or anything that just came out of the water benefits from that acidity scrubbing the palate clean between bites. Plavac Mali, a relative of Zinfandel grown on steep coastal slopes, is a different animal entirely: thick-skinned, tannic, and full-bodied from intense sun exposure, which means it needs fat and char to soften it rather than delicate fish. Peka-roasted lamb, slow-cooked under an iron dome with its rendered fat and smoky crust, is the traditional match precisely because the richness tames the tannin. The general rule holds well beyond Croatia: the wine's structure is dictated by exposure and soil, so match delicate coastal whites to raw or barely cooked seafood, and save the reds for anything grilled.
