Why is Rossese hard to find outside Liguria?

Rossese grows on terraced vineyards clinging to steep hillsides above the Ligurian coast, near Dolceacqua, where machinery is useless and everything from pruning to harvest has to be done by hand. That kind of labor caps yields hard, so there was never much volume to begin with. Add to that a local market happy to drink it young, right there in the trattorias a short drive from the vines, and you get a wine that rarely needs to travel to find buyers. There's no grand conspiracy keeping it out of your local shop, just simple economics: small plots, high labor costs, and a built-in audience that empties the tanks before export ever becomes necessary. If you do find a bottle outside Italy, it's usually because an importer went looking specifically for it, not because there was surplus needing a home. Treat it as a lucky find rather than an oversight on the part of distributors.
