What pairs with Rebula?

Rebula's high acidity is essentially a palate-cleaning tool, which is why it works so well with raw seafood and oysters — the acid slices through brininess and fat the same way lemon does, resetting your palate between bites instead of letting richness build up. Light pasta dishes with olive oil, herbs or shellfish work for the same reason: there's enough acid to cut through oil without a lot of oak or heavy fruit to clash with delicate flavors. If you're drinking a skin-contact version, which has more texture and tannin than standard Rebula, it can handle slightly richer food — grilled fish, soft cheeses, even light charcuterie. Avoid pairing it with very sweet or heavily sauced dishes, which will make the acidity taste sharp rather than refreshing. Serve at 10-12°C, and treat it as a food wine first — it's not really built to be sipped alone without something on the plate to play against.
