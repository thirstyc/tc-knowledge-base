Why does Coda di Volpe fly under the radar?

Campania's reputation abroad rests almost entirely on its reds and on a couple of white grapes, Falanghina and Greco di Tufo, that got there first in export markets. Coda di Volpe has historically played a supporting role, often blended into Lacryma Christi del Vesuvio rather than bottled on its own, which meant it rarely had a chance to build a name for itself outside the region. Single-varietal versions exist and are worth seeking out, but production is small and most of what's made stays close to home, sold to people who already know what it is rather than exported to build a following. That's less a quality problem than a visibility one. The wine itself is exactly the kind of clean, food-friendly white that should travel well, it just hasn't had the marketing push its neighbors got. If you find a bottle, that scarcity is the whole story, not a warning sign.
