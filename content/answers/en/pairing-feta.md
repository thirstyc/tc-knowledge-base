Wine with feta?

A crisp, high-acid white like Assyrtiko, Sauvignon Blanc or Picpoul, or a dry rosé. Feta is salty and tangy, and it needs a wine with bright acidity to match its sharpness.

Greek wines are the obvious starting point. Assyrtiko from Santorini is bone-dry, saline and mineral, a natural partner for feta. Moschofilero is lighter and more aromatic, and Malagousia is another good option.

Sauvignon Blanc from the Loire or New Zealand works well, as do Picpoul de Pinet and Vermentino.

Greek salad, with tomato, olives, cucumber and onion, is fresh and acidic, so a crisp white or a dry rosé is best.

Baked feta, often with honey, herbs or tomatoes, is richer and can take an off-dry white or a fuller rosé.

Spanakopita and other pastries with feta and spinach suit a crisp white with a little texture, like Assyrtiko or Chablis.

Feta with watermelon is a summer favourite that pairs beautifully with a dry rosé.

Prefer red? Pick a light, fresh one with low tannin, like Xinomavro rosé or a chilled Gamay, since salt makes tannin taste harsh.
