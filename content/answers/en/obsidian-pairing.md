What pairs with Obsidian?

This is a tannin-forward, structured red, and tannin needs something to push against or it just reads as bitter and drying. Fat and protein soften it: the tannin molecules bind to proteins in meat, which is why grilled lamb, kebabs and heavier Turkish dishes work so well. Char adds another layer too, the Maillard compounds from grilling pick up on the wine's own smoky, roasted edge if it's seen any oak, so the pairing isn't just about weight matching weight, it's flavour matching flavour. Where this wine falls apart is with anything light or acid-driven, a squeeze of lemon on a salad will make the tannin taste harsher, not softer. Serve it a touch warmer than you would a delicate white or rosé, around 16-18°C, so the tannin doesn't seize up and taste even more astringent than it already is. Bread and rice-based sides are neutral enough to just let the meat and wine do the talking.
