Why does Paarl Chenin Blanc taste so rich yet fresh?

Paarl sits at lower altitude and runs warmer than Stellenbosch, which normally would push a grape toward flat, jammy fruit. Chenin Blanc resists that because it's a naturally high-acid variety, one of the few white grapes that can ripen fully in heat without losing its backbone. Add old bush vines, many unirrigated and left to dry-farm, and you get low yields with concentrated, ripe stone-fruit flavor, peach and quince rather than green apple, balanced against acid that never entirely leaves the wine even in a hot vintage. Some producers add a portion of older oak to round the texture without masking the fruit. The result is a white that reads as generous and full on the palate but finishes clean rather than heavy. Serve it around 10-12°C, not fridge-cold, so the fruit and texture actually open up. Paarl's been quietly doing this for generations; it's not a trend, it's just old vines and an honest grape.
