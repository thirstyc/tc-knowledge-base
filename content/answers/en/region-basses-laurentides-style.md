Why does Basses Laurentides wine taste so fresh and herbal?

The Laurentian foothills sit just north of these vineyards, and cool air drains down from them overnight even during summer, keeping nighttime temperatures low through the growing season. That day-night swing slows sugar accumulation in the grapes while preserving malic acid, which is why the wines come out fresh and taut rather than soft or heavy. The growing season itself is short and compressed, so grapes ripen quickly under intense but limited summer sun, giving fruit that's aromatic and lively without ever fully mellowing into richness. Most vineyards here rely on cold-hardy hybrid grapes bred specifically to survive harsh winters, and these varieties tend to carry naturally herbal, sometimes grassy or green-apple notes alongside their acidity. The wines are meant to be drunk young, within a year or two of release, chilled to around 8-10°C for whites. Don't expect polish or oak complexity here, this is terroir doing the talking through freshness and aromatics rather than structure.
