What pairs with Deux-Montagnes whites?

Deux-Montagnes whites lean on hybrid grapes like vidal and seyval blanc, bred to survive Quebec winters, and they hang onto malic acid because the growing season is short and rarely gets properly hot. That acid is the whole story on the plate. It cuts through the oils in trout without fighting the fish's delicate flavour, and it does the same job with soft, bloomy cheeses, slicing through the fat instead of getting buried by it. A garden salad with a light vinaigrette works for the same reason: the wine's acidity and the dressing's acidity are speaking the same language, so nothing clashes. What you want to avoid is anything rich or heavily sauced, since these whites are usually low in alcohol, around 11 to 12 percent, and light-bodied, so they'll disappear next to a cream sauce or a fatty cut of meat. Serve at 8 to 10°C, straight from the fridge is fine, and drink it young. This isn't a wine built to age or to headline a heavy meal.
