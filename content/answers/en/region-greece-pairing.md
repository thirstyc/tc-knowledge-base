What pairs with Greek Assyrtiko or Agiorgitiko?

Assyrtiko comes mostly from Santorini's volcanic soil, where vines are trained into low basket shapes to survive constant wind and near-total drought, and the result is searing acidity with a saline, mineral edge built to cut through olive oil and fat. That's why it works with grilled octopus or fresh feta: the acid slices through richness and the salinity echoes the brine. Agiorgitiko is the red counterpart, grown mostly around Nemea, medium-bodied with soft tannin and dark cherry fruit, close in weight to a light Merlot - which is exactly why it sits comfortably next to lamb souvlaki without overwhelming it or being overwhelmed. Greek cuisine leans on lemon, olive oil and grilled, charred flavours, and these grapes evolved alongside that food rather than being retrofitted to it. Serve Assyrtiko cold, around 8-10°C, and Agiorgitiko lightly chilled or at cool room temperature, around 14-16°C, rather than treating it like a big structured red.
