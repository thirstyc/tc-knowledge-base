Why is Chilean Cabernet so affordable?

Chile's low prices aren't a sign of inferior wine - they're a function of geography and economics. Land in the Central Valley is relatively cheap and mostly flat, which makes large-scale mechanized farming, machine harvesting, wide row spacing, easy to do well, unlike hillside regions where everything has to be done by hand. Water from Andes snowmelt is abundant and predictable, so irrigation costs stay low and yields stay consistent year to year, without the expensive frost or drought risk baked into pricing elsewhere.

Labor costs are also lower than in Napa or Bordeaux, and a weaker peso against the dollar or euro stretches export pricing further. On top of that, there's brutal competition - dozens of large producers chasing the same value-conscious export market, which keeps margins thin and prices honest. None of this means corners are cut on quality. It just means the overhead that inflates a lot of European and American bottles simply isn't part of the equation here.
