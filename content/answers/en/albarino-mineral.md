Why does Albariño taste so mineral and salty?

There's no actual salt in the glass, but the sensation comes from a real combination of factors. Rías Baixas sits right on Galicia's Atlantic coast, cooled by ocean fog and wind that keeps ripening slow and preserves malic acid in the grapes. That high, precise acidity is what reads as "salty" or "briny" on the palate, it's a textural and mineral impression, not sodium. The soils help too: granite and decomposed schist give the wine a stony, almost flinty edge rather than round fruitiness. Albariño also has thick skins, which contribute phenolic grip and a faint bitterness on the finish that reinforces the mineral effect. Many producers age briefly on lees, which adds a saline, savory note without adding weight. Serve it cold, around 8-10°C, and drink it young, within two or three years, since the whole appeal is that tension and freshness rather than any richness that develops with age.
