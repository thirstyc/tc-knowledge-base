What pairs with Pinot Gris?

Pinot Gris pairs well with casual food because it's built low-drama: moderate acidity, soft texture, modest tannin (it's barely in contact with skins), and fruit that leans pear, apple, sometimes a touch of honey. That combination means it won't fight delicate flavors the way a high-acid Sauvignon Blanc or a tannic red would. Light seafood like sole, shrimp, or scallops needs a wine that supports rather than competes, and Pinot Gris does that without turning the fish metallic the way heavy oak can. Fresh pasta with butter or light cream sauce works for the same reason: the wine's own weight roughly matches the dish, so neither overwhelms. Soft cheese like brie or a young chèvre gets a lift from the wine's acid cutting the fat, but gently, not aggressively like a Riesling would. Serve it around 8-10°C. If you're drinking the richer Alsace style, food isn't strictly necessary, it holds up fine on its own, but the lighter Italian-style Pinot Grigio really does want something on the plate to feel complete.
