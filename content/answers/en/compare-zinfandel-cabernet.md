Zinfandel vs Cabernet?

Both are big California reds, but Zinfandel is jammy, spicy and generous, while Cabernet Sauvignon is structured, firm and built to age.

Zinfandel ripens unevenly, so its wines often mix raisined and fresh berries, giving jammy raspberry and blackberry, black pepper and baking spice. Alcohol often runs high, 14.5% or more, but tannins are softer, so it feels plush rather than grippy.

Cabernet Sauvignon gives blackcurrant, cedar and graphite, with firm tannins and more acidity. It's tighter and more linear, and the best wines need years to soften.

Ageing reflects that. Most Zinfandel is at its best within five to ten years, while serious Cabernet can improve for decades.

At the table, Zinfandel suits barbecue, burgers, pizza and spicy sausages; its fruit handles sweet, smoky sauces. Cabernet wants a steak, lamb or aged cheese, where fat softens its tannins.

Both grow side by side in places like Sonoma and Napa, but old-vine Zinfandel, with vines a century old, is California's most distinctive heritage.

Which to choose? Zinfandel for fun, fruit and barbecue; Cabernet for structure and a serious dinner.
