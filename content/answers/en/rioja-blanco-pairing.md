What pairs with white Rioja?

White Rioja's food match depends on which style you've got. The modern, stainless-steel version - crisp, citrusy, saline-edged - works the way high-acid whites usually do: it cuts through fat and cleans the palate, making it a natural with shellfish, grilled white fish, and fresh pasta in olive oil or lemon-based sauces. The traditional, oak-aged style is a different animal - years in American oak give it a nutty, waxy, almost sherry-adjacent richness that can handle roast chicken, aged Manchego, or anything with a touch of char or smoke. Both styles share that dry, stony backbone from Viura grown on limestone, which is what makes either version food-friendly rather than fruit-forward and soft. Serve the modern style cooler, around 8-10°C, and the traditional oak-aged style a bit warmer, 10-12°C, so its texture and nutty complexity actually show. Don't reach for white Rioja expecting an easy, fruity sipper - it's built for the table, not the patio on its own.
