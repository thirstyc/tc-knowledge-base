What pairs with Australian Cabernet?

Cabernet's firm tannins need fat and protein to soften them, that's the whole game. Tannin binds to protein and fat molecules, which is why a wine that tastes astringent on its own turns smooth and complementary alongside a well-marbled steak or lamb shoulder. Grilled and charred meats work especially well because the Maillard reaction, that browning crust from high heat, produces savoury, slightly bitter compounds that echo the wine's own oak and dried-herb notes. Game meats, with their deeper iron-rich flavour, hold up to Cabernet's weight without being overpowered. Classic Australian barbecue, thick chops, charred sausages, a slow-cooked lamb roast, is genuinely a smart match, not just a cultural cliché. Avoid delicate fish or anything very spicy, where tannin turns metallic and harsh. Serve the wine around 16–18°C, slightly below room temperature, so the fruit stays lifted rather than boozy. If the dish has richness and some char, Cabernet will do its job.
