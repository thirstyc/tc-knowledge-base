Why does Vermouth taste so herbal and aromatic?

Because someone put a garden in it.

Vermouth is aromatised fortified wine: a fairly neutral base wine, strengthened with grape spirit, then infused with botanicals. The one that isn't optional is wormwood, *Artemisia absinthium*, which is where the name comes from by way of the German *Wermut*. It supplies the bitter backbone everything else hangs on.

The rest is the producer's call, and recipes run to fifty ingredients or more: gentian, cinchona, chamomile, coriander, cardamom, citrus peel, rhubarb root, clove, juniper. Most houses treat the list as a trade secret. The botanicals are macerated or distilled separately and then blended in, which is why the aromatics sit on top of the wine rather than dissolving into it.

That separation is also why vermouth works so well in cocktails. The bitterness and the aromatic layer give a drink structure that a plain fortified wine never would — it does something in a Martini or a Negroni that nothing else quite manages.
