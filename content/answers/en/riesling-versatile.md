Why is Riesling so versatile across styles?

Riesling is a shapeshifter because of what happens, and doesn't happen, during fermentation. The grape holds onto acidity even at full ripeness, thanks to a thick skin and a slow sugar curve, so growers can pick early for lean, mineral wines or let it hang for weeks to build intense fruit and even botrytis without the wine turning flabby. Once picked, the winemaker decides how far fermentation runs: stop it early and leave residual sugar for Kabinett or Spätlese, push it fully dry for trocken, or aim for Auslese and Eiswein sweetness with late-harvest or frozen grapes. Alcohol follows the same logic - lower where sugar remains, higher where fermentation runs to completion. Terroir adds another layer: German slate gives steely, petrol-tinged wines, Alsace granite gives richer texture, Austrian loess leans savoury. Same grape, same acid backbone, wildly different results depending on climate, soil, and how much sugar gets left behind. It's not a different grape each time - just a different set of decisions.
