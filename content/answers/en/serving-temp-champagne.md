What temperature to serve Champagne?

Around 8–10°C for most Champagne. Serve simpler non-vintage bottles a little colder, around 6–8°C, and serve vintage and prestige Champagne a little warmer, around 10–12°C, so their complexity can show.

Too cold, and the aromas and flavours are muted; you lose what you paid for. Too warm, and the bubbles become aggressive, the wine foams when poured, and it feels heavy.

How to get there:

- Fridge: about three hours in a standard fridge.
- Ice bucket: 20–30 minutes in a bucket of ice and water, which chills much faster than ice alone. Add a handful of salt to speed it up further.
- Freezer: only in an emergency, and no longer than 15 minutes. Set a timer; a forgotten bottle can freeze and burst.

Serving tips:

- Open it gently, holding the cork and twisting the bottle, so it sighs rather than pops.
- Pour slowly, tilting the glass, to keep the bubbles.
- Use a white wine glass or a tulip-shaped flute rather than a narrow flute or a wide coupe, to capture the aromas.

Between pours, keep the bottle in the ice bucket. It warms quickly on the table.
