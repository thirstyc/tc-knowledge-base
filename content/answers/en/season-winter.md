Best wine for winter?

Fuller, warmer wines that suit heavier food: structured reds, rich whites and fortified wines. Winter cooking is slow and hearty, and the wines can match it.

Reds come into their own. Cabernet Sauvignon, Syrah and Shiraz, Malbec, Rioja Reserva, Barolo, Châteauneuf-du-Pape and Amarone all suit stews, braises and roasts.

Rich whites work too, especially with creamy dishes, roast chicken or fish: oaked Chardonnay, white Burgundy, Viognier, white Rioja and textured Chenin Blanc.

Fortified wines are made for cold evenings. Tawny Port, Oloroso Sherry and Madeira are perfect after dinner, and Port with Stilton is a winter classic.

Winter food pairings to try:

- Beef stew or bourguignon with Burgundy or Rhône reds.
- Roast lamb with Rioja or Bordeaux.
- Cheese fondue with a crisp Savoie or Swiss white.
- Mushroom risotto with Nebbiolo or Pinot Noir.

For holiday gatherings, sparkling wine still belongs on the table, and mulled wine is a good use for inexpensive red.

Serve reds at around 16–18°C, not beside the fire. Even in winter, warm red wine tastes more alcoholic.
