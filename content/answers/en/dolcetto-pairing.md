What pairs with Dolcetto?

Everyone assumes Dolcetto is a high-acid wine because Italian reds usually are, but that's backwards. Dolcetto actually runs low in acidity compared to its Piedmontese cousins Barbera and Nebbiolo, and its tannins are soft, round, often finishing with a distinctive note of bitter almond. That combination is exactly why it works so well as a weeknight wine. Low acid means it won't clash with tomato sauce or fatty cheese the way a screechy Barbera might, and low tannin means it doesn't need a slab of protein to soften it up. This is the grape Piedmont growers plant on their lesser slopes and drink themselves while the Nebbiolo ages away in barrel for a decade. Pour it slightly cool, around 14 to 16 degrees, with pizza, burgers, salumi, or a simple pasta with tomato and basil. It's built for food that doesn't take itself too seriously, which is more or less Dolcetto's whole philosophy.
