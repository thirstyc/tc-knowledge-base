What pairs with Central Otago Pinot Noir?

Central Otago Pinot Noir has real backbone — riper tannin, more concentration and higher alcohol than its cooler Burgundian cousins — so it wants food with some weight and gaminess to match. Venison is the classic pairing for a reason: the wine's dark cherry and earthy spice notes echo the meat's own richness without one overpowering the other. Duck breast works for similar reasons, especially with a fruit-based sauce that plays off the Pinot's berry fruit. Earthy mushroom dishes, whether a risotto or a simple sauté, pick up on the savory, forest-floor undertones you often get from the region's slightly cooler vintages. Avoid anything too delicate — a plain white fish or light salad will just get steamrolled. Serve it a touch cooler than room temperature, around 14 to 16°C, to keep the fruit fresh and stop the alcohol from feeling hot alongside rich, fatty foods.
