Best white wine for beginners?

Start with a fresh, fruity white that's easy to like: Pinot Grigio, Sauvignon Blanc, unoaked Chardonnay or an off-dry Riesling.

Pinot Grigio is light, crisp and gentle, with flavours of lemon, apple and pear. It's hard to dislike and easy to find.

Sauvignon Blanc is zesty and aromatic. New Zealand versions are bursting with passion fruit and citrus; bottles from the Loire Valley are more restrained and mineral.

Unoaked Chardonnay, including Chablis, is clean and fresh, with apple and lemon. It's a good way to learn what Chardonnay tastes like before oak changes it.

Off-dry Riesling, like a German Kabinett, has a touch of sweetness balanced by lively acidity, and it's often low in alcohol. It's a great choice if you think you don't like dry wine.

Albariño is another good option: fresh, a little salty, and perfect with seafood.

If you like sweeter wine, Moscato d'Asti is lightly sparkling, low in alcohol and full of peach and orange blossom.

Once you're comfortable, try oaked Chardonnay, Viognier or Chenin Blanc for richer, fuller styles.

Serve whites chilled but not ice-cold, around 8–10°C. Too cold, and the flavour disappears.
