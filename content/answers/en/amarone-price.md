Why is Amarone so expensive?

The appassimento process behind Amarone is slow and genuinely risky, and that's baked directly into the price. Grapes are dried for three to four months in ventilated rooms, losing something like 30 to 40 percent of their water weight, which means you need a lot more fruit to end up with the same volume of wine than a normal red would require. During that drying period producers have to constantly monitor for mold and rot, since a warm, humid stretch can ruin an entire batch of fruit that's already been through a full growing season. It's labor-intensive in a way most winemaking isn't, someone is checking those racks and crates regularly for months before fermentation even starts. Add to that the higher sugar and alcohol levels, which often mean longer, more careful fermentations, plus extended aging in barrel and bottle before release, and you've got a wine that costs more at every single stage of production, not just in marketing.
