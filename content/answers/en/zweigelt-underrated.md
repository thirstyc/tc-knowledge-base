Why does Zweigelt fly under the radar outside Austria?

Partly logistics, partly branding. Zweigelt is a 1922 crossing of St. Laurent and Blaufränkisch, bred for a cold, tricky climate rather than for prestige, and it became Austria's most planted red almost by default. It ripens reliably, gives good yields, and makes wine that's juicy, dark-cherry-scented, peppery, with soft tannins and the same racy acidity that makes Grüner Veltliner work with food. Serve it lightly chilled, around 13-15C, and it drinks more like a savoury cousin of Beaujolais than a serious cellar red. That's the problem for export: it doesn't fit the story importers want to tell. Austria sold the world on Grüner as its white calling card, and left Blaufränkisch to carry the flag for age-worthy reds, since it has structure and tannin that reward oak and bottle time. Zweigelt sits in between: too light for the cellar crowd, too obscure for the everyday-red crowd who've never heard of it. Most of it gets drunk at home in Austria, young and cool, which is honestly the correct way to drink it anyway.
