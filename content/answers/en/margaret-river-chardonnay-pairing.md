What pairs with Margaret River Chardonnay?

This is a Chardonnay built on structure and acidity rather than oak and butter, so it wants food with some richness to meet it halfway but nothing that will bury its saline, citrus-driven core. Roasted or grilled chicken and turkey work well, especially with a lemon or herb-based sauce that echoes the wine's own citrus notes. Rich fish like salmon, swordfish, or a butter-poached halibut are classic matches, since the wine's acidity cuts through the fat the way a squeeze of lemon would. Soft, washed-rind or triple-cream cheeses are another good call, the wine's freshness balancing the cheese's richness instead of getting flattened by it. Avoid pairing with anything too delicate, like a plain green salad, where the wine's weight will simply overpower the food. Serve at 10 to 12 degrees, and let it sit in the glass a few minutes before drinking; the aromatics open up noticeably as it warms slightly from cellar temperature.
