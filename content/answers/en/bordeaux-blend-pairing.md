What pairs with Bordeaux Blend?

The tannin and acidity that make Bordeaux blends age-worthy are exactly why they need fat and protein on the plate. Tannins bind to proteins and fats, which softens their grip on your palate and makes the wine taste smoother while the food tastes richer - it's a genuine chemical trade-off, not just tradition. That's why lamb, especially with a bit of char from the grill, works so well: the Maillard-browned crust and the fat in the meat both cushion the tannin. Aged beef, duck, and mushroom-based dishes do the same job, adding umami that meshes with the wine's savory, earthy notes as it develops. Avoid delicate fish or very spicy food, where the tannin turns harsh and the alcohol reads as heat. Hard, aged cheeses like a good cheddar or Comté are a classic match for the same fat-and-protein logic. Serve around 16-18°C, decant if it's young and tight, and let the wine breathe for twenty minutes before the first pour - it opens up considerably.
