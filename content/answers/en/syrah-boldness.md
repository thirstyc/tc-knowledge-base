Why is Syrah/Shiraz so bold?

Syrah has thick skins, ripens to high sugar levels in warm places, and carries naturally peppery aromatic compounds. That gives dark colour, plenty of tannin, high alcohol and a distinctive spicy punch.

The name signals the style. Syrah, in the northern Rhône, is grown in a cooler climate and tends to be more restrained: black pepper, olive, smoke and firm structure. Shiraz, in Australia's warmer regions, gets riper, giving blackberry, plum, chocolate and a fuller, softer body.

The pepper note comes from a compound called rotundone, which is more pronounced in cooler growing conditions. It's one of the few wine aromas traced to a single molecule.

Warm sites push alcohol up, often to 14.5% or more, which adds to the sense of weight and richness.

Syrah also takes oak well, and new oak adds vanilla and sweet spice on top of the fruit.

It's a great partner for grilled and smoked meat, peppercorn sauce and hearty stews.

If bold isn't what you want, look for cooler-climate Syrah from the northern Rhône, Victoria, the Adelaide Hills or Chile's coastal regions, which is lighter and more savoury.
