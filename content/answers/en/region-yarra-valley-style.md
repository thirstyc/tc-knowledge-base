Why does Yarra Valley Pinot and Chardonnay taste so cool and precise?

Elevation is doing most of the work here. Vineyards climb into the Yarra Ranges up to around 400 metres, and that height, combined with cooling influence off Port Phillip Bay, keeps daytime temperatures down and creates a real day-to-night swing. That slows ripening, so the grapes build flavor and acid before sugar gets away from them — the opposite of what happens in Australia's hot inland regions like Barossa or McLaren Vale, where heat pushes ripeness fast and alcohol climbs with it. The result is Chardonnay with citrus and stone fruit held up by genuine acidity rather than oak and butter doing the talking, and Pinot Noir that's savory and red-fruited instead of jammy. It's precise wine, built on restraint rather than power, which is exactly why it reads as un-Australian to people who only know the country through big reds. Serve the Chardonnay at 10 to 12°C and the Pinot at 14 to 16°C to keep that precision intact.
