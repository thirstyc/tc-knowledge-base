Why does Sherry taste so oxidative-complex?

Palomino is a fairly neutral grape - the character comes from what happens after fermentation, not the grape itself. Base wine is fortified to around 15% for fino and manzanilla styles, which keeps a layer of flor yeast alive on the surface, protecting the wine from oxygen and giving it that tangy, saline, almost sourdough edge (biological aging). Fortify higher, past 17%, and flor dies off, so the wine ages oxidatively instead - open barrel contact for years, developing walnut, caramel, dried-fig depth. Then there's the solera system: a stack of barrels of different ages, where younger wine is blended into older through partial transfers, so no single vintage really exists - what you drink is a blend spanning decades. That constant fractional blending plus oxygen exposure builds layers of nutty, savory complexity you don't get from a wine bottled straight after one harvest. It's why a plain-looking bottle can taste like it's been thinking about itself for twenty years - because it has.
