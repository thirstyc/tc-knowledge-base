Why does Chasselas taste so light and mineral?

Chasselas is a naturally low-sugar grape, which is the whole story behind its light body, since sugar at harvest is what becomes alcohol in fermentation. It's grown mostly on steep terraces around Lake Geneva in Switzerland's Vaud region, where the lake reflects sunlight back onto the vines and moderates temperature, letting the grapes ripen gently rather than piling on sugar. The soils there are often glacial, limestone or granite depending on the slope, and that mineral, sometimes faintly saline character in the glass comes largely from that terroir rather than any manipulation in the cellar. Winemakers also tend to leave Chasselas alone, minimal intervention, neutral vessels, so nothing masks its naturally subtle, almost delicate aromatic profile. The result is a wine that's more about texture and place than fruit intensity. Serve it well chilled, around 8-10°C, and drink it young since it doesn't age for complexity the way richer whites can. It's built for raclette and fondue, not for sipping alone and overthinking.
