What pairs with Oregonian Pinot Noir?

The wine's acidity and moderate tannin do most of the pairing work, which is why it plays so well outside the usual 'red wine with red meat' box. Grilled salmon is the classic match in the Pacific Northwest itself, since the wine's acid cuts through the fish's oil the same way a squeeze of lemon would, without tannin muscling in and turning the fish metallic. Roasted chicken or turkey works for the same reason, the wine's fruit and earthiness echo the savory pan drippings without overwhelming lean meat. Mushrooms, whether in a risotto or simply sautéed with butter and thyme, latch onto the wine's own earthy, forest-floor notes and make both taste more of themselves. Where it struggles is heavy cream sauces or strongly spiced dishes that can bury its more delicate aromatics, and anything char-heavy or blackened will fight the wine's subtlety rather than complement it. Serve it a touch cooler than room temperature, around 14-16°C, so the fruit stays lifted and the finish doesn't turn flabby against food.
