Why is Sherry underrated?

Sherry got stuck with a reputation as a sweet, dusty thing your grandmother kept behind the good glasses, which is exactly backwards - some of the driest, most technically demanding wines made in Spain are sherries. Aging under flor yeast, called biological aging, is one of the strangest and hardest techniques in wine: a living layer of yeast has to survive on the wine's surface for years, feeding on it and shielding it from oxygen, producing the tangy, saline, almost bread-dough character in fino and manzanilla. Nothing else tastes like it. Layer in the solera system, where wine gets blended across decades rather than bottled by vintage, and you get a consistency and complexity that producers elsewhere spend fortunes trying to fake with barrel tricks. Because sherry is fortified and cheap relative to what's in the glass, it gets dismissed as simple - but a good manzanilla or amontillado delivers more layered, savory nuance per dollar than most bottles of Burgundy. It just doesn't market itself.
