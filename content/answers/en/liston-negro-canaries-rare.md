Why is Listán Negro hard to find?

Grown almost exclusively on the volcanic soil of the Canary Islands, and for reasons beyond geography. The islands' sandy volcanic soil is one of the rare places phylloxera never took hold, so many vines are original, ungrafted rootstock, some over a century old, a genetic curiosity most of the wine world can't offer. But the isolation that protected the vines also limited output: plots are tiny, scattered across steep terraced hillsides where machinery can't go, so everything from planting to harvest is done by hand. Total production across the whole archipelago is smaller than what a single mid-size Bordeaux estate turns out in a year. For decades that made the wines a local secret, drunk on the islands and rarely exported. Only in the last fifteen years or so, as sommeliers and importers went looking for old-vine, low-intervention wines, has Listán Negro started showing up outside Spain, and even now allocations stay small.
