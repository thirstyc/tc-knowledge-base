Why does Friulano taste so herbaceous?

Blame the grape, not the place. Friulano carries the same aromatic family as Sauvignon Vert (confusingly it was once called Tocai Friulano, which made people assume a kinship with Sauvignon Blanc that isn't really there genetically). Its skins are naturally rich in the same class of aromatic precursors that give herbal, grassy, almost sage-and-thyme character, and that comes through regardless of where it's planted. Terroir amplifies it, though: in Collio and Colli Orientali del Friuli, cool air sliding down from the Alps collides with warm currents off the Adriatic, creating big day-to-night temperature swings. That keeps acidity high and slows ripening just enough that the herbal, slightly bitter almond note on the finish stays intact instead of cooking off into tropical fruit. So you get a wine that smells like a walk through a herb garden after rain, with a clean, savory, almost saline finish. It's not a stylistic choice by the winemaker so much as the grape doing what it does, framed by a very specific climate.
