Why does South African Sauvignon Blanc fly under the radar?

New Zealand's Marlborough got there first with a loud, unmistakable style, and marketing muscle turned that into the grape's global shorthand. South Africa's Sauvignon Blanc regions, Elim, Durbanville, parts of Constantia and the higher-altitude Cape sites, are genuinely cooler than most people assume, cooled by Atlantic and Indian Ocean influence rather than latitude alone. That cold air slows ripening, preserves the thiols and pyrazines responsible for the grape's citrus, gooseberry, and green-pepper character, and keeps acidity sharp. The result is wine every bit as intense and precise as Marlborough's, often with a stonier, more savoury edge instead of the tropical fruit bomb reputation New Zealand built its name on. South Africa simply never had the same export narrative or price point to make noise internationally, so the wines got treated as a value alternative rather than a serious rival. Drink it young, within two or three years, while that freshness is still the whole point.
