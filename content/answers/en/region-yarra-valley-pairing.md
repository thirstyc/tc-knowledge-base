What pairs with Yarra Valley Chardonnay or Pinot?

Think about what each wine is actually built to cut through. Yarra Chardonnay has real acid backbone under its citrus and stone fruit, plus judicious oak, so it can handle roast chicken or grilled salmon without getting flattened by the fat — the acid does the work a squeeze of lemon would do. Yarra Pinot Noir is light-bodied with soft tannin and red fruit, which makes it forgiving with delicate proteins and umami-heavy dishes like duck, mushroom risotto, or even salmon again if you want a red instead. The common mistake is treating either wine like a heavier regional cousin and pairing it with something rich and creamy that buries it; these wines are precise, not powerful, so the food should be too. Soft, young cheeses work for the same reason — nothing overwhelms the acid or tannin. Serve the Chardonnay around 10 to 12°C and the Pinot closer to 14 to 16°C; too cold and you lose the fruit, too warm and the alcohol takes over.
