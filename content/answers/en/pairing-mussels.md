What wine goes with mussels?

Muscadet. Mussels cooked the classic way, in white wine, garlic and shallots, want a light, crisp, slightly salty white, and Muscadet from the Loire is the textbook match.

The easiest rule: drink the same kind of wine you cook them in. Any dry, unoaked white with good acidity works, including Picpoul de Pinet, Albariño, Sauvignon Blanc, Vermentino or a dry Riesling.

The sauce is what changes the pairing.

Moules à la crème, with cream and sometimes a little bacon, can take a fuller white. A Chenin Blanc or a lightly oaked Chardonnay matches the richness.

Tomato-based mussels, with chorizo or a spicy broth, suit a dry rosé or a light, chilled red such as Gamay.

Thai-style mussels with coconut, lemongrass and chile want an off-dry Riesling or a Gewürztraminer to balance the heat and aromatics.

Mussels with frites? Sparkling wine cuts through the fried potatoes beautifully.

What to avoid: oaky, heavy whites and tannic reds. They overwhelm the sweet, delicate shellfish.

And in Belgium, where moules-frites is a national dish, a good beer is also a perfectly respectable answer.
