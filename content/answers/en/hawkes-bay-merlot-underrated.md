Why does Hawke's Bay Merlot fly under the radar?

New Zealand built its wine reputation on two loud stories -- Marlborough's zippy Sauvignon Blanc and Central Otago's delicate Pinot Noir -- and decades of export marketing have reinforced both. Hawke's Bay doesn't fit either narrative: it's warmer, older as a wine region, and makes Bordeaux-style reds built around Merlot, Cabernet Franc and Malbec, particularly from the Gimblett Gravels. That's a harder story to sell alongside "New Zealand equals crisp white wine," so it gets left out of the conversation even though the region has been quietly producing structured, age-worthy reds for decades. The wines aren't cheap imitations of Bordeaux either -- they have their own register, riper and more generous, with New Zealand's naturally clean fruit character underneath the structure. If you only know the country through supermarket Sauvignon Blanc, Hawke's Bay Merlot is worth seeking out precisely because it won't taste like what you expect.
