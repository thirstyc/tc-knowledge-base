Can you age Verdicchio?

Yes, and it genuinely surprises people who assume all Italian whites are meant to be drunk within a year. Verdicchio has real structure: decent acidity and a phenolic grip that most simple whites lack, which gives it something to hold onto as it ages. Young, it's citrusy and almondy with a slight bitter almond finish. Give it five years or more and it shifts into something richer, with notes of honey, hazelnut and beeswax, while still keeping that backbone of acidity that stops it from going flabby. The best examples, from Castelli di Jesi in the Marche, are made specifically with this aging potential in mind, sometimes seeing extended lees contact to build texture. Most people drink Verdicchio the week they buy it, so a properly aged bottle tastes like a different, more serious wine. If you find an older vintage on a wine list, it's worth the gamble. Serve slightly warmer than a young white, around 10-12°C, to let those developed aromas open up.
