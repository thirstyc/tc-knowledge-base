What pairs with Nova Scotia sparkling or Tidal Bay white?

Nova Scotia's Atlantic climate is barely warm enough to ripen grapes, so the wines come in lean, high in acid, and low in alcohol, which is precisely the profile that flatters raw and lightly cooked seafood instead of fighting it. A traditional-method sparkling cuts through the brininess of an oyster and resets your palate for the next one; the acid also mirrors the citrus you'd squeeze on top anyway. Tidal Bay, the province's signature white blend, is built to be crisp and aromatic without heaviness, so it slides in next to scallops or lobster without adding buttery weight the way an oaked Chardonnay would. Avoid anything richer or more tannic here, red wine tannin clashes badly with fish oils and can taste metallic. Serve the sparkling around 6-8°C and Tidal Bay closer to 8-10°C. This isn't a stretch pairing dressed up as terroir talk, it's simply cool-climate acid doing the job it's built for at a seafood table.
