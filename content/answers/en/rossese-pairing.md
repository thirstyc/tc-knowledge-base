What pairs with Rossese?

Rossese di Dolceacqua is light-bodied and low in tannin, which means it never fights with delicate proteins the way a heavier red would. That's why it works so well with grilled fish and other seafood, something most Ligurian reds can't pull off. Its acidity is bright enough to cut through olive oil, which matters because Ligurian cooking leans hard on it, and its savory, herbal undertones (think wild rosemary and thyme growing on those same coastal hillsides) echo the herbs used in the region's grilling and pesto. It's also gentle enough for light white meats like rabbit or chicken, especially when herb-roasted. Skip anything heavy, fatty, or richly sauced; the wine has neither the tannin nor the weight to stand up to it. Serve it slightly cool, around 14 to 16°C, closer to a red-wine version of how you'd treat a rosé. Think of it as Liguria in a glass: coastal, herbal, and built for food from the same kitchen it grew next to.
