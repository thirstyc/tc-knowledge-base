Why is Chianti Classico more expensive than basic Chianti?

Smaller zone, tighter rules, more time.

Classico is the historic heart of the region, about 7,000 hectares of hill country between Florence and Siena, and only that. Basic Chianti sprawls across a far larger area taking in flatter, warmer, higher-yielding land. Less ground and lower permitted yields means less wine per hectare, and that shows up in the price before anything else does.

Then the rules. Minimum 80% Sangiovese against 70% for basic Chianti, no white grapes allowed in the blend at all, and a minimum twelve months of ageing — twenty-four for Riserva, thirty for Gran Selezione. Every extra month is a month the producer is holding stock instead of being paid for it.

The black rooster on the neck is the consortium's seal, and it means those rules were followed rather than merely claimed.

Whether the difference is worth it depends on the bottle. A good Classico is a serious wine. A mediocre one is an expensive everyday red with a rooster on it.
