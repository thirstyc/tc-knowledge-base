Why is Teroldego hard to find outside Trentino?

Nearly all Teroldego grows in one place: Campo Rotaliano, a flat alluvial plain in Trentino built up by the Noce and Adige rivers depositing gravel and sand over centuries. That soil is exactly what the vine wants, well-drained ground that stresses the roots just enough to concentrate flavor without waterlogging them. The plain is small and boxed in by mountains, so there's a hard ceiling on how much land can ever be planted. Unlike grapes that get shipped around the world for producers to experiment with, Teroldego never really migrated beyond its native zone, partly tradition, partly because it doesn't show the same character on other soils. A few scattered plantings exist elsewhere, but they're not what defines it, and few producers bother scaling up. The scarcity is genuine geography, not manufactured hype. If you track down a bottle, it's a real curiosity rather than a mass-market wine playing rare, and worth serving around 16°C to see its full range.
