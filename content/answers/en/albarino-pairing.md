What pairs with Albariño?

Albariño's whole personality is built around cutting through fat and echoing brine, which is why raw seafood is the obvious match rather than just a coastal cliché. Its high acidity slices through the richness of oysters and scallops the way lemon would, while its faint salinity mirrors the ocean flavor already in the food instead of fighting it. With ceviche, that same acid lines up with the lime or citrus used to cure the fish, so the wine reinforces the dish rather than competing with it. It also has enough weight and texture, thanks to lees contact many producers use, to handle richer preparations like grilled octopus, garlicky shrimp, or a simple fish stew without getting flattened. Avoid pairing it with heavy red meat or very sweet dishes, the acidity will taste thin against that kind of weight. Serve it cold, 8-10°C, and drink it within a couple years of the vintage since it's not a wine built for aging.
