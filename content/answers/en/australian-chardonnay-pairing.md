What pairs with Australian Chardonnay?

Chardonnay's weight and, in oaked styles, its buttery texture come from malolactic fermentation converting sharp malic acid into softer lactic acid, plus time in barrel adding richness and a creamy mouthfeel. That's why it pairs so naturally with rich, fatty fish like salmon or richer white fish in cream or butter sauces, the wine's texture mirrors the dish rather than fighting it, while its acidity cuts through the fat so neither feels heavy. Roasted chicken or turkey with pan juices works the same way, the wine's body matches the dish's richness without disappearing. Leaner, unoaked Chardonnay styles, more common from cooler sites, behave differently, they're brighter and better with simpler preparations, oysters or plain grilled fish, where oak would clash. Serve full-bodied oaked versions around 10–12°C, slightly warmer than a light white, so the texture and toasty notes actually show. Avoid pairing heavily oaked Chardonnay with delicate raw fish, the wine will overwhelm it.
