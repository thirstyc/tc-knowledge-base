What exactly is Insolia and why does it taste the way it does?

Insolia (also spelled Inzolia) is a white grape native to Sicily, mostly grown on the western side of the island around Marsala and Trapani. It's actually one of the grapes historically used to make Marsala wine, though most Insolia today is bottled as a dry, unfortified white. The heat in western Sicily is brutal, but the grape has adapted by ripening early and holding onto decent acidity even when sugar levels climb, which keeps the wine from tasting flabby or jammy. That's the real trick with Insolia: it's grown somewhere hot enough to bake most grapes into raisins, yet it comes out fresh. Expect a fairly light body, notes of green almond, citrus peel and sometimes a faint herbal or saline edge from the coastal vineyards. It's rarely oaked, so what you taste is mostly grape and place, not winemaking. Don't expect complexity or long finish here, it's a straightforward, honest white, not a wine built for contemplation.
