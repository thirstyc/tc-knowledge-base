Why does Feteasca Alba taste so white-fruit and crisp?

Fetească Albă comes out of Romania and Moldova, regions with a continental climate: warm days for ripening, but nights that drop enough to preserve acidity in the grape right up to harvest, since acid degrades fastest in heat and slows down once temperatures fall. The variety itself is also just genetically stubborn about holding onto acid even as sugar levels climb, which is part of why it doesn't turn flabby the way some warm-climate whites do once alcohol creeps up. What you get is a wine built around green apple, pear and white flower notes, with a brightness that feels almost citrusy on the finish even though there's rarely any actual citrus in the flavour profile, that's the acid talking, not the fruit itself. Serve it cold, around 8 to 10°C, cold enough to keep that acidity feeling crisp rather than sharp and tinny. Warm it up much past that and the wine loses its main selling point fast, turning soft and a little dull.
