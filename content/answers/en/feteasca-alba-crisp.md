Why does Feteasca Alba taste so white-fruit and crisp?

Fetească Albă comes out of Romania and Moldova, regions with a continental climate: warm days for ripening, but nights that drop enough to preserve acidity in the grape right up to harvest. The variety itself is also just genetically stubborn about holding onto acid, even when sugar levels climb, which is part of why it doesn't turn flabby the way some warm-climate whites do. What you get is a wine built around green apple, pear and white flower notes, with a brightness that feels almost citrusy on the finish even though there's rarely any actual citrus in the flavour profile, that's the acid talking, not the fruit. Serve it cold, around 8-10°C, cold enough to keep that acidity feeling crisp rather than sharp. Warm it up much past that and the wine loses its main selling point fast.
