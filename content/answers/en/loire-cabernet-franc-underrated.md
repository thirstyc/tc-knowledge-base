Why does Loire Cabernet Franc fly under the radar?

Sancerre and Champagne pull most of the region's attention north and east, one for its Sauvignon Blanc, the other for obvious reasons, and that leaves Chinon and Bourgueil sitting quietly in the middle without much of a marketing engine behind them. There's no grand cru system here to catch a collector's eye, no château culture like Bordeaux, and Cabernet Franc itself gets treated everywhere else as a blending grape rather than a star, so people don't expect it to shine on its own. But these appellations have been making serious, ageworthy red wine from limestone and tuffeau soils for centuries, at prices that haven't caught up to the quality. A good Chinon can rival a mid-tier Right Bank Bordeaux for a fraction of the cost, since it's essentially the same grape and similar soil without the brand premium. That gap is exactly why sommeliers and value-focused drinkers have quietly kept these wines on their lists for years, even as the wider market looks elsewhere.
