What pairs with Mavrud?

Mavrud is built around big, grippy tannin and dark, brambly fruit, so it needs food with enough fat and protein to soften that structure. Think grilled lamb, a slow-cooked beef stew, or kavarma, the Bulgarian meat stew simmered with peppers and onions - the kind of dish where the sauce has some weight to it. The tannin binds to protein and fat on your tongue, which is why a lean salad or delicate fish would just taste harsh next to it. Aged sheep's cheese works too, and so does anything with smoke or char, since the wine's dark fruit and slight rusticity echo grilled flavors rather than clashing with them. Serve it around 16-18°C, properly at red wine temperature, not cellar-cool, or the tannin will feel even more austere than it already is. Avoid pairing it with anything too delicate or spicy - the heat will fight the tannin and make the wine taste more bitter than it should. This is a wine that wants a plate, not a snack.
