What pairs with Côtes du Rhône?

This wine grows in garrigue country, the scrubby, herb-covered hillsides of the southern Rhône, and that wild thyme, rosemary and lavender scent actually makes it into the glass. It's why the wine feels so at home with Mediterranean cooking, herbes de Provence on grilled meat or vegetables isn't a coincidence, it's an echo of the vineyard. The moderate tannin from Syrah and Mourvèdre in the blend gives it enough grip to handle charred, smoky flavors off the barbecue without disappearing, while Grenache's soft acidity keeps things from feeling heavy even with rich, oily dishes like ratatouille or lamb. It's not a wine that demands a tasting menu, it's built for the kind of eating that happens outside with your hands a little messy. Serve it around 15-16°C rather than room temperature, a slight chill keeps the fruit lively and stops the alcohol from feeling hot. Casual is the right word for it, but casual doesn't mean simple, there's real character behind the ease.
