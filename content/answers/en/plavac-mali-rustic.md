Why does Plavac Mali taste so dark and rustic?

Blame the sun and the rock. Plavac Mali is Zinfandel's Croatian offspring, grown on brutally steep terraces along the Dalmatian coast where vines cling to limestone and get blasted with reflected heat off the Adriatic. That heat pushes sugar levels way up, which means high alcohol, and the vine responds to all that stress by thickening its skins for protection. Thick skins mean more tannin and more color pigment extracted during fermentation, so you end up with a wine that's dense, dark, and grippy almost by necessity. Yields are naturally tiny on those terraces too, concentrating everything further. The best sites, like Dingač and Postup, are so steep they're basically farmed by hand, which adds to the intensity but also the price. This isn't a wine to serve cold and think of as casual red; give it air, serve around 16-18°C, and pair it with something fatty enough to soften the tannin, like grilled lamb.
