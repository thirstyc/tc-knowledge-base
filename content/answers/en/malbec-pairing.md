What pairs with Malbec?

Malbec's reputation as a steak wine isn't marketing - it comes down to tannin and altitude. Grown at elevation in Mendoza, the grape gets intense sunlight but cool nights, which builds thick skins and plenty of tannin, while the day-night swing keeps the fruit from turning jammy. The result is ripe dark fruit - plum, blackberry - sitting on a tannic frame that's firm but not harsh. Tannin binds to protein and fat, softening on the palate and tasting smoother the richer the food is, which is exactly what a grilled ribeye or a rack of short ribs provides. Char from the grill also brings smoky, roasted compounds that echo the oak aging many Malbecs get, so the flavours don't just complement the meat, they mirror it. It's less effective with lean or delicate proteins, where the tannin has nothing to grip and just reads as harsh. Serve it around 16-18°C, slightly below room temperature, so the fruit stays fresh instead of tasting stewed.
