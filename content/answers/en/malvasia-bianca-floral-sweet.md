Why does Malvasia Bianca taste so floral-sweet?

Malvasia isn't one grape but a sprawling family spread across Italy, Spain, Croatia and beyond, and Malvasia Bianca is one branch of it. What makes it smell so perfumed is a heavy load of terpenes, the same aromatic compounds behind Muscat and Gewürztraminer, giving orange blossom, jasmine and ripe apricot before you even taste it. Winemakers often pick it a little late or let it hang for partial raisining to build sugar, and many bottlings stop fermentation early or leave deliberate residual sugar, which is why it lands off-dry to genuinely sweet rather than crisp. Serve it cool, around 8-10°C — cold enough to keep the sweetness in check, not so cold that you mute the aromatics, which is the easiest way to ruin a floral white.
