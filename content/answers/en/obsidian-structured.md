Why does Obsidian taste so dark and structured?

Öküzgözü is grown mainly around Elazığ in eastern Anatolia, at altitude near Lake Hazar, where volcanic and basaltic soils and big swings between hot days and genuinely cold nights do a lot of the work. Those diurnal swings let the grape build sugar, colour and tannin in the skins during the day while the cool nights preserve acidity that would otherwise cook off, so you get a wine that's dark and structured without being flabby. On its own the grape can actually lean lighter and more elegant, closer to something like Pinot Noir in body, which is why it's frequently blended with Boğazkere, a much more tannic native variety whose name literally translates to something like throat-scorcher, added specifically to give backbone and grip. Extended maceration and time in oak add further structure and darker, roasted notes on top. So the tannin and depth you're tasting is really volcanic terroir plus a deliberate blending and winemaking choice, not just one grape acting alone.
