Why does Barossa Shiraz taste so big and jammy?

Barossa sits in a warm, dry inland basin with long hours of sunshine and little rainfall during the growing season, so the vines are rarely stressed by cold or excess water. That heat pushes sugar levels up fast, which is why alcohol often lands well above 14%, and it also thickens grape skins, concentrating the colour and tannin compounds that give the wine its dense, almost chewy texture. Add in old, low-yielding vines, and you get small berries packed with flavour rather than diluted juice. Traditionally much of this Shiraz aged in American oak, which layers in coconut, vanilla and dill notes on top of the blackberry and plum fruit, reinforcing that jammy, generous character. Serve it around 16-18°C, slightly cooler than room temperature, so the alcohol doesn't dominate the nose. It's built for grilled or braised red meat with real fat and char, not delicate food. This is Australia leaning fully into ripeness rather than apologizing for it.
