Why is Chenin Blanc so underrated?

Sauvignon Blanc built its reputation on a single, instantly recognizable flavour — grassy, zesty, unmistakable in a blind lineup. Chardonnay did it through oak and buttery malolactic fermentation becoming an expected house style. Chenin never settled on one identity, so it never got the same marketing hook. Vouvray can be bone-dry or syrupy sweet from one vintage to the next. Savennières makes some of the most austere, long-lived dry whites in France, built to age a decade or more, while South Africa — now the world's largest Chenin producer — turns old bush vines from Swartland and Stellenbosch into everything from lean, mineral whites to rich, barrel-aged versions that rival white Burgundy in weight. That range is a genuine strength on the table, since there's a Chenin for nearly any dish or mood, but it's a weakness on the shelf, where shoppers gravitate toward grapes with one predictable flavour. The wines suffering most from being underrated are also, not coincidentally, some of the best value in white wine right now.
