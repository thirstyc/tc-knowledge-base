What pairs with Australian Pinot Grigio?

Its high acidity and modest intensity make it a palate cleanser rather than a wine that wants to dominate the plate. With light seafood - grilled prawns, oysters, a simple white fish - the acid cuts through any richness and doesn't compete with delicate flavours the way a heavier, oakier white would. Fresh salads work for the same reason: the wine's citrus and green apple notes echo a squeeze of lemon dressing instead of clashing with it. Soft, fresh cheeses like young goat's cheese or fresh mozzarella suit it too, since the wine's brightness balances the cheese's creaminess without either side overwhelming the other. What to avoid is anything with real weight - rich cream sauces, red meat, heavily spiced dishes - because there simply isn't enough concentration in the wine to stand up to them; it'll just disappear. Think of Pinot Grigio as a supporting player at the table, not the main event, and serve it well chilled, around 8-10°C, to keep that acid crisp.
