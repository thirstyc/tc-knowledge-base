Why is Savagnin hard to find outside Jura?

Making Savagnin the Jura way is slow and unforgiving. Vin Jaune sits under voile in barrel for a minimum of six years and three days, unfilled and untopped, losing volume to evaporation the whole time, the local version of sherry's angel's share. That's years of barrel space and capital tied up in a wine that might not turn out right, which is a hard sell anywhere land and time aren't cheap. The resulting style, nutty, curry-like, deliberately oxidative, is also polarizing in a way Chardonnay or Riesling never has to be; it either clicks for you or it reads as a flaw. Jura plantings are small to begin with, so there's little surplus to export and experiment with elsewhere. Add a climate and limestone-clay soil that suits the grape's tendency to concentrate under oxidation, and you get a wine that's genuinely tied to one place, known intimately in France and almost nowhere else.
