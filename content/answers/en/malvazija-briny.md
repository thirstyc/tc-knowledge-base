Why does Malvazija taste so briny and mineral?

Malvazija Istarska grows along Croatia's Istrian peninsula, on limestone and flysch soils sitting right up against the Adriatic, and that combination does a lot of the work. Limestone drains fast but holds enough moisture to keep vines from stressing too hard, which tends to preserve acidity in the grapes rather than letting sugars run away in the heat. The sea proximity moderates daytime temperatures and brings in cool nights and salt-laden air, both of which slow ripening just enough to keep that acid backbone intact at harvest. The result is a wine with real cut and a savory, almost saline edge that reads as briny even though there's no actual salt in it, more a function of soil minerality and high natural acidity than anything mystical about ocean spray. That acidity is also why the wine feels so refreshing and food-friendly rather than austere. Serve it properly cold, around 8 to 10°C, since warmer temperatures blunt the very freshness that makes it interesting in the first place.
