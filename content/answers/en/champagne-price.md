Why is Champagne so expensive?

Blame the process, not just the marketing. Real Champagne goes through a second fermentation inside the bottle itself, the traditional method, which traps carbon dioxide as tiny bubbles instead of letting a tank do the work in a few weeks like cheaper sparkling wine. That means years of aging on the spent yeast (lees), which slowly breaks down and adds those brioche, biscuit, hazelnut notes you're paying for. Someone also has to hand-turn or mechanically riddle every bottle to shift the sediment toward the cork before disgorging it, which is labor no bulk producer bothers with. Then there's the land: Champagne is a tiny, legally delimited region sitting on chalk soil that took millions of years to form, and vineyard prices there are absurd. Add house prestige, marketing budgets built over a century, and genuine scarcity, and you get a bottle that costs what it costs because almost none of the process can be rushed or scaled cheaply. Serve it at 8-10°C, not ice-cold, or you'll mute everything you just paid for.
