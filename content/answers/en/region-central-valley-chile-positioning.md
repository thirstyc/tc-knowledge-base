Why is Chilean wine such good value?

Chile got lucky with geography and history at the same time. Land is cheap relative to Napa or Burgundy, labor costs are low, and because the country never suffered phylloxera, most vines grow ungrafted on their own roots, which cuts vineyard costs further and simplifies farming. Big flat valleys allow mechanization instead of hand labor. None of that alone makes good wine, but it means producers aren't clawing back huge sunk costs through pricing. What changed quality is investment: money poured into cooler coastal and mountain sites (Casablanca, San Antonio, Elqui) starting in the 1990s, better clonal selection, and less reliance on high-yield bulk farming. So you get vineyards planted with real intent, farmed efficiently, in a country where nobody's paying a reputation tax. A $15 bottle from Chile is often doing what a $30 bottle elsewhere does, simply because the underlying cost structure is lower, not because the wine is dumbed down.
