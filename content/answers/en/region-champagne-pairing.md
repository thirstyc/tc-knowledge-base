What pairs with Champagne?

Champagne's high acidity and fine, persistent bubbles aren't just a texture gimmick — they actively scrub fat and salt off your palate the way sparkling water clears a heavy meal, but with more finesse. That's why fried chicken is a genuinely great match rather than a novelty pairing: the crunch and grease meet a wine that resets your mouth after every bite instead of piling on more richness. Oysters work for a related reason — the wine's acidity and faint brininess play off the oyster's own salinity without overwhelming it. Salty snacks, chips, fries, cured meats, all fall into the same category: salt makes acid taste less sharp and more refreshing, so the wine actually feels friendlier alongside them than on its own. Serve it properly cold, around 6-8°C, since warmer temperatures make the acid taste harsher and dull the bubbles. Skip pairing it with anything very sweet, since Brut Champagne's dryness will taste austere next to sugar.
