What pairs with New Zealand Pinot Noir?

New Zealand Pinot Noir, especially from Central Otago and Martinborough, tends to have moderate tannin, bright acidity and cool-climate notes of red cherry, forest floor and sometimes mushroom or earth, particularly as it ages. That structure is exactly why it works with foods that would be crushed by a bigger red. Grilled salmon or ocean trout have enough fat and char to stand up to the tannin without the wine turning metallic, which happens when heavy tannin meets delicate white-fleshed fish. Roasted poultry, especially duck or a well-seasoned chicken, mirrors the wine's savoury, earthy side without fighting its acidity. Mushroom dishes are almost too easy a match, since the wine's own earthy notes echo the ingredient directly. What doesn't work is anything too rich or heavily sauced, cream-based dishes or fatty red meat will bury the wine's delicacy rather than complement it. Serve at 14 to 16°C, cooler than a heavy red, so the fruit and acidity stay lively instead of turning flat and cooked-tasting.
