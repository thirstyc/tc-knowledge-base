Why is Fiano one of Italy's most underrated whites?

Campania's fame runs through Taurasi and Aglianico, and Prosecco pulls attention toward the north, so Fiano di Avellino gets treated as a footnote even though it's arguably the more distinctive wine. What makes it worth seeking out is aging capacity that's genuinely rare among Italian whites: the combination of firm acidity and phenolic texture from its thick skins lets it develop for five, ten, sometimes more years, gaining deeper honey, nut, and dried-fruit complexity rather than just fading. Most Italian whites are built to be drunk within a year or two; Fiano is one of the few built to reward patience, closer in trajectory to a good Chablis than to a typical Mediterranean white. It rarely gets the marketing budget or the name recognition of Soave or Gavi, which keeps prices reasonable for what's in the bottle. Buy it young if you want freshness, or cellar it if you want to see what it actually does.
