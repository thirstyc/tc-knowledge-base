What pairs with Pisco?

Pisco's natural home is the Pisco Sour, and there's a reason that cocktail became the national drink rather than some other combination: the sharp, high-alcohol spirit needs the lime juice, simple syrup and frothy egg white to balance it into something drinkable rather than just hot and grapey. On its own, pisco works best served chilled, somewhere around 10-12°C, sipped slowly as a digestif rather than gulped. In Peru it's also poured alongside ceviche, and that pairing makes sense structurally, the citrus acidity in both the dish and the spirit's floral intensity play off each other instead of clashing. Avoid serving it warm or neat at room temperature, the alcohol dominates and the aromatics turn hot and blowsy instead of perfumed. If you're drinking Chilean pisco, which is often softer and slightly oak-aged, it holds up fine in a simple mixed drink or over ice. The through-line either way is that pisco is built to be cut, chilled or mixed, not treated like a delicate sipping spirit.
