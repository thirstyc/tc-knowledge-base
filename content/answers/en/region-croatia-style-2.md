Why does Croatian Plavac Mali taste so intense?

The intensity comes from geography doing exactly what it does on few other coasts in Europe. Plavac Mali grows on steep, terraced slopes on the Dalmatian coast and islands, where the sun beats down directly and then bounces back up off the Adriatic below, effectively giving the grapes two suns' worth of exposure. Add limestone soils that reflect heat and drain fast, forcing vines to struggle and yields to stay low, and you get thick-skinned, sugar-loaded grapes by harvest. That sugar becomes alcohol, often reaching 15% or higher, while those thick skins bring heavy tannin and deep color along for the ride. It's a direct relative of Zinfandel, sharing the parent grape Crljenak Kaštelanski, but it never left its brutal home terroir the way its American cousin did. The result is a wine that tastes like the place made it, not the winemaker.
