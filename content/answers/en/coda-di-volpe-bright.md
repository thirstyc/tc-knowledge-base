Why does Coda di Volpe taste so bright and crisp?

Coda di Volpe comes from Campania, mostly grown on the volcanic soils around Vesuvius and in the hills of Irpinia, and both the soil and the altitude are doing real work here. Volcanic soils drain fast and retain little water, which stresses the vine just enough to concentrate acids in the grape rather than diluting them. Add cool nighttime temperatures at elevation, and the vine holds onto malic acid instead of burning through it during ripening, which is exactly what gives the wine its citrus snap and that clean, almost saline finish. The name means fox's tail, a nod to the curled shape of the grape cluster, not a flavor descriptor, so don't go looking for anything foxy in the glass. Serve it cool, around 8 to 10°C, and drink it young. This isn't a wine built for structure or age, it's built for freshness, and that freshness fades fast once the bottle's been open a day or the vintage gets a few years old.
