What is a wine auction?

A wine auction is a sale where bottles, often from private collections, are sold to the highest bidder, either in a saleroom or, increasingly, online.

Auctions are where much of the world's rare, older and collectible wine changes hands. They can be a good source of mature bottles that are no longer available from merchants.

How it works: the auction house publishes a catalogue with estimates, descriptions and condition notes for each lot. You bid in person, online or by leaving a maximum bid in advance. The winning price is the "hammer price".

What you actually pay is more than the hammer price. Auction houses add a buyer's premium, a percentage on top, plus taxes, delivery and sometimes storage fees. Check these before bidding, since they change the real cost significantly.

Things to check on each lot:

- Provenance: where the wine has been stored, ideally professionally.
- Condition: fill level, label and capsule condition, and any signs of seepage.
- Lot size: some lots are single bottles, others full cases.

Set a maximum price in advance, including fees, and stick to it. Bidding wars are how people overpay.
