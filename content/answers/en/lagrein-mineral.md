Why does Lagrein taste so full-bodied and mineral?

Lagrein comes from Alto Adige, in Italy's far north, and most of the best examples actually grow on the valley floor around Bolzano, not high up the mountainside - the alpine part comes from what surrounds the vineyards, not necessarily their elevation. The valley's gravelly, alluvial soils drain fast and don't hold much organic richness, which stresses the vines just enough to concentrate flavor and give that mineral, almost stony edge. The grape itself has thick skins loaded with anthocyanins and tannin, which is where the deep color and full body come from. What really shapes the wine is the diurnal swing: hot days trapped in the valley basin ripen the fruit and soften tannins, while cold air draining down from the surrounding peaks at night preserves acidity. That push-pull is what gives Lagrein its structure - full-bodied and dark-fruited, but not flabby. Serve it around 16-18°C, slightly cooler than a big Bordeaux, to keep that acidity honest.
