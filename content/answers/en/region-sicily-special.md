What are Sicilian wines?

Sicily has gone from bulk wine to one of Italy's most exciting regions in a generation, and its range runs from volcanic whites to juicy Mediterranean reds.

Mount Etna is the headline. Vineyards sit high on the volcano's slopes, on black volcanic soil, with big differences between day and night temperatures. The reds, from Nerello Mascalese and Nerello Cappuccio, are pale, perfumed and mineral, closer to Burgundy or Barolo than to a warm southern red. The whites, from Carricante, are taut and saline.

Elsewhere, Nero d'Avola makes plush, dark reds, and Frappato makes something lighter and floral. Blend the two and you get Cerasuolo di Vittoria, the island's only DOCG.

Whites come from Grillo, Catarratto and Inzolia, and they're fresher than the island's hot reputation suggests, thanks to altitude and sea breezes.

Sicily also has a sweet and fortified tradition: Marsala, and the raisiny Passito di Pantelleria made from Zibibbo.

The island has a lot of organic vineyard, helped by its dry, windy climate.

Where to start: an Etna Rosso if you like elegant reds, a Grillo for seafood.
