How long does wine last in a wine fridge?

Unopened wine stored properly in a wine fridge lasts as long as the wine itself is built to last, from a year or two for everyday bottles to decades for age-worthy wines. The fridge preserves it; it doesn't change how long the wine can age.

A wine fridge provides the conditions wine needs: steady temperature, darkness, low vibration, and often some humidity. Set a single-zone fridge to around 12–14°C for long-term storage.

How long different wines last unopened:

- Everyday whites, rosés and light reds: one to three years.
- Most everyday reds: two to five years.
- Quality whites like white Burgundy and Riesling: five to twenty years.
- Age-worthy reds like Bordeaux, Barolo and northern Rhône: ten to thirty years or more.

Opened wine is different. Recorked, it lasts about three to five days for most still wines, and one to three days for sparkling wine with a stopper. Keep opened bottles in a regular fridge, which is colder and slows oxidation more.

A wine fridge is not the same as a kitchen fridge. Kitchen fridges are too cold and dry for long-term storage, and their vibration and door opening disturb the wine.
