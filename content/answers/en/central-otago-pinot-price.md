Why is Central Otago Pinot Noir expensive?

Central Otago is a small, remote, high-altitude wine region on New Zealand's South Island, and everything about growing Pinot Noir there is expensive. The climate is marginal and continental, with real frost risk in spring and a narrow ripening window, so vineyards need frost protection, careful site selection on steep schist slopes, and a lot of hands-on labor that machines can't replace. Yields are naturally low because the vines are stressed by both the climate and the soils, which is great for concentration but bad for volume. Land is limited, mostly small family holdings rather than big estates, and New Zealand's overall wine production is tiny compared to France, Italy or Spain, so there's no scale to spread costs across. Combine genuine scarcity with strong international demand from collectors who've decided this is the country's flagship red, and prices climb accordingly. You're not paying for a brand story here; you're paying for a genuinely difficult, low-yield site doing what it does well, in small enough quantities that everyone wants a piece of it.
