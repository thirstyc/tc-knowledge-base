Why does Bobal have such high tannin?

Bobal grows mostly around Requena and Utiel-Requena in Valencia, a high plateau, often 600 to 800 metres up, with a continental climate: hot days, cold nights. Skins are the wine's tannin factory, and Bobal builds thick ones as protection against the intense daytime sun. Thicker skin means more phenolic compounds packed in there, and when the grapes are fermented with skin contact, all that material extracts into the wine, giving you real grip and structure. The cool nights matter too: they slow ripening and keep acidity intact even as sugars build up during hot afternoons, so you get a wine that's tannic and structured but not flabby. That combination, big tannin plus preserved acid, is why old-vine Bobal can age respectably despite being an unfashionable, underpriced grape for most of its history. It's also why young, cheaply made versions can taste tough: the tannin needs either time in bottle or a gentler winemaking hand to soften into something drinkable rather than just chewy.
