Why does Montepulciano d'Abruzzo taste so earthy?

First, the confusion to clear up: Montepulciano d'Abruzzo is a grape grown in Abruzzo, on Italy's Adriatic side - it has nothing to do with Vino Nobile di Montepulciano, a Tuscan wine made from Sangiovese in a hilltown that happens to share the name. Different grape, different coast, different wine entirely. The earthiness in Montepulciano d'Abruzzo comes from a mix of thick skins, which load the wine with tannin and dark, savory compounds, and the clay and limestone soils around the Apennine foothills, where the vines sit at enough elevation to get real day-night temperature swings. That cooling holds onto acidity and pushes the wine toward dark plum, tar, and dried herb rather than pure fruit. Traditional producers ferment with minimal new oak, which lets that rustic, dusty character come through instead of masking it with vanilla and toast. It's a grape shaped by climate and soil to make honest, food-driven wine rather than something polished for sipping alone - which is exactly what makes it worth seeking out at the table.
