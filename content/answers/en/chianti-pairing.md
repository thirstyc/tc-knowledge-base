What pairs with Chianti?

Tomatoes, mostly. That's the real answer, and it isn't a coincidence.

Sangiovese has high acidity and moderate tannin, which is exactly what tomato sauce needs. Acidic food makes a low-acid wine taste flat and flabby; match acid against acid and both sides taste brighter. Pasta al pomodoro, lasagne, pizza, ragù — Chianti grew up next to that food and evolved alongside it.

Past tomatoes it handles anything savoury and a bit fatty. Grilled sausage, roast pork, bistecca, salami, aged pecorino. The tannin is firm enough to cut through fat without the wine having to be heavy about it.

Serve it at 16 to 18 degrees, cooler than most people pour a red. Warm Chianti loses its edge and the acidity starts reading as sour rather than fresh.

Where it struggles: delicate fish, cream sauces, anything sweet. The acidity that makes it so good against tomato turns hostile against butter and sugar.
