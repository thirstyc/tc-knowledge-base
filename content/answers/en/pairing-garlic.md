Wine with garlic?

It depends on how the garlic is cooked. Raw and sharp garlic suits crisp, herbal whites; roasted, mellow garlic suits fuller whites and medium-bodied reds.

Garlic changes dramatically with cooking, and so does the best wine.

- Raw garlic, in aioli, pesto or gazpacho: Sauvignon Blanc, Vermentino, Albariño or a dry Provence rosé.
- Sautéed garlic with shrimp or clams: Picpoul de Pinet, Albariño or Verdicchio.
- Roasted garlic, sweet and soft: oaked Chardonnay, Viognier or a light red like Pinot Noir.
- Garlic bread: a crisp white, Barbera or Chianti.
- Garlic-heavy meat dishes, like roast lamb with garlic and rosemary: Rioja, Côtes du Rhône, Syrah or Grenache.
- Chicken with forty cloves of garlic: white Burgundy or a Rhône white.
- Garlic with chili and soy, in stir-fries: off-dry Riesling or Grüner Veltliner.

Regional instincts are a good guide. The cuisines that use the most garlic, like Provence, Spain, southern Italy and Greece, all make wines that suit it: fresh, herbal whites, dry rosés and savoury reds.

Avoid very delicate or aged wines, whose subtle flavours strong garlic will overwhelm.
