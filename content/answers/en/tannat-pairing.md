What pairs with Tannat?

Tannat's tannins are aggressive because they come from thick skins and long maceration, and those tannins latch onto the proteins in your saliva, stripping the lubrication that makes wine feel smooth. Fat and protein interrupt that process: they bind the tannins before your mouth does, so the wine feels rounder and the food feels richer. That's why the classic pairing is grilled or braised beef, the fattier the better, short rib, brisket, a well-marbled steak with real char on it. Uruguay built its whole asado culture around this pairing for a reason. Lamb works the same way, as does aged hard cheese, which brings its own fat and salt to the fight. Skip anything delicate: fish, light salads, soft cheese all get flattened by the tannin. Serve it around 16 to 18°C, slightly below typical red-wine room temperature, which keeps the tannin from feeling even more aggressive than it already is.
