What pairs with Marlborough Riesling?

Marlborough Riesling usually carries a bit of residual sugar alongside its naturally high acidity, and that combination is exactly why it works so well with food that would flatten a drier white. Spicy dishes, particularly Thai and Sichuan cooking, are a classic match: the touch of sweetness cools down chili heat while the acidity keeps the wine from tasting cloying next to bold spice. Seafood, especially anything with a citrus or herb element, plays well off the wine's lime and green-apple character. Asian cuisine more broadly, from Vietnamese salads to Cantonese roast duck, tends to have a sweet-salty-sour balance that this style of Riesling was practically built to sit alongside. The wine's minerality, a product of the region's stony, free-draining river soils, also gives it enough backbone to stand up to richer sauces without disappearing. Serve cold, around 8 to 10 degrees, which keeps the sweetness in check and lets the acidity do its job.
