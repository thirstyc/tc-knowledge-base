Why does Assyrtiko taste so mineral and citrusy?

It comes down to volcanic soil and a brutal climate, not magic. Santorini's vines grow in pumice and volcanic ash, sandy, mineral-poor, and so well-draining that the plants are constantly water-stressed, which concentrates flavor and keeps yields naturally tiny. The vines are also trained into low, basket-like coils called kouloura, hugging the ground to dodge the island's relentless wind and catch overnight humidity, since it barely rains there. Hot days followed by sharply cooler nights slow sugar accumulation while preserving malic acid, which is where that high, almost electric acidity comes from. The citrus character, lemon, lime, sometimes grapefruit pith, is the grape's own aromatic signature, amplified by that acid backbone. As for 'mineral,' it's not the wine tasting literal volcanic rock; it's a sensory shorthand for a saline, flinty, almost smoky impression that high acidity and low fruitiness create, plus real sea-spray exposure on grapes grown a short walk from the Aegean. Serve it cold, 8-10°C, to keep all of that tension intact.
