Why isn't Washington wine more famous?

Partly geography, partly arithmetic. Washington's vineyards sit east of the Cascades in a high desert that most wine drinkers never picture when they think of the state, so there's none of the built-in tourism and name recognition that California's coastal valleys have had for decades. The industry is also just smaller: fewer wineries, less capital for marketing, less shelf space fought for internationally. What it does have is remarkably consistent quality, because that desert climate with its long sunlight hours and cold nights makes ripening reliable in a way that's harder to guarantee in cooler or more variable regions. Sommeliers and serious buyers have known this for years, which is why Washington Cabernet and Merlot show up so often on restaurant lists without much fanfare. It's a quiet reputation built on repeat performance rather than hype, the kind of region insiders point to when they want good structure and ripeness for less money than an equivalent Napa bottle. Fame just hasn't caught up to the quality yet.
