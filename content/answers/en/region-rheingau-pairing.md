What pairs with Rheingau Riesling?

Rheingau Riesling carries more weight and riper fruit than its Mosel cousin, and it's usually fermented closer to fully dry, so it needs food with some richness to match it rather than something delicate. Roast pork and schnitzel work because the wine's acidity cuts through the fat and crisp crust the way a squeeze of lemon would, while its body stands up to the meat instead of disappearing next to it. Rich fish in cream or butter sauce is the same logic: the sauce coats the palate, and the wine's acid resets it bite after bite. This is the German Riesling that argues with steak better than most people expect, and it can handle a bit of spice too, since the fruit has enough ripeness to buffer chili heat without turning sweet on you. Serve it a touch warmer than a Mosel, around 10 to 12°C, so the structure and texture actually show instead of hiding under the chill.
