What pairs with Baga?

The classic answer is leitão — Bairrada's roast suckling pig, skin cracked and blistered — and it's not a coincidence. Baga's tannin and searing acidity exist specifically to cut through pork fat and crisp skin; the wine practically requires grease to make sense. That's the local logic: this grape and that dish evolved together. Outside that context, aim for anything with real fat and char — grilled lamb, a slow-cooked stew, a hard aged cheese with some bite of its own. Skip anything delicate; Baga will flatten it. Also skip young Baga on its own — pour it with food or the tannin just reads as harsh. If you've got an older bottle, the tannin has softened and it plays nicer with simpler roasted meats. Serve around 16-18°C, standard red-wine range, nothing warmer or the alcohol and tannin both get more aggressive on the palate.
