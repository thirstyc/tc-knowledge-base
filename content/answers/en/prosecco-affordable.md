Why does Prosecco taste so affordable and festive?

The whole style is engineered for volume and speed, not complexity. Prosecco is made using the tank method (Charmat), where the secondary fermentation that creates the bubbles happens in large pressurized steel tanks over a few weeks, rather than in each individual bottle over years like Champagne. That's dramatically cheaper and faster, and it also preserves fresh, simple fruit aromas instead of building the toasty, yeasty notes you get from bottle aging on lees. Puglia-scale production numbers apply here too: Prosecco comes overwhelmingly from the flatter Veneto plains, farmed for quantity, not from steep, low-yield hillsides. Add minimal aging requirements and you get a wine built to be bottled and sold within the year. None of this is a knock. Serve it cold, around 6-8°C, within a year or two of purchase, and treat it as a mood, not a moment for contemplation. It's built to be poured generously, not sipped slowly.
