What pairs with Vancouver Island Pinot Gris or Ortega?

Vancouver Island's Pinot Gris and Ortega both come from a genuinely marginal, cool-climate site — short growing season, maritime humidity, grapes picked with high acid and modest alcohol — which is exactly the profile that works with delicate seafood rather than fighting it. Fresh oysters need a wine with enough acid to stand up to their brine without adding sweetness or weight, and both grapes deliver that bracing, saline-edged freshness with little to no oak getting in the way. Steamed clams work the same way: the wine's acidity cuts through the butter or wine sauce they're usually cooked in, and Ortega's slight aromatic lift (something between pear and honeysuckle) plays nicely against the clams' brininess. Light seafood pasta, especially anything with lemon, white wine, or a light cream, sits right in the wine's lane too. Serve cold, around 8 to 10 degrees, straight from the fridge — these wines lose their nerve fast as they warm, so drink them quickly once poured.
