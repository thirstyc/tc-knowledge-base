What pairs with New Zealand Sauvignon Blanc?

This wine's high acidity and aromatic intensity—grassy pyrazines plus tropical thiols—give it real cutting power, which is why it works so well with fat and salt: raw or grilled seafood, oysters, ceviche. The green, herbal side of the wine finds a natural echo in dishes built on fresh herbs, lime, or lightly spicy preparations, especially Thai and Vietnamese food, where the wine's acid tames chili heat instead of getting steamrolled by it (low residual sugar and modest alcohol help there too). Fresh goat cheese is the classic match because its own tang meets the wine's acid on equal footing rather than one flattening the other. Avoid rich, buttery, oak-driven dishes or heavily creamed sauces—the wine has almost no fat or oak of its own to bridge that gap, so it ends up tasting thin against them. Serve at 8–10°C, and drink it young; this isn't a wine that improves with bottle age.
