Wine for a picnic?

Light, fresh wines that taste good slightly chilled: rosé, sparkling wine, crisp whites and light reds you can chill. Picnic food is usually simple, and the wine should be easy to drink outdoors.

Rosé is the classic picnic wine. A dry Provence rosé suits sandwiches, salads, cheese and charcuterie.

Sparkling wine feels festive and works with almost any picnic food. Prosecco, Cava and pét-nat are easy choices, and pét-nat often comes with a crown cap, which means no corkscrew is needed.

Crisp whites like Sauvignon Blanc, Vinho Verde and Picpoul de Pinet are refreshing on a warm day.

Light reds, like Beaujolais, Gamay, Frappato and Lambrusco, taste great with a light chill and pair well with cold meats.

A few practical tips:

- Screwcaps and cans make life easier.
- Bring an insulated bag or a cooler with ice packs.
- Pack a corkscrew just in case.
- Use unbreakable glasses.
- Bring plenty of water.

What to avoid: heavy, high-alcohol reds and oaky whites. They feel heavy in the heat and don't taste their best warm.

Before you go, check whether drinking alcohol is allowed where you're picnicking. Rules vary a lot between parks and cities.
