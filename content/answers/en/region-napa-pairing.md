What pairs with Napa Cabernet?

Napa Cabernet is built around ripe, dark fruit, firm tannin, and often a good hit of new oak, all of which need something substantial on the plate or the wine just overwhelms the food. Fat and protein are the key: a ribeye or short rib gives the tannins something to grip onto, since tannin binds to protein and fat and softens in the process, making the wine taste smoother and the meat taste richer in return. Charring on a grilled steak echoes the toasted oak notes in the wine, which is why steakhouse pairings feel so obvious. Aged cheddar works for the same reason — enough fat and salt to cut through the tannin, plus its own concentrated, savoury intensity that doesn't get lost next to a bold wine. Avoid delicate fish, light salads, or anything with high acidity and little fat, since the Cabernet will just steamroll it. Serve around 16-18°C, slightly cooler than room temperature, so the alcohol doesn't dominate the fruit.
