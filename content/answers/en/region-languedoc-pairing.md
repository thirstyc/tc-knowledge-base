What pairs with Languedoc reds?

Languedoc reds are usually blends built around Grenache, Syrah, Carignan and sometimes Mourvèdre, grown on sun-baked hillsides that push sugar and tannin ripeness further than cooler French regions ever manage. That combination of moderate acidity, solid tannin, and warm dark fruit is built for fat and char. Grilled lamb works because the tannin grips the fat and clears it off your palate, cassoulet works for the same reason with the added benefit of the wine's earthiness matching the dish's slow-cooked, rustic depth. Charcuterie is an easy match too, the fruit and mild spice notes stand up to cured, salty meat without being overwhelmed. These aren't delicate wines meant for subtle food, they're built with enough weight and structure to sit alongside hearty, fat-forward cooking without disappearing. Serve them around 16 to 18°C, slightly cooler than room temperature, which keeps the alcohol in check and the fruit from tasting jammy. This is unpretentious wine matched to unpretentious, generous food, and it works because both sides are pulling in the same direction.
