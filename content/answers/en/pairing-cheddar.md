Wine with cheddar cheese?

It depends on the age of the cheddar. Mild cheddar suits a fruity white or light red, while aged, sharp cheddar can take something bolder: a structured red, a Tawny Port or a dry Sherry.

Mild, young cheddar is creamy and gentle. Try an unoaked Chardonnay, a Chenin Blanc or a light, fruity red like Pinot Noir or Beaujolais.

Aged cheddar is sharper, saltier and often has crunchy crystals. Its intensity can handle a medium to full-bodied red. Rioja Reserva, Cabernet Sauvignon and Zinfandel all work, because the cheese's fat and salt soften their tannins.

For a more interesting match, try Tawny Port or a dry Amontillado Sherry. Their nutty, caramel notes suit aged cheddar perfectly.

Traditional farmhouse cheddar from England is also great with English sparkling wine or a dry cider.

Smoked cheddar pairs well with smoky, spicy reds like Shiraz or Zinfandel.

Cheddar with apple or chutney, as on a cheese board, suits an off-dry Riesling or Chenin Blanc, which match the fruit.

What to avoid: very delicate wines with aged cheddar, which the cheese simply overwhelms.
