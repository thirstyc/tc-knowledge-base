Why is Moscato underrated?

People hear sweet and assume simple, but that's a bias about sugar, not a fact about the wine. Moscato is a large family of Muscat grapes loaded with terpenes, the same aromatic compounds behind lavender and citrus blossom, so the aromatics are intense and layered even before fermentation touches them. What reads as one-dimensional is usually just youth: Moscato is almost always drunk within a year or two, so it never gets the chance to show secondary complexity the way an aged Riesling might. But within that youthful window there's real range, from the floral, low-alcohol fizz of Moscato d'Asti to fuller, still, dry Muscats grown from Alsace to Greece to Australia, some with real weight and texture. The sweetness also isn't crude; a well-made Moscato balances sugar against acidity so it never feels syrupy. It's an easy wine, not a dumb one, and dismissing it on principle just means missing a genuinely aromatic, well-crafted category.
