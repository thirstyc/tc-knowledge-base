Why is Vinho Verde so affordable?

Vinho Verde is cheap because everything about how it's made is built for volume and speed, not for scarcity. Minho, in northern Portugal, is a large, historically high-yielding region, and vines are often trained high on pergolas, partly to fight the constant humidity and rot pressure there, which lets growers push volume without much fuss. Grapes are picked early, before they build much sugar, which keeps alcohol low and cuts the time and cost of extended ripening or barrel aging. Winemaking is stainless steel and straightforward, with little or no oak and minimal time in cellar before release. The whole system is designed to get a fresh, light wine into bottle and onto a shelf as fast and cheaply as possible, not to produce something collectible. That's not a flaw, it's the design brief: Vinho Verde is a patio wine, meant to be drunk within a year or two of release, cold, in quantity, without ceremony. Comparing it to a serious white Burgundy misses the point entirely.
