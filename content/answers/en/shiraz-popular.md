Why is Shiraz so popular?

Because it's one of the easiest reds to like, and that's down to the grape itself, not just clever branding. Syrah/Shiraz ripens readily in warm climates and packs in sugar (hence alcohol and body) while its acidity drops more gently than something like Sangiovese or Nebbiolo. The tannins are soft and round rather than grippy, so there's little to "get past" before you enjoy it. Warm-climate versions from Barossa or McLaren Vale lean into dark fruit, chocolate, and vanilla-forward oak, flavours that read as generous and food-friendly rather than austere. Cooler regions, like the Northern Rhone with Syrah, or Victoria, pull out more pepper and violet, but the fruit-forward Australian style is what built its mass appeal from the 1990s onward, when it was marketed as a fuss-free alternative to Bordeaux. Low acid and soft tannin also mean it doesn't demand food the way a high-acid Barolo does; it drinks fine on its own, at room temperature or slightly below (around 16-18C), which makes it the path of least resistance for casual drinking.
