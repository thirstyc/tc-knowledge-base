What pairs with Moldovan wine?

Moldovan wine was built around a specific table, and it still shows. Mămăligă is dense, fatty cornmeal porridge, and it needs a wine with real acid to cut through it rather than get flattened — a Feteasca Alba or a lighter Rara Neagra does that job well. Grilled pork, heavy with rendered fat, wants either grippy tannin or bright acidity to reset the palate between bites, which is where a Feteasca Neagra earns its keep. Sharp aged cheese brings salt and concentrated umami, and that needs a wine with enough fruit sweetness to balance rather than clash — again, nothing over-oaked or delicate. None of this is precious food; it's peasant cooking, and the wines were made to survive it, not decorate it. Serve reds a touch cool, around 14-16°C, rather than room temperature — Moldovan summers get hot and warm reds go flabby fast. Whites belong at 8-10°C, cold enough to keep that acid honest.
