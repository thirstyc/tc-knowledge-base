Why does Refosco fly under the radar?

Refosco suffers from an identity problem more than a quality one. It's native to a stretch of northeastern Italy and Slovenia — Friuli, the Veneto, the Karst plateau — but it shows up under different names and slightly different genetic profiles depending on where you are, and it overlaps confusingly with Teran across the border in Istria. No single DOC or producer group ever staked its reputation on Refosco the way Barolo did for Nebbiolo or Chianti did for Sangiovese, so it never got the marketing push or the price ceiling that comes with a flagship wine. It's also historically been treated as a workhorse grape, blended away or bottled simply for local drinking rather than aged and taken seriously. That's a shame, because well-made Refosco — structured, peppery, built for food — holds its own against far more expensive reds. It just never had anyone in its corner telling the world so. Worth seeking out precisely because almost nobody else is looking.
