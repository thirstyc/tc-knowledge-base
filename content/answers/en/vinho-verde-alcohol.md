Why is Vinho Verde so low in alcohol?

Two things stack together here. First, the Minho region is cool and rainy, which slows sugar accumulation in the grapes since sugar is what yeast later converts into alcohol. Second, producers pick early on purpose, chasing acidity over ripeness, which caps sugar levels even further. Less sugar in the grape means less potential alcohol in the fermented wine, straightforward chemistry, no shortcuts involved. The result usually lands between 9 and 11.5 percent, noticeably lighter than most whites, which typically sit closer to 12.5 to 13.5. That lightness is not a flaw or a marketing gimmick, it is a direct consequence of climate and harvest timing. Practically, it means you can pour a full glass at lunch without the afternoon getting away from you, and it is part of why the wine reads as refreshing rather than heavy, even before the fizz and acidity get involved.
