What pairs with Banyuls?

Dark chocolate is the textbook match, and for once the textbook is right — Banyuls' dried-fruit, rancio richness lines up almost exactly with cocoa's bitterness, so the two don't compete, they extend each other. Beyond chocolate, look for anything with concentrated dark fruit or spice: a fig tart, a spiced cake, dishes with cinnamon or clove. It also does something less obvious well, which is stand up to blue cheese — the sweetness balances the salt and funk the same way a good port would. What it doesn't need is a delicate dessert; anything too light gets steamrolled by the alcohol and sugar. Serve it a little cool, around 14-16°C, not room temperature — that keeps the sweetness from feeling syrupy and keeps the rancio notes from turning flabby. Pour smaller amounts too; it's rich enough that a full glass is usually more than the pairing needs.
