Why is Santorini Assyrtiko so expensive?

The island has barely 3,000 acres of vineyard, all worked by hand on volcanic soil that no tractor could handle anyway. The vines are ancient and ungrafted, spared from phylloxera by that same sandy volcanic ground, and they're trained into low coiled baskets called kouloura to survive the island's constant wind and dry climate. That shape makes every pruning and harvesting pass slow, manual work, and it also caps yields hard, since a stressed, drought-adapted vine simply doesn't produce much fruit. Meanwhile Santorini's land is under enormous pressure from tourism, so vineyard real estate competes directly with hotel development, and prices reflect that scarcity, not just wine value. Layer global demand from sommeliers and collectors chasing a distinctive, high-acid, food-friendly white onto a genuinely tiny and shrinking supply, and prices rise fast. It's a case where the scarcity story isn't marketing spin, it's a literal count of acres, vines and hours of hand labor that can't be scaled up no matter how popular the wine gets.
