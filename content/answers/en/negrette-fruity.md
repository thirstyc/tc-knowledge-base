Why does Negrette taste so light and fruity?

Négrette is a thin-skinned red grape grown almost exclusively around Fronton, northwest of Toulouse, on gravelly clay-limestone soils along the Tarn river. Thin skins mean less tannin and anthocyanin extraction during fermentation, which is why the wine comes out soft, juicy and low in grip rather than structured or chewy. What it lacks in tannin it makes up in aromatics — violets, redcurrant, raspberry, sometimes a black-pepper lift, all fairly upfront since the wine isn't built to age and evolve slowly like a tannic red would. Producers often blend in a bit of Syrah, Cabernet Franc or Gamay to give it more backbone, but pure or nearly-pure Négrette stays light on its feet. Serve it slightly cool, around 14-16°C, which sharpens the fruit and keeps it refreshing. This is a grape to drink young, within a couple years of the vintage — cellaring it does nothing but let the fruit fade with no structure underneath to replace it.
