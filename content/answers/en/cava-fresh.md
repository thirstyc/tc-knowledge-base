Why does Cava taste so fresh and elegant?

Cava gets its snap from two things: grapes and geography. Macabeo, Xarel·lo and Parellada are all naturally high-acid, thin-skinned varieties grown around Penedès, often on limestone-heavy soils that don't retain much water, stressing the vines just enough to keep sugars in check. Then comes the traditional method: a second fermentation happens inside the sealed bottle, trapping CO2 as fine, persistent bubbles rather than the coarse fizz you get from injecting gas into a tank. The wine rests on its spent yeast (lees) for a while after that, which softens texture and adds a faint bread-dough note, though usually less than Champagne since aging times tend to be shorter. The result is a wine that's crisp, precise and light on its feet rather than rich or toasty. Serve it properly cold, around 6 to 8°C, straight from the fridge. Warmer than that and the acidity that makes it refreshing starts to feel flabby instead.
