What temperature should I serve Oregon Pinot Gris at?

Colder than you think is wrong here. Most people dump white wine into an ice bath and serve it at fridge temperature, around 4°C, which mutes everything interesting about this wine. Oregon Pinot Gris has real texture and some aromatic complexity, so serving it too cold flattens the pear and floral notes into nothing and just leaves you tasting acid. Aim for 10 to 12°C, which is cool but not painful. Practically, that means pulling it from the fridge about 20 to 30 minutes before you pour, or using a wine fridge if you have one. If you're at a restaurant and the bottle arrives glacial, let it sit in the glass for a few minutes before judging it. The wine needs a little warmth to unclench and show its actual structure, that slightly waxy, almost stone-fruit character underneath the acidity. Serve it too warm, though, past 14°C or so, and it turns soft and loses the snap that makes it good with food in the first place.
