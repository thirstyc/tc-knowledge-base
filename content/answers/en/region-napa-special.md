What makes Napa Valley wines special?

Napa Valley is California's most famous wine region, and its reputation rests on Cabernet Sauvignon: ripe, powerful and polished, with blackcurrant, cocoa and sweet oak.

The valley is small, about 30 miles long, but its conditions vary a lot. Cool fog rolls in from San Pablo Bay in the south, while the northern end near Calistoga is much warmer. Hillside sites like Howell Mountain and Spring Mountain give firmer, more tannic wines than valley-floor areas like Oakville and Rutherford.

Napa's international standing dates from the 1976 Judgment of Paris, a blind tasting in which California wines beat French classics and upended assumptions about the New World.

Beyond Cabernet, Napa makes Chardonnay, Merlot and Sauvignon Blanc, sometimes labelled Fumé Blanc when it's oaked.

The catch is price. Land and demand have made Napa one of the world's most expensive wine regions, and entry-level bottles rarely show what the place can do.

Looking for value nearby? Sonoma, Lake County and Paso Robles offer similar styles for less.
