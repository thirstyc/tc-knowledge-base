Do sulfites cause headaches?

Probably not, for most people. Sulfites are a common scapegoat for wine headaches, but the evidence doesn't support it, and there are more likely explanations.

Sulfite sensitivity is real but uncommon, and it mainly affects a minority of people with asthma, where it triggers breathing symptoms rather than headaches.

The clearest argument against the sulfite theory is comparison. White and sweet wines contain more sulfites than reds, yet red wine is the one people most often blame for headaches. Dried apricots contain far more sulfites than any wine and rarely get blamed.

More likely culprits include alcohol itself, which dilates blood vessels and dehydrates you; histamines and tyramine, found in higher amounts in red wine; tannins, which some research links to headaches; and simply drinking more than you meant to.

Practical steps: drink water alongside, eat something, go slower, and see whether a particular style or producer is the problem rather than wine in general.

If wine reliably gives you headaches or other symptoms, that's worth raising with a doctor rather than self-diagnosing a sulfite allergy.
