What pairs with Chianti Classico?

Heavier than basic Chianti, because the wine is heavier. Classico has more structure, more concentration and usually some oak behind it, so it wants food with more to push against.

Bistecca alla fiorentina is the local answer and it happens to be the right one — a thick, rare, charred T-bone with nothing on it but olive oil and salt. Tannin needs protein and char to soften against. Roast lamb, wild boar ragù, duck, game birds, anything braised for most of an afternoon.

Aged cheese works better here than with young Chianti: pecorino toscano, parmigiano, something with crystals in it.

A Riserva or Gran Selezione, with more time behind it, softens into something you can pour beside slower food — mushroom risotto, truffle pasta, a long Sunday roast.

What to skip is the pizza. Save the everyday bottle for that. Classico costs two or three times more and the difference vanishes under tomato and mozzarella.
