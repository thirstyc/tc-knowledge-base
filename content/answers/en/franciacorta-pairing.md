What pairs with Franciacorta?

The high acidity and fine mousse make Franciacorta genuinely food-friendly rather than just a toasting wine, which is the mistake people make with any traditional-method sparkling. As an aperitivo it works because the acid resets your palate between bites, cutting through salty things like prosciutto or aged parmesan without fighting them. With seafood, especially anything raw or lightly cooked like crudo, scallops or a simple risotto ai frutti di mare, the wine's citrus and mineral edge lifts the dish instead of competing with it. Lighter pastas with butter or cream sauces work too, since the bubbles and acid cut through richness the way a squeeze of lemon would. If you're drinking a riserva with more lees aging, its nuttier, more savory profile can handle heavier fare, think mushroom risotto or roasted chicken, better than a younger, fruitier bottling would. Serve at 8-10°C, and don't over-chill it to the point the aromatics shut down, which is easy to do with sparkling wine straight from the fridge door.
