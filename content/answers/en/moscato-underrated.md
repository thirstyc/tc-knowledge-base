Why does Moscato get dismissed so easily?

Moscato gets tagged as unserious mostly because of volume: cheap, mass-produced versions flood the market and are often a lot of people's first wine ever, which cements an association with beginners rather than craft. Sweetness itself gets unfairly read as a lack of skill, when in fact making a balanced low-alcohol sweet wine is technically demanding. The winemaker has to stop fermentation at precisely the right point, chilling and filtering out the yeast before it converts too much sugar, while still preserving enough acidity to keep the wine tasting fresh rather than syrupy. A well-made Moscato d'Asti balances real grape sweetness against racy acidity so it never feels heavy, and the low alcohol, typically 5-7%, keeps it light and aromatic instead of cloying. The problem isn't the style, it's that the good examples get lumped in with the bulk stuff on supermarket shelves. Judge it the way you'd judge any other wine, by balance and precision, not by how much sugar is in the glass.
