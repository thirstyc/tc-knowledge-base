What pairs with Basses Laurentides whites and light reds?

These are cool-climate wines from hybrid grapes, picked with bright natural acidity and modest alcohol, so the pairing logic is about matching that freshness rather than overpowering it. Grilled chicken works because the char adds just enough smokiness to meet the wine halfway without fighting its lightness. Fresh herbs, dill, tarragon, chives, and leafy greens echo the herbal, sometimes grassy notes you get from grapes like Frontenac gris or Seyval blanc grown this far north. Mild, soft cheeses are the move over anything aged or pungent, since old cheese needs a wine with more weight and tannin to stand up to it. Serve the whites cold, around 8-10°C, and the light reds slightly cooler than usual, around 12-14°C, to keep that acidity crisp rather than sour. Avoid rich cream sauces or heavily spiced dishes, they'll flatten the wine. Think simple, herb-forward, lightly cooked food, and the pairing takes care of itself.
