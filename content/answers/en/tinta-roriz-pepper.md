Why does Tinta Roriz taste so dark and peppery?

Tinta Roriz is the Douro's name for Tempranillo, grown on the region's steep schist terraces where vines fight for every drop of water and the sun is relentless. Schist retains heat and drains fast, forcing roots deep and concentrating whatever fruit the vine manages to ripen - small berries, thick skins, lots of tannin and pigment packed into not much juice. The extreme heat and long hang time push the grape toward dark fruit, dried herb and a peppery, spice-box note that shows up especially when it's blended with Touriga Nacional and Touriga Franca, the usual partners in Douro dry reds. None of this is Port, despite the region's fame for it - these are table wines, structured and built to age, with the tannin from those thick skins giving them real backbone. Serve at 16-18°C, decant a young one, and don't expect softness early; give it a few years or pair it with something fatty enough to meet it halfway.
