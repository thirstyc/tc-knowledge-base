Why does Alvarinho taste so fresh and citrusy?

Alvarinho comes from Portugal's Minho region, in the northwest corner where the Atlantic sits close enough to keep summers mild and nights cool - exactly the conditions that preserve malic acid as the grape ripens. It's grown within Vinho Verde, though Alvarinho-based wines from the Monção e Melgaço subregion are typically more structured and varietal than the light, faintly spritzy blends the Vinho Verde name usually brings to mind. The grape itself has thick skins and naturally high acidity, and granite-based soils add a saline, stony edge rather than fruit weight. Winemaking is almost always stainless steel with no oak, sometimes with extended lees contact for a little texture without dulling the citrus and green-apple brightness. It's the same grape as Albariño just across the border in Rías Baixas, Spain - here with a Portuguese accent: leaner, saltier, less overtly fruity. Serve cold, around 8-10°C, with grilled fish or shellfish; the wine is basically doing the job of a lemon wedge already.
