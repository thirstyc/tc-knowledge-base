What pairs with Burgundy Pinot?

Pinot Noir is a thin-skinned grape, which means very little tannin and a lot of bright acidity instead, the opposite structural toolkit from Bordeaux. That acidity is what makes it work with rich, earthy dishes: mushroom risotto's creaminess needs something to cut through it, and Pinot's acid does that job without overwhelming the mushroom's delicate flavor the way tannin would. Game birds and venison share Burgundy's own earthy, forest-floor and undergrowth notes, so the pairing is more echo than contrast. Soft, washed-rind or bloomy cheeses work for the same reason cheese generally prefers acid over tannin: the wine's brightness balances the cheese's fat instead of clashing with it. Avoid heavy, tannin-hungry dishes like a fatty steak; Pinot has nothing there to soften and will just taste thin by comparison. Serve slightly cooler than Bordeaux, around 14-16°C, to keep the fruit and acid lifted rather than let alcohol dominate. A wine this transparent about its terroir deserves food that lets it stay the star of the plate, not a supporting act.
