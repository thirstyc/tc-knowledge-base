Why does Albariño fly under the radar outside Spain?

Part of it is just branding. Sancerre gets to say one word, Sauvignon Blanc, and a region everyone's heard of. Rías Baixas has to explain itself: a small, rainy corner of Galicia in northwest Spain, a grape name most people can't pronounce confidently, and a wine style that didn't have an export-ready marketing push behind it the way New Zealand built for Marlborough Sauvignon Blanc. Production is also genuinely small, many estates are family-run and modest in size, so there's less volume flooding restaurant lists and supermarket shelves compared to the big commercial whites. Sommeliers have known for years that it's one of the best acid-driven, food-friendly whites being made anywhere, precise, saline, aromatic without being cloying, but that knowledge hasn't fully filtered down to casual drinkers who default to familiar names. It's a classic case of quality outpacing recognition. The wine doesn't need to change; the market just needs to catch up to what's already in the glass.
