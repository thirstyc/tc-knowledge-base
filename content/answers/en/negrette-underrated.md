Why does Negrette fly under the radar?

Négrette is grown almost nowhere else of consequence besides Fronton, a small appellation northwest of Toulouse, so there's no critical mass of producers or regions building its reputation the way Cabernet or Merlot benefit from being planted worldwide. It's also thin-skinned and finicky, sensitive to rot and inconsistent yields, which keeps production small and mostly local. Because the wine is meant to be drunk young rather than cellared, it never generates the collector buzz or investment-grade pricing that builds fame for a grape or region — nobody's stashing cases of Fronton in a cellar for twenty years. Add to that its geographic shadow: it sits a few hours from Bordeaux, arguably the most famous wine region on earth, so it's easy for a light, obscure red from the southwest to get overlooked entirely. None of that reflects on quality — it just means you're more likely to find good Négrette by asking a specialist shop than by stumbling onto it on a big-box shelf.
