What pairs with Barbaresco?

Nebbiolo's whole structure is built on high tannin and high acid with relatively pale colour, so the wine reads as austere and sour if you drink it on its own. It needs fat and umami to fill in the middle. Braised or grilled red meat with some char works because fat coats the palate and softens the grip of the tannin, while the acid slices back through the richness so the wine never feels heavy. Mushrooms, especially porcini, echo the tar, dried rose and forest-floor notes that come from long skin contact and extended aging in large old barrels. Shaved white truffle over risotto or egg pasta is the classic Piedmont pairing for exactly that reason, the wine's savoury undertone doesn't compete with the truffle, it mirrors it. Aged Parmigiano works too, the salt and umami taming the tannin. Skip delicate fish or anything spicy, the tannin turns harsh and metallic against both. Serve at 16-18°C, cooler than room temperature, so the acid stays bright instead of flabby.
