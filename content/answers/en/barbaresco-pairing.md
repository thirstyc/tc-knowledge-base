What pairs with Barbaresco?

Nebbiolo's tannin and acid are both high, which means the wine needs fat and umami on the plate or it tastes austere and sour on its own. Braised or grilled red meat, especially something with a bit of char, softens the tannin and the acid cuts straight through the richness. Mushrooms, particularly porcini, echo the earthy, tar-and-tobacco side of the wine, and shaved truffle over risotto or egg pasta is the classic Piedmont match because the wine's savoury undertones mirror it rather than compete. Aged hard cheeses like a mature Parmigiano work too, the salt and umami taming the tannin. Avoid delicate fish or anything too spicy, the tannin will turn harsh and bitter against them. Serve at 16-18°C, slightly cooler than room temperature, so the acid stays lively instead of flabby.
