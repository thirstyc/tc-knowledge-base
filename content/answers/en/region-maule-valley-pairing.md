What pairs with Maule Valley Carignan?

Carignan from Maule is built on structure, not fruit, so you want food that meets its tannin and acid head-on rather than trying to soften it. Grilled sausage with a good char works because the fat coats the palate and the smoke echoes whatever time the wine spent in old oak or concrete. Stewed lamb does something similar but slower, the collagen and richness taming the grip over a long braise. A rustic hard cheese, something aged and a little crystalline, gives the wine's acidity a job to do by cutting through the fat. What you want to avoid is anything delicate, since these are old, dry-farmed vines making wines with real backbone, and a light dish just gets flattened. Think Sunday lunch food, not a light Tuesday salad. Serve it a touch cooler than a typical red, around 15-16°C, so the tannins stay tight and don't read as hot or jammy.
