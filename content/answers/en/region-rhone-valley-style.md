Why does Rhône wine taste so peppery and rich?

It's really two different wines wearing one regional name. In the north, Syrah grows on steep granite and schist slopes around Côte-Rôtie and Hermitage, often trained on terraces because the incline is too brutal for tractors. That granite reflects heat but drains fast, stressing the vine just enough to concentrate flavour, and the grape itself carries natural white and black pepper compounds (rotundone, if you want the technical name) that show up more in cool, marginal ripening conditions. That's your smoked-meat, cracked-pepper, olive-tapenade profile. Head south to Châteauneuf-du-Pape and the Rhône widens into flat, baked terrain with those famous galet stones that store daytime heat and release it at night. Grenache thrives there, giving warmth and weight, while Mourvèdre and Syrah fill in structure and dark fruit. The result is rounder, riper, higher in alcohol, lower in that peppery edge. Same river, same grape family in places, completely different geology and sun exposure. Serve northern Syrah around 16-17°C, southern blends closer to 16°C so the alcohol doesn't dominate.
