What pairs with Chianti or Brunello?

Sangiovese, the backbone of both Chianti and Brunello, runs high in acid and tannin but relatively light in fruit sweetness — which is precisely why it was bred, over generations, around tomato sauce. Tomatoes are acidic themselves, and a wine with matching acidity won't taste dull or flat next to them; it cuts through instead of clashing. Grilled steak, especially something like a bistecca, brings fat and char that soften Sangiovese's tannin the same way lamb does for tannic reds elsewhere — protein and fat bind to tannin and smooth it out. Aged pecorino adds salt and concentrated umami, which makes the wine's fruit taste brighter by contrast. Serve Chianti around 16°C and give Brunello a bit more, closer to 18°C, since its longer aging in bigger barrels needs slight warmth to open up. The rule holds across the board: this is wine built to sit beside food, not to be tasted alone.
