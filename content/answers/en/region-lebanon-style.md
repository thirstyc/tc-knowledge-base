Why does Lebanese Cabernet taste so structured?

Lebanese Cabernet gets its backbone from geography, not winemaking tricks. The Bekaa Valley is a high plateau, roughly 1,000 metres above sea level, walled in by Mount Lebanon and the Anti-Lebanon range - that elevation and distance from the coast means intense daytime heat but a sharp temperature drop after dark. Vines respond to that swing by ripening fully while still holding onto malic acid, which is what gives the tannins their grip and the wine its savory, almost herbal edge instead of New World sweetness. Cabernet Sauvignon and Cabernet Franc were planted here generations ago, originally under French influence, and decades of adapting to this specific soil and climate have pushed the fruit character away from generic Bordeaux and into something more its own - dustier, more spice-driven, less about pure fruit. Layer on limestone-rich soils that stress the vines just enough to concentrate flavor, and you get wines built for aging and for food. Serve at 16-18°C, ideally after an hour of air, alongside grilled meat or something with real fat to meet those tannins.
