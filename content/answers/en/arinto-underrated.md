Why does Arinto fly under the radar?

Portuguese whites in general get skipped over in favour of Spanish and Italian bottles, and Arinto has the added problem of mostly hiding in blends — it's a key component in Vinho Verde but rarely gets its name on the label there. The place it really shows on its own is Bucelas, a small region near Lisbon, and production is tiny enough that most people never encounter it. That's the real loss, because Arinto has a trait most cheap whites don't: it holds onto acidity even at full ripeness, so it ages well, developing honeyed, waxy notes over five to ten years instead of just fading. It's essentially a age-worthy, high-acid white doing Riesling's job at a fraction of the price and none of the reputation. If Vinho Verde is the only place you've met it, track down a Bucelas bottling — it's a different, more serious wine entirely.
