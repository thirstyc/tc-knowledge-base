Why does Arinto fly under the radar?

Portuguese whites get passed over for Spanish and Italian bottles as a rule, and Arinto has the extra problem of mostly working anonymously inside blends, it's a key structural grape in Vinho Verde but almost never gets billed by name there. The one place it stands alone is Bucelas, a small appellation near Lisbon with production small enough that most drinkers never cross paths with it. That's a real loss, because Arinto has something most inexpensive whites lack: it holds onto acidity even at full ripeness, which means it actually ages, picking up honeyed, waxy complexity over five to ten years instead of just going flat and tired. It's essentially doing Riesling's long-game trick, high acid built to outlast the fruit, at a fraction of the price and with none of Riesling's reputation to trade on. If Vinho Verde is the only version you've had, a Bucelas bottling is a genuinely different, more serious wine, worth seeking out on its own terms.
