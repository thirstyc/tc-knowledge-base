What pairs with rosé?

Rosé works at the table because it splits the difference between white and red without committing fully to either: it has white wine's acidity and chill-friendly freshness, but often enough fruit and texture to stand up to slightly richer food than a delicate white could handle. That acidity cuts through the oil in grilled fish and the fat in charcuterie, while low tannin means it won't clash with a vinaigrette-dressed salad the way a tannic red would. It's also forgiving with spice - the fruitiness and the chill both blunt heat rather than amplify it, unlike a high-alcohol red. Practically, that makes rosé one of the easiest wines to pour on a summer table full of mixed dishes: grilled shrimp next to a niçoise salad next to some saucisson, all things that would send you hunting for different bottles otherwise. Serve it properly cold, 8-10°C, since warmth flattens the acidity that's doing most of the pairing work. It's not a wine to overthink - the versatility is the whole point.
