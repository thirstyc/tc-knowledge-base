Why is Cava so affordable?

Cava is made the exact same way as Champagne — second fermentation happens inside the sealed bottle, producing the same fine, long-lasting bubbles — but almost everything else about the economics is different. Land in Penedès is a fraction of the cost of vineyard land in Champagne, labor costs are lower, and production happens at a much larger scale, which spreads out the fixed costs of aging, riddling and disgorging. The grapes matter too: Macabeo, Xarel·lo and Parellada are workhorse varieties, not the pricier Pinot Noir and Chardonnay that dominate Champagne. None of that makes Cava a lesser wine, just a wine without a famous name attached and the pricing that comes with it. You're paying for the bubbles and the technique, not the real estate or the marketing. Serve it cold, around 6 to 8°C, and don't feel obligated to save it for special occasions — that's kind of the point.
