What pairs with Petite Sirah?

Petite Sirah, actually a grape called Durif, a cross of Syrah and Peloursin, has thick skins and small berries, which means a huge ratio of skin to juice. Skins are where tannin and color come from, so the wine ends up densely colored and mouth-drying, with tannins that can feel almost gritty on their own. That's why it needs food with real fat and protein to soften it, think short ribs, a well-marbled steak, or a slow-braised stew. The fat coats your palate and binds with the tannin, making the wine taste rounder and the food taste richer in return. Aged hard cheeses work for the same reason. Grilled meat with a charred crust is another good match, the char and smoke echo the wine's dark, brooding fruit. Avoid delicate dishes, fish, salads, anything subtle, the tannin will flatten them completely. Serve around 16 to 18°C, and don't be afraid to pair it with something genuinely heavy.
