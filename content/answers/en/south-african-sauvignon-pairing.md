What pairs with South African Sauvignon Blanc?

The grape's signature is a bundle of thiols and pyrazines that read as passionfruit, gooseberry, and cut grass, backed by acidity high enough to cut through fat and salt. That's the mechanism behind the pairings, not just habit. With seafood, especially oysters, ceviche, or grilled fish, the acid mirrors citrus and mineral notes without dulling the flesh. With spicy food, particularly Cape Malay curries or anything with chilli heat, the wine's coolness and lack of oak keep the palate from fatiguing, while a touch of residual sugar in some styles softens the burn. Fresh, unaged cheeses like chèvre or a young feta work because their tang matches the wine's own tartness rather than clashing with it. Avoid rich, buttery, or heavily oaked dishes, that's Chardonnay territory, not this. Serve it cold, around 8 to 10°C, so the acidity stays taut. Warmer than that and the herbaceous edge turns blowsy fast.
