Pinot Grigio vs Chardonnay?

Pinot Grigio is light, crisp and simple; Chardonnay is fuller, rounder and far more variable.

Pinot Grigio, in its common Italian style, is picked early to keep it fresh. Expect lemon, green apple and pear, light body, high acidity and no oak. It's an easy, refreshing wine that rarely demands attention.

Chardonnay covers much more ground. Unoaked versions like Chablis are crisp and mineral, not far from Pinot Grigio in spirit. Oaked versions add vanilla, toast and a creamy texture, and can be full-bodied and rich.

Note the naming: the same grape as Pinot Grigio is called Pinot Gris in France, where Alsace makes a richer, spicier and sometimes off-dry style closer in weight to Chardonnay.

With food, Pinot Grigio suits light seafood, salads and antipasti. Chardonnay handles richer dishes: roast chicken, creamy pasta, lobster.

Price and ageing differ too. Most Pinot Grigio is inexpensive and best young; Chardonnay runs from cheap to some of the most expensive white wine in the world, and the best can age for decades.

Which to choose? Pinot Grigio for easy refreshment; Chardonnay when you want texture and depth.
