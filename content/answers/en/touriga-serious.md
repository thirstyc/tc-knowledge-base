Why is Touriga Nacional so serious?

It comes down to what's in the grape itself: thick skins loaded with tannin and anthocyanins, plus a natural tendency to ripen with high sugar, which means high potential alcohol, often above 13.5%. That's exactly why Touriga Nacional is the backbone of Port, it was bred by nature and centuries of selection in the Douro's schist terraces to survive fortification and still taste like something. As a dry table wine it carries that same DNA: firm tannic backbone, dark fruit, often a floral, almost violet-like lift that keeps it from feeling heavy. It needs time, either in the glass or in the bottle, for the tannins to integrate; young examples can taste closed and gripping. This isn't a wine to pour and immediately expect charm from, give it air, food, or a few years, and the seriousness turns into structure that actually holds a meal together rather than fighting it.
