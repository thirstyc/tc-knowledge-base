What's the Difference Between Barolo and Barbaresco?

Same grape, same province, different address. Barolo's zone spans five communes with heavier limestone-clay marl, especially around Serralunga and Monforte, which pushes tannin and structure hard — this is why Barolo needs real time, with DOCG rules mandating roughly 38 months of aging, at least 18 in wood. Barbaresco sits closer to the Tanaro river, where sandier, looser soils and a slightly warmer mesoclimate let grapes ripen a touch earlier and soften the tannic edges. Its minimum aging is shorter too, around 26 months with 9 in wood. Neither is objectively bigger in quality — Barbaresco is often described as more perfumed and approachable in youth, Barolo as more structured and built for the cellar, but great examples of both can go the distance. Serve both around 16-18°C, decant an hour or two ahead, especially younger bottles with tight tannins. If you want to drink now, lean Barbaresco. If you can wait a decade, Barolo rewards the patience.
