Why does the Douro matter beyond Port?

The Douro was demarcated as a wine region centuries before most of the world had formal appellation systems, and for most of that history the valley existed to grow Port, meaning the best fruit and the best sites were reserved for fortified wine while the dry table wines were treated as an afterthought. That's flipped over the last few decades. Producers realized the same old vines on those brutally steep, terraced hillsides, some of them worked entirely by hand because machinery can't handle the slope, could make serious dry reds if they stopped skimming off only the fortification-grade fruit. The terraces themselves are the giveaway: built up over hundreds of years on schist soil that vines have to fight through cracked rock to root into, which is part of why the wines carry so much concentration and minerality. So when people say Douro table wine is underrated, it's not marketing, it's a region redirecting fruit and attention that spent centuries going somewhere else. Worth seeking out before everyone else catches on.
