What pairs with Pinotage?

Pinotage carries genuinely high tannin, a legacy of its thick-skinned Cinsault and Pinot Noir parentage, plus it often gets extra structure from oak aging or heavy extraction. Tannin needs fat and protein to soften it, which is why grilled meat and lamb work so well: the char adds smoky notes that echo the wine's own roasted or reductive character, while the fat coats the palate and blunts the grip. Lamb specifically matches Pinotage's earthier, tar-and-plum undertones better than a cleaner cut like chicken would. Spicy food is a slightly trickier but rewarding match: the wine's ripe, often jammy fruit balances chili heat, while the tannin gets tamed by the same fat that usually accompanies spicy dishes, think braised short rib with chili rather than a lean chili-lime salad. What doesn't work is anything delicate, white fish, light salads, soft cheese, since the tannin and smoke will steamroll it. Serve Pinotage around 16-18°C, not warmer, since heat exaggerates both the alcohol and any residual banana ester notes from fermentation.
