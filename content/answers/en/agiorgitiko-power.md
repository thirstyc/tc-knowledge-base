Why does Agiorgitiko taste so structured and powerful?

Agiorgitiko is Greece's most planted red grape, centered on Nemea in the Peloponnese, and it gets its backbone from a mix of climate and soil. Nemea sits at higher elevation with cooler nights, which preserves acidity even as the grapes ripen fully under the Greek sun. The soils, often clay and limestone, add minerality and encourage moderate yields, concentrating flavor in the skins where tannin comes from. Despite the name suggesting toughness, Agiorgitiko's tannins are actually medium and velvety rather than aggressive, more like a plush Merlot than a grippy Nebbiolo. What you're tasting as 'powerful' is really a combination of dark fruit intensity, a savory herbal edge, and structure that supports aging without punishing the palate. Producers increasingly use French oak aging to add further depth, layering vanilla and spice over the core of cherry and plum. It's a grape built for both easy drinking and serious cellaring, depending on how it's made.
