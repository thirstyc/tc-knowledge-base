What pairs with Chardonnay?

Chardonnay's weight and texture, whether from lees contact, oak, or both, mean it needs food with enough richness to meet it halfway, otherwise the wine just steamrolls the plate. Roast chicken works because the fat and browned skin echo the wine's own roundness. Lobster or halibut with a butter or cream sauce is close to a classic pairing because the acidity in the wine cuts through the fat while the texture matches. Cream-based sauces generally are a safe bet for the same reason. Where Chardonnay struggles is with anything delicate and raw, like sashimi, since oaked versions especially will just flatten those subtle flavors. If you're working with an unoaked, high-acid style, closer to Chablis, you can go lighter, shellfish or simply grilled fish, and let that mineral edge do the work instead of butter. Serve it around 10-12°C. Any warmer and the alcohol and oak start to dominate; any colder and you lose the texture that made you order it in the first place.
