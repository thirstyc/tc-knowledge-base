Are natural wines worth the hype?

Some are excellent, some are faulty, and the category as a whole is neither a revolution nor a scam. It's worth trying with an open mind and a critical palate.

The case for: at its best, natural wine tastes vivid, energetic and distinctly of somewhere. Low-intervention winemaking can preserve character that heavy processing smooths away, and the movement has pushed the whole industry toward better farming and more transparency.

The case against: the absence of rules means quality varies wildly. Faults like mousiness, excessive volatile acidity and unwanted refermentation appear more often than in conventional wine, and some drinkers are told these are features rather than flaws.

A fair middle position: natural is a method, not a quality rating. A well-made natural wine is a joy; a badly made one is a bad wine, whatever its philosophy.

How to try it without disappointment: buy from a shop or bar that tastes what it sells, start with lighter, fresher styles like chillable reds and pét-nats, and don't be talked into liking something you don't.

The best natural wines don't need the label as an excuse.
