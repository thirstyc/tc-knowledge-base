What pairs with Uruguayan Tannat?

Grilled beef is the obvious match and for good reason — Uruguay's asado culture built this wine's reputation, and Tannat's dense tannin needs the fat and char of a long-cooked short rib or flank steak to soften into something drinkable rather than gripping. The tannin binds to the fat on your palate, smooths out, and what's left is dark fruit and a smoky, savoury finish that plays right into the meat. Aged cheese works on the same principle — hard, salty cheeses like a well-aged Manchego or aged cheddar have enough fat and umami to tame the structure. Charcuterie, especially anything cured and fatty like chorizo or dry sausage, does the same job on a smaller scale. What you want to avoid is anything delicate: Tannat will flatten a salad or overwhelm plain grilled fish, since there's nothing on the plate to absorb the tannin. Serve around 16 to 18 degrees, and give a young bottle a decant if you can — an hour of air rounds off the roughest edges.
