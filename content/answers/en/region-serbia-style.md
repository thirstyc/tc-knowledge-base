Why does Serbian red taste so structured?

Continental climate in Vojvodina — cold winters down to -20°C, summers pushing 35°C — forces vines to build thick skins as protection, and thick skins mean more tannin and colour compounds get extracted during fermentation. That's the structural backbone you taste: firm, sometimes chewy tannin and deep colour, not the plush fruit-forward stuff you get from moderate maritime climates. Add in Šumadija's hillier terrain and its heritage grape, Prokupac — an old, tannic red variety grown there for centuries — and you get wines with genuine rustic character: earthy, savoury, sometimes a little dusty on the finish, closer to old-school Nemea or rustic Languedoc than to anything glossy. This isn't a flaw, it's terroir talking without international-varietal polish. Serve slightly cool, around 16-17°C, and give it air — an hour in a decanter softens the tannin — then pair with grilled meats or stews that can stand up to the structure. This isn't a wine for sipping alone on a patio; it wants a plate of something substantial.
