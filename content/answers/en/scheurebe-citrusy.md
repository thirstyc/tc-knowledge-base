Why does Scheurebe taste so citrusy and herbal?

Scheurebe is a German crossing usually traced back to Riesling, and it inherited Riesling's acid backbone along with something more overtly tropical. The grapefruit and blackcurrant-leaf character comes from the same thiol compounds responsible for Sauvignon Blanc's punch, but Scheurebe layers in more terpene-driven perfume, so it often reads as louder and more exotic, sometimes bordering on passionfruit or black currant rather than just citrus and grass. It's grown mainly in cooler German regions like the Pfalz and Rheinhessen, where slow ripening keeps acidity high even as sugar and aroma compounds build, which is why it can go bone dry or off-dry and still taste vibrant rather than flabby. Serve it cold, 8 to 10°C, colder if it's on the sweeter side to keep it from feeling cloying. It's a strong match for Asian and spiced food, goat cheese, and anything that would otherwise bully a more delicate white off the table.
