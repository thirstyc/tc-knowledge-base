Why Is Zinfandel So Jammy and High Alcohol?

Zinfandel ripens unevenly, one cluster can have shriveled raisiny berries next to plump ones, which means growers often wait for the stragglers to catch up, and by then the ripe berries have accumulated a lot of sugar. Ferment that sugar fully and you get high alcohol, often 14.5% and up, sometimes pushing past 16%. That alcohol reads on the palate as warmth and weight, and it amplifies the perception of ripe, jammy fruit, think blackberry jam and baked plum rather than fresh berries. California's warm days push sugar accumulation hard, and a lot of Zinfandel is also grown in old, dry-farmed vineyards where low yields concentrate flavour further. None of this is subtle, and it's not meant to be. This is a wine built for barbecue and bold spice, not delicate food, and it wants to be served slightly cooler than red wine default, around 16-17°C, to keep the alcohol from feeling hot. Not elegant, but genuinely fun, and there's nothing wrong with that.
