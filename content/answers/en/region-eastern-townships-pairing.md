What pairs with Eastern Townships Frontenac?

Frontenac from the Townships is a hybrid grape bred for cold survival, and that heritage shows up as high acid and a bright, almost cranberry-cherry fruit character rather than deep, brooding fruit. That acidity is exactly why it works with rich, fatty Quebec food: poutine's gravy and cheese curds, duck confit, aged cow's milk cheeses. The acid cuts through fat and resets your palate for the next bite instead of leaving a greasy film. Serve it lightly chilled, around 14-15°C, cooler than you would a typical red, because that acid needs the temperature to stay lively rather than tip into sharpness. Avoid pairing it with anything delicate or sweet-fruited, it will run right over subtle dishes. Think hearty, fatty, salty; this is a food wine first and a sipping wine second, born from a climate too cold for classic vinifera to ripen reliably, and made to stand up to a table, not a tasting room.
