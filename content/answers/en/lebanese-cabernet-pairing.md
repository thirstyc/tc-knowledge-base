What pairs with Lebanese Cabernet?

Lebanese Cabernet Sauvignon, mostly grown up in the Bekaa Valley at real altitude, gets a strange combination of hot, intense daytime sun and cool nights. That swing pushes sugar and tannin ripeness way up while the acidity holds on better than you'd expect from the heat alone, which is why these wines tend to land big, dark-fruited, tannic and often quite high in alcohol. That structure needs food with equal weight and some char or fat to soften the tannins, think grilled lamb, kafta, kebabs, anything off live coals. The smoky, slightly bitter edge from grilling mirrors the wine's own roasted, spice-box notes, and traditional Lebanese seasoning, cumin, allspice, sumac, cinnamon, actually plays well with Cabernet's dark fruit and pepper. Fat from lamb also tames the tannin, making the wine feel smoother than it does on its own. Serve it around 16 to 18 degrees, a bit cooler than room temperature, so the alcohol doesn't dominate the glass.
