What pairs with Turkish Boğazkere?

Boğazkere means 'throat-scratcher' for a reason: it's thick-skinned and tannin-heavy, with the drying grip of an underripe black tea. That structure needs fat and char to soften it, which is exactly what grilled lamb kebabs bring — the charred crust and rendered fat coat the palate and blunt the tannin before it can grab your gums. Spiced meatballs work the same way, but the cumin and pepper also echo the wine's own baking-spice notes from oak aging. Aged kashar, a firm sheep's-milk cheese, adds salt and protein, which binds to tannin molecules and makes the wine taste rounder and less austere. Serve it around 16-18°C — any warmer and the alcohol and tannin both turn aggressive. Avoid delicate or acidic dishes; this wine will flatten anything that doesn't have some weight and grease to push back against it. It's built for smoke and lamb fat, not lemon and herbs.
