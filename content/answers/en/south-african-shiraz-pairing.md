What pairs with South African Shiraz?

South African Shiraz tends to carry ripe dark fruit, firm tannin, and a peppery lift, all of which make it built for smoke and char. Grilled and barbecued meats work because the wine's tannins bind with the proteins and fat, softening on the palate while the char echoes the wine's own charred, savoury notes from oak or sun-ripened skins. Spicy food works for a related but different reason, the pepper compound in the wine (rotundone) actually harmonises with chilli heat rather than fighting it, so a peppery boerewors or a spiced lamb dish feels like an extension of the glass rather than a contrast. Avoid delicate or acid-driven dishes, the wine's weight and tannin will flatten them. Serve slightly below room temperature, around 16 to 18°C, since South African Shiraz often runs high in alcohol and warmth exaggerates that heat. A short decant, thirty minutes or so, helps loosen the tannin on younger bottles.
