Why is Châteauneuf-du-Pape so expensive?

Châteauneuf-du-Pape was among the very first wine regions in France to get formal appellation protection, back in the 1930s, and the rules that came with that status were deliberately restrictive: capped yields per hectare, a minimum alcohol threshold, mandatory hand harvesting in many cases, and sorting requirements that push growers to discard subpar fruit before it ever reaches the press. All of that limits how much wine any single producer can legally make, and it can't be scaled up the way high-yield regions can. Add genuinely old vines, some of which have been in the ground for decades and produce naturally small, concentrated yields on their own, and you get a structural ceiling on supply that predates any modern hype. Add on top of that the fact that a handful of estates get scored highly by international critics, and the mix of real scarcity, established prestige and consumer demand does the rest. It's not artificial scarcity, but it is scarcity by design.
