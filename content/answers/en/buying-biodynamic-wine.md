Best biodynamic wine?

Biodynamic wine comes from vineyards farmed organically with additional practices based on the ideas of Rudolf Steiner from the 1920s: special compost preparations, treating the farm as a self-sustaining ecosystem, and timing work to a lunar and astronomical calendar.

Look for certification from Demeter, the main international biodynamic certifier, or from Biodyvin, common in France.

Many highly regarded producers farm biodynamically, particularly in Burgundy, the Loire, Alsace, the Rhône and Champagne, and increasingly in Germany, Austria, Italy, Oregon and New Zealand.

Some of the practices, like burying cow horns filled with manure or following the lunar calendar, are scientifically contested, and there's little evidence for their specific effects. What's harder to dispute is that biodynamic farming demands intense attention to the vineyard, and attention tends to produce better grapes.

So the practical view: biodynamic certification tells you a producer farms very carefully and without synthetic chemicals. It doesn't guarantee a great wine, and many excellent non-biodynamic producers farm just as carefully.

As with organic, judge the wine in the glass. The certification is useful information, not a quality score.
