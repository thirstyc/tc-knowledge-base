What actually is Tocai from Friuli, and why does everyone argue about the name?

Tocai Friulano is a white grape from Friuli-Venezia Giulia, in Italy's northeast corner near Slovenia. Genetically it has nothing to do with Hungarian Tokaji, which is a sweet wine made from Furmint and other grapes. The EU forced Italy to drop the name Tocai in 2007 to avoid confusion with the Hungarian product, so bottles made after that point say Friulano instead. Same grape, same vineyards, just a relabeling. The grape itself gives a wine that's medium-bodied, dry, with a texture that often reads slightly waxy or almond-like, plus a savory, almost bitter finish on the back of the palate that's actually the calling card of the variety, not a flaw. It grows well in the gravelly, well-draining soils of Friuli's hills, which push the vine to work harder and concentrate flavor without piling on sugar. Don't expect fruit-bomb enthusiasm here. It's a quieter wine that rewards attention at the table rather than showing off in the glass.
