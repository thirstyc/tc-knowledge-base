What pairs with Okanagan Merlot or Riesling?

The Okanagan Valley is a semi-arid desert climate wedged between mountains, with hot days and cool nights that let grapes ripen fully while still holding onto acidity, so both the Merlot and the Riesling come out riper and rounder than their cooler-climate counterparts but not flabby. Merlot here tends toward soft, dark-fruited tannins with enough structure to stand up to fat, which is why grilled salmon works so well, the char and richness of the fish match the wine's body while the tannin cleans your palate between bites. Roast duck does the same job with an added layer, duck fat needs a wine with acid and tannin to cut through it, and Okanagan Merlot usually has both in balance. Riesling, drier and more citrus-driven from this region than its German cousins, is the better call with soft cheese, the acid slices through creaminess the way a squeeze of lemon would. Serve the Merlot around 16-17°C and the Riesling chilled, 8-10°C.
