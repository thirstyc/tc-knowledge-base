What pairs with Plavac Mali?

Plavac Mali comes from Croatia's Dalmatian coast, grown on brutally steep, sun-baked terraces like those at Dingač, and it's a genetic offspring of the same grape family that gave us Zinfandel. That parentage shows up in the glass: high alcohol, dense dark fruit, and tannin that can feel downright chewy in its youth. That structure is why it demands food with real fat and char rather than anything delicate. Grilled lamb, octopus cooked over coals, or peka-style slow-roasted meat all give the tannins something to grip and soften against, and the char specifically mirrors the wine's smoky, sun-baked intensity. A leaner dish just leaves the tannin feeling aggressive and the alcohol exposed. Aged hard cheeses work too, for the same reason, fat cuts tannin. Serve it around 16-18°C, slightly cooler than room temperature, since Plavac Mali's alcohol can taste hot if it's too warm. This isn't a wine for salads or white fish, it's built for the grill.
