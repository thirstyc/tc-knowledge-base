Why does Hárslevelű fly under the radar?

Tokaji Aszú gets to represent the region -- the sweet, botrytized wine collectors chase and sommeliers namecheck -- so most people never learn there's a dry sibling. Hárslevelű is actually one of Tokaj's two principal grapes alongside Furmint, and outside the sweet blends it makes a floral, waxy-textured dry white with real character: linden blossom, ripe stone fruit, decent acid, and a broader palate than most people expect from Hungarian whites. Almost none of it leaves the country, though. Domestic drinkers know it as an everyday white; producers don't bother exporting something international markets have no category for. Add a name most non-Hungarian speakers stumble over, and you've got a grape that will likely stay a well-kept local secret for a while yet. Worth tracking down if you find a bottle -- it drinks nothing like the Aszú its own region is famous for.
