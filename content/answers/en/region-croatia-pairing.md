What pairs with Croatian Plavac Mali?

This isn't a wine for subtlety, so don't pair it with subtlety. Plavac Mali carries high alcohol (often 14-16%), dense tannin and low acidity, which means it needs fat, char and salt to show well rather than delicate herbs or citrus. Grilled octopus works because the smoky char and olive oil mirror the wine's own sun-baked intensity without fighting it. Lamb peka, cooked slowly under a cast-iron dome with vegetables, gives you the tender fat the tannins are built to grip. Aged sheep cheese, especially something crumbly and salty like Pag, cuts through the wine's weight and resets your palate for the next sip. Serve it around 16-18°C, slightly cooler than room temperature, and give it air, a young Plavac Mali can be closed and stern straight from the bottle. Avoid anything light, raw or acid-driven; it will just make the wine taste hot and clumsy.
