Wine with Brie?

Champagne or Chardonnay. Brie is soft, creamy and buttery, and it wants a wine with enough acidity to cut through that richness.

Champagne is a classic partner. Brie comes from just outside Paris, not far from the Champagne region, and the bubbles and acidity cleanse the palate after every bite. Other sparkling wines, like Crémant or English sparkling wine, work just as well.

Chardonnay is another natural match. A crisp Chablis cuts through the cream, while a lightly oaked white Burgundy echoes the buttery texture.

Prefer red? Keep it light and low in tannin. Pinot Noir and Beaujolais work; big, tannic reds can taste bitter and metallic against the rind.

Baked Brie, often served with jam, honey or nuts, is sweeter and richer. An off-dry Riesling, a Gewürztraminer or a fruity Beaujolais handles those flavours better than dry whites.

Truffle Brie pairs especially well with Champagne or a Pinot Noir.

Brie with fruit, like grapes, figs or pears, works well with Chenin Blanc or a light sparkling wine.

What to avoid: heavy, oaky reds and very tannic wines. They overpower the delicate cheese and clash with the rind.
