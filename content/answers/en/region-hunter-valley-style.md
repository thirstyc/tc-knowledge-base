Why does Hunter Valley Semillon taste so lean young and honeyed old?

Winemakers pick Hunter Semillon early, well before the grapes reach full flavour ripeness, specifically to keep sugar and eventual alcohol low, often around 10.5 to 11%, and acidity high. There's no oak, no malolactic fermentation, just a clean stainless steel ferment that locks in that lean, citrusy, almost neutral profile. Young, it can taste like little more than lemon rind and green apple. But that high acid acts as a preservative, and over years in bottle the wine slowly develops toast, honey, hay and lanolin characters through non-enzymatic browning reactions, similar to what you'd expect from oak aging, except none ever happened. It's essentially aging itself from the inside out. That's the paradox: a wine engineered to taste plain on release is actually engineered for a second act, and the acid that makes it austere young is the same acid that lets it survive long enough to get interesting.
