Why does Icewine taste so sweet and concentrated?

The concentration comes from freezing, not rot, that's the common mix-up. Unlike Sauternes or Tokaji, which rely on botrytis (noble rot) to shrivel grapes and concentrate sugars, Icewine grapes are left on the vine into deep winter and picked while frozen solid. When they're pressed, the water inside stays locked as ice crystals and gets left behind, so only a small amount of intensely sweet, acid-rich syrup is extracted. That's why the wine tastes so concentrated: what you're drinking is essentially the sugar and acid of a normal grape crop, minus most of the water. The high acidity is what keeps it from tasting cloying, it balances the sugar rather than fighting it. Serve it cold, around 8 to 10 degrees, in a small glass, and expect flavors of dried apricot, honey, and citrus peel rather than jammy fruit. It's a sipping wine, not something to pour by the glassful.
