Why does Marlborough Riesling fly under the radar?

Sauvignon Blanc turned Marlborough into arguably the most recognizable regional wine brand in the world, and that success is so total that it crowds out everything else grown there, including a Riesling that deserves real attention. The same stony, free-draining soils and cool climate that give Sauvignon Blanc its zip also suit Riesling extremely well, producing wines with pronounced lime and green-apple fruit, a mineral edge, and often a touch of residual sugar balanced by serious acidity. But retailers and restaurants build their New Zealand sections around Sauvignon Blanc because that's what sells, so Riesling gets a token slot at best, and most drinkers never think to look for it. It's a shame, because the style Marlborough produces, somewhere between bone-dry and off-dry, ages gracefully and offers a genuine alternative to German or Alsatian Riesling at a friendlier price. If you only know the region for one grape, you're missing a wine that quietly punches above its reputation.
