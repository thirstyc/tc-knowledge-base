What makes Oregon Pinot Gris taste different from Italian Pinot Grigio?

Same grape, different personality, and the reason is climate plus intent. Willamette Valley sits at a cooler latitude with long, mild growing seasons and volcanic or sedimentary soils that hang onto water unevenly, stressing the vine just enough to build flavour without cooking off acidity. Oregon winemakers also tend to let the grape ripen a bit further and sometimes leave it on lees or skins briefly, so you get more weight, a touch of pear and honeysuckle, and a rounder mouthfeel than the lean, almost neutral Italian style built for speed and freshness. Italian Pinot Grigio is often picked early and processed fast for a crisp, forgettable everyday pour. Oregon treats the same grape like it has something to say. Neither is more correct, they're just answering different questions. If you want a wine to sip while thinking about nothing, go Italian. If you want one that holds up next to actual food and rewards a little attention, Oregon is the move.
