Why does Finger Lakes Riesling taste so crisp and fruity?

The Finger Lakes sits at roughly the same latitude as the Rhone, but the deep glacial lakes change everything. They act as thermal batteries, absorbing summer heat and releasing it slowly through autumn, which lets riesling hang on the vine longer without cooking in the sun. Nights still get properly cold, so the grape keeps its malic acid instead of burning it off the way it would in a hot climate. That's the mechanism behind the crispness: acid retention, not some vague coastal magic. The fruit character (green apple, lime, sometimes a petrol note as it ages) comes from riesling's naturally aromatic oils, thin skins and low sugar accumulation relative to acid. Most producers ferment cool and slow, often leaving a touch of residual sugar to balance that acid rather than fight it, which is why a lot of Finger Lakes riesling reads as fruity rather than austere. Serve it at 8-10°C, cold enough to keep the acid feeling bright rather than sharp. Drink it young for the fruit, or hold five to ten years if you want the honeyed, mineral side to develop.
