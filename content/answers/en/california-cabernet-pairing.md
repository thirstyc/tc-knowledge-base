What pairs with California Cabernet?

California Cabernet is built around ripeness: warm-climate growing seasons push sugar levels up, which means alcohol often lands around 14.5 to 15.5 percent, well above a typical Bordeaux, and tannins ripen fully rather than staying green and astringent. That combination of heat, alcohol, and plush tannin means the wine needs food with real fat and protein to balance it, otherwise it can taste hot and heavy on its own. Ribeye and other well-marbled cuts are the classic match, since the fat coats the palate and softens the tannin's grip, but lamb, short ribs, and aged cheddar work for the same reason. Char from grilling is a bonus, it echoes the toasted oak most of these wines see during aging. What doesn't work is anything light, delicate, or acid-driven, a green salad or a squeeze of citrus will make the wine taste clumsy and overheated by comparison. Serve around 18°C, slightly below typical room temperature, so the alcohol doesn't dominate the nose.
