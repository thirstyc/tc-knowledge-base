What pairs with Brunello?

Brunello di Montalcino is Sangiovese at its most concentrated, high acid and firm tannin built up through extended maceration and years of aging, so it needs food with enough richness and structure to match it. Grilled meats are the obvious call, but this is really a wine for slow-cooked, deeply savory dishes: braised beef, wild boar, game birds, anything with a dark, reduced sauce. Truffle dishes are a classic match in Tuscany itself, the earthy, umami quality of the truffle echoing the wine's own dried-herb and forest-floor notes that develop with bottle age. Aged hard cheeses work well too, pecorino especially, since the fat and salt soften the tannin. Skip anything delicate or lightly sauced, it will get steamrolled. Serve at 16 to 18 degrees, and decant an hour or two beforehand if the bottle is young, the tannins need the air to loosen up before they're pleasant rather than punishing.
