What pairs with Tokaji?

Tokaji Aszú's whole trick is that its sugar arrives with equally aggressive acidity, courtesy of noble rot (botrytis cinerea) that shrivels Furmint grapes on the vine and concentrates both sugar and acid at once. That's why it doesn't sit on the palate like syrup, it has a spine. Foie gras works because the fat needs a sweet counterweight and the acid keeps it from feeling greasy; the pairing predates most modern wine-matching logic by centuries. Blue cheese runs on the same principle: salt and funk need sugar to balance, and the acidity keeps the match from turning cloying. Fruit tarts are the safe, obvious match, but the wine is interesting enough to also handle something savory and a bit aggressive: hard aged cheeses, spiced nuts, dishes with a touch of chili. Serve it cool, around 10-12°C, not fridge-cold, so the aromatics, dried apricot, honey, orange peel, actually show. Small pours; this is a wine you sip, not drink.
