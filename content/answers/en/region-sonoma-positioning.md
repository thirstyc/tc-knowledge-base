Why does Sonoma feel less flashy than Napa?

Napa built its identity on one thing done extremely well - Cabernet, cult pricing, a single famous valley you can drive end to end in under an hour. Sonoma is nearly three times the planted acreage, fractured into sub-regions with almost nothing in common: cool, fog-drenched Russian River Valley making Pinot and Chardonnay, warmer inland pockets doing Zinfandel and Cabernet, coastal Petaluma Gap getting hit by wind straight off the Pacific. That sprawl works against a tidy marketing story, but it means real diversity instead of one grape wearing a famous label. Growers here have historically sold fruit to négociants and built reputations slowly rather than chasing allocation lists and triple-digit price tags, so the culture leans toward viticulture over branding. You're paying for what's in the glass, not a name recognized at dinner parties. Same maritime fog, same coastal cooling that makes Napa's western hillsides work - just without the decades of hype inflating the price. Sonoma is the harder story to tell and the easier bottle to enjoy on a Tuesday.
