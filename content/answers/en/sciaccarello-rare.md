Why is Sciaccarello hard to find outside Corsica?

The island keeps most of its wine for itself, and this grape barely grows anywhere else. Corsica's total vineyard area is small to begin with, and Sciaccarello is concentrated in a couple of pockets, mainly around Ajaccio, where it's the signature grape. Tourism absorbs a large share of what's produced, since visitors drink it on-site during the season, leaving little incentive or surplus to build export markets. The grape itself is also part of the problem: it's fussy about where it's planted, wanting that specific granite soil and elevation to perform, so growers elsewhere haven't had much reason or success trying to replicate it. No large negociants have pushed it internationally the way they have with more marketable grapes, so it never built distribution outside France. A true regional specialty in the strictest sense, more like a local dish than a commodity wine, and finding a bottle abroad usually means someone imported it in small volume on purpose.
