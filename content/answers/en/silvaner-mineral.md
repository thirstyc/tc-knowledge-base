Why does Silvaner taste so dry and mineral?

Silvaner isn't actually a high-acid grape in the way Riesling is—its real trick is that it's aromatically quiet. It carries very little of the terpene content that gives grapes like Riesling or Gewürztraminer their perfume, so there's nothing loud standing between you and the vineyard. In Franconia, where the best versions come from, vines are planted on Muschelkalk (shell limestone) and Keuper soils, and that stony, saline character comes through unobscured because the fruit isn't shouting over it. Winemakers reinforce this by fermenting cool in stainless steel and finishing fully dry (trocken), with no oak to soften or sweeten the picture. The result reads as dry, taut and mineral—less about acid backbone and more about transparency. It's a wine that shows its address rather than its grape. Serve it around 8-10°C; any warmer and the delicate stony quality flattens out into something forgettably neutral.
