Why does Chianti taste so accessible and food-friendly?

Because Sangiovese is built for the table rather than for the glass.

Three things do the work. High acidity, which makes your mouth water and resets the palate between bites — the same reason a squeeze of lemon lifts a dish. Moderate tannin, firm enough to handle fat but not so grippy that it bullies delicate food. And alcohol that usually sits between 12.5 and 13.5%, low enough that a second glass over dinner doesn't flatten you.

The flavour runs savoury rather than sweet: sour cherry, dried herbs, tomato leaf, a little earth and leather as it ages. Nothing jammy or heavily oaked getting between you and the meal.

That's why Chianti is the default red on Italian wine lists worldwide, and why it so rarely feels like a gamble. It isn't trying to be the most interesting thing on the table. It's trying to make the meal work, which is a harder trick and a more useful one.
