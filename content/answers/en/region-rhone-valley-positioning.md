Why does the Rhône matter?

The Rhône splits into two very different halves that both happen to matter enormously. In the north, steep granite terraces around Hermitage and Côte-Rôtie produce Syrah on its own, structured and peppery, some of the longest-lived reds France makes. Head south and Châteauneuf-du-Pape does the opposite: appellation rules there famously permit over a dozen grape varieties, and blending Grenache, Syrah, Mourvèdre and others became not a compromise but the whole point, proof that a well-judged blend can command the same collector prices and cellar potential as any single-varietal wine from Burgundy or Bordeaux. Vines have grown here since Roman times, and the region's iconic galets, those large round stones covering Châteauneuf's vineyards, store daytime heat and release it overnight to help ripen grapes through cooler evenings. Between the two ends, the Rhône stands as France's third great red region, distinct in method and philosophy from its more famous neighbors but every bit as serious.
