What is Bordeaux wine?

Bordeaux is a large wine region in southwest France, best known for structured red blends of Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, plus dry and sweet whites.

The Gironde estuary splits the region in two. On the Left Bank, gravel soils suit Cabernet Sauvignon, giving firm, age-worthy wines. On the Right Bank, clay and limestone suit Merlot, giving rounder, softer ones.

Bordeaux is also where the idea of wine as a luxury investment took hold. The 1855 classification ranked Left Bank châteaux into five growths, with the first growths at the top, and those rankings still shape prices today.

But the region is enormous, and most Bordeaux is not expensive. Bottles labelled simply Bordeaux or Bordeaux Supérieur, or from appellations like Fronsac, Castillon or the Médoc, offer the same grapes and style at everyday prices.

Whites come from Sémillon and Sauvignon Blanc, made dry in Pessac-Léognan and Entre-Deux-Mers, and sweet in Sauternes, where noble rot concentrates the grapes.

Expect blackcurrant, cedar, graphite and firm tannins in the reds. Serve them with lamb, beef or hard cheese, and give young, serious bottles time or a decanter.
