Why does South African Shiraz fly under the radar?

South Africa built its red-wine reputation on Cabernet and Pinotage, so Shiraz gets treated as an afterthought even though the Swartland and Stellenbosch versions can be excellent. Old bush vines, many dry-farmed, force roots deep for water, which concentrates flavour without pushing sugar and alcohol too high. The result sits between the fruit-forward style of Barossa Shiraz and the peppery restraint of the Rhone's Syrah: dark plum and blackberry, but also white pepper, cured meat and dried herbs from cooler nights and granite or shale soils. Winemakers here often use less new oak than their Cabernet, letting the grape's savoury side show instead of stacking it with vanilla. Because the country's marketing dollars go toward Chenin Blanc and the Big Five reds, these bottles stay underpriced for the quality in the glass. Decant an hour before drinking and serve around 16-17°C. If you like Northern Rhone Syrah but want a break from the price tag, this is exactly where to look.
