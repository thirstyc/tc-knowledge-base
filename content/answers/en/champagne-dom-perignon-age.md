Can you age Dom Pérignon?

Yes, and it's built specifically for it. Dom Pérignon is only made in years the house considers strong enough, it's a vintage-only wine, and before release it spends an unusually long time aging on its lees, well beyond what most Champagne gets. That extended contact with spent yeast cells builds structure and a reserve of compounds that keep developing after disgorgement, so the wine has both the acidity and the depth to keep evolving for 20 years or more in a good cellar. Young, it shows citrus, white flowers and a chalky mineral edge; with a decade or two it shifts toward honey, toasted hazelnut, brioche and dried fruit, while the mousse becomes finer and more integrated. The main risk isn't holding it too long, it's storing it badly, heat and light degrade Champagne fast regardless of pedigree. Keep it lying down, cool and dark, and consistent. Whether it functions as an investment is a separate question from whether it improves in the glass, it genuinely does, but buy it to drink, not just to watch appreciate.
