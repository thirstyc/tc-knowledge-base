Why is Bobal expensive outside Spain?

It's barely exported, so what little reaches other markets tends to be the top, age-worthy tier: producers picking their best old-vine parcels, aging in oak, bottling small runs meant to prove Bobal can be serious rather than just bulk blending material. Domestic Bobal, by contrast, is often cheap, young, and made for everyday drinking around Requena and Utiel-Requena, where it's a workhorse grape used in huge volume for local consumption and blending. Exporters aren't shipping container loads of that everyday stuff; the economics don't justify it for a grape most foreign buyers have never heard of. So the Bobal that lands on a shelf in Montreal or Paris has usually already survived a selection process at the winery, low yields, older vines, longer aging, before it ever crosses an ocean. You're not paying a premium for the grape's reputation, since it barely has one outside Spain; you're paying for the fact that only the ambitious bottlings get exported at all.
