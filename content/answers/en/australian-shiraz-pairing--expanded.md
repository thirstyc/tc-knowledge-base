What pairs with Australian Shiraz?

Australian Shiraz, especially the Barossa style, is built around ripe dark fruit, soft-ish tannins from warm-climate phenolic ripeness, and often a good hit of American oak giving vanilla and coconut notes. That combination wants fat and char rather than delicacy. Charred red meat works because the Maillard compounds on a seared steak or lamb chop echo the toasty oak, while the fat coats your palate and needs the wine's tannin and alcohol to cut through it and reset your mouth. Rich barbecue sauces, with their sugar and smoke, match the wine's own sweetness of fruit (not residual sugar, just ripeness) without being overwhelmed. Spiced dishes work too, but for a specific reason: some Shiraz carries a compound called rotundone, which reads as black pepper, and it plays nicely against dishes with actual pepper or warm spice rather than chilli heat, which just amplifies the alcohol. Avoid pairing it with anything delicate or acid-driven, like fish or salads, the wine will flatten them completely. Serve it a touch below room temperature, around 16-18°C, so the alcohol doesn't dominate.
