What pairs with Georgian wine?

Georgian wine was built around a table, not a tasting room, so it pairs best with the food it grew up next to. Khachapuri, that molten cheese-stuffed bread, needs a wine with enough acid and grip to cut through the fat, which is exactly what amber qvevri whites deliver thanks to extended skin contact. Grilled lamb wants the tannin and dark-fruit backbone of Saperavi, a grape naturally loaded with both. Pkhali, those walnut and vegetable pâtés, echo the nutty, oxidative notes that come from months fermenting on skins in buried clay vessels. Slow-braised stews, heavy on spice and fat, need that same structural tannin to reset the palate between bites. None of this is delicate food, and none of this is delicate wine. Serve reds around 16°C and ambers a touch cooler, closer to 12°C, so the tannins stay firm rather than flabby. It's rustic pairing logic: match weight to weight, texture to texture.
