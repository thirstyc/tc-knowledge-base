Why does Cortese taste so dry and mineral?

Cortese is a grape that just doesn't accumulate much sugar or phenolic weight, even when fully ripe, which is why it stays lean, dry and high in acid rather than turning round and fruity. It's grown mostly in the hills of southeastern Piedmont, in cooler sites near the Ligurian border where the grape can hang long enough to develop flavour without losing that acid backbone to heat. Because Cortese itself is fairly neutral aromatically, without a strong signature fruit character, whatever the vineyard and soil are doing comes through more clearly. That's the 'mineral' people taste: not literal rock flavour, but the chalky, saline, almost wet-stone impression that shows up when a wine has high acid, low sugar and not much else competing for attention. It's a texture and structure wine more than a fruit-forward one, closer in spirit to Muscadet than to a New World Sauvignon Blanc. Serve it properly cold, around 8-10°C, to keep that acidity feeling crisp rather than sharp.
