What wine goes with turkey?

A fruity, medium-bodied red like Pinot Noir or Beaujolais, or a rich white like Chardonnay. Turkey itself is lean and mild, so the wine mostly needs to handle everything else on the plate.

A turkey dinner usually comes with stuffing, gravy, cranberry sauce and a dozen sides. That's why flexible wines with good acidity and plenty of fruit work best.

Pinot Noir is the go-to red. It's light enough for the white meat, and its red-berry flavours match cranberry sauce.

Beaujolais crus like Morgon or Fleurie are juicy, low in tannin and easy to share, and they don't cost much, which helps with a big table.

Zinfandel is a traditional choice in North America. Its ripe fruit and spice suit dark meat, stuffing and rich gravy.

For white, a Chardonnay with some oak or a dry to off-dry Riesling works well, especially with sweet sides like squash or sweet potatoes.

Sparkling wine is a smart starter and keeps working through the meal.

What to avoid: big, tannic reds. They can make the turkey taste dry and clash with cranberry sauce.

Buying for a crowd? Pick two bottles, one red and one white, and let people choose.
