What pairs with Swartland Chenin Blanc or Syrah?

Swartland wines are built on texture and savoriness, not just fruit, so they want food with some fat, char or funk to meet them halfway. Grilled vegetables, especially anything with blackened skin, echo the smoky, wild-ferment character you get from indigenous yeast and minimal sulfur. Spiced lamb, think North African or Cape Malay spicing, works because the Syrah's peppery edge and the Chenin's texture can stand up to cumin and chili without getting flattened. Funky, washed-rind or aged cheeses match because both wine and cheese carry a certain barnyard complexity that would clash with something more polished. What you want to avoid is delicate food, poached fish or plain salads disappear against a wine this textured and forthright. Serve the whites around 10 to 12°C, not colder, since chilling too hard flattens the phenolic grip that makes Swartland Chenin interesting, and reds around 16°C so the wilder, earthier notes stay open rather than closing down.
