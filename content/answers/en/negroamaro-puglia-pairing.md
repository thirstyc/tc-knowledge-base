What pairs with Negroamaro?

Negroamaro literally translates as "black bitter," and that's the clue to how it works at the table. The grape brings dark plum and dried-herb fruit, moderate-to-high tannin, and a savory, faintly bitter finish that cuts through fat rather than fighting it. That's why it's built for grilled meats, sausages, lamb, and Puglian classics like orecchiette with cime di rapa or slow-cooked stews with tomato and peperoncino. The tannin grips the richness of the meat, the bitterness resets your palate between bites, and the fruit keeps things from tasting austere. Serve it around 16-18°C, slightly cooler than room temperature, so the tannin doesn't feel harsh. Avoid delicate dishes, fish, and anything cream-based, the wine will just steamroll them. It's a wine for smoke, char, and tomato, not finesse. If you want a single move, put a bottle beside a plate of grilled sausage and rapini, that pairing is basically what the grape was built for over centuries in Salento.
