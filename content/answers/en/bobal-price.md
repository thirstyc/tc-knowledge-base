Why is Bobal sometimes expensive?

Old, low-yielding vines in Spain produce concentrated fruit worth aging, and that's really the core of it. Vines planted decades ago naturally throw fewer, smaller bunches as they age, and lower yield means each grape carries more concentrated flavor and tannin because the vine's limited energy goes into fewer clusters instead of being spread thin. Many of Requena's Bobal vines are old by circumstance rather than design, the grape simply fell out of fashion for years, so nobody bothered replanting, and now those neglected old vines are suddenly an asset. Producers making serious wine from that fruit also tend to age it longer in oak or bottle, which costs money and time before a single bottle sells. Add to that the fact that premium Bobal bottlings are still genuinely rare, most of the grape still goes into cheap bulk wine or blends, and you get a small supply of well-made, structured wine chasing a slowly growing reputation. Scarcity plus real concentration equals a real price.
