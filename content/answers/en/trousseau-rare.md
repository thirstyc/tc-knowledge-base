Why is Trousseau hard to find outside Jura?

Trousseau is a headache to grow, which is the real reason it never left Jura. The skins are thin, it buds early and ripens late, so it's exposed to spring frost and autumn rot for the whole season, and yields are stingy even when everything goes right. Jura itself covers only a couple thousand hectares total, most of it planted to Chardonnay, Savagnin, and Poulsard, so there was never much room for a third finicky red variety to expand into. Growers who did bother tended to keep it for themselves rather than export it, which is part of why it built a cult following rather than a commercial one. Curiously, the same grape turns up in Portugal under the name Bastardo, used there in port and dry reds, proof that it's not that the vine can't travel, just that nobody outside a few obsessives cared to plant it elsewhere. Serve it lightly chilled, around 13-14°C, and it drinks like a cross between Pinot Noir and cranberry juice.
