What temperature to serve rosé?

Around 8–12°C. Light, pale rosés taste best at the colder end, while fuller, darker rosés show more flavour a little warmer.

By style:

- Pale, delicate rosé, like Provence: 8–10°C.
- Fruity, medium-bodied rosé: 10–12°C.
- Fuller, more structured rosé, like Tavel or Bandol: 12–13°C.
- Sparkling rosé: 6–10°C.

Too cold, and even a good rosé tastes of nothing but cold. Too warm, and it feels flabby and alcoholic.

Practical tips:

- Two to three hours in the fridge is about right for most rosé.
- If it's straight from the fridge at 4–5°C, let it sit for 10 minutes before pouring.
- Twenty minutes in an ice bucket with water chills a room-temperature bottle quickly.
- Outdoors in summer, keep the bottle in an ice bucket between pours, but let glasses warm slightly in your hand.

Ice cubes in rosé are common in the south of France on hot days. They dilute the wine, but for a simple, refreshing rosé by the pool, nobody minds.
