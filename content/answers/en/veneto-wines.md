What are Veneto wines?

The Veneto, in northeast Italy around Venice and Verona, produces more wine than almost any other Italian region, from everyday Pinot Grigio and Prosecco to prestigious Amarone.

The key wines:

- Prosecco: the famous sparkling wine, from the Glera grape. The hilly Conegliano Valdobbiadene zone makes the finest.
- Valpolicella: light, cherry-scented reds from Corvina and related grapes, grown in the hills north of Verona.
- Valpolicella Ripasso: Valpolicella refermented on the leftover skins of Amarone, giving more body and depth.
- Amarone della Valpolicella: rich, powerful red made from grapes dried for months before fermenting. Recioto is its sweet sibling.
- Soave: crisp, almond-edged whites from Garganega, especially from the Classico hills.
- Bardolino: light, fresh reds and Chiaretto rosé from the shores of Lake Garda.
- Pinot Grigio: much of Italy's volume comes from here.

The Veneto's range is wide because it spans flat, fertile plains, where most volume wines are grown, and cooler hillside zones that produce its best wines. Words like "Classico" or "Superiore" often point to the better sites.

At the table: Prosecco for aperitifs, Soave for seafood, Valpolicella for pasta and pizza, Amarone for braised meats and aged cheese.
