Why does Lebanese Cabernet taste so powerful?

Blame the altitude, not the stereotype of hot-climate wine. The Bekaa Valley sits around 1,000 metres above sea level, which means brutal daytime sun but genuinely cold nights. Grapes ripen fast and fully during the day, then the vine essentially pauses and locks in flavour and colour overnight instead of burning off acidity. That diurnal swing is why Lebanese Cabernet Sauvignon comes in dense, dark-fruited and structured, with real tannin and alcohol to match, but without turning into jam. The soils help too: limestone and gravel drain hard, stressing the vines just enough to concentrate the grapes rather than bloat them. So yes, it reads New World in the glass, all cassis, tobacco, and grip, but the mechanism is closer to a very sunny, very high vineyard than a hot, flat one. Decant it, serve around 17-18°C, and give it something fatty and grilled. Lamb kafta is not a coincidence pairing, it is basically the default.
