Why does Yarra Valley Chardonnay taste so elegant and mineral?

Credit the elevation. The Yarra sits in the Dandenong foothills outside Melbourne, with vineyards planted anywhere from 50 to over 400 metres, and that altitude means a real diurnal swing: warm days for ripening, cold nights that lock in acidity instead of letting it burn off. Grapes come in with sugar and acid balanced rather than sugar racing ahead. Add restrained oak (a lot of producers use older barrels or a light touch of new wood) and extended lees ageing, which builds texture without piling on flavour, and you get a wine that reads as taut and saline rather than tropical and blowsy. The 'mineral' note isn't literally rocks in the glass, nobody's proven that, it's the sensation acid and low fruit-sweetness create on the palate: a chalky, tightening finish. Serve it around 10-12°C, cellar-cold kills it. This is Chardonnay built for structure and ageing potential, not for maximum fruit up front, which is exactly why it reads as serious next to warmer-climate versions.
