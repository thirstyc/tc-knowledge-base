Why does Margaret River Cabernet taste so polished?

The polish comes from a climate that almost never lets the vine panic. Margaret River juts into the ocean between two bodies of water, so daytime heat gets tempered by consistent maritime breezes, meaning grapes ripen slowly and evenly instead of racing to sugar ripeness while tannins are still green. That slow hang time is what gives you tannins that feel fine-grained and integrated rather than gritty. The gravel-over-loam soils drain fast and are naturally low in vigor, so vines aren't gorging on water and diluting flavour, they're concentrating it. Structurally it leans on classic Cabernet backbone, cassis, graphite, a savoury herbal edge, with oak used judiciously rather than to mask anything. Serve it around 16 to 18°C, a touch cooler than room temperature, so the fruit stays lifted and the tannins don't feel hot. It's a young region by world standards, first vines went in during the 1960s, but the site was simply built for this grape.
