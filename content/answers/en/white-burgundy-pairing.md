What pairs with White Burgundy?

White Burgundy earns its reputation at the table because it operates on two axes at once: bright acidity and, depending on the appellation, real textural weight from lees ageing and partial oak. That combination is what makes it work with fish rather than fighting it. A simple Chablis or village Meursault, all steel and citrus-stone tension, cuts through raw oysters, scallops, or a lemony sole - the acid resets your palate the way lemon on the fish already does. Move up to a richer Meursault or Puligny-Montrachet with more lees-derived weight and a touch of oak, and the wine has enough body to sit alongside lobster, buttered turbot, or chicken in cream sauce without disappearing. Soft, washed-rind or bloomy cheeses work too - the acidity slices through the fat, and the wine's subtle nuttiness echoes the cheese's own. Avoid anything smoked or heavily spiced; it fights the wine's precision. Serve around 12-14°C - too cold and you mute the aromatics, leaving just tartness with nothing behind it.
