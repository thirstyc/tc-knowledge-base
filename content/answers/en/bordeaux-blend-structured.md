Why does Bordeaux Blend taste so structured and age-worthy?

The structure comes from the division of labor between the grapes. Cabernet Sauvignon has thick skins loaded with tannin and pigment, which gives the blend its backbone, firm grip, and aging potential, but on its own it can be austere and closed in youth. Merlot has thinner skins, ripens earlier, and fills in with soft, fleshy fruit and roundness, smoothing out Cabernet's edges. Cabernet Franc adds lift - aromatic, peppery, sometimes herbal notes - and a streak of bright acidity that keeps the whole thing from feeling heavy or flat. Winemakers adjust the proportions based on vintage and soil: gravelly Left Bank sites favor more Cabernet Sauvignon because it thrives on well-drained, warm soils, while the cooler clay of the Right Bank favors Merlot. None of the three grapes alone would give you this balance of power and polish; the blend is a deliberate hedge against any single grape's weaknesses. Serve at 16-18°C and give it time in the glass - this structure needs air to unwind.
