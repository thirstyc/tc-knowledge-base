Why does Campania deserve more attention?

Campania has been making serious wine since before Rome was an empire, and somehow it still gets treated as a footnote to Tuscany and Piedmont. Aglianico earns the nickname 'Barolo of the south' honestly — it has the tannin, the acid, and the ageing potential of Nebbiolo, just wrapped around darker, more brooding fruit. Fiano and Greco aren't simple summer whites either; done well, they develop nutty, honeyed complexity after five or ten years in bottle, which puts them in rare company among Italian whites. Some of these vineyards are still farmed on the same slopes the Greeks planted when they first brought vines to Italy, and a lot of that history survives because Campania was one of the few places phylloxera never fully wiped out. The wines are also still underpriced for what's in the glass, which is either an argument to buy them now or a sign nobody's paying attention yet. Both are true.
