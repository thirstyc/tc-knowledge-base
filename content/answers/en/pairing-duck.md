Best wine for duck?

Pinot Noir is the classic answer. Its acidity cuts through duck's rich fat, and its red-cherry, earthy flavours suit the slightly gamey meat.

Roast or pan-seared duck breast is where red Burgundy shines. Oregon and New Zealand Pinot Noir work too, with a little more fruit.

Duck with a fruit sauce, like cherry or plum, likes a riper red with matching fruit: a New World Pinot, a Beaujolais cru like Morgon, or even a Zinfandel.

Duck confit and cassoulet come from southwest France, and so do their best partners. Cahors, made from Malbec, and Madiran, made from Tannat, have the structure to stand up to all that fat and beans.

Duck à l'orange and Peking duck bring sweetness and spice. An off-dry Riesling or an Alsace Gewürztraminer handles those flavours better than most reds, and a juicy Pinot Noir is a good red option.

Smoked duck pairs well with Syrah or a northern Rhône red, whose smoky, peppery notes echo the meat.

What to avoid: very oaky, high-alcohol reds, which can make duck feel heavy, and delicate whites, which disappear next to it.
