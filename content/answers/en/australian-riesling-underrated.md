Why does Australian Riesling fly under the radar?

Australia built its international reputation on big, ripe Shiraz, so a bone-dry, low-alcohol white from a couple of small inland valleys never had a chance at the marketing budget. Clare Valley and Eden Valley, both in South Australia, have a specific combination that makes for extraordinary Riesling: high altitude and big diurnal temperature swings, meaning hot days and genuinely cold nights, which lets the grapes ripen in flavour while holding onto searing natural acidity. The wines are typically fermented bone dry with little to no oak, so nothing hides the fruit or the acid. The result is racy, lime-and-flint wines that age astonishingly well, developing toasty, honeyed complexity over a decade or more, not unlike the way great German or Austrian Riesling ages. Because the region never chased the same fame as Barossa Shiraz, prices stayed sane, so you're often getting a wine that could sit alongside serious European Riesling for a fraction of the cost. It's one of the genuine bargains left in fine wine.
