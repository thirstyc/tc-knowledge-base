What pairs with Similkameen Syrah or Riesling?

Similkameen Syrah runs peppery, dark-fruited and full-bodied thanks to that valley's huge diurnal swing — hot days build sugar and flavour, cold nights lock in acidity, so the wine ends up powerful but never flabby. That kind of structure needs food with weight and fat: grilled lamb, its own gamey richness meeting the wine's white-pepper spice, or spiced sausage where the char and fat cut through the tannin. Sharp aged cheese works too — acidity slices through the fat while the tannin grips the protein. Riesling here sits at the other end: high acid, often bone-dry to off-dry depending on the producer, with the same rocky-soil minerality running through it. That wants something with its own intensity to match — spicy Asian dishes, a good curry, salty cured fish — anything a shy wine would get steamrolled by. Serve the Riesling cold, 8-10°C, and the Syrah a touch below room temperature, 16-17°C, so the alcohol doesn't run hot. Either way: this is not sipping wine for delicate food, it wants a plate willing to argue back.
