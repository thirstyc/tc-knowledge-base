Why is Viognier so full-bodied for a white?

Viognier's body comes from what it lacks as much as what it has. It's naturally low in acid — none of the citrusy backbone of Riesling or Sauvignon Blanc — so nothing cuts through the fruit, and the wine reads as round and generous rather than lean. It also ripens late and packs on sugar fast, meaning growers who wait for the aromatics to develop often end up with alcohol pushing past 14%, and that alcohol registers on the palate as weight and warmth. Add naturally thick skins and a good load of glycerol, and you get a texture that's almost oily, closer to a peach halved and left to drip than a crisp apple. The classic descriptors — apricot, honeysuckle, beeswax — aren't just flavour notes, they're a clue to the whole structure: soft, aromatic, low-acid, high-alcohol. Serve it a touch cooler than you'd think, around 10-12°C, or that fullness tips into flabby. This isn't a wine to chill hard like a Sauvignon Blanc — you want the aromatics open but the alcohol kept in check.
