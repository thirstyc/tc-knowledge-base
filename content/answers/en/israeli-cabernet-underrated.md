Why does Israeli Cabernet fly under the radar?

Israel's wine reputation is still dragging around the ghost of sweet, syrupy kosher wine made for ritual use rather than pleasure, and that association is hard to shake even decades after the industry moved on. The real shift started in the early 1980s when growers began planting at high altitude on the Golan Heights, discovering that volcanic soil and cool nights up there could produce serious, age-worthy Cabernet and Bordeaux blends. Since then, wineries have brought in international consultants, invested in modern equipment, and started entering blind tastings where their wines hold their own against Napa and Bordeaux names. But the kosher label still leads people to assume sacramental sweetness, and Israel isn't part of the usual fine-wine conversation the way France, Italy or even Chile are, so the wines get overlooked by default rather than on merit. The quality is there; the marketing and habit just haven't caught up.
