What pairs with Marsala?

It depends which Marsala you've got, because the style swings from bone-dry to syrupy. Sweet Marsala (dolce) is built on fortified base wine sometimes blended with cooked-down must, giving it caramel, dried fig and toffee notes that echo straight into zabaglione and tiramisu, both of which are essentially eggy, sugared vehicles for that same flavour profile. It also has enough sweetness and weight to stand up to genuinely spicy food, where the sugar tempers heat the way a mango lassi does with a curry. Dry Marsala (secco) is a different animal entirely, closer to a light sherry: try it as an aperitif with salted almonds, aged pecorino or cured meats, where its nutty, oxidative character plays off salt and fat. And of course there's the kitchen use, deglazing pans for chicken or veal marsala, where the wine's acidity and caramelized sugar build a sauce almost by themselves. Serve sweeter styles around 12 to 14°C, dry ones closer to 10°C.
