Why does Braucol taste so dark and earthy?

Braucol is the local Southwest France name for Fer Servadou, and that name is a clue - "fer" means iron, a nod to both the grape's naturally high tannin (rigid, almost metallic in youth) and the iron-rich clay and limestone soils around Gaillac and Marcillac where it's most planted. Thick skins and small berries concentrate tannin and color, giving the wine its dense, brooding character, while the region's soil and relatively cool, hilly terrain push it toward dark fruit, black pepper, and a genuine iron or bloody minerality rather than the plush ripeness you'd get further south. It's rarely aged in heavy new oak, so what you taste is largely the grape and the ground it grew in, not a winemaking veneer. This isn't a wine for delicate food: it wants grilled or braised red meat, something with fat and char to soften that tannic grip. Serve around 16-17°C, and don't be put off by the initial austerity - give it a few minutes in the glass and the earthiness rounds out.
