Why does Valpolicella taste so light and fruity?

Basic Valpolicella is built to be drunk young and unserious, and that's by design, not accident. It's a blend from the Veneto, mainly Corvina with Rondinella and sometimes Molinara, grown on the hills around Verona, and for the everyday version the grapes go straight to fermentation without the extended skin contact or drying process that would extract more tannin and concentration. That's the key thing people miss: this is the same grape family used to make Amarone, which is dried for months before fermenting, turning that light, cherry-forward red into something dense, raisiny, and 15%+ alcohol. Regular Valpolicella skips all that, so you get bright sour-cherry and red-plum fruit, soft tannin, and a slightly bitter almond note on the finish, low enough in weight to serve lightly chilled, around 13°C, like a red trying to moonlight as a rosé. It's meant for weeknights, not cellars.
