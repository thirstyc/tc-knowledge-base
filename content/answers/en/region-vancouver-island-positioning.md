Why is Vancouver Island still a niche pick?

It's still niche because the industry is genuinely young and small, with growers only recently figuring out which grapes actually thrive in a cool, damp, maritime climate that doesn't behave like the Okanagan just a few hundred kilometres away. Ripening whites and aromatic varieties work better here than big reds, since heat accumulation is limited and rain during harvest is a real risk some years. What's generating real buzz is the island's potential for sparkling wine: the cool climate preserves acidity beautifully, which is exactly what traditional-method sparkling needs to age well and stay fresh rather than flabby. But total plantings are still tiny, production is limited, and quality is inconsistent producer to producer as everyone works out their vineyard sites and winemaking approach. Worth seeking out a bottle or two to see where things are headed, but not yet a region to build a cellar around. Give it five to ten more years before judging it fully.
