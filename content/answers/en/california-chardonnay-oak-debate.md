Why is California Chardonnay controversial?

The fight is really about two winemaking choices layered on top of each other: malolactic fermentation and oak. Full malolactic conversion softens sharp malic acid into creamier lactic acid and throws off diacetyl, the same compound that makes butter taste buttery. Combine that with aging in new French oak barrels, which add vanilla, toast and a rounder texture, and you get the big, buttery, popcorn-and-vanilla style that dominated California Chardonnay through the 1990s and 2000s. Plenty of drinkers loved it; plenty of sommeliers eventually rebelled, and the 'ABC: Anything But Chardonnay' backlash was really a backlash against that specific style, not the grape itself. Since then, a lot of California producers, especially in cooler spots like the Sonoma Coast or Santa Rita Hills, have pulled back on new oak and malolactic to make leaner, more Chablis-like versions. So the controversy isn't really about Chardonnay at all, it's about how heavily a winemaker leans on cellar technique versus letting the fruit and site speak.
