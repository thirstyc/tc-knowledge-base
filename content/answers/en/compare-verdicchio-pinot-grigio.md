Verdicchio vs Pinot Grigio?

Both are dry Italian whites, but Verdicchio has more texture, depth and a bitter-almond finish, while most Pinot Grigio is light, neutral and simple.

Verdicchio comes from the Marche, on Italy's Adriatic coast, mainly from two zones: Castelli di Jesi and Matelica. It has high acidity, lemon and green apple fruit, herbal notes and a distinctive almond finish. Good examples have real weight and can age for a decade, gaining honey and nutty complexity.

Pinot Grigio is grown across northern Italy, especially in the Veneto and Friuli. Most is made in huge volumes for easy drinking: light, crisp and quite neutral, with pear and lemon. Better versions from Alto Adige and Friuli have more intensity and texture.

The same grape is called Pinot Gris in France, where Alsace makes a much richer, spicier style.

At the table, Pinot Grigio suits light salads, simple seafood and aperitifs. Verdicchio can handle more: seafood pasta, fish stews, roast chicken and herb-driven dishes.

Value is on Verdicchio's side. A good bottle often costs little more than a basic Pinot Grigio.

Which to choose? Pinot Grigio for simple refreshment; Verdicchio for more flavour and character.
