Why is pure Corvina hard to find outside Italy?

Corvina is genetically unstable on its own, its color and tannin are so light that a 100% Corvina wine can end up thin and prone to oxidation if it's not handled carefully. That's why for generations it's been the backbone grape rather than the whole show: in Valpolicella and Amarone it's blended with Rondinella, which contributes more color and structure, and traditionally Molinara, which adds acid lift. Corvina brings the fruit and finesse, the others fill in what it's missing. Amarone complicates things further because the grapes are dried for months before pressing, concentrating sugar and flavor in a way that changes the whole equation, so even there you're rarely tasting Corvina in isolation. A handful of producers do bottle it varietally, usually in Bardolino or as a deliberate stylistic statement, but it takes very precise fruit and careful winemaking to keep it from falling flat. It's less that pure Corvina is forbidden and more that it's genuinely harder to pull off well.
