What pairs with Prosecco Superior?

The extra structure and lees-driven texture in Prosecco Superiore let it handle more than the aperitivo-only role of basic Prosecco. It still works for celebrations and aperitivo, but the fuller mouthfeel and slightly higher acidity give it enough backbone to sit through a real seafood course, think grilled shrimp, scallops, or a simple fish crudo, rather than just appetizers. The bready, almond note from extended lees aging also plays nicely with lightly fried or breaded seafood, echoing rather than clashing with those flavours. It's still not built for heavy sauces or red meat; the wine's charm is freshness and lift, not tannin or weight, so overloading the plate will just bury it. Serve slightly warmer than standard Prosecco, around 8-10°C, in a proper tulip glass to let the aromatics open up. Treat it as the step between casual party fizz and something you'd actually plan a menu around, still festive, but with enough seriousness to earn a seat at the table.
