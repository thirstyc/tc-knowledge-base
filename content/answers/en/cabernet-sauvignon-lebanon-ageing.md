Is Lebanese Cabernet Sauvignon worth ageing?

Some, yes, but don't assume every bottle is a cellar candidate just because it's Cabernet. The structural stuff, firm tannin and enough acid to carry it, is there in the better examples, particularly from producers who aren't rushing the wine to market. Five to ten years isn't unreasonable for a well-made one, and it'll soften the tannins and let the fruit shift from primary plum and cassis toward something more savoury and leathery. But plenty of what gets exported is made to drink now, within two or three years, with oak and ripeness doing the heavy lifting instead of true ageing potential. If you're unsure which you've got, taste it: if the tannins feel aggressive or drying young, that's actually a decent sign it has somewhere to go. If it's already soft and fruit-forward, drink it, don't sit on it hoping for magic that isn't coming.
