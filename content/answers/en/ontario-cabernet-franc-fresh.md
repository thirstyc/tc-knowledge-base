Why does Ontario Cabernet Franc taste so fresh and herbal?

It comes down to where the fruit ripens. Niagara sits between Lake Ontario and the Escarpment, and that lake acts as a giant thermal buffer: it delays spring budbreak, moderates summer heat, and slows the autumn cooldown, which stretches out the growing season without letting sugars spike. Cabernet Franc is thin-skinned and naturally retains malic acid well in cooler sites, so instead of the jammy, dark-fruited style you get in Bordeaux's Right Bank or in warmer New World regions, Ontario fruit holds onto brighter red-fruit character and firmer acidity. The herbal, sometimes green-pepper note people notice is pyrazine, a compound the grape produces that breaks down with heat and full ripeness. In a cool climate it never fully disappears, so you get that leafy, savoury edge alongside the fruit rather than pure fruit alone. Serve it around 15 to 16 degrees, slightly cooler than a typical red, and that freshness and herbal lift come through even more clearly.
