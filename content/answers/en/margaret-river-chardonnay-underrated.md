Why does Margaret River Chardonnay fly under the radar?

Margaret River made its name on Bordeaux-blend Cabernet Sauvignon, and for good reason, the region's gravelly soils and maritime climate are genuinely suited to it. But that early reputation set the narrative internationally, and wine media coverage still skews heavily toward the reds, so the Chardonnay program, which is arguably just as serious, gets treated as an afterthought. It doesn't help that Australian Chardonnay as a category spent decades fighting off a reputation for being overripe and over-oaked, a style that was never really what Margaret River was doing but that consumers still associate with the country. The reality is that producers here have been making tightly structured, barrel-fermented, lees-aged Chardonnay in a restrained style for a long time, closer in spirit to Burgundy than to the broad, buttery stereotype. Because the reds soak up the attention and the country's reputation works against it, this Chardonnay ends up seriously underpriced for the quality in the bottle.
