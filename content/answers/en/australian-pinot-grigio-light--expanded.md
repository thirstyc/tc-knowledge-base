Why does Australian Pinot Grigio taste so light and fruity?

Cooler regions like Victoria and Tasmania give grapes a longer, slower ripening season with a wide gap between warm days and cold nights - that diurnal swing lets sugars build gradually while malic acid is preserved, so the finished wine keeps bright acidity instead of turning flabby. Grapes are typically picked earlier here than in warmer inland areas, locking in green apple, pear and citrus flavours rather than letting them tip into stone fruit or tropical territory. Winemakers reinforce that freshness with stainless steel fermentation and no malolactic conversion, keeping the wine linear rather than gaining the softer, creamier texture malo would bring. Skin contact is usually brief or absent, keeping the wine pale and tannin minimal, which is part of why it reads as light-bodied even though the same grape, picked riper or given skin contact, can produce something much richer - that's Pinot Gris territory, same grape, different intention. Serve it properly chilled, around 8-10°C, to keep that acidity singing rather than dull.
