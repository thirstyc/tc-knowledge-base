Why does German Riesling fly under the radar?

Decades of cheap, sugary exports gave the whole category an undeserved reputation, and that stigma has been remarkably durable considering how little it reflects what's actually in the bottle today. Mosel and Rheingau make some of the most precise, age-worthy white wine on Earth, built on steep slate and volcanic soils that stress the vines just enough to concentrate flavor without overripening them. The result is wine with searing acidity, low to moderate alcohol, often 8 to 12 percent, and a mineral, almost electric quality that dry-wine drinkers usually associate with white Burgundy, not German whites. The other issue is the label itself: German wine terminology, Kabinett, Spätlese, trocken, feinherb, is genuinely confusing, and most shoppers give up and reach for something with a French name they already recognize. That's a shame, because bottle for bottle, top-tier German Riesling remains some of the best value in age-worthy white wine anywhere, precisely because the market still underprices it.
