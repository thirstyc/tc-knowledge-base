What is natural wine vs organic wine?

Organic wine is about how the grapes are grown, and it's legally certified. Natural wine is about how the wine is made, with minimal intervention, and it has almost no legal definition.

Organic wine:

- Grapes are grown without synthetic pesticides, herbicides or fertilisers.
- Certification is regulated, with official logos in the EU, the US and elsewhere.
- Winemaking rules are looser: organic wine can still use many additives and processes, though with limits, including lower sulfite levels.
- In the US, "organic wine" must have no added sulfites, while "made with organic grapes" allows some.

Natural wine:

- Usually starts with organic or biodynamic grapes, often hand-harvested.
- Fermented with native yeasts, with nothing added or removed: no added sugar, acid or colour, and little or no added sulfites.
- Often unfined and unfiltered, so it can be cloudy.
- Only France has an official label, "Vin Méthode Nature", and it's voluntary.

So a natural wine is almost always organic in the vineyard, but an organic wine isn't necessarily natural.

Style differs too. Organic wine can taste like any conventional wine. Natural wine is often fresher, more unpredictable and sometimes funkier.
