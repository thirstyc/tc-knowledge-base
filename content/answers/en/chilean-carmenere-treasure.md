Why is Chilean Carménère a national treasure?

Carménère is actually an old Bordeaux grape, one of the original six blending varieties in Médoc before phylloxera tore through Europe in the 1860s and growers replanted with hardier vines, mostly Merlot. Chile got cuttings before the blight hit, and because phylloxera never crossed the Atacama desert, Andes, and Pacific barrier, those vines never needed grafting. The twist: for over a century Chilean growers thought they were growing Merlot. It wasn't until 1994 that an ampelographer confirmed the vines were actually Carménère, misidentified because the two look similar in the vineyard. So Chile isn't just marketing a grape, it's the only country farming this variety at real scale on its own roots. It's a genuinely late ripener, and if picked too early it turns green and peppery from unresolved pyrazines, so Chilean growers had to learn to hang it longer for real ripeness. Get that right and you get a savory, dark-fruited red with a signature herbal edge that no other country can really replicate.
