How to authenticate wine?

Provenance is the best protection: buy from reputable sources that can show where a bottle has been. Physical checks help, but expert counterfeits can be very convincing.

Start with the paper trail. Ask for the bottle's history: where it was bought originally, how it has been stored, and any invoices or records. Established merchants and major auction houses verify this; a private seller with no history is a risk, especially for expensive or old wines.

Physical checks worth doing:

- The label: paper, printing quality, fonts and ageing should match what's typical for that producer and vintage.
- The capsule: it should match the period and show no signs of having been removed and replaced.
- The cork: when possible, check that the branding and vintage printed on it match the label.
- The fill level: unusually high or low fill for the wine's age deserves questions.
- The glass: bottle shape, embossing and colour should be consistent with the producer.

Many top producers now add anti-counterfeiting features like tamper-proof seals, unique serial numbers and scannable tags.

If a deal on a famous old wine looks too good, it probably is. For valuable bottles, specialist authentication services exist.
