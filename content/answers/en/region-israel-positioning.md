Why is Israel a serious wine country now?

Wine has been made in this part of the world for thousands of years, older than the appellations we now treat as historic, but the modern industry was essentially built from scratch starting in the late 19th century and really only found its footing in the last few decades. What changed things was altitude. Growers pushed vineyards up into the Golan Heights and the Judean Hills, where elevation brings cooler nights and slows ripening in a climate that would otherwise be too hot for balanced wine. Combine that with heavy investment in modern equipment, temperature-controlled fermentation, and increasingly serious winemaking talent trained abroad, and you get wines that can compete on real quality terms rather than just religious or ceremonial ones. It's a genuinely strange position: one of the oldest wine-producing regions on earth, essentially relaunched as a modern wine country within living memory. Worth seeking out on its own merits, not filed away as a kosher-aisle curiosity.
