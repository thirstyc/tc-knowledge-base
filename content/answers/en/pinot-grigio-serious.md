Can Pinot Grigio be serious?

Yes, and the confusion comes from treating Pinot Grigio as one grape when it's really one grape farmed two completely different ways. In the mountainous northeast of Italy, Alto Adige, Friuli, Collio, cooler sites and lower yields concentrate flavor instead of diluting it, and some producers use extended skin contact, the ramato style, which gives the wine a coppery-pink tint and real texture along with the fruit. Add lees aging, sometimes a touch of oak, and you get a wine with actual mineral tension and weight, not just a cold, inoffensive glass of nothing. These bottles usually run $25 and up because the yields are lower and the vineyard sites, often on steep alpine slopes, cost more to farm. They can age a few years too, gaining honeyed, nutty complexity that supermarket Pinot Grigio never had in the first place. If you've only had the cheap version, the good stuff will genuinely surprise you.
