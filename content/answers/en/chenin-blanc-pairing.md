What pairs with Chenin Blanc?

Chenin's high acidity is the whole story here — it comes from the grape's naturally slow ripening and the Loire's cool nights, which let it hang on the vine building sugar and aromatic complexity while malic acid stays intact. That acid acts like a squeeze of lemon on everything you put next to it: it cuts through the fat in roast chicken skin, resets your palate after each bite of pork with an apple or apricot glaze, and slices through the richness of a triple-cream or washed-rind cheese without a fight. This isn't a wine that needs delicate food to look good — it wants something with some heft so it has work to do. Off-dry versions do the same job but soften the edges around anything with a little spice or sweetness of its own, like a fruit glaze or a coconut-milk curry. Serve it around 10-12°C, cool enough to keep that acid bright but not so cold it mutes the honeyed, waxy fruit underneath. Skip pairing it with anything delicate and lean; it'll flatten it.
