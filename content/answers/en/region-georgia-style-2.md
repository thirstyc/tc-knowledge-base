Why does Georgian Saperavi taste so dark and earthy?

Saperavi is a teinturier grape, meaning the flesh itself carries pigment, not just the skin, so the wine starts out dark before any skin contact even begins. Traditional qvevri fermentation pushes that further: whole clusters ferment on skins, seeds and stems for weeks inside buried clay vessels, and because the qvevri sits underground, temperature stays cool and stable without any mechanical control. That slow, steady fermentation extracts tannin and colour gradually instead of in a rush, giving structure rather than harshness. The clay is porous enough to let in a trace of oxygen over time, similar to what oak does, but without adding any flavour of its own, so what you taste is pure fruit, tannin and earth, with no vanilla or toast in the way. Serve Saperavi around 16-17°C, a touch cooler than a big Bordeaux, so the tannin doesn't read as aggressive. This is a wine for stews and grilled meat, not delicate fare.
