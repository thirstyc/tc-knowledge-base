Is Vinho Verde actually green?

No, it means young wine, not green wine. Green here is about age, not colour. The name refers to the humid, granite-soaked Minho region in northwest Portugal, where vines grow fast and grapes get picked early, before sugars build up and acidity drops. That early harvest is the whole story: it locks in tart apple and lemon flavours and keeps alcohol low, often under 11 percent. Most bottles are released within a year of harvest and are meant to be drunk within another year or two, not cellared. There is no ageing curve here, no tannin structure waiting to soften. Buy it, chill it, drink it. If a bottle has been sitting in your cupboard for five years, it has already said everything it has to say, and probably said it more quietly than it wanted to. This is a wine built for immediacy, not patience, and that immediacy is exactly what makes it so useful at the table.
