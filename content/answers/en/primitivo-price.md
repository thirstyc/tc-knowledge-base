Why is Primitivo usually so affordable?

Puglia, way down in Italy's heel, grows grapes at massive scale, and Primitivo is the region's workhorse red. Flat land, hot dry climate, mechanized harvesting, and none of the hillside terracing or hand-labour that drives up cost in places like Piedmont or Tuscany. There's also no prestige tax: Puglia never built the DOCG mythology that lets Barolo or Brunello charge for a name as much as for what's in the glass. Primitivo is genetically the same grape as Zinfandel, so you're getting that same ripe, jammy, high-alcohol character, just from vineyards that cost a fraction to farm. Yields run generous rather than restricted, which keeps prices down but also means most bottles are made for early, easy drinking rather than aging. Most land in the $10-20 range and drink best young, slightly cool at 16-17°C, with something grilled or tomato-heavy. Don't expect Napa Zinfandel complexity here; expect honest, fruit-forward value, which is exactly the point.
