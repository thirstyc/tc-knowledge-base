What pairs with Mourvèdre?

Mourvèdre's tannin and structure are built for fat and protein, so lean toward grilled or roasted meats rather than anything delicate. Lamb is the classic match, especially cooked with herbes de Provence, since the wine's own dried-herb and garrigue notes echo what's on the plate. Game meats, braised short rib, or anything with a charred, smoky crust also work because the char picks up the wine's savory, slightly gamey edge. Its natural black pepper spice makes it a good partner for genuinely spicy food too, chili-rubbed meats or North African spicing, rather than fighting the heat the way a delicate white would. What you want to avoid is anything too light or acid-driven, like fish or fresh salads, where the tannin will just come across as harsh and drying. Serve it around 16-18°C, and if you're drinking a young bottle, decant it for an hour beforehand; the tannins are firm enough that a little air genuinely helps soften the experience.
