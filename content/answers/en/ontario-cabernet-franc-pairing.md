What pairs with Ontario Cabernet Franc?

The trick is working with the herbal, peppery edge rather than fighting it. Anything with fresh herbs, cracked pepper, or a vinaigrette echoes the pyrazine notes instead of clashing with them, which is why a salad with a mustardy dressing works better here than it would with a bigger, riper red. Grilled fish, especially something with a bit of char like salmon or trout, plays well against the wine's firm acidity, and the acid also handles richer preparations like mushrooms sautéed in butter without the wine feeling heavy. It's a good match for roast chicken or pork with a herb crust too, since the wine's weight is closer to a light-to-medium red than a dense one. Serve it around 15 to 16 degrees rather than room temperature, which in a heated house is usually too warm for a wine built on freshness. Chilling it down slightly keeps the acidity and herbal lift in focus instead of letting alcohol dominate.
