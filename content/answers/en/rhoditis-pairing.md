What pairs with Rhoditis?

Rhoditis works the way most high-acid, low-tannin whites do: the acidity cuts through fat and salt instead of fighting them, which is exactly what grilled octopus, sardines, or fried whitebait need. Think Greek taverna food rather than anything rich or cream-sauced. Fresh cheeses like feta or a young graviera play well too, since the wine's brightness balances their saltiness without clashing. Light pastas with olive oil, lemon, and herbs are a natural fit for the same reason, there's nothing heavy in the glass to compete with. Avoid pairing it with anything smoked, deeply spiced, or butter-based; the wine's structure is too delicate to stand up to that kind of weight and it'll just taste thin by comparison. Serve it properly chilled, around 8°C, colder than you'd pour a fuller-bodied white, because that chill sharpens the acidity and makes the pairing work. This is warm-weather, outdoor-table wine, built for meze-style eating rather than a formal sit-down meal.
