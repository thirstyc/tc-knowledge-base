What pairs with Sagrantino?

Sagrantino's tannin is aggressive enough that it needs fat and protein to have something to grab onto besides your tongue. Tannin binds to protein, which is why a wine that seems mouth-drying on its own turns smooth and even pleasant next to the right dish. Think braised or grilled red meat with real char, wild boar ragu, lamb shoulder cooked low and slow, or hard aged cheeses like pecorino. Umbrian tradition pairs it with cinghiale (wild boar) for exactly this reason, and it's a good template even if boar isn't on your local menu. Avoid anything delicate, light fish, salads, or subtle sauces, since the tannin and the wine's savoury, earthy intensity will flatten them completely. Serve it around 17-18°C, not room temperature in the modern overheated-house sense, since warmth exaggerates the sensation of alcohol and tannin together. Older bottles with resolved tannin have more flexibility, but this is fundamentally a wine built for hearty, fatty, slow-cooked food, not for sipping solo.
