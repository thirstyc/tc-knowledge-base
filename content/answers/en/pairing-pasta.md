Wine with pasta?

Match the wine to the sauce, not the pasta. A good rule: if the dish is Italian, an Italian wine from the same region is often the best partner.

Tomato sauces, like marinara or arrabbiata, are acidic, so they need a wine with plenty of acidity. Sangiovese, including Chianti, Barbera and Montepulciano d'Abruzzo, are ideal.

Meat sauces like Bolognese or ragù can take a slightly bigger red: Chianti Classico, Barbera, Langhe Nebbiolo or Primitivo.

Cream-based sauces, like Alfredo, suit a fuller white such as lightly oaked Chardonnay, Soave or Fiano.

Carbonara and cacio e pepe are rich and salty. A crisp Roman white like Frascati, a Verdicchio or a light red like Barbera cuts through them.

Pesto is herby and garlicky. Vermentino, from the same part of Italy as the sauce, is a natural match, as is Sauvignon Blanc.

Seafood pasta, like spaghetti alle vongole, wants something light and fresh: Vermentino, Verdicchio, Pinot Grigio or Albariño.

Mushroom or truffle pasta pairs beautifully with Nebbiolo or Pinot Noir.

What to avoid: heavy, oaky reds with delicate or creamy sauces. They drown the dish.
