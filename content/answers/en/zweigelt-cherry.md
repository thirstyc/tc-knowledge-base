Why does Zweigelt taste so light and cherry-forward?

Zweigelt is Austria's most-planted red grape, a crossing developed in the 1920s specifically to ripen easily in a cool climate, and that parentage explains almost everything about how it tastes. Because it doesn't need a long, hot growing season to reach ripeness, it retains bright acidity and rarely builds the thick, tannic skins that give reds like Cabernet their grip -- tannin comes largely from skin contact and phenolic ripeness, and Zweigelt simply doesn't accumulate much of either. What you get instead is fresh red and dark cherry fruit up front, sometimes a whisper of white pepper, on a light, juicy frame closer to Beaujolais than to Bordeaux. Most producers also pick early enough and use gentle extraction to preserve that freshness rather than chase weight or oak. Serve it slightly chilled, around 13-15°C, which sharpens the fruit and makes the low tannin feel even more refreshing -- treat it more like a case-appropriate red for warm weather than a wine for the cellar.
