Why does Refosco taste so dark and peppery?

The pepper note is real chemistry, not a tasting-note flourish. Refosco contains rotundone, the same aromatic compound responsible for the black-pepper character in Syrah and Grüner Veltliner, and it tends to show up more strongly in cooler-climate reds and grapes with thicker skins — Refosco has both going for it, growing across the cooler pockets of Friuli, the Karst plateau, and Slovenia. The darkness comes from a mix of naturally high anthocyanin content in the skins and the grape's tendency toward firm tannin and racy acidity, which read on the palate as structure and grip rather than softness. Terra rossa and red clay soils common to the region add to the impression of density and dark fruit. None of this is subtle: Refosco is a grape that announces itself, closer to a good rustic stew than a delicate broth. Give it a few years in bottle or a solid decant, because young Refosco can feel more like chewing on a pepper mill than drinking wine.
