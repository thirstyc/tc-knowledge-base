Why does Cortese fly under the radar?

Cortese has a branding problem, not a quality problem. Almost everyone who's had it knows it as Gavi, the appellation name from the town in southeastern Piedmont where it's grown, and very few people connect that name back to the grape itself. It's the same dynamic that keeps Melon de Bourgogne hidden behind Muscadet: the place became the marketing story, and the grape stayed anonymous. That anonymity isn't helped by Cortese being naturally quiet and neutral in character, no big showy fruit or aromatics, so it's easy to dismiss as 'just a crisp white' rather than recognizing the specific interplay of high acid, low phenolics and site-driven minerality that makes a good Gavi genuinely distinctive. It also doesn't have the marketing muscle of Chardonnay or Sauvignon Blanc behind it, and Piedmont's reputation is built overwhelmingly on Nebbiolo and Barolo, so the region's whites get overshadowed at home too. The wine deserves more attention than the grape ever gets credited for.
