What pairs with PEC Chardonnay or Pinot Noir?

The Chardonnay here is lean and high-acid rather than buttery, so it wants food that's simple and doesn't fight for attention: roast chicken, grilled trout, a soft-ripened cheese like a young Brie. The acidity cuts through fat and cleans your palate between bites, but there's not enough weight or oak to stand up to anything heavily sauced or smoked. Pinot Noir from the County tends to be light-bodied and savoury rather than fruit-forward, so treat it like you would a village-level Burgundy: roast duck, mushroom dishes, charcuterie, anything earthy rather than rich. Avoid pairing either wine with big, creamy sauces or intensely spiced food, since the whole point of these wines is precision and tension, and that gets flattened fast by anything heavy. Serve the Chardonnay around 10-12°C and the Pinot slightly warmer, 14-16°C, so the aromatics actually show instead of staying locked up.
