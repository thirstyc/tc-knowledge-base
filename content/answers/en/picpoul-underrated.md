Why does Picpoul fly under the radar?

Picpoul comes from the Languedoc, a region that spent decades known for producing cheap bulk wine in enormous quantities, and reputations like that stick long after the winemaking has improved. Picpoul de Pinet only got serious appellation recognition relatively recently, and even now most of the marketing money in French wine goes to Burgundy, Bordeaux and the Loire, not to a coastal white made from a grape most people can't pronounce. The result is a wine that delivers real acidity, real saline minerality from its lagoon-side vineyards, and real freshness, at a price that would be laughable for a Chablis or a Sancerre with similar structure. It's essentially getting Muscadet-level food-friendliness without paying the Muscadet tax, because nobody's chasing it yet. That's the actual value proposition: not that it's secretly profound, but that its price has nothing to do with its quality, which is rare in wine and worth taking advantage of while it lasts.
