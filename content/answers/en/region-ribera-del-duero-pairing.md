What pairs with Ribera del Duero?

Ribera del Duero sits at high altitude on Spain's northern plateau, where big daily temperature swings let Tempranillo (called Tinto Fino here) ripen fully during the day while retaining acidity overnight. That combination gives you a wine with real fruit density and structure but without the flabbiness you'd get from a hot, low-altitude region, which is exactly why it can stand up to fatty, intensely flavoured food instead of getting flattened by it. Grilled lamb is the classic match because the char and the fat need a wine with enough tannin to grip and enough acid to cut through, and Ribera has both. Wild game works similarly, its earthiness and slight gaminess need a wine with weight and dark fruit to match rather than compete. Aged Manchego, with its dense, salty, slightly crystalline texture, needs a red with enough tannin and acid to reset the palate between bites, otherwise the cheese just coats your mouth. Serve it around 16-18°C, and give younger bottlings some air before drinking.
