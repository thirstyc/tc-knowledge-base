## Overview

Red Grape / California, Argentina

Zinfandel

California's own. High alcohol, high fruit, high tannin. The raspberry bomb that made wine fun again. Range is huge—light and spicy or jammy and powerful depending on producer and vintage.

Also Called: Primitivo (Italy), Zin
Typical Alcohol: 14–16%
Tannin: High
Homes: California (USA), Paso Robles

## Zinfandel — What To Know

Zinfandel is California's signature red, but it isn't originally from California. DNA research showed it's the same grape as Italy's Primitivo, and both trace back to a Croatian variety called Crljenak Kaštelanski.

The grape ripens unevenly, so a single bunch can hold raisined berries next to green ones. That's partly why Zinfandel can reach high alcohol levels and taste of jammy raspberry, blackberry, black pepper and baking spice all at once.

Some of California's oldest vineyards are Zinfandel, with vines over a hundred years old in places like Sonoma's Dry Creek Valley, Lodi and Amador County. Old vines give smaller crops and more concentrated, complex wines, and "old vine" on a label is worth noticing.

Styles range widely, from lighter, peppery versions to rich, heady ones. And yes, "White Zinfandel" is the same grape, made as a sweet pink wine that outsold the red for years.

Drink it with barbecue, burgers, pizza and spicy sausages, but go easy on chile heat: the alcohol amplifies it.
