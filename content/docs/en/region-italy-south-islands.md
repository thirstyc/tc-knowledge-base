## Southern Italy — overview {#southern-italy-overview}

The Italian south (Mezzogiorno) and islands were long dismissed as sources of cheap bulk and blending wine, but are now one of the most exciting frontiers in Italian wine — rich in ancient indigenous grapes, old bushvines, volcanic and coastal terroirs, and warm, sunny, largely disease-free growing conditions. The wines combine southern ripeness with, in the best sites, real freshness from altitude and sea breezes.
Recommend when: the user wants bold, warm, characterful reds or distinctive indigenous whites, often at excellent value.

## Campania {#campania}

Campania (around Naples), with volcanic soils and altitude, makes some of the south's greatest wines from ancient grapes:

- Aglianico — the south's noble red grape, called "the Barolo of the south"; the DOCG Taurasi is its powerful, tannic, ageworthy peak — dark fruit, leather, and firm structure that rewards cellaring.
- Fiano (di Avellino) — a textured, ageworthy white; honey, hazelnut, pear, minerality.
- Greco (di Tufo) — a mineral, structured, citrus-and-stone-fruit white.
- Falanghina — a fresh, floral, citrusy everyday white.
- Lacryma Christi del Vesuvio — red and white from Vesuvius's volcanic slopes.

Serve at: 16–18°C Taurasi; 9–11°C whites.
Recommend when: the user wants a powerful ageworthy southern red (Taurasi), or a serious mineral southern white (Fiano, Greco).

## Puglia (Apulia) {#puglia-apulia}

Puglia, the sun-baked "heel," is a volume powerhouse turning increasingly to quality:

- Primitivo — genetically identical to Zinfandel; ripe, jammy, high-alcohol reds with sweet dark fruit and spice. Primitivo di Manduria is the benchmark. Crowd-pleasing and often great value.
- Negroamaro — the "black bitter" grape; rich, savory, warm reds with a characteristic bitter-plum finish; behind Salice Salentino and much Salento red and rosato.
- Puglia also makes some of Italy's best rosato (rosé).

Serve at: 16–18°C.
Recommend when: the user wants a ripe, bold, fruity, affordable red (Primitivo); a savory southern red (Negroamaro); or a characterful Italian rosé.

## Basilicata & Calabria {#basilicata-calabria}

- Basilicata — remote and mountainous; its star is Aglianico del Vulture, grown on the slopes of an extinct volcano — powerful, structured, mineral Aglianico, a value rival to Taurasi.
- Calabria — the "toe"; best known for Cirò, made from the local Gaglioppo grape — medium-bodied, savory, herbal reds; rising quality and rediscovered indigenous varieties.

Serve at: 16–18°C.
Recommend when: the user wants a powerful volcanic Aglianico at value (Vulture), or an off-the-beaten-path southern red (Cirò/Gaglioppo).

## Sicily {#sicily}

Sicily (Sicilia), the Mediterranean's largest island, is one of Italy's most dynamic regions:

- Nero d'Avola — the island's flagship red grape; ripe, full, dark-fruited, and versatile, from easy everyday bottles to serious ageworthy wines.
- Etna — the volcanic slopes of Mount Etna are a cult zone, making high-altitude, mineral, elegant, almost Burgundian wines: reds from Nerello Mascalese (pale, red-fruited, ash-and-mineral) and whites from Carricante (racy, mineral, ageworthy). A modern Italian phenomenon.
- Grillo, Catarratto, Inzolia — native whites (Grillo also historic in Marsala).
- Marsala — the historic fortified wine of western Sicily, from dry apéritif to sweet dessert styles (and a famous cooking wine).
- Passito di Pantelleria — a lusciously sweet dried-Zibibbo (Muscat of Alexandria) wine from the island of Pantelleria.

Serve at: 15–17°C reds; 9–11°C whites.
Recommend when: the user wants a versatile ripe red (Nero d'Avola), an elegant mineral volcanic wine (Etna Rosso/Bianco), a fortified (Marsala), or a sweet Muscat (Pantelleria).

## Sardinia (Sardegna) {#sardinia-sardegna}

Sardinia has a distinct, part-Spanish-influenced wine identity:

- Cannonau — the island's name for Grenache/Garnacha; warm, red-fruited, herbal reds, sometimes linked to local longevity ("Blue Zone") lore.
- Vermentino (di Gallura, the island's only DOCG) — crisp, saline, citrusy white; a benchmark seafood wine.
- Carignano (Carignan) — old-bushvine reds in the southwest (Sulcis); rich and characterful.

Serve at: 15–17°C reds; 8–10°C Vermentino.
Recommend when: the user wants a warm Mediterranean red (Cannonau), a crisp saline white for seafood (Vermentino), or a Sardinian old-vine red (Carignano).

## Quick regional summary italy-south {#quick-regional-summary-italy-south}

- Campania: Aglianico (Taurasi — "Barolo of the south," powerful/ageworthy); mineral whites Fiano, Greco, easy Falanghina.
- Puglia: ripe Primitivo (= Zinfandel; Manduria); savory Negroamaro (Salice Salentino); great rosato.
- Basilicata: volcanic Aglianico del Vulture (value power).
- Calabria: Cirò/Gaglioppo.
- Sicily: flagship Nero d'Avola; cult volcanic Etna (Nerello Mascalese reds, Carricante whites); Marsala fortified; sweet Passito di Pantelleria.
- Sardinia: Cannonau (Grenache), saline Vermentino di Gallura, old-vine Carignano.
