## Rioja — overview {#rioja-overview}

Rioja is Spain's most famous wine region, in the north along the Ebro river, and one of only two with the top DOCa status. It is best known for oak-aged red wine built on Tempranillo, usually blended with Garnacha, and often Graciano and Mazuelo (Cariñena). Rioja's identity has traditionally been defined by ageing — the Crianza/Reserva/Gran Reserva ladder — and by the vanilla-and-coconut signature of American oak, though a modern camp favors French oak, riper fruit, and single-vineyard expression.
Recommend when: the user wants a savory, oak-aged, food-friendly red; a classic Spanish red; or great value in mature wine.

## Rioja — the three subzones {#rioja-the-three-subzones}

- Rioja Alta — higher, cooler, Atlantic-influenced west; elegant, structured, ageworthy reds with bright acidity. The classic quality heart.
- Rioja Alavesa — also cool and high, on limestone soils; perfumed, fine-boned reds, often from Tempranillo on chalky clay.
- Rioja Oriental (formerly Rioja Baja) — lower, warmer, Mediterranean east; riper, fuller, higher-alcohol wines, more Garnacha. Good for warmth and body.

Modern Rioja increasingly emphasizes village and single-vineyard bottlings over the old blended-across-zones house style.
Recommend when: matching style — Alta/Alavesa for elegance and structure, Oriental for warmth and Garnacha richness.

## Rioja — traditional vs modern styles {#rioja-traditional-vs-modern-styles}

- Traditional Rioja — long ageing in American oak, savory and mellow, with vanilla, coconut, dill, dried fruit, and leather; released ready to drink (classic Gran Reserva). Silky and mature.
- Modern Rioja — riper fruit, French oak, more color and concentration, released younger; single-vineyard focus.

Both coexist; neither is "correct." The traditional style is one of the wine world's great food partners and value propositions in mature wine.
Recommend when: the user wants classic savory maturity (traditional) vs bold modern fruit (modern).

## Rioja whites {#rioja-whites}

White Rioja, from Viura (Macabeo) often with Malvasía and white Garnacha, ranges from fresh and unoaked to a remarkable oak-aged traditional style — nutty, honeyed, and extraordinarily long-lived, one of the wine world's underrated whites. A modern crisp style also exists.
Recommend when: the user wants a distinctive, ageworthy white (traditional oaked white Rioja) or a fresh Spanish white.

## Ribera del Duero {#ribera-del-duero}

Ribera del Duero, on the high Duero plateau in Castilla y León, is Rioja's great rival for Tempranillo — here called Tinto Fino or Tinta del País. The high altitude (up to ~900m) gives big day-night temperature swings, yielding deeply colored, powerful, structured reds with firm tannin and dark fruit. It's home to some of Spain's most prestigious (and expensive) reds. Generally richer and more muscular than Rioja.
Serve at: 16–18°C.
Recommend when: the user wants a powerful, concentrated, ageworthy Tempranillo; a bold red for red meat; or a prestigious Spanish red.

## Toro {#toro}

Toro, west of Ribera del Duero along the Duero, makes bold, rustic, high-alcohol reds from Tinta de Toro (a local Tempranillo). Old bushvines on sandy soils (many ungrafted, having escaped phylloxera) give intense, powerful, sometimes brawny wines. Great value for sheer concentration.
Serve at: 16–18°C.
Recommend when: the user wants a big, powerful, warming red at a friendly price.

## Rueda {#rueda}

Rueda, also in Castilla y León (near the Duero), is Spain's benchmark for crisp white wine from the Verdejo grape — aromatic, zesty, with notes of fennel, citrus, green apple, and a slightly nutty, herbal bite. Sometimes blended with or sold alongside Sauvignon Blanc. A reliable, affordable, refreshing white.
Serve at: 8–10°C.
Recommend when: the user wants a crisp, aromatic, affordable white; an alternative to Sauvignon Blanc; a match for seafood and salads.

## Navarra {#navarra}

Navarra, north of Rioja, once famous for rosado (rosé) from Garnacha, is a versatile region making reds (Tempranillo, Garnacha, and international grapes like Cabernet and Merlot), whites, and still-excellent dry rosés. Often overshadowed by neighboring Rioja but a reliable source of value and variety.
Recommend when: the user wants a dry Spanish rosé, or good-value reds including international-grape blends.

## Aragón — Garnacha country {#arag-n-garnacha-country}

East of Rioja and Navarra, Aragón (Calatayud, Campo de Borja, Cariñena) is a rising source of old-vine Garnacha value — sweetly juicy, warm, spicy reds from gnarled bushvines on high, dry slopes, kept fresh by altitude and the cold *cierzo* wind. Somontano, at the foot of the Pyrenees, is a cooler outlier growing both local and international grapes.
Recommend when: the user wants great-value, old-vine Garnacha; a warm but fresh red; or a hidden-gem Spanish region.

## Galicia — Rías Baixas & Albariño {#galicia-r-as-baixas-albari-o}

Green, rainy, Atlantic Galicia in Spain's northwest is a white-wine paradise. Rías Baixas is the star DO, home to Albariño — crisp, aromatic, saline, with white peach, citrus, and a maritime tang. Thick-skinned Albariño shrugs off Galicia's rain and is the definitive Spanish seafood white.
Serve at: 8–10°C.
Recommend when: the user is eating shellfish, ceviche, or seafood, or wants a crisp, aromatic, mineral white.

## Galicia — Godello, Mencía & the inland DOs {#galicia-godello-menc-a-the-inland-dos}

Inland Galicia and its edges make distinctive wines beyond Albariño:

- Godello — a textured, mineral white grape (Valdeorras, Monterrei) that can rival white Burgundy in weight and ageability.
- Mencía — an aromatic, fresh red grape (Ribeira Sacra, Valdeorras) with floral, red-fruit, and mineral notes, grown on dramatic steep river terraces; medium-bodied and food-friendly.
- Ribeiro — historic whites from Treixadura and others.

Recommend when: the user wants a serious mineral white (Godello) or a fresh, aromatic, lighter red (Mencía) — a great Pinot/Cab Franc alternative.

## Bierzo — Mencía {#bierzo-menc-a}

Just east of Galicia in Castilla y León, Bierzo is a rising star for Mencía — old bushvines on slate and clay giving fresh, mineral, floral-and-red-fruited reds of real finesse. Prized by the modern Spanish wine movement for terroir expression.
Serve at: 15–16°C.
Recommend when: the user wants a fresh, mineral, medium-bodied red with character; a Mencía; or an alternative to Northern Rhône or cool-climate reds.

## Txakoli (Basque Country) {#txakoli-basque-country}

Up on the Bay of Biscay around Bilbao and San Sebastián, the Basque Country makes Txakoli (Txakolina / Chacolí) — a piercingly tart, light, low-alcohol, slightly spritzy white from the local Hondarrabi Zuri grape. Traditionally poured from a height to aerate it. The definitive pintxos (Basque tapas) wine.
Serve at: 7–9°C.
Recommend when: the user wants a super-fresh, low-alcohol, zippy white; a pintxos/tapas apéritif; or something unusual.

## Quick regional summary spain {#quick-regional-summary-spain}

- Rioja (DOCa): Tempranillo-led, oak-aged; Alta/Alavesa (elegant) vs Oriental (warm); traditional (American oak, savory) vs modern (French oak, fruit); ageworthy whites too.
- Ribera del Duero: powerful high-altitude Tempranillo (Tinto Fino).
- Toro: bold, rustic old-vine Tempranillo.
- Rueda: crisp Verdejo whites.
- Navarra: dry rosado and value reds.
- Aragón: old-vine Garnacha value.
- Galicia: Albariño (Rías Baixas), Godello, Mencía (Ribeira Sacra); crisp, mineral, Atlantic.
- Bierzo: fresh, mineral Mencía.
- Basque: tart, spritzy Txakoli.
