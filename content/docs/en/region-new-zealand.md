## New Zealand — overview {#new-zealand-overview}

New Zealand is a cool, maritime, southerly wine country that rose to global fame astonishingly fast, largely on the back of one wine: Marlborough Sauvignon Blanc. Its clean, intense, aromatic style — driven by cool temperatures, bright sunlight, and a long growing season — defines modern New Zealand wine, but the country also makes world-class Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, and aromatic whites. Labeled by grape variety; quality-focused and largely screwcapped (NZ led the shift away from cork).
Recommend when: the user wants intensely aromatic, zesty whites, or cool-climate Pinot Noir.

## Marlborough Sauvignon Blanc {#marlborough-sauvignon-blanc}

Marlborough, at the top of the South Island, is New Zealand's largest and most famous region, and its Sauvignon Blanc is a global phenomenon: piercingly aromatic, zesty, and pungent, with passionfruit, gooseberry, grapefruit, green capsicum, and a razor-sharp acidity. It's the benchmark for the punchy, fruit-explosive style of the grape (a contrast to the more restrained, mineral Loire version). Marlborough also makes excellent Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, aromatic whites, and traditional-method sparkling.
Serve at: 7–9°C.
Recommend when: the user wants a bold, aromatic, zesty white; a crowd-pleasing Sauvignon Blanc; or a match for goat cheese, salads, seafood, or Asian food.

## New Zealand Pinot Noir {#new-zealand-pinot-noir}

Pinot Noir is New Zealand's flagship red, made in an elegant, perfumed, cool-climate style across several regions:

- Central Otago — the world's southernmost wine region; dramatic, continental (not maritime); ripe, vibrant, structured Pinot with intense red and dark fruit. NZ's most famous Pinot.
- Martinborough / Wairarapa — small, top-quality, savory and structured Pinot.
- Marlborough — lighter, fruit-forward, good value Pinot.
- Nelson, Waipara/North Canterbury — additional cool Pinot sources.

Serve at: 14–16°C.
Recommend when: the user wants an aromatic, elegant, fruit-driven cool-climate Pinot Noir; Central Otago for ripe intensity, Martinborough for savory structure.

## Other New Zealand wines {#other-new-zealand-wines}

- Chardonnay — increasingly excellent, from restrained to Burgundian (strong in Hawke's Bay, Gisborne, Marlborough).
- Aromatic whites — Riesling (dry to off-dry), Pinot Gris, and Gewürztraminer thrive in the cool climate.
- Hawke's Bay (North Island) — warmer; New Zealand's best region for Bordeaux-style reds (Merlot/Cabernet) and Syrah (elegant, peppery), plus Chardonnay. The Gimblett Gravels sub-zone is prized for reds.
- Sparkling — high-quality traditional-method fizz (Marlborough).

Recommend when: the user wants NZ Chardonnay, aromatic whites, or a cool-climate Bordeaux blend/Syrah (Hawke's Bay).

## Quick regional summary new-zealand {#quick-regional-summary-new-zealand}

- New Zealand: cool, maritime, southerly; screwcap country; labeled by grape.
- Marlborough: the famous zesty, aromatic Sauvignon Blanc (passionfruit/gooseberry); also Pinot and sparkling.
- Pinot Noir: elegant and perfumed — Central Otago (ripe, intense), Martinborough (savory), Marlborough (value).
- Hawke's Bay (North Island): warmer; Bordeaux blends & Syrah (Gimblett Gravels), Chardonnay.
- Also: fine Chardonnay and aromatic Riesling/Pinot Gris/Gewürztraminer.
