## Oregon — overview {#oregon-overview}

Oregon, in the cool, damp Pacific Northwest, built its reputation on Pinot Noir made in an elegant, Burgundian style — a deliberate contrast to California's riper reds. Its heart is the Willamette Valley, where marine-sediment and volcanic (Jory) soils and a cool, marginal climate yield perfumed, red-fruited, silky, earthy Pinot Noir of real finesse. A culture of small, artisan, often organic/biodynamic producers prevails.
Recommend when: the user wants an elegant, cool-climate, Burgundian-style Pinot Noir; an artisan/organic producer; or a lighter alternative to California reds.

## Willamette Valley & Oregon whites {#willamette-valley-oregon-whites}

The Willamette Valley and its nested sub-AVAs (Dundee Hills, Eola-Amity Hills, Ribbon Ridge, and others) are Pinot Noir central, but Oregon also excels at aromatic, cool-climate whites: Pinot Gris (its signature white — textured, pear-and-citrus), Chardonnay (increasingly fine and Burgundian), and Riesling. Southern Oregon is warmer and grows a broader range.
Recommend when: the user wants a textured Oregon Pinot Gris, a cool-climate Chardonnay, or aromatic whites.

## Washington State — overview {#washington-state-overview}

Washington is the US's second-largest premium wine state, and its climate is the opposite of Oregon's: most vineyards lie in the warm, dry, sunny, high-desert Columbia Valley east of the Cascade Mountains, irrigated by the Columbia River. Long summer days and cool nights yield deeply colored, ripe-but-fresh reds and whites. It successfully bridges Old and New World in style.
Recommend when: the user wants ripe-yet-balanced reds, or a value alternative to Napa.

## Washington grapes & sub-regions {#washington-grapes-sub-regions}

- Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot — the flagship reds; structured, dark-fruited, often excellent value versus California.
- Syrah — a Washington strength; ranges from savory/peppery to rich.
- Riesling — a historic Washington white, dry to off-dry, crisp and aromatic.
- Chardonnay and others also widely grown.
- Key sub-AVAs: Walla Walla Valley (prestige reds), Red Mountain (powerful Cabernet), Yakima Valley, Horse Heaven Hills.

Recommend when: the user wants value Cabernet/Merlot, characterful Syrah, or aromatic Riesling from Washington.

## New York State {#new-york-state}

New York is the major eastern US wine state, cool-climate and defined by water bodies that moderate harsh winters:

- Finger Lakes — the star; deep glacial lakes enable superb, racy, dry and off-dry Riesling (the region's calling card), plus Cabernet Franc, Chardonnay, and sparkling.
- Long Island — maritime, milder; Bordeaux varieties (Merlot, Cabernet Franc) and whites.
- Hudson Valley — historic, small.

The Finger Lakes is one of North America's premier cool-climate Riesling regions.
Recommend when: the user wants a racy, cool-climate American Riesling (Finger Lakes) or a maritime East Coast Bordeaux-style wine (Long Island).

## Other US states {#other-us-states}

- Virginia — humid mid-Atlantic; a signature in Viognier and Bordeaux varieties (notably Cabernet Franc, Petit Verdot); rising quality.
- Texas — High Plains and Hill Country; Mediterranean and Rhône/Spanish varieties (Tempranillo, Viognier) suited to heat.
- Michigan — cool, lake-moderated; aromatic whites (Riesling) and sparkling.
- Arizona, New Mexico, Idaho, and others — small but growing scenes.

Recommend when: the user asks about wine from a specific US state, or wants East Coast (Virginia) or emerging-region options.

## Quick regional summary usa {#quick-regional-summary-usa}

- Oregon: cool, damp; elegant Burgundian Pinot Noir (Willamette Valley); textured Pinot Gris; artisan/organic culture.
- Washington: warm, dry Columbia Valley (east of Cascades); ripe-but-fresh Cabernet/Merlot/Syrah and Riesling; great value; Walla Walla & Red Mountain.
- New York: cool, water-moderated; Finger Lakes Riesling is the star; Long Island Bordeaux styles.
- Emerging: Virginia (Viognier, Cab Franc), Texas (Rhône/Spanish), Michigan (aromatic whites).
