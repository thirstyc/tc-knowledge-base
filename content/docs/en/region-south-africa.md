## South Africa — overview {#south-africa-overview}

South Africa bridges Old and New World in style: long-established (winemaking dates to the 1650s) yet re-energized since the end of apartheid and its return to world markets in the 1990s. Vineyards cluster in the Western Cape, in a Mediterranean climate cooled by the Atlantic and the Cape Doctor (a strong southeasterly wind). The scene today is dynamic, with a celebrated new generation making fresh, characterful, terroir-driven wines — often from old bushvines — alongside great value at every level. Labeled by grape, with WO (Wine of Origin) denoting geographic origin.
Recommend when: the user wants distinctive, food-friendly, Old-meets-New-World wines, often at excellent value; or old-vine and artisan bottlings.

## Chenin Blanc — the signature white {#chenin-blanc-the-signature-white}

Chenin Blanc (historically "Steen") is South Africa's most-planted grape and its calling card — the country has the world's largest Chenin plantings, including precious old bushvines. Styles run from fresh, easy, and fruity to serious, textured, barrel-aged, age-worthy whites of real complexity (honey, quince, stone fruit, a mineral core). Old-vine Cape Chenin is one of the wine world's great values and a source of genuinely world-class whites.
Serve at: 9–11°C.
Recommend when: the user wants a textured, food-friendly white; a great-value serious white; or an alternative to Chardonnay/white Burgundy.

## Pinotage — the local red {#pinotage-the-local-red}

Pinotage is South Africa's own grape, a crossing of Pinot Noir and Cinsault created in 1925. It makes deeply colored, robust reds with dark fruit, smoke, and (in riper styles) a distinctive banana/rooibos or mocha note. Divisive historically — over-extracted versions gave it a rough reputation — but modern, carefully made Pinotage (and fresh, juicy "Cape blends" pairing it with Bordeaux/Rhône grapes) can be excellent.
Serve at: 15–17°C.
Recommend when: the user wants to try South Africa's unique grape, a smoky barbecue red, or a distinctive Cape blend.

## Other South African wines {#other-south-african-wines}

- Cabernet Sauvignon, Syrah/Shiraz, Merlot, Cinsault — the key reds; Syrah (often labeled the French way by quality producers) is a modern star, from savory-cool to rich; old-vine Cinsault is prized by the new wave.
- Sauvignon Blanc — crisp and successful, especially from cooler coastal zones.
- Chardonnay — fine and increasingly Burgundian.
- Cap Classique — South Africa's traditional-method sparkling wine (Champagne method); high quality and good value.
- Sweet & fortified — a historic legacy including the legendary sweet wines of Constantia (Muscat-based "Vin de Constance") and fortified styles.

Recommend when: matching to taste — modern Cape Syrah, crisp coastal Sauvignon, Cap Classique sparkling, or historic sweet Constantia.

## Key regions south-africa {#key-regions-south-africa}

- Stellenbosch — the prestige heartland; benchmark Cabernet, Bordeaux blends, and Syrah.
- Swartland — the epicenter of the new-wave movement; dry-farmed old bushvines, Rhône varieties (Syrah, Grenache, Chenin), minimal-intervention wines.
- Franschhoek, Paarl — historic warm inland zones; varied reds and whites.
- Constantia — cool, historic Cape Town suburb; Sauvignon Blanc and the sweet Vin de Constance.
- Hemel-en-Aarde (Walker Bay), Elgin — cool, coastal/high; excellent Pinot Noir and Chardonnay.
- Cape Town / Cape South Coast — cooler sites driving the freshness movement.

Recommend when: matching a region to style — Stellenbosch for structured reds, Swartland for old-vine/natural, Hemel-en-Aarde/Elgin for cool-climate Pinot & Chardonnay.

## Quick regional summary south-africa {#quick-regional-summary-south-africa}

- South Africa: Old-meets-New World; Western Cape, Atlantic/Cape-Doctor-cooled; WO origin system; dynamic new-generation scene; great value.
- Signature white: Chenin Blanc (largest plantings, old bushvines, fresh to serious).
- Signature red: Pinotage (Pinot × Cinsault; smoky, robust) and Cape blends.
- Modern stars: Cape Syrah, old-vine Cinsault/Chenin (Swartland), cool-climate Pinot/Chardonnay (Hemel-en-Aarde/Elgin), Cap Classique sparkling.
- Key regions: Stellenbosch (Cabernet/Syrah), Swartland (new-wave old-vine), Constantia (sweet & Sauvignon).
