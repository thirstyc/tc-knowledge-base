## Tuscany — overview {#tuscany-overview}

Tuscany (Toscana) is Italy's most famous wine region alongside Piedmont — the land of rolling hills, cypress trees, olive oil, and above all Sangiovese, the grape behind Chianti, Brunello, and Vino Nobile. Tuscan reds are defined by high acidity, firm tannin, and savory red-fruit-and-herb character built for the table. Tuscany is also the birthplace of the "Super Tuscan" movement that reshaped Italian wine.
Recommend when: the user is eating Italian food (especially tomato-based or grilled meats), wants a high-acid savory red, or a benchmark Sangiovese.

## Chianti & Chianti Classico {#chianti-chianti-classico}

Chianti is the famous Sangiovese-based red of central Tuscany. The key distinction:

- Chianti Classico DOCG — the historic heartland between Florence and Siena (the black-rooster seal); higher quality, mostly Sangiovese, with a quality ladder of Annata (normal), Riserva, and the top Gran Selezione. Savory cherry, dried herbs, leather, and bright acidity.
- Chianti DOCG — the broader surrounding zone (with subzones like Rufina, Colli Senesi); ranges from everyday to serious.

Old "diluted with white grapes" recipes are gone; modern Chianti Classico is Sangiovese-dominant and can be excellent.
Serve at: 16–18°C.
Recommend when: the user wants a classic, food-friendly Tuscan red; a match for pasta, pizza, grilled meats, or salumi; or great mid-priced Italian red.

## Brunello di Montalcino {#brunello-di-montalcino}

Brunello di Montalcino is Tuscany's most prestigious Sangiovese — from a special clone ("Brunello") around the hilltop town of Montalcino, in a warmer, drier corner. It's powerful, structured, and long-lived, with required long ageing (including years in oak) before release; it can age for decades. The earlier-drinking, more affordable version is Rosso di Montalcino (Brunello's "little brother").
Serve at: 17–18°C, decant.
Recommend when: the user wants a profound, ageworthy, special-occasion Tuscan red; a match for bistecca, game, or braises; Rosso di Montalcino for the same character at a friendlier price.

## Vino Nobile di Montepulciano {#vino-nobile-di-montepulciano}

Vino Nobile di Montepulciano is a historic, ageworthy Sangiovese-based red (here the clone is called Prugnolo Gentile) from the town of Montepulciano in southeastern Tuscany. Elegant and structured, it sits stylistically between Chianti Classico and Brunello. (Note: this is a place, not to be confused with the Montepulciano grape of Abruzzo — a common trap.) The junior version is Rosso di Montepulciano.
Serve at: 16–18°C.
Recommend when: the user wants an elegant, ageworthy Tuscan Sangiovese; clarifying the Vino Nobile (place) vs Montepulciano grape confusion.

## Super Tuscans & Bolgheri {#super-tuscans-bolgheri}

The "Super Tuscans" are top Tuscan wines that broke DOC rules — using Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, and Syrah, and/or 100% Sangiovese in non-traditional ways — and were originally labeled mere table wine, now IGT Toscana or specific DOCs. The coastal Bolgheri DOC (near the Maremma) became their epicenter, making Bordeaux-style Cabernet/Merlot blends of world renown. These are polished, powerful, internationally styled reds.
Serve at: 16–18°C.
Recommend when: the user wants a polished, Bordeaux-style Italian red; a prestigious modern Tuscan; or an explanation of the Super Tuscan category.

## Tuscan whites & Vin Santo {#tuscan-whites-vin-santo}

- Vernaccia di San Gimignano — Tuscany's notable dry white, crisp and savory with an almond note, from the medieval towered town.
- Vermentino — increasingly grown on the Tuscan coast; fresh and citrusy.
- Vin Santo — the traditional Tuscan sweet "holy wine," made from grapes dried on mats then aged for years in small barrels (caratelli); amber, nutty, caramel and dried-fig notes, classically dunked with almond cantucci biscuits.

Recommend when: the user wants a Tuscan white (Vernaccia), or a traditional sweet wine for dessert (Vin Santo with biscotti).

## Umbria {#umbria}

Landlocked Umbria, next to Tuscany, offers:

- Sagrantino di Montefalco — one of Italy's most tannic, powerful, ageworthy reds, from the local Sagrantino grape; dense and structured. Also a sweet passito version.
- Orvieto — a historic dry white blend (Grechetto, Trebbiano); crisp and easy, historically sometimes off-dry.
- Good Sangiovese-based reds (Montefalco Rosso, Torgiano).

Recommend when: the user wants a big, tannic, cellar-worthy red (Sagrantino) or an easy Italian white (Orvieto).

## Marche {#marche}

Adriatic Marche is best known for:

- Verdicchio (dei Castelli di Jesi / di Matelica) — one of Italy's finest native whites; crisp, mineral, with a signature bitter-almond finish and real ageing potential.
- Reds from Montepulciano and Sangiovese (Rosso Conero, Rosso Piceno).

Recommend when: the user wants a distinctive, ageworthy Italian white (Verdicchio) or a mid-weight Adriatic red.

## Abruzzo {#abruzzo}

Mountainous, Adriatic Abruzzo delivers reliable value:

- Montepulciano d'Abruzzo — the grape Montepulciano here; deeply colored, juicy, smooth, affordable reds with dark fruit and gentle tannin. A great everyday Italian red.
- Trebbiano d'Abruzzo — mostly simple white, but a few producers make profound versions.
- Cerasuolo d'Abruzzo — a deep, characterful rosé from Montepulciano.

Serve at: 15–17°C reds.
Recommend when: the user wants a smooth, juicy, affordable Italian red (Montepulciano d'Abruzzo) or a serious rosato (Cerasuolo).

## Lazio & Emilia-Romagna {#lazio-emilia-romagna}

- Lazio (around Rome) — mostly easy whites; Frascati (Malvasia/Trebbiano) is the historic Roman house white.
- Emilia-Romagna — home of Lambrusco, the fizzy red (dry versions are genuinely food-friendly with the region's rich cuisine), plus Sangiovese di Romagna and the white Albana.

Recommend when: the user wants an easy Roman white (Frascati) or a fun, food-friendly fizzy red (dry Lambrusco with charcuterie).

## Quick regional summary italy-central {#quick-regional-summary-italy-central}

- Tuscany: Sangiovese country — Chianti Classico (food-friendly, black rooster), Brunello di Montalcino (powerful, ageworthy), Vino Nobile di Montepulciano (elegant); Super Tuscans / Bolgheri (Bordeaux-style, IGT); whites Vernaccia; sweet Vin Santo.
- Umbria: tannic Sagrantino; easy Orvieto white.
- Marche: mineral Verdicchio white; Rosso Conero reds.
- Abruzzo: juicy, value Montepulciano d'Abruzzo; Cerasuolo rosé.
- Lazio: Frascati white. Emilia-Romagna: fizzy Lambrusco (dry = food-friendly).
- Watch: Vino Nobile di Montepulciano (Tuscan place, Sangiovese) is NOT Montepulciano d'Abruzzo (the grape).
