Why is Ontario wine emerging?

Short Answer

Ontario built its reputation on ice wine, a labour-intensive, cold-climate specialty made from grapes left on the vine until they freeze solid, usually around minus 8°C or colder, concentrating sugars and acids into something intensely sweet. That proved the Niagara Escarpment could ripen fruit properly despite brutal winters, thanks to Lake Ontario's moderating effect, which holds heat in autumn and buffers frost. Now the same cool-climate logic is producing serious dry wines. Riesling thrives here because the grape actually wants marginal, high-acid conditions, and Cabernet Franc has emerged as the red of choice because it ripens earlier and handles cooler nights better than thicker-skinned varieties like Cabernet Sauvignon. Both benefit from long, slow ripening seasons that build flavour complexity without pushing alcohol too high. The wines are genuinely competitive with cool-climate benchmarks from the Loire or Mosel, the issue has simply been visibility, ice wine overshadowed everything else for years, and outside Canada Ontario still isn't on most wine drinkers' radar. The quality has quietly outpaced the reputation.

What pairs with Niagara Riesling?

Niagara's cool continental climate, tempered by Lake Ontario, gives Riesling here a backbone of high, natural acidity, since grapes retain more acid when they ripen slowly in cooler conditions rather than baking in heat.
