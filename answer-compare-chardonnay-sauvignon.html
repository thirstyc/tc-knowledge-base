Chardonnay vs Sauvignon Blanc?

Short Answer

Chardonnay is shaped by the winemaker; Sauvignon Blanc is shaped by the grape. That's the core difference between the world's two most popular white grapes.

Chardonnay has a fairly neutral flavour of its own, so oak, malolactic conversion and lees ageing define its style. It ranges from lean and mineral in Chablis to buttery and tropical in warm climates.

Sauvignon Blanc is intensely aromatic no matter where it grows: cut grass, green pepper, grapefruit and passion fruit, with high acidity. It's usually fermented in steel to protect those aromas and drunk young.

Body is the other difference. Chardonnay is medium to full-bodied with softer acidity; Sauvignon Blanc is light to medium with sharper acidity.

With food, Chardonnay suits richer dishes: roast chicken, creamy sauces, lobster. Sauvignon Blanc goes with goat cheese, salads, asparagus, herbs and seafood, and it's one of the few wines that handles vinaigrette.

Chardonnay can age, especially from Burgundy. Most Sauvignon Blanc is best within a couple of years.

Which to choose? Chardonnay for texture and richness; Sauvignon Blanc for freshness and aroma.
