Best wine with low sulfites?

Short Answer

Look for organic, biodynamic and natural wines, which have lower sulfite limits or none added, and remember that dry red wines generally need fewer sulfites than sweet whites.

Some context first: all wine contains some sulfites, since yeast produces them naturally during fermentation. Most winemakers also add a little to protect the wine from oxidation and spoilage.

Wines with lower sulfite levels:

  • Natural wine: often made with little or no added sulfites.
  • Organic wine: the EU's organic rules set lower maximum limits than conventional wine, and in the US, wine labelled "organic" must have no added sulfites.
  • Biodynamic wine: certification limits sulfites below conventional levels too.
  • Dry red wines: tannins help protect red wine, so it generally needs less added sulfur than white or rosé.

Wines usually higher in sulfites: sweet wines, especially sweet whites, where sugar needs extra protection.

If you think sulfites affect you, it's worth knowing that dried fruit often contains much more sulfite than wine. Headaches after drinking are more often linked to alcohol, dehydration or other compounds. A genuine sulfite sensitivity is real but uncommon, and a doctor can help confirm it.
