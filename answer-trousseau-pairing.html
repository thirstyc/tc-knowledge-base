What pairs with Trousseau?

Short Answer

Because Trousseau is light-bodied with cutting acidity and almost no tannic weight, it plays best with food that would flatten a heavier red. Grilled fish like trout or mackerel works because the acid cuts through any oil without overwhelming the delicate flesh. Poultry, charcuterie, and mushroom dishes, especially anything earthy like a mushroom risotto or duxelles, echo the wine's own savoury, forest-floor notes that come from the Jura's cooler climate and its often oxidative winemaking touches. Cheese-wise, aim for something washed-rind or Comté rather than a big blue, the acid needs a partner with some fat but not overwhelming funk. Serve it at 13-14°C, cooler than a standard red, which keeps the acidity lively rather than sharp. Avoid pairing it with anything too rich or cream-based; the wine has no tannin or weight to stand up to that kind of fat, and it'll just taste thin by comparison.

Why does Trousseau taste so mineral and tense?

Trousseau grows in the Jura on clay-limestone and marl soils that don't offer much in the way of nutrients, forcing the vine to work harder and yielding thin-skinned, high-acid grapes even at full ripeness.
