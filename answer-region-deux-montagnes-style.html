Why does Deux-Montagnes wine taste so crisp?

Short Answer

The crispness comes down to soil and air working together. The sandy, glacial soil around the Lake of Two Mountains drains fast and doesn't hold heat, so vines never get the slow, warm ripening that would soften acid levels. Add the cool air pulled off the lake, especially at night, and you get grapes that hang onto their malic acid right up to harvest instead of losing it to heat the way grapes further south would. That's why these wines land bright and light-bodied rather than round or heavy: the vine simply doesn't get the conditions to build up sugar and drop acid the way a Mediterranean vineyard does. It's also why growers here rely on cold-hardy hybrid grapes rather than classic vinifera varieties, since anything less tolerant of frost wouldn't survive the winters in the first place. The result is a wine that tastes like where it's from: a short growing season, sandy ground, and water nearby. Serve cold, drink young, and don't expect a heavyweight.
