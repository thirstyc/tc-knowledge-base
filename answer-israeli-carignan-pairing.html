What pairs with Israeli Carignan?

Short Answer

Old-vine Carignan brings real tannin and earthy, rustic fruit, so it wants food with fat and char to soften that grip rather than anything delicate. Grilled meat is the natural match, kebab especially, where the smoke and spice mirror the wine's earthiness instead of clashing with it. Lamb works for the same reason grilled lamb works with Cabernet, the fat coats the palate and tames the tannin. Slow-cooked stews, particularly tomato-based ones with warm spices like cumin or cinnamon, are another strong pairing because the acidity in both wine and dish keeps things from feeling heavy. Roasted eggplant, another Mediterranean staple, has enough richness and slight bitterness to sit comfortably alongside it too. What you want to avoid is pairing it with anything too light or delicate, a subtle fish or a plain salad will just taste thin next to the wine's tannin and earthy weight. Serve around 16-17°C so the tannin doesn't harden further.
