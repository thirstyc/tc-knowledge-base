What pairs with Grüner Veltliner?

Short Answer

Grüner Veltliner's trick is a rare combination of high acidity, real body from lees contact, and a peppery, herbal streak that comes from a compound called rotundone — the same one found in cool-climate Syrah. That white pepper note lets it sit comfortably next to genuinely difficult foods. Asparagus and artichoke destroy most wines by making them taste metallic or dull, but Grüner's vegetal, green edge matches rather than clashes with them. Sushi wants acid and neutrality, not oak or fruit sweetness, and Grüner delivers both while still having enough weight to stand up to rice and soy. Wiener schnitzel works because the acid cuts through the fried fat the same way a squeeze of lemon would. Serve it around 8-10°C — cold enough to keep the acidity crisp, warm enough that the white pepper and citrus don't disappear.
