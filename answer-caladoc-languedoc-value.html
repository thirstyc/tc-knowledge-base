Why is Caladoc underrated?

Short Answer

Caladoc suffers from a branding problem more than a quality one. The Languedoc-Roussillon spent decades known internationally as France's bulk-wine tap, the region kept Europe's table wine glut going, and reputations built over generations don't shift quickly even after producers clean up their act. On top of that, Caladoc itself is an obscure grape name that most wine drinkers have never heard of and can't place on a map or a flavor spectrum, so it doesn't carry any of the recognition that lets a Cabernet or a Syrah charge more for the same quality in the glass. Meanwhile land and labor costs in the Languedoc remain lower than in Bordeaux or Burgundy, so producers aren't pricing in prestige they don't have. The upshot is a grape bred specifically to ripen fully and deliver color and fruit density in a hot climate, sold at a price that has nothing to do with how good it actually tastes. Worth grabbing before word gets out.
