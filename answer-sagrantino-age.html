Can you drink young Sagrantino?

Short Answer

You technically can, but you'll spend the whole glass fighting the wine rather than enjoying it. Sagrantino has some of the highest tannin and polyphenol levels of any grape grown anywhere, the product of thick skins built to survive Umbria's sun and wind. Young, that tannin sits as a dry, gripping wall that numbs your palate before the fruit gets a word in. Given time in bottle, those tannin molecules link into longer chains and eventually fall out as sediment, which softens the texture without stripping the wine of its depth. A Montefalco Sagrantino generally wants a decade before it starts showing its dried-cherry, leather and tobacco side properly, and the serious ones keep improving well past that. If you've got a bottle now and no patience, decant it hard for several hours, serve around 17-18°C, and put something fatty on the table to blunt the tannin. But if you can wait, wait.
