Why is Alsace an outlier in France?

Short Answer

Alsace sits on the French side of the Rhine but has flipped between French and German control several times over the centuries, and its wine culture never fully picked a side. The grapes, Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Pinot Gris, are German in origin, yet the winemaking sensibility, dry, precise, terroir-focused, is thoroughly French. That hybrid identity shows up on the label itself: Alsace is one of the only French regions that names the grape variety front and center, the way Germany or the New World does, instead of leaning purely on village or vineyard names the way Burgundy or Bordeaux do. It's a genuine exception to how French wine usually presents itself, and it exists because the region's history simply didn't allow for a single, tidy tradition to take hold. The result is wine that reads instantly to anyone used to varietal labeling, while still carrying the structural discipline of French winemaking underneath.
