What pairs with Vidal Blanc?

Short Answer

Vidal Blanc is a cold-hardy hybrid grape, thick-skinned enough to hang on the vine into a hard freeze without rotting — which is exactly why it's the backbone of Canadian icewine. In dry, still form it keeps that same high-acid backbone, with flavours running green apple, grapefruit, sometimes a tropical edge when riper. That acidity is what makes it work with fresh seafood: shellfish, oysters, scallops. It cuts through brininess and resets your palate between bites, the same job a squeeze of lemon does on an oyster plate. Fresh cheeses like chèvre or young cheddar work because the wine's acid meets the cheese's tang rather than fighting it. Light pasta with lemon, herbs or a cream sauce is a good match too, since the wine's brightness stops cream from feeling heavy. Serve it cold, 8-10°C, and drink it young — Vidal Blanc has neither the tannin nor the structure to age like a good Riesling. It's built for immediate, bright drinking, not the cellar.

Why does Vidal Blanc taste so citrusy and fresh?

Vidal Blanc is a hybrid, bred by crossing Ugni Blanc with a French-American variety called Rayon d'Or, specifically to survive cold climates that would kill most European vines outright.
