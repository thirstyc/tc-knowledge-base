Best wine for seafood?

Short Answer

A crisp, high-acid white is the safe bet: Muscadet, Chablis, Albariño or Sauvignon Blanc. Acidity does for seafood what a squeeze of lemon does.

But "seafood" covers a lot, so match the wine to how rich the dish is.

Raw and briny, like oysters, clams and crudo, wants something lean and salty. Muscadet, Chablis and Champagne are classics for a reason.

Light white fish, grilled or steamed, suits Albariño, Vermentino, Picpoul de Pinet or a Loire Sauvignon Blanc. Keep the wine as delicate as the fish.

Rich shellfish in butter, like lobster, crab or scallops, can handle a fuller white. A lightly oaked Chardonnay from Burgundy or California matches that creamy texture.

Oily, meaty fish such as salmon, tuna and swordfish can take rosé or a light red. Pinot Noir with salmon is a classic, and chilled Gamay works with tuna.

Fried seafood loves bubbles. Fish and chips with sparkling wine is hard to beat, because the acidity and fizz cut right through the batter.

Spicy seafood, like Thai curries or Cajun shrimp, wants an off-dry Riesling or a Grüner Veltliner.

The one rule worth remembering: big, tannic reds can make fish taste metallic, so save the Cabernet for the steak.
