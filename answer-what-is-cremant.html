What is Crémant?

Short Answer

Crémant is French sparkling wine made the same way as Champagne, but from outside the Champagne region. It's usually the best value in French bubbles.

Like Champagne, Crémant gets its bubbles from a second fermentation inside the bottle, followed by ageing on the spent yeast, which gives it fine bubbles and bready, nutty notes. The rules require at least nine months on the lees.

There are several Crémant appellations across France, and each uses its local grapes:

  • Crémant d'Alsace: mostly Pinot Blanc, often Riesling or Pinot Gris.
  • Crémant de Bourgogne: Chardonnay and Pinot Noir, closest in spirit to Champagne.
  • Crémant de Loire: Chenin Blanc and Cabernet Franc.
  • Crémant de Limoux: from the south, where French sparkling wine may have started.
  • Crémant du Jura, de Bordeaux, de Die and de Savoie round out the family.

Prices are typically a fraction of Champagne's, because the grapes and land cost less, not because the method is simpler.

Use it anywhere you'd use Champagne: as an aperitif, with fried food or oysters, or for a toast without the premium.
