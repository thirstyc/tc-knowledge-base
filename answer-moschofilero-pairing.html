What pairs with Moschofilero?

Short Answer

Moschofilero is a pink-skinned grape vinified white, grown high up in the Peloponnese around Mantinia, where altitude keeps things cool even under a Greek sun. That's the whole trick: elevation preserves acid and keeps alcohol modest, usually around 11-12%, while the grape still piles on rose petal, citrus blossom and a faint Muscat-like perfume. On the table that combination means it never fights with delicate food. Light seafood, especially shellfish or anything with lemon, works because the acid mirrors the citrus you'd add yourself. Fresh herb-driven pasta, soft unaged cheeses, feta-based salads, even sushi if you're feeling adventurous, all sit comfortably next to it because the wine is aromatic without being heavy or oaky. Serve it cold, around 8-10°C, closer to how you'd treat a Sauvignon Blanc than a full-bodied white. Avoid pairing it with anything rich, buttery or heavily spiced; the wine is built for freshness and float, and anything too dense will just flatten it.

Why does Moschofilero taste so floral and citrusy?

Moschofilero comes from Mantinia in the Peloponnese, grown at fairly high altitude, often above 600 metres, where cool nights slow ripening and preserve the acidity that gives it its lift.
