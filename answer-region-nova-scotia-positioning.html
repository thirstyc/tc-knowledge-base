Why is Nova Scotia betting on sparkling wine?

Short Answer

Traditional-method sparkling wine needs grapes picked underripe, with high natural acidity and modest sugar, because that acid is what keeps the wine lively through a long second fermentation in bottle and years of lees aging. Nova Scotia's cool, damp Atlantic climate produces exactly that kind of fruit almost by default, the same conditions that would frustrate a producer chasing big ripe reds become an asset for fizz. Rather than pretend otherwise, the province leaned into it, and its sparkling program has become the most credible thing coming out of the region. Tidal Bay, a still white blend with its own province-wide standard for freshness and aromatic character, was created the same way, as a deliberate signature rather than an attempt to copy Sauvignon Blanc or Chardonnay from somewhere warmer. It's a small industry making a specific, climate-honest bet instead of chasing styles its vineyards were never suited to produce. Worth watching, and worth trying before assuming it's just a curiosity.

What pairs with Nova Scotia sparkling or Tidal Bay white?
