Why is Lagrein hard to find outside Alto Adige?

Short Answer

Lagrein is basically a one-valley grape. It's native to the area around Bolzano, in Alto Adige, where it does best on the warm, alluvial flats of the valley floor rather than the steep mountain slopes that dominate the region. That flat, warm land is scarce and expensive, and most of it is already spoken for by apple orchards or other vines, so plantings of Lagrein stay small no matter how much people want it. It's also a dark-skinned, tannic grape that needs real heat to ripen fully, which rules out most of the higher, cooler sites nearby. On top of that, Alto Adige has heavy local demand: tourists, ski towns, and Austrian-leaning restaurant lists soak up a lot of it before it ever gets to an export container. So it's not mystique or gatekeeping, it's simple math: tiny suitable acreage plus strong local thirst equals not much left over. If you find a bottle outside the region, buy it, because that supply chain is thin by nature, not by design.
