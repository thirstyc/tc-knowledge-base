Why is my wine cloudy?

Short Answer

Usually because it wasn't filtered, or because sediment got stirred up. Cloudiness on its own is not a fault, and plenty of good wine is deliberately hazy.

The common causes:

  • Unfined and unfiltered wine: many natural and low-intervention wines skip these steps to keep texture and flavour, so they arrive cloudy by design. The label often says "non filtré" or "unfiltered".
  • Disturbed sediment: older reds throw a deposit, and carrying or pouring the bottle roughly mixes it back in. Stand the bottle upright for a day and decant carefully.
  • Protein haze: a harmless cloudiness in white wine, usually caused by heat during storage or transport.
  • Cold: chilling can make tartrate crystals form. Those are little glassy crystals, not a haze, and they're harmless.

When cloudiness is a warning sign: if the wine also smells of vinegar, nail-polish remover or rotten eggs, or tastes fizzy when it shouldn't, something has gone wrong microbially.

The test is simple. Smell it and taste it. If it smells and tastes good, drink it.
