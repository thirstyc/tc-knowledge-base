What pairs with Muscadet?

Short Answer

Oysters, above all. Muscadet is light, bone-dry, high in acidity and slightly saline, which makes it one of the most food-friendly and seafood-friendly whites in the world.

Muscadet comes from the western end of the Loire Valley, near Nantes and the Atlantic coast, and is made from the Melon de Bourgogne grape. Its subtle flavours of lemon, green apple and sea spray never compete with food; instead, its acidity refreshes the palate like a squeeze of lemon.

Great pairings:

  • Raw oysters: the classic, perfect match.
  • Mussels, especially moules marinières.
  • Clams, prawns and other shellfish.
  • Grilled or pan-fried white fish.
  • Fish and chips, and other fried seafood.
  • Sushi and sashimi.
  • Goat cheese, salads and light vegetable dishes.
  • Charcuterie and salty snacks.

Look for "sur lie" on the label, meaning the wine was aged on its yeast lees, which adds texture and a subtle bready note. The single-village "cru" Muscadets, aged longer on the lees, are richer and can age for a decade.

Avoid pairing it with heavy, creamy or strongly spiced dishes, which overwhelm its delicacy.

Serve it well chilled, around 8°C.
