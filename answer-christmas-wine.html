Wine for Christmas?

Short Answer

It depends on what's on the table, but sparkling wine, Pinot Noir and a good red Bordeaux cover most Christmas dinners, with Port or Sauternes for dessert.

Start with something sparkling. Champagne, Crémant or English sparkling wine suits canapés, smoked salmon and the festive mood.

For turkey, pick a fruity, medium-bodied red like Pinot Noir or Beaujolais, or a rich white like Chardonnay.

For roast beef, choose a structured red: Bordeaux, Rioja Reserva or Cabernet Sauvignon.

For ham, which is sweet and salty, try an off-dry Riesling, a Beaujolais or a dry rosé.

For goose or duck, go with Pinot Noir or a northern Rhône Syrah.

For seafood, including the oysters served at a French Christmas Eve feast, Chablis or Champagne works beautifully.

Foie gras is traditionally paired with Sauternes, whose sweetness balances the rich liver.

For Christmas pudding and fruitcake, pour Tawny Port, sweet Madeira or Pedro Ximénez Sherry.

For the cheese board, Stilton with Port is a British Christmas tradition.

Buying for a crowd? Pick versatile, crowd-pleasing wines that work with several dishes, and make sure you have enough of each.
