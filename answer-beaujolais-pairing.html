What pairs with Beaujolais?

Short Answer

Almost everything, which is rather the point of it. Beaujolais is low in tannin and high in acidity, so it travels alongside food instead of steamrolling it.

Charcuterie is the classic — the wine's brightness cuts fat without needing any weight of its own. Roast chicken, pork, sausages, anything off a rotisserie. It handles grilled vegetables and lentils better than most reds, because there's no tannin picking a fight with the earthiness.

It's also one of the few reds that genuinely works with fish. A cru Beaujolais with salmon or seared tuna is a good idea, not a compromise.

Serve it cool. Thirteen or fourteen degrees, twenty minutes in the fridge. Warm Beaujolais goes flabby and loses the freshness that makes the whole thing work; cool, it tastes like crushed raspberries with a twist of pepper.

The one thing to skip is heavily charred red meat. There isn't enough structure there to stand up to it, and you'll taste the gap.
