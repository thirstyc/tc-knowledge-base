Why did Merlot's reputation take a hit?

Short Answer

Blame a single line in a 2004 movie - a character trashed Merlot and refused to drink it, and casual buyers took the cue, treating it as the safe, boring grape while Cabernet got all the prestige. That reputation never matched reality. Merlot ripens earlier than Cabernet Sauvignon and thrives on the clay and limestone soils of Bordeaux's Right Bank, in Pomerol and Saint-Émilion, where it's the backbone of some of the most expensive wine on earth. The grape's thinner skin means softer tannins and a plusher mouthfeel, which is exactly why it blends so well with the firmer structure of Cabernet Franc. None of that changed when the movie came out; what changed was perception. Cheap, overcropped Merlot flooding the market in the '90s didn't help either, giving people a real basis for the complaint even before the film existed. The grape itself never stopped being capable of serious, age-worthy wine - the trend just moved on, and it's slowly moving back.

What pairs with Merlot?
