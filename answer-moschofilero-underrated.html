Why does Moschofilero fly under the radar outside Greece?

Short Answer

Greek wine spent decades saddled with a cheap-jug-wine image abroad, largely thanks to mass exports of resinated wine in the mid-20th century that shaped foreign perceptions long after quality producers moved past it. Moschofilero got caught in that reputational undertow even though it has little in common with retsina. It's a genuinely refined grape: pink-skinned, aromatic, grown at altitude in Mantinia where cooler nights preserve acidity that most warm-climate whites can only dream of. That acid, paired with low alcohol and delicate floral perfume, makes it one of the more food-friendly whites you can find at a reasonable price. The problem is distribution and awareness more than quality: it rarely gets shelf space next to the usual Sauvignon Blancs and Pinot Grigios, and most drinkers have simply never been introduced to it. If you find a bottle, don't judge Greek wine by outdated assumptions. This is a serious, precise, low-intervention style that deserves to be tried with an open mind rather than dismissed on reputation alone.
