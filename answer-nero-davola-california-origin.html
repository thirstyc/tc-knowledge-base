Is Nero d'Avola even a grape that grows in California, or is this some marketing gimmick?

Short Answer

It's real, just small. Nero d'Avola is Sicily's signature red, built for heat and drought, so a handful of California growers in warm pockets like Lodi or the Sierra Foothills started planting it as the climate got hotter and Rhone and Italian varieties made more sense than another tired Cabernet block. There's no gimmick here, just growers reading the weather and picking a grape that actually wants to ripen at 35 degrees without turning into jam. The difference from Sicilian versions is mostly about restraint: California fruit tends to get riper and rounder, sometimes losing the volcanic-soil tension that Sicilian bottlings get from places like Etna or Vittoria. It's still a minority planting, so you won't trip over it at every wine shop, but if you find one, it's usually made by someone curious enough to bother with an oddball grape instead of chasing what already sells. Worth trying once you're bored of the usual suspects.
