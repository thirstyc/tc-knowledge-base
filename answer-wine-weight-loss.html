Wine and weight loss?

Short Answer

Wine can fit into a weight-loss plan in small amounts, but it makes losing weight harder. It adds calories without filling you up, and it tends to increase appetite and lower willpower around food.

A standard 150 ml glass of dry wine at about 13% alcohol contains roughly 120–130 calories, mostly from the alcohol itself, which provides 7 calories per gram, almost as much as fat. Sweet and fortified wines contain more.

Other ways wine affects weight loss:

  • Your body burns alcohol first, pausing fat burning while it does.
  • Alcohol stimulates appetite and lowers restraint, which is why snacks disappear during a second glass.
  • It disrupts sleep, and poor sleep is linked to hunger and weight gain.

If you want to keep enjoying wine while losing weight:

  • Set a limit, such as a glass on a few days a week, and have alcohol-free days.
  • Choose dry, lower-alcohol wines, like Vinho Verde, German Riesling Kabinett or a dry rosé.
  • Pour smaller glasses and sip slowly with food.
  • Try a spritzer with sparkling water.
  • Count wine in your daily calories.

Cutting back on alcohol is one of the simplest changes for many people trying to lose weight.

Wine and diabetes?

Many people with diabetes can drink wine in moderation, but it's important to talk to your doctor first, because alcohol affects blood sugar and interacts with some diabetes medications.
