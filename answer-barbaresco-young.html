Can you drink young Barbaresco?

Short Answer

Yes, and more comfortably than Barolo. Both come from Nebbiolo, but Barbaresco's vineyards sit on warmer, sandier soils around the town itself, which ripens the grape a touch faster and gives softer tannin and slightly more perfume, less structural weight than Barolo's clay-heavy hills. That means Barbaresco needs less time in bottle to become approachable. At three or four years old it's already drinkable, though you'll still get a firm, grippy edge and those high-toned rose, tar and liquorice aromas before they've fully knit together. Give it five to ten years and the tannin resolves into something silkier while the fruit shifts from red cherry toward dried cherry and leather. If you're opening one young, decant it for an hour to soften the initial harshness, and put it next to something fatty, grilled meat or a mushroom dish, so the tannin has something besides your palate to grab onto. Serve at 16-18°C. It doesn't demand a decade in the cellar, but it does get genuinely better with one if you have the patience.
