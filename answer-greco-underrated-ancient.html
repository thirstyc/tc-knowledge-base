Why is Greco underrated?

Short Answer

Greco gets overlooked mostly because southern Italian whites in general live in the shadow of the north, Soave and Pinot Grigio hog the shelf space while Campania's volcanic whites quietly outclass them in complexity. Part of the problem is marketing: Greco di Tufo, its best known expression, doesn't have the brand recognition of a Chablis or a Riesling, even though its combination of high acidity, saline minerality and textural weight puts it in similar company. It's also a grape with real history, likely brought by Greek colonists over two thousand years ago, so you're drinking something with genuine lineage rather than a modern varietal trend. Because the vineyards are often planted in volcanic soil at elevation, yields are naturally low and the wines develop concentration without needing intervention in the cellar. For the price, which is still reasonable compared to more famous mineral-driven whites, you're getting a wine with real ageing potential and food-pairing versatility. It deserves a place next to the usual suspects, not as a curiosity but as a serious alternative.

What pairs with Greco?

Greco's high acidity and saline, volcanic minerality make it a workhorse with anything oily or briny, since the acid cuts fat and the minerality echoes shellfish and cured fish rather than fighting them.
