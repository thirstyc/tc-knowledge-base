Why does Hawke's Bay matter beyond Marlborough Sauvignon Blanc?

Short Answer

Marlborough gets the postcard shot, but Hawke's Bay is where New Zealand quietly proved it could do structure, not just brightness. This is the country's oldest wine region, warm enough and old enough in vine age to grow proper red grapes, and Gimblett Gravels is the reason why: a stretch of old riverbed with almost no topsoil, forcing vines to dig deep and struggle a little, which is exactly what you want for concentrated, age-worthy Merlot and Cabernet-based blends. It does not shout the way Marlborough Sauvignon Blanc does, and that is the point. Where Marlborough sells on immediate, zippy appeal, Hawke's Bay is playing a longer game, closer in ambition to Bordeaux than to anything sold by the glass at a beach bar. If you think New Zealand only does crisp whites, a serious Gimblett Gravels red will genuinely recalibrate that assumption.
