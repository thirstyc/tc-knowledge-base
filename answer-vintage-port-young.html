Can you drink young Vintage Port?

Short Answer

You can drink it young, but you're fighting the wine's whole design. It's bottled early, roughly two years after harvest, unfiltered, with all its tannin and structure intact and none of the decades of bottle aging that would soften it. Young, it drinks like a fist: jammy, monolithic fruit, aggressive grip, alcohol that hasn't integrated. Tannins need years, often decades, of slow polymerization in bottle to round out, which is also when the wine throws its heavy sediment, so proper aging means careful decanting later, not just popping a cork. If you're patient, 20 to 30 years is where it starts showing what it was built for. If you must drink it young, decant hard and serve around 16 to 18°C, but understand you're tasting potential, not the finished wine. This is a cellar wine, not a Tuesday-night bottle.

Why does Vintage Port taste so dark and powerful?

Vintage Port gets fortified partway through fermentation: grape spirit is added while there's still plenty of sugar left, which kills off the yeast and locks in both high alcohol, around 20 percent, and a hefty dose of residual sugar.
