What pairs with Coonawarra Cabernet?

Short Answer

Coonawarra Cabernet is built around firm, grippy tannin and a cool-climate backbone, so you want food with enough fat and protein to soften the tannin without drowning the wine's minty, eucalyptus-tinged fruit. Grilled lamb works because the char and fat coat the palate right as the tannin hits. Beef Wellington does the same job with richer, buttery pastry fat. A hard, aged cheese — something like a mature cheddar or Comté — brings salt and protein that binds to tannin and softens it, which is exactly why cheese and structured red wine became a cliché in the first place; it actually works chemically. Avoid anything too delicate or too sweet, since the tannin will just steamroll it. Serve around 16-18°C, slightly below typical red wine serving habits, since Coonawarra's cooler fruit character gets muddled if the wine is too warm. This isn't a wine that wants subtlety on the plate — it wants something with enough structure to meet it halfway.
