What is zero-alcohol wine?

Topic

Fermentation

Difficulty

Beginner

Short Answer

Zero-alcohol wine, also called dealcoholised or non-alcoholic wine, is made like ordinary wine and then has the alcohol removed.

The grapes are grown, pressed and fermented as usual. The alcohol is then stripped out, most often by vacuum distillation, which uses low pressure so the wine can be heated gently, or by reverse osmosis, which filters the wine under pressure. Spinning cone technology is another method.

Labelling varies by country. "Alcohol-free" usually means below about 0.05% alcohol by volume, while "de-alcoholised" or "low alcohol" can mean up to around 0.5%, similar to some fruit juices and breads.

The hard part is what removing alcohol takes with it: body, texture and much of the aroma. Producers often add grape juice, a little sugar or carbonation to compensate, which is why many taste sweeter than the wine they came from.

Quality has improved a lot, particularly for sparkling styles, where bubbles and acidity distract from the missing weight.

If you're avoiding alcohol entirely, check the label carefully, since 0.5% is not the same as zero.
