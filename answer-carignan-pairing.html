What pairs with Carignan?

Short Answer

Carignan's calling card is brutal acidity paired with rustic, high tannin, which means it needs food that fights back. Think daube, cassoulet, or anything braised for hours in red wine and stock — the wine's acid slices through the collagen and fat the same way it would cut a steak. Grilled meat works for the same reason: char and smoke need something sharp to reset the palate between bites, and Carignan's structure doesn't back down. Mushroom dishes, especially anything with a long-cooked, earthy sauce, echo the wine's own rustic, slightly wild character instead of clashing with it. What you want to avoid is anything delicate — poached fish, cream sauces, subtle herbs — because old-vine Carignan will flatten them without noticing. Serve it around 16 to 18 degrees, a touch cooler than room temperature, so the tannins stay in check and the acidity doesn't turn shrill. This is a wine built for the table, not for sipping alone before dinner.
