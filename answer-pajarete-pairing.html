What food actually works with Pajarete?

Short Answer

Think concentrated, nutty, or salty rather than fruity and light, because the wine's own sweetness and oxidative depth will flatten anything delicate. Aged cheeses are the classic move, something like an old Manchego or a hard blue cheese, where the saltiness cuts through the sugar and the wine's dried-fruit character mirrors the cheese's nuttiness. It's also excellent with almonds, walnuts, or a plain dark chocolate that isn't already loaded with sugar, since the wine supplies plenty of sweetness on its own. Foie gras is another classic pairing, the richness matching the wine's weight almost pound for pound. Where people go wrong is reaching for fresh fruit desserts or anything citrusy, which just makes the wine taste flat and syrupy by comparison. It's also genuinely good on its own after dinner, no food required, which is honestly how a lot of Andalusians drink it. Skip pairing it with anything delicate or high-acid; this wine wants density and salt, not brightness.
