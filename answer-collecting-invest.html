Should I invest in wine?

Short Answer

That's a financial decision, so it's worth talking to a licensed financial adviser before putting money into wine. What we can do is explain how the market works and its risks.

Fine wine is traded like other collectibles. A small share of the world's wine, mostly top Bordeaux, Burgundy, Champagne, some Italian and a few others, has historically risen in price as it ages and becomes scarcer. Indices track these prices, and specialist merchants and platforms offer investment services.

The risks are significant:

  • It's illiquid: selling can take time and depends on buyers being available.
  • Costs add up: professional storage, insurance, merchant fees and auction premiums can eat into returns.
  • Prices can fall, as they have in several past downturns.
  • Fraud and counterfeiting are real concerns, so provenance is everything.
  • Tax treatment varies widely between countries.

Most investment wine is kept in professional, often bonded, storage, and never drunk.

A useful rule of thumb for collectors: only buy wine you'd be happy to drink if its value didn't rise. That way, the downside is a great bottle rather than a loss.
