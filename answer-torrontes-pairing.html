What pairs with Torrontés?

Short Answer

Torrontés is built for foods that flatten most white wines. Its perfumed nose of orange blossom and lychee stands up to chile heat without adding more burn, because it's usually low in tannin and only moderately acidic, so it cools the palate rather than amplifying spice the way a high-acid, high-alcohol wine can. That makes it a genuinely good match for Thai, Vietnamese, or Indian dishes with real heat, not just a token white wine pairing. It also works well with fresh, unaged cheeses like a young goat cheese or fresh mozzarella, where its floral fruit contrasts nicely against the cheese's tang without a wall of oak or tannin getting in the way. Grilled shrimp, ceviche and other citrusy seafood dishes are another natural fit, since the wine's own fruit echoes those bright, acidic flavors. Serve it well chilled, around 8-10°C, and drink it young. Avoid pairing it with heavy cream sauces or big reds' usual partners like red meat, where it'll just get lost.

Why does Torrontés taste so floral and fruity?

Torrontés is a Muscat cross, and Muscat's signature is linalool and geraniol, aromatic terpenes that read as orange blossom, jasmine and lychee before you even taste the wine.
