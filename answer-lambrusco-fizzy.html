Why does Lambrusco taste so fizzy and fruity?

Short Answer

The fizz comes from how it's made, not from anything mystical in the grape. Most Lambrusco goes through a second fermentation in a sealed tank (the Charmat or ancestral method), where the CO2 produced by yeast gets trapped in the wine instead of escaping, giving you that light, foamy sparkle rather than fine Champagne-style bubbles. The Lambrusco family itself (there are several biotypes grown around Emilia-Romagna) is naturally high in acid and loaded with dark berry, violet and rhubarb-like fruit, and because maceration is short, tannin stays low. That combination, bright acid, soft tannin, juicy fruit, and gentle carbonation, reads as fun and easy rather than serious. One correction worth making: not all Lambrusco is sweet. Secco versions are properly dry, amabile ones have a touch of sugar, and both get their liveliness from the same fermentation trick. Serve it cold, around 10 to 12 degrees, straight out of the fridge, and don't overthink it.
