Why does Stellenbosch red wine taste so structured yet earthy?

Short Answer

Structure comes from two things working together: mountains and granite. Stellenbosch is ringed by peaks like the Helderberg and Simonsberg, which trap cool air pushed in from False Bay and create a real day-night temperature swing. Grapes ripen slowly, building tannin and phenolic weight without losing acidity, which is why the reds have backbone rather than just fruit. The earthy note underneath comes from the soil itself: decomposed granite breaks down into clay and quartz that lend a savory, almost stony undertone beneath the black fruit. It's not Old World tension or New World ripeness, it's both stacked on top of each other, which is why a Stellenbosch Cabernet or Bordeaux-style blend can taste generous and tightly wound in the same sip. Give these wines air, a decant of thirty minutes to an hour helps, and serve reds around 16 to 18°C rather than room temperature, which in most houses runs too warm and mutes the structure entirely.
