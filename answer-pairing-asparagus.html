QA-PAIRING-ASPARAGUS

Wine with asparagus?

Topic

Riesling

Difficulty

Beginner

Short Answer

Sauvignon Blanc or Grüner Veltliner. Asparagus is famously hard to pair because its compounds can make many wines taste bitter, metallic or strangely vegetal. Wines with their own green, herbal notes handle it best.

Sauvignon Blanc from the Loire or New Zealand already tastes of cut grass and green pepper, so it meets asparagus on the same terms.

Grüner Veltliner is the classic match in Austria, where asparagus season is celebrated every spring. Its peppery, green notes work well.

In Germany, asparagus season is just as big, and dry Silvaner or dry Riesling are traditional partners.

How you prepare it matters.

With hollandaise or butter, you need a white with a little more richness: an unoaked Chardonnay, Pinot Blanc or a dry Muscat from Alsace.

Grilled or roasted asparagus picks up smoky, sweeter notes. A dry rosé or a crisp Sauvignon Blanc works well.

In risotto or with cheese, try a Soave or a lightly textured Chardonnay.

What to avoid: oaky whites and most red wines, which tend to taste harsh and metallic next to asparagus.

If you're unsure, pick a crisp, unoaked white with high acidity. It's the safest choice.
