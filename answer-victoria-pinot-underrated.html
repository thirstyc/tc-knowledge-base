Why does Victoria Pinot Noir fly under the radar?

Short Answer

Australia sold the world on Shiraz, big, ripe, oak-forward bottlings from the Barossa and McLaren Vale, and that image is still what most people picture when they hear 'Australian red.' Cooler Victorian sites are making something almost opposite: lighter-bodied, savoury, Burgundian-leaning Pinot Noir that doesn't fit the sunburnt-country stereotype at all, so it gets overlooked by drinkers who never think to look past the big names. It's also a matter of scale, these cool pockets (Mornington Peninsula, higher Yarra Valley sites, Geelong) are small compared to the volume regions, so there's less of it and less marketing muscle behind it. The wines can genuinely compete with mid-tier Burgundy on structure and elegance, often at a friendlier price, but Australia's reputation was built on the opposite style, so the country's own success with Shiraz has made it harder for its best Pinot to get noticed.
