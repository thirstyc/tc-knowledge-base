What pairs with Barolo or Barbaresco?

Short Answer

Barolo and Barbaresco are both Nebbiolo, a grape that produces wines with high tannin and high acid but relatively pale color and light-seeming body, a combination that confuses people expecting a big dark red to taste like one. That tannin and acid need fat and umami to soften against, which is exactly what Piedmont's own cuisine provides. Truffle risotto works because the earthy, funky truffle aroma mirrors the wine's own tar-and-rose complexity while the creamy rice coats the palate and tames the tannin. Braised beef, slow-cooked until the connective tissue breaks down into gelatin, gives the wine fat and richness to grip onto instead of just biting back. Aged Parmesan, salty and dense with umami, does the same job in a smaller bite. Serve these wines around 16-18°C, and give a young bottle real time in a decanter, an hour or more, since the tannins need air almost as much as the food does.
