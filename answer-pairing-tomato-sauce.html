Wine pairing with tomato sauce?

Topic

Nebbiolo

Difficulty

Beginner

Short Answer

A red with high acidity, like Sangiovese, Barbera or Montepulciano d'Abruzzo. Tomatoes are acidic, and a wine with less acidity than the sauce tastes flat and flabby next to it.

Italian reds are the natural fit, because they grew up alongside tomato sauce. Chianti and other Sangiovese-based wines have bright cherry and herbal notes that sit perfectly with tomato. Barbera is juicy and low in tannin, great for everyday pasta and pizza. Montepulciano d'Abruzzo is softer and very good value.

Match the sauce's weight too:

  • A light, fresh marinara suits a lighter red or even a dry rosé.
  • A long-cooked meat sauce or ragù can take Chianti Classico, Langhe Nebbiolo or Primitivo.
  • Spicy arrabbiata wants a fruity, lower-alcohol red, so the heat doesn't build.

Beyond pasta, the same logic works for pizza, shakshuka, tomato-based stews and eggplant parmigiana.

Prefer white? Choose one with lots of acidity, like Vermentino, Verdicchio or Sauvignon Blanc. They work especially well with tomato and seafood dishes.

What to avoid: low-acid, oaky wines, which the tomato makes taste dull and heavy.
