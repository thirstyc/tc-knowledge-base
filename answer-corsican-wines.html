What are Corsican wines?

Short Answer

Corsica, the mountainous French island in the Mediterranean, makes distinctive wines from grapes shared with Italy, especially its own signature reds, Nielluccio and Sciaccarello, and the white Vermentino.

The island's history under Genoese rule before becoming French shows in its grapes:

  • Nielluccio: the island's main red, closely related to Sangiovese. It makes structured, savoury reds and rosés, especially in Patrimonio in the north.
  • Sciaccarello: a Corsican specialty with a pale colour, peppery spice and red fruit, especially around Ajaccio on the west coast.
  • Vermentino, called Vermentinu locally: crisp, herbal, saline whites.

Other important areas include Figari and Sartène in the south, and Cap Corse, known for the sweet Muscat du Cap Corse.

Rosé is a big part of production, much of it pale, dry and Provence-like.

The island's mountains, granite and schist soils, and sea breezes keep the wines fresh despite the hot sun. Many vineyards are small, family-run and farmed organically.

Corsican wines rarely leave France in large quantities, so they're still a discovery for most drinkers, and often good value.

At the table: Vermentinu with seafood, Sciaccarello with charcuterie and grilled fish, Nielluccio with lamb and wild boar.
