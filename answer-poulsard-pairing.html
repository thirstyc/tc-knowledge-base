What pairs with Poulsard?

Short Answer

Because Poulsard is so light-bodied and low in tannin, it basically does double duty as a red that drinks like a rosé, which makes it flexible at the table in a way heavier reds aren't. It's a natural with charcuterie and Comté, the classic Jura combination, since the wine's savory, earthy notes echo the nutty, aged cheese rather than fighting it. Grilled or roasted poultry works well too, as does fresh pasta with mushroom or herb sauces, where the earthiness has something to latch onto. Delicate fish like trout or even salmon can work better than you'd expect from a red, because there's no heavy tannin to clash with the fish's texture. Serve it lightly chilled, around 12-14°C, and treat it the way you'd treat a good rosé or Beaujolais: as an easy, food-friendly wine rather than something to reach for with a steak.

Why does Poulsard taste so light and earthy?

Poulsard, sometimes spelled Ploussard, has famously thin skins and low pigment content, so even though it's technically a red grape it often looks closer to a dark rosé in the glass.
