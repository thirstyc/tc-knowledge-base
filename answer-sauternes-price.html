Why is Sauternes so expensive?

Short Answer

Because almost nothing about making it is efficient. Noble rot doesn't develop evenly across a vineyard, or even across a single vine, so pickers have to go through by hand multiple times over several weeks, picking only the berries that have shrivelled correctly that day and leaving the rest to keep developing. That's the opposite of a normal harvest, where you pick a block once and move on. Yields end up tiny, since a shrivelled botrytised grape has lost most of its water weight, and vintages where the fungus doesn't cooperate at all can mean a much smaller crop or none worth bottling. Add barrel aging, which most serious Sauternes goes through, and you're paying for labour, risk and time all at once. It's genuinely one of the most hands-on styles of winemaking that still exists at any real scale, which is part of why prices haven't followed the rest of the sweet wine market downward.
