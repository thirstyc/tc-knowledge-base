Why is Vin Santo expensive?

Short Answer

The grapes get dried on straw mats or hung from rafters for months after harvest, losing most of their water weight before they're ever pressed. That alone tanks the yield you get per hectare. Then the must goes into tiny sealed barrels called caratelli, often stashed in an attic where temperature swings all year, and fermentation crawls along for years instead of weeks. Some producers use a governo system, seeding each batch with a bit of "mother" from the last one, like a sourdough starter. Every step is slow, hands-on, and shrinks the volume further: less juice, less wine, more barrel space and cellar time tied up per bottle. That's before you factor in the losses to evaporation over years in wood. You're paying for a process that fights against efficiency at every stage, not for a fancy label.
