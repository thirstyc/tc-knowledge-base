Best wine under $30?

Short Answer

This is the sweet spot for wine value. Around $20–30 you move past everyday bottles into wines with real character, from good producers in less famous regions and entry-level wines from famous ones.

Where your money goes furthest:

  • Reds: Crianza and Reserva Rioja, Chianti Classico, Côtes du Rhône Villages and named villages like Rasteau or Vacqueyras, cru Beaujolais like Morgon or Fleurie, Douro reds, and Barbera d'Alba.
  • Whites: Chablis, Sancerre's neighbours Menetou-Salon and Quincy, Mâcon whites, German Riesling Kabinett, Austrian Grüner Veltliner and Albariño from Rías Baixas.
  • Sparkling: Crémant from Alsace, Burgundy or the Loire, and good Cava.
  • New World: cool-climate Chilean and South African whites, Oregon and New Zealand Pinot Noir at the entry level, and Australian Riesling.

What's usually not worth it at this price: bottles from the most famous names (Burgundy villages, Napa Cabernet, Champagne), which often need more money to be good.

Ask your local shop for their favourites in this range. Merchants love this price point because it's where they can surprise people most.
