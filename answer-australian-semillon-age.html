Can you age Australian Semillon?

Short Answer

Yes, and it's one of the strangest, most reliable aging tricks in wine. Young Hunter Valley Semillon is deliberately picked early, low in alcohol, often around 10-11%, with no oak and searing acidity, so on release it tastes almost austere: lemon, green apple, not much else going on. That's the point. Over 10, 15, even 20 years in bottle, with no help from oak or lees work, it transforms on its own into something rich and complex, honeyed, toasty, waxy, with notes of buttered toast and beeswax, while somehow keeping that original acid spine intact. It's one of the few whites in the world that does this transformation from raw materials alone rather than through winemaking technique. Because the starting wines are cheap and the aging happens in your cellar rather than the winery's, well-aged bottles are a genuine bargain compared to equivalent white Burgundy. If you find an old bottle, don't be put off by the deep gold colour, that's exactly what it's supposed to look like. Serve it a touch warmer than you would a young white, around 10-12°C.
