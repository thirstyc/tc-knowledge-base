Why is Marzemino hard to find outside the Alps?

Short Answer

Marzemino is a genuinely obscure grape outside its home turf because it's planted almost nowhere else in meaningful quantity. It's a cool-climate variety that needs the specific diurnal temperature swings of Trentino's foothills to ripen properly while keeping its acidity, and it doesn't transplant well to warmer, flatter regions where most large-scale wine production happens. Yields are modest, the growing area is small, and there's no large export infrastructure built around it the way there is for Chianti or Barolo. Most bottles get consumed regionally, in the trattorias and homes of the area that grows it, rather than shipped abroad. It's also not a grape that ages into something more marketable over time, so producers have little incentive to hold back stock for international distribution. If you've heard of it at all, it might be from Mozart's Don Giovanni, where it gets a specific mention, which is a strange kind of fame for a wine that most people will never actually taste.
