Why is Sancerre expensive?

Short Answer

Sancerre's appellation covers a modest stretch of hillside around a handful of villages on the Loire, and unlike Bordeaux or Burgundy it can't simply expand production to meet demand - the chalk and flint soils that define the wine only exist within that footprint. Add steep slopes that often require hand-harvesting, plus yields naturally capped by terroir and appellation rules, and you get a hard ceiling on supply that global thirst for crisp Sauvignon Blanc keeps slamming into. Reputation doesn't hurt either - Sancerre became shorthand for serious Sauvignon Blanc decades before New Zealand entered the conversation, so it commands a premium that has as much to do with scarcity and name as with production cost. You're not paying for oak barrels or extended aging - most Sancerre sees neither - you're paying for a small patch of French hillside that everyone wants a piece of. If you want the same acid-driven tension for less, look to neighbouring Loire appellations riding on Sancerre's coattails without the name.
