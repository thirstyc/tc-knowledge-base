What pairs with Israeli wine?

Short Answer

Israeli reds tend to be built around ripe, concentrated fruit with real tannic structure, which is exactly what you need to cut through grilled lamb, especially anything with cumin, garlic or a char from live fire. The fat in the lamb softens the tannins, the tannins cut the fat, and the spice doesn't fight the wine because the fruit is already dense enough to stand up to it. Hummus and mezze spreads work differently: they're about acidity and texture rather than power, so lighter reds or a structured rosé with decent acid keep things fresh rather than heavy, since tahini and olive oil can otherwise coat the palate and flatten a big wine. Roasted vegetables, especially eggplant and peppers with some char, pick up smoky, savory notes that echo oak-aged reds nicely. The common thread is a full Mediterranean table with olive oil, char, and spice everywhere, so you want wines with enough fruit and structure to keep pace rather than something delicate that just disappears.
