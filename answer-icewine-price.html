Why is Icewine so expensive?

Short Answer

To qualify as true Icewine, grapes have to stay on the vine well into winter and be picked while still frozen solid, usually at temperatures of around -8 degrees Celsius or colder, which in most regions means harvesting by hand in the middle of the night when it's finally cold enough. Frozen grapes contain very little liquid, since the water has turned to ice and stays behind in the press, so what comes out is a tiny trickle of intensely concentrated, sugar-and-acid-rich juice. It can take several times the normal amount of fruit to produce a single bottle. On top of that, growers are gambling an entire crop on the weather cooperating, birds, rot, and early winter thaws can wipe out the harvest before it ever gets cold enough to pick. That combination of low yield, labor-intensive night harvesting, and real risk of losing the whole crop is why Icewine carries a price tag closer to a luxury good than a table wine.
