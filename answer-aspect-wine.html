How does aspect (sun exposure) affect wine?

Short Answer

Aspect is the direction a vineyard slope faces, and it controls how much sun and warmth the vines receive. In cool regions, the right aspect can decide whether grapes ripen at all.

In the Northern Hemisphere:

  • South-facing slopes get the most sun and warmth, ideal in cool climates.
  • East-facing slopes get gentle morning sun and dry off dew early, reducing disease, while avoiding the hottest afternoon heat.
  • West-facing slopes get the hot afternoon sun, useful in cool places but risky in warm ones.
  • North-facing slopes are coolest, sometimes used in hot regions to keep freshness.

In the Southern Hemisphere, north-facing slopes are the warmest.

Slope steepness matters too. A steep slope facing the sun receives light more directly, drains cold air and water away, and warms faster.

Famous examples:

  • Germany's Mosel, where the best Riesling grows on very steep, south-facing slate slopes above the river.
  • Burgundy's Côte d'Or, where the top vineyards sit on east and southeast-facing slopes.
  • The Northern Rhône's Côte-Rôtie, meaning "roasted slope".

In warmer regions, growers increasingly choose cooler aspects to preserve acidity and avoid overripe, high-alcohol wines.

Aspect is one reason neighbouring vineyards can make very different wines.
