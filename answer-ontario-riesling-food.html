What pairs with Ontario Riesling?

Short Answer

Riesling's whole appeal at the table is that sharp acidity, and in Ontario's cool-climate versions it stays high even when the wine has some ripe stone-fruit or citrus flavour. That acid does real work with food: it cuts through the fat in oily fish like salmon or mackerel, and it resets your palate between bites of anything rich or buttery. With Asian dishes that carry chili heat, a Riesling with a touch of residual sugar is especially useful, since a little sweetness genuinely dulls the burn of capsaicin while the acidity keeps the wine from tasting cloying. That's why Riesling turns up so often alongside Thai, Vietnamese, or Szechuan food, it's one of the few whites that can stand up to real spice without falling apart. Shellfish and raw oysters are another natural match, since the wine's acidity plays the same role lemon juice would. Serve it cold, around 8 degrees, colder than most whites, to keep that acid feeling crisp rather than tart.

Why is Ontario Riesling emerging?

Riesling has actually been in Niagara for decades, but for a long time the region's Riesling story was really the ice wine story: grapes left on the vine into winter, frozen, and pressed into small amounts of intensely sweet wine that became Canada's signature export.
