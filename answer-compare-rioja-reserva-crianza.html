What is Rioja Reserva vs Crianza?

Short Answer

Both are aged Rioja, and the difference is time. Crianza is aged for at least two years, Reserva for at least three, and the extra year gives Reserva more depth and savoury character.

The legal minimums for red Rioja:

  • Crianza: two years of ageing, at least one of them in oak barrels.
  • Reserva: three years of ageing, at least one in oak and six months in bottle.
  • Gran Reserva: five years, at least two in oak.

Producers often age their wines longer than the minimum, and Reservas are typically made from better grapes or vineyards, not just held back longer.

In the glass, Crianza is fresher and fruitier: red cherry and plum, with some vanilla and spice from the oak. It's an ideal everyday food wine.

Reserva is rounder and more complex, with dried fruit, leather, tobacco and a softer texture, as the oak and bottle time integrate.

At the table, Crianza suits tapas, burgers, pizza and grilled chorizo. Reserva is better with roast lamb, game and aged Manchego.

Price follows the ageing. Reserva costs more, but still offers some of the best value in aged red wine anywhere.
