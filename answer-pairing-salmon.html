What wine goes with salmon?

Short Answer

Pinot Noir. Salmon is one of the few fish rich enough for red wine, and Pinot's low tannin and bright acidity won't make it taste metallic.

Grilled, roasted or pan-seared salmon is the classic match for Pinot Noir from Oregon, Burgundy or New Zealand. A dry rosé works just as well, especially in summer.

Richer preparations, like salmon in butter or cream sauce, pair well with a fuller white: lightly oaked Chardonnay or a white Burgundy such as Meursault.

Smoked salmon wants bubbles or bright acidity. Champagne, especially a Blanc de Blancs, is a classic, and a dry Riesling or Sauvignon Blanc also works.

Raw salmon in sushi, sashimi or tartare is delicate and clean. Choose a crisp, unoaked white like Chablis, Albariño or Grüner Veltliner.

Teriyaki or miso-glazed salmon brings sweetness and umami. A juicy Pinot Noir or an off-dry Riesling handles both.

Spicy salmon dishes suit an off-dry Riesling too.

What to avoid: big, tannic reds like young Cabernet Sauvignon. The tannin clashes with the fish oils and leaves a metallic taste.
