Wine with butter sauce?

Short Answer

A white with enough acidity to cut the richness, or enough body to match it: Chardonnay, Chenin Blanc, white Burgundy or Champagne.

Butter sauces are rich but delicate, so the wine shouldn't overwhelm them. There are two good approaches:

  • Match the richness: a lightly oaked Chardonnay or white Burgundy echoes the buttery texture. This is the classic partner for lobster or scallops in butter.
  • Cut through it: a high-acid white like Chablis, Chenin Blanc, Muscadet or sparkling wine refreshes the palate between bites.

By sauce:

  • Beurre blanc, with shallots, vinegar and white wine: Muscadet, Sancerre or Chablis. It was created in the Loire, and Loire whites are the natural match.
  • Hollandaise and béarnaise: Chardonnay, or Champagne with eggs Benedict. With steak béarnaise, a medium-bodied red like Pinot Noir or a Bordeaux works.
  • Brown butter and sage, with pasta or gnocchi: Chardonnay, Pinot Gris or a light Pinot Noir.
  • Lemon butter with fish: Chablis, Albariño or Sauvignon Blanc.

Avoid tannic reds, which clash with butter and fish, and very aromatic whites, which can smother a subtle sauce.
