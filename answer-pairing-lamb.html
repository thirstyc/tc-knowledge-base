What wine pairs with lamb?

Short Answer

A structured red with some savoury character: Bordeaux, Rioja, Syrah or a Grenache blend. Lamb is rich and a little gamey, so it wants a wine with enough tannin and flavour to match.

Roast leg or rack of lamb is a classic partner for red Bordeaux, especially from Pauillac, where Cabernet's cedar and blackcurrant meet the meat's richness. A Rioja Reserva works just as well and often costs less.

Herbs change the picture. Rosemary, thyme and garlic point toward the southern Rhône: Côtes du Rhône, Gigondas or Châteauneuf-du-Pape, where Grenache and Syrah carry the same garrigue notes.

Slow-cooked shoulder and tagines with cumin, cinnamon and dried fruit suit riper, spicier reds like Grenache, Malbec or a Barossa Shiraz.

Lamb chops and kebabs off the grill love Syrah. The char brings out its smoky, peppery side.

Pink, delicately cooked lamb can take a lighter red too: a fuller Pinot Noir from Burgundy or Central Otago is lovely here.

Mint sauce is the tricky one. Its sugar and vinegar flatten most reds, so go light on it, or pick a Cabernet that already has a minty edge, like one from Coonawarra.

Wine with Indian food?

Aromatic, off-dry whites like Riesling and Gewürztraminer handle most Indian dishes, because a touch of sweetness balances chile heat and their perfume matches the spices.
