Is this wine safe to drink?

Short Answer

Almost always yes. Wine that has gone bad tastes unpleasant, but the alcohol and acidity make it a hostile environment for the bacteria that cause food poisoning.

A corked, oxidised or vinegary wine is unpleasant rather than dangerous. You can cook with it or pour it away, but you won't get ill from a sip.

The situations where you should be more careful:

  • Visible mould inside the bottle or on the wine's surface, rather than on the outside of the cork.
  • A bottle that has been opened and left at room temperature for weeks, especially a low-alcohol or sweet one.
  • Any bottle where the seal has clearly failed and the contents may have been exposed to contamination.
  • A bottle that fizzes unexpectedly and smells actively foul, which can indicate a refermentation.

Mould on the outside of a cork is normal in a damp cellar and doesn't affect the wine.

When in doubt, use the ordinary food rule: if it smells genuinely foul rather than just disappointing, don't drink it.

If you have a specific health concern, ask a doctor rather than relying on general guidance.
