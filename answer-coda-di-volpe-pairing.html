What pairs with Coda di Volpe?

Short Answer

The acidity that defines Coda di Volpe is also what makes it so useful at the table. Seafood is the obvious match, especially anything raw or lightly cooked, since the wine's citrus edge does the same job a squeeze of lemon would. Fried food is where it really earns its keep though, particularly Campania's own fritto misto, because high acidity cuts through oil the way a heavier, flatter white can't. Fresh pasta with a light seafood or vegetable sauce works for the same reason. Soft, young cheeses, ricotta or a fresh mozzarella, play well too since the wine's brightness balances their creaminess instead of getting lost in it. What you want to avoid is anything heavily spiced or richly sauced, since this isn't a wine with the weight or residual sweetness to stand up to that. It's a food wine in the most literal sense, built to react with what's on the plate rather than stand alone.

Why does Coda di Volpe taste so bright and crisp?

Coda di Volpe comes from Campania, mostly grown on the volcanic soils around Vesuvius and in the hills of Irpinia, and both the soil and the altitude are doing real work here.
