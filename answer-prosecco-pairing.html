What pairs with Prosecco?

Short Answer

Think texture and contrast, not deep matching. Prosecco's low alcohol, bright acidity, and fine mousse make it a palate cleanser more than a food wine in the structured sense, so it works best alongside salty, fried, or creamy appetizers where the bubbles cut through richness: think fritto misto, prosciutto, soft cheeses, or a simple bruschetta. It's the default aperitivo because the sugar level (usually extra dry or brut) is gentle enough not to clash with whatever comes next, and the fizz resets your palate between bites. Avoid pairing it with anything too delicate or too intensely flavoured; it doesn't have the acid backbone or concentration to stand up to rich sauces or aged cheese the way a vintage Champagne might. Serve it well chilled, around 6-8°C, in a simple tulip glass rather than a flat coupe, which lets the bubbles and aroma last longer. The point of the wine is momentum, not scrutiny: pour it, talk over it, keep the glasses topped up.
