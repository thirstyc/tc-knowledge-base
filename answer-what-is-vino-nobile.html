What is Vino Nobile di Montepulciano?

Short Answer

Vino Nobile di Montepulciano is a red wine from the hilltop town of Montepulciano in southern Tuscany, made mostly from Sangiovese. It sits between Chianti and Brunello di Montalcino in style and price.

The name causes confusion. It has nothing to do with the Montepulciano grape, which makes Montepulciano d'Abruzzo on Italy's Adriatic coast. Vino Nobile is named after the town, and the grape is Sangiovese, known locally as Prugnolo Gentile.

The rules require at least 70% Sangiovese, often blended with small amounts of other local or international grapes. The wine must be aged for at least two years before release, and Riserva for at least three.

In the glass, expect red cherry, plum, dried herbs and earthy, savoury notes, with firm tannins and bright acidity. It's usually a little softer and rounder than Brunello, and more structured than most Chianti.

It's one of Italy's oldest named wines, praised for centuries, and a DOCG, the country's highest classification.

For something younger and cheaper from the same place, try Rosso di Montepulciano.

Serve it with roast meats, wild boar ragù, mushroom dishes or aged Pecorino.
