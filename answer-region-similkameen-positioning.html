Why do winemakers rave about Similkameen?

Short Answer

Organic and biodynamic farming isn't a marketing checkbox here, it's close to the default — a large share of the valley's vineyards farm this way, partly because the desert-dry climate makes it genuinely feasible: low humidity means less disease pressure, so growers don't need the chemical arsenal a damper region would require. Combine that with dramatic terrain — a narrow valley, steep benches, rattlesnake-and-sagebrush scrubland pressed right up against the vines — and you get wines with real specificity, farmed by people who are there because they want to make something particular, not because they're chasing volume. Production is tiny compared to the Okanagan next door, so most of it never leaves British Columbia, let alone Canada. That scarcity, paired with the farming philosophy, is exactly why sommeliers and importers talk about it the way people used to talk about Priorat or the Jura before everyone caught on — a small, serious region making distinctive wine the broader market hasn't found yet. Grab a bottle if you see one; it won't be there next visit.

What pairs with Similkameen Syrah or Riesling?

Similkameen Syrah runs peppery, dark-fruited and full-bodied thanks to that valley's huge diurnal swing — hot days build sugar and flavour, cold nights lock in acidity, so the wine ends up powerful but never flabby.
