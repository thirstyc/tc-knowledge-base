Why does Washington Syrah fly under the radar?

Short Answer

Washington built its reputation on Riesling for whites and Cabernet Sauvignon for reds, so Syrah never got its own marketing moment even though the Columbia Valley's climate, hot days, cold nights, low rainfall, suits it beautifully. Producers also send mixed signals: some make lean, peppery, Northern Rhone-style Syrah, others go riper and more Australian in style, so there's no single flavour profile for consumers to latch onto the way there is with Napa Cab or Oregon Pinot. Add to that Washington's tiny production compared to California and its wines rarely leave the Pacific Northwest and a couple of export markets, so most drinkers simply never encounter it. That obscurity is good news for anyone who does track it down: quality is high, ambition is high, and because the grape isn't chasing a trend, prices haven't caught up to what's in the bottle. It's one of the better values in serious North American red wine right now.
