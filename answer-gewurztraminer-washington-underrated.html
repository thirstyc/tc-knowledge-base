Why don't more people talk about Washington Gewürztraminer compared to Riesling from the same region?

Short Answer

Riesling got the marketing push in Washington because it's more versatile across dry-to-sweet styles and ages better, so it became the cool-climate white the state built its reputation on, especially out of sites like the Yakima Valley. Gewürztraminer got left in Riesling's shadow even though the growing conditions suit it just as well, that same big day-night temperature swing preserves acid while letting the grape's naturally intense aromatics develop fully. Part of the problem is genuinely the grape's own reputation, a lot of people had a bad experience with a cheap, cloying bottle years ago and wrote off the variety entirely, which is a shame because a well-made dry or off-dry version from a serious producer is bright, textured, and food-friendly rather than sweet and clumsy. It's also just a hard name to sell, nobody wants to ask for it by name at a bar. If you find one, drink it within two to three years of the vintage, this isn't a wine built for the cellar, it's built for a Tuesday dinner with something spicy on the table.
