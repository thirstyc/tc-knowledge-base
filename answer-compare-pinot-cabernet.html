Pinot Noir vs Cabernet Sauvignon?

Short Answer

They sit at opposite ends of the red wine spectrum. Pinot Noir is light, silky and perfumed; Cabernet Sauvignon is dark, firm and structured.

It starts with the grape skins. Pinot Noir has thin skins, so it makes pale wines with gentle tannins. Cabernet has thick skins, giving deep colour and plenty of tannin.

Flavours differ too. Pinot Noir tastes of red cherry, raspberry and, with age, mushroom and forest floor. Cabernet leans black: blackcurrant, cedar, graphite and often a minty or herbal note.

In the vineyard, Pinot Noir is fussy and needs a cool climate; Cabernet ripens late and prefers warmth, which is why it thrives in Bordeaux, Napa and Coonawarra.

At the table, Pinot Noir is the more flexible of the two. It works with salmon, chicken, duck and mushrooms. Cabernet wants fat and protein: steak, lamb, aged cheese.

Both age well, but differently. Cabernet softens over a decade or more; Pinot Noir trades fruit for savoury complexity.

Which to choose? If you want elegance and food-friendliness, Pinot Noir. If you want power and structure, Cabernet Sauvignon.
