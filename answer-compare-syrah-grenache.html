Syrah vs Grenache?

Short Answer

They're the two pillars of the Rhône Valley and they play opposite roles: Syrah brings colour, structure and pepper; Grenache brings ripe fruit, warmth and alcohol.

Syrah is dark and firm, with blackberry, black pepper, olive and smoke. In the northern Rhône it makes serious, age-worthy wines.

Grenache is paler with softer tannins, tasting of strawberry, raspberry and dried herbs. It ripens late and builds a lot of sugar, so its wines are often 14.5% alcohol or more.

That contrast is why they're blended in the southern Rhône, in wines like Châteauneuf-du-Pape and Côtes du Rhône, often with Mourvèdre. Grenache fills the middle, Syrah adds backbone and colour.

With food, Syrah suits grilled meat, peppercorn sauce and smoky dishes. Grenache is the friendlier one, good with lamb, roast vegetables, charcuterie and spiced stews.

Grenache also makes some of the best rosé, especially in Provence and the southern Rhône, and sweet fortified wines in Roussillon.

Which to choose? Syrah for structure and spice; Grenache for warmth and easy drinking.
