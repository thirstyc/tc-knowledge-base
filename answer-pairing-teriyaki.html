Wine with teriyaki?

Short Answer

A fruity, low-tannin wine that can handle the sauce's sweetness and salt: off-dry Riesling, Pinot Noir, Gamay or a juicy Zinfandel.

Teriyaki glaze is sweet, salty and savoury, made from soy, sugar and mirin. That sweetness is the key challenge, since it makes dry, tannic wines taste thin and bitter.

By protein:

  • Teriyaki chicken: off-dry Riesling, Gewürztraminer or a light Pinot Noir.
  • Teriyaki salmon: Pinot Noir is the classic match. Its bright fruit and gentle tannin suit the rich fish and glaze. A dry rosé also works.
  • Teriyaki beef: a fruit-forward red like Zinfandel, Grenache, Merlot or a ripe Shiraz.
  • Teriyaki tofu or vegetables: off-dry Riesling or Chenin Blanc.

Why off-dry works: a touch of sweetness in the wine matches the glaze, and the high acidity keeps things fresh and balances the salt.

What to avoid: bone-dry, tannic reds like young Cabernet Sauvignon or Nebbiolo, which turn bitter against sugar and soy, and heavily oaked whites.

If the dish is grilled with smoky char, a slightly chilled fruity red is especially good.
