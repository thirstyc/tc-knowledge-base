Why is Jura the natural wine world's favorite region?

Short Answer

Because Jura was already doing the things natural wine fans care about, long before anyone gave them a name.

Jura is a small, cool region in eastern France, tucked between Burgundy and the Swiss border. It was never fashionable, which meant nobody pressured its growers to make wine the way the rest of the world did. Old habits survived: indigenous grapes, old barrels, very little intervention.

Its most famous wine proves the point. Vin Jaune is made from Savagnin and aged in barrels that are never topped up. A film of yeast called the voile forms on the surface and protects the wine as it slowly evaporates. It can't be released until more than six years after the harvest, and the result is nutty, salty and completely unlike anything else. By conventional rules it should be a fault. Here it is the region's pride.

The reds are just as off-script. Poulsard is so pale it can pass for rosé, and Trousseau is spicy and light on its feet. Both are the kind of juicy, low-tannin wines natural wine bars love to pour.

Then there are the people. Growers around Arbois, Pierre Overnoy most famously, were making wine with little or no added sulfur decades ago, and a younger generation followed them.

So Jura didn't join the natural wine movement. The movement found Jura and realised it had been there all along.
