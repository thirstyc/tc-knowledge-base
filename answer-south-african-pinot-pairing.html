What pairs with South African Pinot Noir?

Short Answer

Its lighter body and bright acidity make it a food wine rather than a sipping-alone wine. Grilled or seared salmon works because the wine's acid cuts the oil without a big tannic clash. Roast chicken or duck breast is the classic match — the wine's red-fruit brightness plays well against poultry fat, and duck's slight gaminess actually flatters the wine's earthier notes. Mushrooms, whether in a risotto or simply sautéed, echo Pinot's savoury, forest-floor undertones almost eerily well — this is one of the few grape-and-ingredient pairings that isn't hype. Avoid heavy cream sauces or anything too rich; South African Pinot doesn't have the tannic backbone to cut through fat the way a Cabernet would. Serve it slightly cooler than room temperature, around 14-16°C — too warm and the alcohol and fruit blur together, losing the acidity that makes it work at the table. Give it a light chill in the fridge for twenty minutes before pouring if it's been sitting out.
