Wine with Indian food?

Short Answer

Aromatic, off-dry whites like Riesling and Gewürztraminer handle most Indian dishes, because a touch of sweetness balances chile heat and their perfume matches the spices.

Indian cooking is too varied for a single answer, so think about heat, richness and spice.

Mild, creamy dishes like korma, butter chicken and malai kofta suit a fuller white: Viognier, Pinot Gris or an off-dry Chenin Blanc.

Tomato-based dishes like tikka masala and rogan josh can take a dry rosé or a soft, fruity red with low tannin, such as Grenache or Gamay.

Hot dishes like vindaloo and chicken 65 need low alcohol and a little sweetness. Off-dry Riesling is the safest choice.

Tandoori and grilled dishes are smoky and less saucy, and pair well with a dry rosé, Grenache or even a light Syrah if the spice is moderate.

Vegetable dishes and dals are earthier and often work with Grüner Veltliner or a dry rosé.

Sparkling wine is a good all-rounder: bubbles and acidity refresh the palate between spicy bites.

What to avoid: high-alcohol, oaky or very tannic wines. Alcohol makes chile burn more, and tannin turns bitter against spice.
