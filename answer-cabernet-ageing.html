How long does Cabernet age?

Short Answer

It depends on the wine. Everyday Cabernet Sauvignon is best within 3–5 years, good regional bottles improve for 10–15, and the top wines from Bordeaux, Napa and similar regions can go 20–50 years or more.

What lets it age is structure: high tannin, good acidity and concentrated fruit. Over time, tannins link up and drop out as sediment, so the wine softens, and the flavours shift from fresh blackcurrant toward cedar, tobacco, leather and dried fruit.

A rough guide:

  • Supermarket-level Cabernet: drink now, up to about 5 years.
  • Serious regional bottles: 10–15 years.
  • Classified Bordeaux and top Napa: 20 years and beyond.

Storage matters as much as the wine. Keep bottles cool (around 12–14°C), dark, and away from temperature swings. A warm cupboard will age a wine much faster, and not in a good way.

Vintage matters too: cooler, structured years usually last longer than very ripe ones.

If you're unsure whether a bottle is ready, buy two. Open one and see.

And remember most wine, including most Cabernet, is made to be drunk young.
