Why does Ratafia taste so fortified and sweet?

Short Answer

Ratafia isn't really a wine in the fermented sense — it's grape juice that never got the chance to become one. Producers in Champagne (and parts of Burgundy) take fresh, unfermented must and blend it with grape spirit, usually their own marc, pushing the alcohol up to somewhere around 18%. That alcohol shock kills off the yeast before fermentation can start, so all the grape's natural sugar stays locked in rather than being converted to alcohol and dryness. The result tastes like grape juice with a serious kick: sweet, syrupy, often with notes of dried fruit, honey and spice from a bit of barrel or bottle age. It's the same logic behind Pineau des Charentes or Floc de Gascogne, just done with Champagne fruit. Serve it cold, around 8-10°C, in a small glass — it's sipped, not gulped, closer to a fortified aperitif than a table wine.
