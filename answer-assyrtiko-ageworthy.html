Can you age Assyrtiko?

Short Answer

Yes, and it surprises people every time. Assyrtiko comes from Santorini, where the vines are trained low in coiled basket shapes called kouloura to protect the grapes from constant wind, growing in volcanic soil made of ash and pumice that gives the wine its distinct saline, flinty edge. That volcanic minerality comes paired with serious acidity, and acidity is what lets a white wine age — it's the preservative. Over five to ten years, Assyrtiko moves away from its youthful citrus and sea-spray character toward something closer to Riesling at maturity: honeyed, waxy, sometimes with that petrol-like note Riesling drinkers will recognize. Most people drink it young and never see this side of it, which is the real shame, since it's one of the few whites from a hot Mediterranean climate that genuinely rewards patience. Serve young bottles around 10°C, and let older ones warm slightly, closer to 12°C, to show that developed complexity.
