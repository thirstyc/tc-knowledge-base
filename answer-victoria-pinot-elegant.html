Why does Victoria Pinot Noir taste so elegant and structured?

Short Answer

Victoria isn't one climate, it's a patchwork, and the good Pinot Noir comes from its coolest corners: Mornington Peninsula, the higher slopes of the Yarra Valley, Geelong. Ocean proximity and elevation both slow the ripening clock, so the grapes hang longer without piling on sugar and alcohol. Cool nights protect acidity, and Pinot Noir's thin skins mean gentle handling doesn't drag out heavy tannin the way it would with a thicker-skinned grape. The result is fruit that stays in red territory, cherry and cranberry rather than stewed plum, with the earthy, mushroomy undertone that good Pinot develops instead of raw fruit power. Structure comes from acid and fine-grained tannin working together, not brute extraction. Serve it a touch cool, 14 to 16°C, to keep that freshness intact. Worth knowing: inland, warmer pockets of Victoria make heavier, riper styles, so 'Victoria Pinot' on a label isn't a guarantee of this profile.
