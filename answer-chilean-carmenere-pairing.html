What pairs with Chilean Carménère?

Short Answer

Carménère's structure - moderate tannin, decent acid, and often a whiff of green pepper or bay leaf even when fully ripe - makes it a natural match for grilled or slow-cooked meats, where char and smoke play off its own savory, slightly peppery character. Lamb, especially seasoned with rosemary or cumin, works well because the wine's spice notes extend the dish rather than fight it. It's also one of the better reds for anything actually containing bell pepper, eggplant or roasted vegetables, since its own pyrazine-driven green notes won't clash the way they can with a purely fruit-forward red.

Spicy food works if the heat is balanced with fat - think braised short rib with chili rather than a sharp vinegar-based hot sauce, since heat with no fat can make tannin read as harsh. Skip delicate white fish or raw preparations, where the tannin and herbal edge will dominate. Serve slightly cooler than a big Cabernet, around 62°F, to keep the herbal notes fresh instead of dulled.
