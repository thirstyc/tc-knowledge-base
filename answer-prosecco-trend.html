Why is Prosecco production exploding?

Short Answer

It's not just fashion, though fashion helped light the fuse. Prosecco is made with the Charmat method, where the second fermentation happens in large pressurized tanks rather than individual bottles like Champagne. That's faster, cheaper, and infinitely more scalable, so producers can meet demand without the years of aging Champagne requires. The Glera grape itself ripens easily and gives generous yields in the flat vineyards of the Veneto, keeping raw material costs low. Combine that with a lighter, fruitier style that doesn't intimidate newcomers the way drier, more austere sparkling wines can, and you have a drink that's cheap to make, easy to like, and fast to bring to market. Bars figured out that Aperol spritz and prosecco-based cocktails sell by the gallon, and that demand rippled straight back to the vineyards. So yes, it's trendy, but the trend stuck because the production model was built to say yes to more people, faster, than almost anything else in sparkling wine.
