Why does Malvasia Bianca taste so floral and sweet?

Short Answer

Malvasia Bianca belongs to the Malvasia family, a loose group of related grapes scattered across Italy and the Mediterranean rather than one strict variety, and this branch carries an unusually high concentration of terpenes, the aromatic compounds that also drive Muscat and Gewürztraminer, which is why it smells like orange blossom, jasmine and honeysuckle before it hits your tongue. The sweetness isn't incidental: growers commonly let the grapes hang longer to build sugar, and some bottlings involve partial drying or an early halt to fermentation that leaves real residual sugar behind. The floral aromatics and the sweetness reinforce each other, which is why the wine reads as dessert-adjacent rather than a crisp aperitif white. Pour it cold, roughly 8-10°C, enough to balance the sugar without freezing out the perfume — Malvasia loses its whole personality served too warm or too icy.
