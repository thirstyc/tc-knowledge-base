Best fruity wine?

Short Answer

Fruity wines put ripe fruit flavours front and centre. Warm climates, aromatic grapes and minimal oak all push wine in that direction.

Fruity reds:

  • Beaujolais and other Gamay: juicy red berries and low tannin.
  • Zinfandel and Primitivo: jammy blackberry and spice.
  • Grenache and Garnacha: ripe strawberry and raspberry.
  • Malbec: plum and black cherry, soft and generous.
  • Merlot and Shiraz from warm regions.

Fruity whites:

  • Sauvignon Blanc from New Zealand: passion fruit and gooseberry.
  • Viognier: apricot and peach.
  • Torrontés and Muscat: grapey, floral and fragrant.
  • Riesling: lime, peach and apple, from dry to sweet.
  • Albariño: peach and citrus.

Rosé and sparkling:

  • Most dry rosé, with strawberry and watermelon notes.
  • Prosecco: pear, apple and melon.

Remember that "fruity" isn't the same as "sweet". Many fruity wines are fully dry; they just taste ripe. If you want both fruit and sweetness, look for Moscato d'Asti or off-dry Riesling.

To find more wines you'll like, tell your shop you prefer "fruit-forward" wines. They'll know exactly what you mean.
