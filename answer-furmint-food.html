What pairs with dry Furmint?

Short Answer

Dry Furmint's job at the table is the same one its acidity does in the sweet Tokaji version: cutting through richness and fat without needing residual sugar to do it. That makes it a natural with Hungarian food, goulash, paprikás, dishes built on lard, smoked meat, and sweet paprika, where the wine's high acid slices through the fat and its mineral, sometimes flinty character (a real signature of Tokaj's volcanic soils) plays against the spice instead of getting overwhelmed by it. It's just as good with seafood, particularly shellfish and dishes with a little richness like a beurre blanc, since the acidity and saline edge do the same cutting work. Bright, young cheeses, a fresh sheep's milk cheese or a young Gouda, also work well, the wine's structure keeping pace with the cheese's tang. Serve it cool, around 10 to 12 degrees, cooler than you'd pour a big Chardonnay, so that acid stays crisp and doesn't read as flabby.
