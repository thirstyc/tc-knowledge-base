Best California wine?

Short Answer

California is large and varied enough that the best wine depends on the grape. Each style has a home.

  • Cabernet Sauvignon: Napa Valley, plus Sonoma's Alexander Valley and Paso Robles for more value.
  • Pinot Noir and Chardonnay: cool coastal areas, especially Sonoma's Russian River Valley and Sonoma Coast, Santa Rita Hills and Santa Maria Valley in Santa Barbara County, and Anderson Valley in Mendocino.
  • Zinfandel: Sonoma's Dry Creek Valley, Lodi and the Sierra Foothills, especially from old vines.
  • Rhône varieties like Syrah and Grenache: Paso Robles and Santa Barbara.
  • Sparkling wine: Carneros and Anderson Valley.

The Central Coast, between San Francisco and Santa Barbara, is often the best source of value: cool-climate Pinot and Chardonnay at lower prices than Sonoma or Napa.

Lodi is worth knowing too, with some of the oldest Zinfandel vines in the state at friendly prices.

Style shifts matter. California has moved from the very ripe, oaky style of the 1990s and 2000s toward fresher, more restrained wines from many producers, so the same grape can taste quite different depending on who made it.

Where to start: a Central Coast Pinot Noir, a Sonoma Zinfandel and a Paso Robles Rhône blend.
