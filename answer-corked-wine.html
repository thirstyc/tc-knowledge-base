What is "corked" wine?

Short Answer

Corked wine is wine tainted by TCA, a compound that can form in natural cork and makes the wine smell musty. It has nothing to do with crumbled cork in your glass.

TCA stands for trichloroanisole. Humans are extraordinarily sensitive to it, detecting it at just a few parts per trillion.

What it smells like: wet cardboard, a damp basement, mouldy newspaper or a wet dog. In mild cases there's no bad smell at all; the wine just tastes muted and short, with the fruit stripped away.

How to check: smell the wine, not the cork. A wet, stained cork is normal; a musty smell in the glass is not. Leaving the glass a few minutes often makes it more obvious.

It's not harmful to drink, just unpleasant, and it can't be fixed. It's also no reflection on the producer, since the taint comes from the cork itself.

Estimates of how often it happens vary, but roughly one bottle in a hundred is a common figure today, down from far worse rates a few decades ago thanks to better cork production and alternatives.

Screwcaps and glass stoppers avoid the problem entirely. If a bottle is corked, take it back: shops and restaurants will normally replace it.
