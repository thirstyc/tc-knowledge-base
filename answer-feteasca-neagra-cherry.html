Why does Feteasca Neagra taste so dark and cherry?

Short Answer

Fetească Neagră, literally 'black maiden grape,' is a native Romanian variety with genuinely thick skins, and skin is where tannin and colour pigments live, so thick skins mean more of both get pulled into the wine during fermentation. That's the source of the structure, real, grippy tannin, not the soft stuff you get from thinner-skinned reds. The dark cherry and plum character, sometimes with a leathery edge, comes from the grape's own flavour-compound profile, the same basic principle that gives Syrah its dark-fruit signature, just a different chemical fingerprint underneath. Romania and Moldova's continental climate, hot days and cool nights, lets the grape hang long enough to build tannin and sugar ripeness without losing all its acid, which is why the wine stays structured instead of turning jammy. Producers often give it some oak time too, which adds spice and files down the tannin's rougher edges. Serve it around 16 to 17°C, a touch below room temperature, alongside something with fat, braised meat, aged hard cheese, to soften that grip.
