What pairs with Humagne Rouge?

Short Answer

Humagne Rouge is a rustic, high-acid red grape native to the Valais in Switzerland's Alpine south, and everything about how it's built points toward mountain food. The acid and firm tannin are there because Valais vineyards sit at real altitude on steep, rocky slopes, and the grape needs structure to survive that terrain and a short growing season. That same structure is what cuts through the fat in raclette, gruyère and other hard alpine cheeses, and why it works so well with grilled or roasted lamb -- the tannins grip the protein and fat while the acid resets your palate between bites. Grilled meat generally is a safe bet for the same reason: char and smoke need a wine with enough grip to match, not something delicate. Serve it a touch cooler than a big Bordeaux, around 15-16°C, to keep that acidity feeling fresh rather than sharp. It's not a subtle wine, and it isn't trying to be.
