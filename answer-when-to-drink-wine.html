How do I know if my wine is ready to drink?

Short Answer

Assume it's ready. The vast majority of wine is made to be drunk on release, and only a small fraction improves with age.

If it's a wine that can age, a few signals help. Colour: young reds are purple at the rim, older ones brick or orange; whites deepen from pale straw to gold. Aroma: fresh fruit gives way to dried fruit, leather, mushroom and nuts. Texture: tannins that felt grippy soften over time.

Practical approaches:

  • Check what the producer says. Many list a drinking window on their website or back label.
  • Buy several bottles and open one every year or two. It's the only way to learn a wine's curve.
  • Use vintage charts as a rough guide, not gospel.
  • Trust the structure: high acidity and firm tannin mean it can wait; soft, fruity and low-acid means drink up.

Storage decides as much as time. A bottle kept warm ages fast and badly.

If a wine seems closed or clumsy, decant it and give it an hour.

And remember that far more good bottles are opened too late than too early.
