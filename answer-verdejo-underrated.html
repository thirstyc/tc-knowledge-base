Why don't more people know Verdejo?

Short Answer

Because Spain's marketing budget goes to Rioja and Ribera del Duero, and Verdejo doesn't come with a story about old vines or noble families, just a grape from Rueda that makes genuinely good, cheap white wine. Verdejo has a natural bitter almond note on the finish, a bit like Sauvignon Blanc but rounder and less shrill, thanks to lower acidity and a thicker skin that gives it some texture. Rueda's high plateau, with big day-night temperature swings, keeps the fruit fresh without the wine turning sharp. Producers there also mostly resisted the temptation to overoak it, so what you get is a clean, aromatic white that costs a fraction of a comparable Burgundy or Sancerre. It never needed a marketing campaign because it was busy being the house white at every tapas bar in Spain. Serve it around 8-10°C, and don't wait for a special occasion, it's built for Tuesday night dinner, not a cellar.
