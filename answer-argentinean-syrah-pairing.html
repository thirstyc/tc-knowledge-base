What pairs with Argentinean Syrah?

Short Answer

Argentinean Syrah carries more black pepper and spice than its Malbec neighbour, largely thanks to a compound called rotundone that develops more readily in the grape under Syrah's typical ripening conditions, and that spice note is the key to pairing it well. It loves dishes with their own peppery or smoky edge, lamb with rosemary and crushed pepper, grilled sausage, anything with a char from live fire, because the wine's spice and smoke echo rather than compete. High-altitude sites in Salta and Mendoza give it enough acid and tannin structure to handle fattier cuts of meat the same way Malbec does, so classic asado pairings still work. Where it pulls ahead of Malbec is with actual spiced food: cumin, chili, North African-style rubs, where its pepper character bridges the gap instead of the fruit just trying to survive the heat. Serve slightly cooler than a Northern Rhône Syrah, around 16 degrees, since the fruit here runs riper and benefits from a touch more chill to keep it from tasting heavy.
