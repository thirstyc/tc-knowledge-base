Why is Tokaji so expensive?

Short Answer

Tokaji's sweetness comes from botrytis, the noble rot that shrivels grapes on the vine and concentrates their sugar, acid and flavour, but botrytis doesn't infect a vineyard evenly or all at once. Pickers have to walk the same rows repeatedly over weeks, sometimes into late autumn, selecting only the individual berries that have shriveled properly that day and leaving the rest for the next pass. A skilled picker might gather only a few kilos of these aszú berries in a full day, and it takes a large volume of them to produce even one bottle of the classic sweet style, since so much water has evaporated out of the fruit. Weather adds risk on top of labor: too much rain and the crop rots badly instead of nobly, wiping out a vintage's worth of work. That combination of low yield, high risk and hand labor with no mechanization possible is why the good stuff costs what it does. It's one of the few sweet wines where the price is earned in the vineyard, not the marketing.
