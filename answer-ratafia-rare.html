Why is Ratafia hard to find?

Short Answer

Ratafia exists because Champagne houses have leftover unfermented must and marc spirit sitting around after the main harvest is pressed and vinified, so making a bit of ratafia is a way to use it rather than waste it. It's not built for volume — there's no big export market, no international demand driving production up, and no strict appellation machinery pushing consistency or scale the way there is for Champagne itself. Many producers make just enough for their own cellar door, family, and local restaurants, and some don't bottle it commercially at all. It's essentially a side project, made when there's material to spare rather than to a fixed annual plan. That means output varies year to year, distribution rarely extends beyond the region, and international listings are close to nonexistent. If you find a bottle outside Champagne, it likely came home in someone's suitcase rather than through a normal supply chain.
