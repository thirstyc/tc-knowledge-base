Why does Ontario Riesling taste so mineral-crisp?

Short Answer

It's Lake Ontario doing the heavy lifting. The Niagara Escarpment sites sit close enough to the lake that it moderates temperature through the growing season, delaying ripening and forcing the vines to hang fruit longer into cool autumn air. Riesling responds to that slow ripening by piling up malic and tartaric acid while sugars creep up gradually, which is why these wines can taste racy and green-apple sharp even at moderate alcohol. The 'mineral' note people chase isn't dissolved rock, since vines can't actually absorb minerals that way; it's more likely sulfur compounds and the reductive winemaking style, low intervention, minimal oak, often a little residual CO2, that reads as flinty or wet-stone on the palate. Many producers also pick across multiple passes, blending riper and less ripe fruit to build tension rather than relying on one harvest date. Serve it at 8-10°C, cold enough to keep the acid focused but not so cold it mutes the aromatics. Give it five to ten years if you want to see the fruit fade and that stony character take over.
