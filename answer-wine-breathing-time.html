How long before wine is ready to drink after opening?

Short Answer

Most wine is ready to drink as soon as it's opened. Only young, tannic reds and some complex whites really benefit from time in the air, usually 30 minutes to a couple of hours in a decanter.

Simply pulling the cork does very little, since only the small surface in the neck touches the air. To let a wine breathe, pour it into a decanter or into glasses.

A rough guide:

  • Everyday whites, rosés, sparkling and light reds: drink immediately.
  • Medium-bodied reds: 15–30 minutes in a decanter or glass can help.
  • Young, powerful reds like Barolo, Cabernet Sauvignon, Syrah or Bordeaux: one to two hours, sometimes more.
  • Young, closed whites like top white Burgundy or Chenin Blanc: 20–30 minutes.
  • Old, fragile wines: decant only to remove sediment, then drink soon, since they fade quickly.

The best test is to taste. Pour a little when you open it, then again every 20–30 minutes. When it tastes more open and fragrant, it's ready.

Swirling the wine in your glass gives a quick burst of air too.

If a wine smells faintly of sulfur or eggs when first opened, a few minutes of air, or vigorous swirling, usually clears it.
