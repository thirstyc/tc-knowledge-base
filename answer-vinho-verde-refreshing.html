Why does Vinho Verde taste so refreshing?

Short Answer

Two separate things are doing the work, and they're worth telling apart. The freshness comes from geography: Minho, in northern Portugal, is cool, green and rainy, so the grapes — mostly Alvarinho, Loureiro and Arinto — rarely get very ripe, which keeps alcohol low, often under 11%, and preserves natural malic acidity. That acid is what makes the wine feel sharp and cleansing rather than soft or flabby. The fizz is a separate, smaller effect: a light natural spritz left over from bottling the wine young, or in cheaper versions a touch of added CO2, giving a gentle prickle rather than genuine sparkling-wine pressure. Together, low alcohol, high acid and a slight fizz make the wine feel almost weightless, which is entirely the point — it's not trying to be complex. Serve it properly cold, 6-8°C, straight from the fridge, and drink it young. It has no ambition to age gracefully; extra bottle time makes it duller, not better.
