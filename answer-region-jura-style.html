Why does Jura wine taste so oxidative and nutty?

Short Answer

The nuttiness isn't a flaw or an accident, it's a controlled version of what happens to wine when oxygen gets in slowly. Savagnin destined for vin jaune is aged in barrel for years without topping up (no ouillage), so a film of yeast called voile forms on the surface, the same biological mechanism as flor in sherry. That film feeds on the wine below, converting alcohol into acetaldehyde, which is what produces the walnut, curry, and bruised-apple character. It's slow, deliberate oxidation, not spoilage. Poulsard and Trousseau, the region's red grapes, are a different story: pale colour, high acid, thin skins, made in a way that keeps them light and often slightly reductive rather than oxidative. Serve vin jaune around 14 to 16°C in a small glass, it opens with air over an hour or more. Nothing else in France tastes like this because almost nowhere else lets wine sit exposed on purpose for years and calls it a technique instead of a mistake.
