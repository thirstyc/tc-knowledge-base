What's the Difference Between Chianti, Brunello, and Vino Nobile?

Short Answer

All three wines start from the same grape, but different rules, different sub-zones and different required aging turn Sangiovese into three distinct experiences. Chianti is a blend built around Sangiovese, grown across a wide Tuscan territory, released relatively young and meant to be an everyday, food-friendly red rather than a monument - reliable, affordable, occasionally a little rustic. Vino Nobile di Montepulciano, from a small zone southeast of Siena, is Sangiovese-based too but held to longer required aging than basic Chianti, giving it more structure and depth without Brunello's price tag. Brunello di Montalcino sits at the top because its rules are strictest: essentially pure Sangiovese, grown in a defined area around Montalcino from a biotype sometimes called Sangiovese Grosso, and required to age the longest before release. That extra time, stricter rules and lower yields explain the price gap far more than marketing does. None of the three is objectively better - they're the same grape dialed to different intensities. Chianti is the honest, unpretentious entry point; the other two are where you go once you already love the grape.
