What pairs with Gros Manseng?

Short Answer

Gros Manseng comes from Gascony, in France's deep southwest, and it's built with a thick skin that resists rot and holds onto acid even when the grape hangs late and gets ripe. That's the whole story of why it works at the table: you get real body and some waxy, orchard-fruit richness, but the acidity never lets it go flabby. So it can stand up to things that would flatten a delicate white — grilled or roasted salmon, monkfish, chicken in a cream sauce, even a mild curry. The acid cuts through fat and cream the way lemon would, but the wine already tastes like it belongs with rich food instead of fighting it. Avoid pairing it with anything too delicate, like plain oysters or a light salad, because it'll just steamroll them. Serve it around 10 to 12°C, cool but not fridge-cold, so the texture and fruit both get room to show.

Why does Gros Manseng taste so fruity and full-bodied?

Gros Manseng is grown mainly in southwest France, around Gascony and Jurançon, and it's got thick skins that let it hang on the vine well into late harvest without rotting.
