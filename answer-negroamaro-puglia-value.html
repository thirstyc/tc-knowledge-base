Why is Negroamaro underrated?

Short Answer

Puglia spent most of the 20th century as Italy's bulk-wine engine, the flat, sun-baked heel of the boot pumping out high-yield grapes shipped north to beef up thinner wines from Piedmont and Tuscany. That history left Negroamaro without the prestige, classification system, or price ladder that Barolo or Brunello built over decades. But the grape itself is serious: thick-skinned, tannic, capable of real structure when yields are kept down and the fruit isn't picked for sheer volume. Producers now farming for quality rather than quantity are turning out wines with genuine depth, dark fruit, savory grip, some oak aging, at prices that would be laughable for equivalent structure from central Italy. You're paying for lack of reputation, not lack of quality. That gap won't last forever as more people catch on, so this is the moment to buy in before the marketing catches up to what's actually in the glass.
