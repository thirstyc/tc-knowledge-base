What are wine notes/tasting notes?

Short Answer

Tasting notes are written descriptions of how a wine looks, smells and tastes. They help people remember wines, compare them and decide what to buy.

A typical tasting note covers:

  • Appearance: colour and intensity, like "pale lemon" or "deep ruby".
  • Nose: the aromas, like "black cherry, violets and a hint of vanilla".
  • Palate: sweetness, acidity, tannin, alcohol and body, plus the flavours.
  • Finish: how long the flavour lasts after swallowing.
  • Conclusion: quality, readiness to drink and ageing potential.

Aroma descriptions compare the wine to familiar things. Nobody adds blackcurrant to a red wine that smells of it; the same aromatic compounds occur naturally in the grape and the fruit. Descriptors fall into families:

  • Primary: from the grape, like fruit, flowers and herbs.
  • Secondary: from winemaking, like butter, bread and vanilla from oak.
  • Tertiary: from ageing, like leather, mushroom, nuts and honey.

Professional notes can be flowery, but they're only useful if they help you. Your own notes can be simple: what you tasted, whether you liked it, and what you ate with it.

Keeping short notes, or even photos of labels with a word or two, is the fastest way to learn what you enjoy.
