Gamay vs Pinot Noir?

Short Answer

Both are light, low-tannin reds from Burgundy's neighbourhood, but Gamay is juicier and more playful, while Pinot Noir is more perfumed and serious.

Gamay is the grape of Beaujolais. It tastes of red cherry, raspberry and banana or bubblegum in some styles, a result of the carbonic maceration often used there, where whole grapes ferment in a sealed tank.

Pinot Noir, from Burgundy just to the north, is more delicate and aromatic, with red cherry and earthy, savoury notes that deepen with age.

Gamay is usually cheaper. The Beaujolais crus, like Morgon, Fleurie and Moulin-à-Vent, make more structured wines that can age for several years, and they cost a fraction of village Burgundy.

Both love the same food: roast chicken, charcuterie, mushrooms, salmon and soft cheese. Both are best served slightly chilled.

There's history here too. In 1395 the Duke of Burgundy banned Gamay from the Côte d'Or in favour of Pinot Noir, which is part of why the two grapes ended up in different places.

Which to choose? Gamay for value and easy pleasure; Pinot Noir when you want complexity.
