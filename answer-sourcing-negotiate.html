How to negotiate wine prices?

Short Answer

Mostly, by buying in quantity. Case discounts are the most common and reliable way to pay less, especially at independent shops.

What usually works:

  • Ask about a case discount. Many independent shops take a percentage off six or twelve bottles, often including mixed cases.
  • Buy from bin ends and clearance shelves, where shops sell off the last bottles of a vintage.
  • Ask when new vintages arrive. Previous vintages are sometimes discounted to clear space.
  • Join a shop's mailing list for sale notices and offers.
  • Buy en primeur or on pre-release for wines you want in quantity, though this ties up money and carries some risk.

What usually doesn't work: haggling over a single bottle in a shop, or at a restaurant. Prices in government-run stores, like those in Quebec and Ontario, are fixed, though they run their own promotions.

Being a good customer matters too. Shops remember regulars and often offer them better prices, first access to limited bottles and honest advice.

At auctions, the "negotiation" is setting your maximum bid, including fees, and sticking to it.
