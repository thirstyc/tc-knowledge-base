Wine for Easter?

Short Answer

It depends on the main dish, and Easter usually means lamb, ham or fish. Pinot Noir is the most flexible choice, since it works with all three.

For roast lamb, choose a savoury red with some structure: Bordeaux, Rioja, a Côtes du Rhône or a fuller Pinot Noir. Herbs like rosemary and garlic point toward southern French reds.

For glazed ham, pick an off-dry Riesling, a Beaujolais or a dry rosé. They handle the salt and the sweet glaze better than big reds.

For salmon or other fish, go with Pinot Noir, a dry rosé or a lightly oaked Chardonnay.

For a vegetarian Easter, with spring vegetables, asparagus and eggs, Sauvignon Blanc and Grüner Veltliner are ideal.

Brunch is a great excuse for sparkling wine, which also works with eggs, a notoriously tricky food for wine.

For dessert, whether chocolate eggs or hot cross buns, pour a Tawny Port, a Moscato d'Asti or, with chocolate, a Banyuls.

Easter falls in spring, so fresh, lighter styles often feel right: rosé, light reds and crisp whites.

Hosting a crowd? Pick one versatile red, one white and a sparkling wine, and you'll cover most of the table.
