What pairs with Grignolino?

Short Answer

Treat Grignolino like a red that behaves like a white. Its low tannin and bright acidity mean it doesn't need fatty, heavy food to soften it, it actually gets crushed by rich sauces or aged cheese. Where it shines is with things that would usually make red wine taste harsh: salads with vinaigrette, cured meats, charcuterie boards with a little fat but not much heaviness. Grilled or pan-seared fish works too, since there's no tannin to clash with the proteins, and fresh pasta with a light tomato or herb sauce lets the wine's acidity cut through without a fight. It's also a rare red that takes a slight chill well, 13-14°C, which makes it a good warm-weather bottle when you want red wine character without the heat and heaviness. Think of it as a bridge wine for people who claim they only drink white or rosé, it gives them red fruit and spice without the tannic weight they're trying to avoid.
