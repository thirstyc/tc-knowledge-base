Can You Drink Young Sangiovese or Does It Need Age?

Short Answer

Sangiovese's issue, if you can call it that, is thin skins carrying disproportionately high tannin alongside ferocious natural acidity - a combination that's brilliant with age and brutal without it. Young, the tannins haven't had time to soften and polymerize; they grab your palate and don't let go, and the acid, unbuffered, comes across as sharp rather than lively. The aging requirements aren't bureaucratic red tape, they're a real reflection of that structure. Chianti, released after a few years, is approachable but still benefits from another year or two in bottle. Vino Nobile di Montepulciano sits in the middle, needing more time than basic Chianti to round out fully. Brunello di Montalcino, made from a specific Sangiovese biotype and held to the longest required aging, is the extreme case - open one too young and you get a tannic, closed wine with no hint of the dried cherry and leather it's capable of. If you must drink young Sangiovese, decant hard, an hour minimum, and serve around 16-18°C so the tannins don't read even harsher than they already are.
