Why is Marsanne so full-bodied?

Short Answer

Marsanne's weight comes from what it lacks as much as what it has. It's a grape with naturally low acidity, which means there's nothing sharp cutting through the fruit to make it feel lean. Left on the vine to ripen fully in the northern Rhône's warm, south-facing slopes around Hermitage and Crozes-Hermitage, it builds sugar and phenolic compounds rather than malic acid. Those phenolics show up as a waxy, almost lanolin texture, similar to how a ripe pear or quince mouth-feel differs from a green apple. Winemakers often let it sit on its lees after fermentation, stirring occasionally, which adds a creamy weight on top of the natural richness. Alcohol tends to land on the higher side, another byproduct of that full ripening, and that alcohol reads on the palate as body and warmth rather than fire. Serve it around 12-13°C, a touch warmer than a crisp white, or the texture seizes up and the wine tastes flat instead of generous.
