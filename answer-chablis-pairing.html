What pairs with Chablis?

Short Answer

Chablis pairing works because of geology as much as grape. It's Chardonnay grown on Kimmeridgian limestone and clay full of fossilized oyster shells from an ancient seabed, and the wine picks up a saline, flinty edge from that soil. Most producers skip new oak and often limit malolactic fermentation, so the wine keeps its cutting acidity instead of going round and buttery. That acid is the whole point at the table: it slices through the briny fat of a raw oyster and amplifies the mineral, seawater flavor rather than fighting it, which is why the two taste made for each other. The same logic extends to other raw or lightly cooked seafood, sushi, and simple salads with a sharp vinaigrette. Soft, young goat cheese also works because the acid mirrors the cheese's own tang. What doesn't work: anything creamy or oak-driven, since Chablis has none of the fat or vanilla richness to match. Serve it cold, around 10-12°C, and skip anything smoked or heavily spiced, which will flatten it.

Why does Chablis taste so mineral and steely?

Chablis sits on Kimmeridgian limestone, a soil packed with fossilized oyster shells from an ancient seabed, and while wine geeks argue endlessly about whether vines can literally taste soil, there's no doubt this terroir produces a distinctive flinty, saline character that shows up glass after glass.
