Wine with sushi?

Short Answer

A crisp, dry white or a dry sparkling wine: Champagne, Grüner Veltliner, Albariño or dry Riesling. Sushi is delicate, and it comes with soy sauce, wasabi and pickled ginger, so the wine needs to be fresh and not too heavy.

Sparkling wine is the easy answer. Its acidity and bubbles refresh the palate between bites and handle the saltiness of soy sauce.

Grüner Veltliner has a peppery note that works with wasabi. Albariño, Chablis and Muscadet bring a salty, mineral freshness that suits raw fish.

Dry or off-dry Riesling is especially good with spicy rolls or sweet sauces, since a touch of sweetness balances heat.

Match the fish. Lean white fish like sea bream or flounder suits the lightest wines. Fattier fish like salmon and tuna can take a dry rosé, or even a light, chilled red like Pinot Noir or Gamay.

Eel with sweet sauce (unagi) pairs well with off-dry Riesling or a fruity Pinot Noir.

What to avoid: oaky whites and tannic reds. Oak overwhelms the fish, and tannin can clash with it, leaving a metallic taste.

Don't forget sake. It's made for this food and worth exploring alongside wine.
