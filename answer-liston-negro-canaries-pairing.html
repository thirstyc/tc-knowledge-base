What pairs with Liston Negro?

Short Answer

Its whole appeal is high acid, light tannin and low body, which makes it one of the few reds that genuinely likes fish. The acidity cuts through oil the way lemon does, so grilled sardines, mackerel or a plate of octopus won't fight it the way they would a heavier red. On the islands it's the classic match for papas arrugadas, the salt-crusted wrinkled potatoes served with mojo sauce, where the wine's pepper note echoes the mojo's own chili and garlic kick. It also works with charcuterie and light pork, anywhere you want red wine character without weight or heavy tannin drying out the dish. Serve it at 13-14°C, cool enough to keep the acidity crisp but not so cold you lose the fruit. Skip anything rich in cream or heavy red sauce, the wine is too light-bodied to stand up to that kind of density and will just taste thin by comparison.
