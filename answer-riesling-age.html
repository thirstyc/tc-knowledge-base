Can you age Riesling?

Short Answer

Yes, and it's one of the best white wines for ageing. Its high acidity preserves it, and good Riesling can improve for ten to thirty years or more, especially sweeter styles.

How long depends on style and quality:

  • Everyday dry Riesling: best within three to five years.
  • Good dry Riesling from Germany, Austria, Alsace or Australia's Clare and Eden valleys: ten to twenty years.
  • German Kabinett and Spätlese: often ten to twenty-five years.
  • Auslese, Beerenauslese, Trockenbeerenauslese and Eiswein: decades, sometimes more than fifty years.

How it changes: young Riesling tastes of lime, green apple, peach and flowers. With age, it develops honey, toast, dried apricot and a distinctive note often called "petrol" or kerosene, from a compound called TDN. Many lovers of aged Riesling consider it the grape's most complex expression. Sweetness also seems to fade over time, so an off-dry Riesling can taste drier with age.

Some Rieslings go through a "dumb phase" a few years after release, when they seem closed and quiet, before opening up again.

Store bottles cool, dark and steady. Screwcap-sealed Riesling ages very well too.
