Why is Vinho Verde a summer wine?

Short Answer

It's built for heat by design, not accident. Vinho Verde comes from Portugal's Minho region, up near the Atlantic, where rain and granite soils keep yields high and sugar low. Grapes like Alvarinho, Loureiro and Trajadura get picked early, before they load up on sugar, which keeps alcohol down around 9-11% and acidity high. That acidity is the whole point: it makes the wine taste like it's vibrating, cutting through heat and grease the way lemon juice cuts through fried food. Many versions also carry a slight prickle of CO2, either trapped from a quick second fermentation or added back in, which adds to the sense of freshness without ever being full sparkling wine. There's basically no oak, no bottle aging, none of the weight or contemplation a serious white demands. Serve it cold, around 7-9°C, straight from the fridge, and drink it within a year or two of the vintage. This isn't a wine to think about. It's a wine to drink fast, outside, before it gets warm in the glass.
