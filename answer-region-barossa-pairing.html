What pairs with Barossa Shiraz?

Short Answer

Barossa Shiraz comes from old, low-yielding vines in a warm climate, which means ripe fruit, soft-but-present tannins, and alcohol that runs high. That combination needs fat and char to work with, not against, the wine. Fat coats the palate and buffers alcohol's heat, so lamb shoulder or a well-marbled rib actually makes the wine taste more balanced than it does on its own. Char matters too — the Maillard compounds from a good crust echo the roasted, smoky notes the wine picks up from oak aging, so the pairing isn't just about weight, it's about matching flavor compounds directly. Where this style struggles is with anything delicate or acid-driven, since the wine's tannin and alcohol will flatten it. Serve around 16-18°C — much warmer and the alcohol turns hot and boozy, much cooler and the tannins seize up and taste harsh.
