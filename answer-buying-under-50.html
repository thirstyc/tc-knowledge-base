Best wine under $50?

Short Answer

At $30–50 you can buy genuinely serious wine: bottles that reflect a specific place, age well and feel special on the table.

What this budget opens up:

  • Entry-level wines from famous regions: good village Burgundy, non-vintage Champagne from grower producers, Barolo and Barbaresco from less famous names, Brunello's younger sibling Rosso di Montalcino, and northern Rhône Crozes-Hermitage or Saint-Joseph.
  • Top wines from value regions: Gran Reserva Rioja, old-vine Priorat and Montsant, the best Douro reds, and top Chenin Blanc from the Loire or South Africa.
  • Age-worthy whites: Premier Cru Chablis, German Riesling Spätlese and Grosses Gewächs from good growers, and Savennières.
  • New World: excellent Oregon and New Zealand Pinot Noir, and Cabernet from Washington State or Margaret River.

This is also where it pays to think about ageing. Many wines in this range improve for five to fifteen years, so buying two bottles, one to open now and one for later, is a great way to learn.

A good rule: at this price, choose the producer before the region. A great producer in a modest appellation beats a mediocre one with a famous name.
