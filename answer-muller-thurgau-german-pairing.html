What pairs with Müller-Thurgau?

Short Answer

Because it's low in alcohol and acidity and built for immediacy rather than structure, Müller-Thurgau needs food that won't flatten it. Anything rich, fatty or heavily sauced will just swallow the wine's soft floral character whole. Think instead about dishes with similar weight and freshness: green salads with a light vinaigrette, fresh herbs, simple grilled vegetables, soft unripened cheeses like a young chèvre. Fresh pasta with a butter or lemon sauce works too, since the wine's gentle acid can still cut through mild fat without a fight. It's also a natural aperitif wine on a warm afternoon, served on its own, chilled to around 8°C, before anything heavier arrives at the table. Spicy Asian dishes can work as well, since the wine's low alcohol won't amplify chili heat the way a bigger, hotter wine would. The rule of thumb: match its lightness rather than trying to counter it. This isn't a wine that stands up to anything, it's one that gets out of the way and lets delicate food shine.
