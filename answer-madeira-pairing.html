What pairs with Madeira?

Short Answer

It depends entirely on where the wine sits on the dry-to-sweet spectrum, since Madeira isn't one style but four grapes doing different jobs. Dry Sercial, with its high acid and lean citrus character, works as an aperitif or alongside consommé and salty snacks like almonds - closer in role to a fino sherry. Verdelho, a notch sweeter, suits richer soups and mild cheeses. The two sweet styles, Bual and Malmsey, are where most people actually meet Madeira, and they're built for the classic pairings: blue cheese, walnuts, dried fruit, crème brûlée, dark chocolate. The reason it works is acidity - Madeira's is unusually high for a fortified wine, so even the sweetest versions don't turn cloying next to something salty or fatty, since the acid resets your palate between bites. Serve dry styles cooler, around 12-14°C, and sweet styles a touch warmer, 15-17°C. It's a slow wine, meant for the end of a meal when nobody's rushing off.
