What is pet-nat wine?

Short Answer

Pét-nat is sparkling wine bottled before its first fermentation has finished, so the last of the bubbles form inside the bottle. The name is short for pétillant naturel, French for "naturally sparkling".

The technique is older than Champagne's. It's sometimes called the ancestral method, and it involves one fermentation rather than two: the winemaker bottles the wine partway through, and the remaining yeast and sugar create carbon dioxide with nowhere to escape.

Because the yeast stays in the bottle, most pét-nat is cloudy, and it's usually sealed with a crown cap, like a beer bottle. No corkscrew needed.

Expect softer, foamier bubbles than Champagne, lower alcohol, and flavours that lean fresh, yeasty and sometimes a little cidery or funky. Bottles vary more than conventional sparkling wine, which is part of the appeal and part of the risk.

It can be made from almost any grape, white, red or rosé, and it's a staple of natural wine bars.

Serve it very cold, open it over a sink since it can foam, and drink it young. Decide for yourself whether to pour the sediment or leave it behind.
