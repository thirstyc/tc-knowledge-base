What pairs with Priorat?

Short Answer

Priorat wines come off llicorella, that black slate and quartzite soil that forces old Garnacha and Cariñena vines to struggle for every drop of water. The result is tiny yields, concentrated fruit, alcohol often pushing 15%, and serious tannin. That structure needs food that fights back. Grilled wild boar or venison, slow-braised short rib, anything with char and fat, because the tannin needs protein to soften and the smoke echoes the wine's own roasted, mineral edge. Aged Manchego works for the same reason: salt and hard fat cut through the grip. Skip delicate fish or anything acidic and light, it'll get flattened. Serve around 16 to 18°C, not straight from the cellar, and give it real air time, an hour in a decanter isn't excessive for a young bottle. This is not a sipping wine for a quiet Tuesday, it's built for slow-cooked, fatty, smoky food and a table that's in no hurry.
