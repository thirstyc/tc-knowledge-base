Can You Age Dry Assyrtiko?

Short Answer

Yes, and it's underrated for it. Most Assyrtiko gets drunk within a year or two because that's when the citrus and saline snap is loudest, but the wine's structural backbone is really built for the long game. That backbone is acidity: it slows oxidation, so instead of falling apart the wine slowly develops honeyed, waxy, sometimes petrol-adjacent tertiary notes over five to eight years, not unlike aged Riesling. It won't turn into something as dramatically transformed as Sauternes, but the shift from bracing and linear to deeper and honeyed is real and worth tasting through if you can hold a few bottles back. Whether aged is 'better' is a matter of taste, not a hierarchy. Young Assyrtiko is about purity and tension; aged is about texture and complexity. Serve young bottles cold, around 8-10°C, to keep that citrus edge sharp. Aged bottles show better a touch warmer, closer to 12°C, so the honeyed and oxidative notes actually open up instead of staying locked down by the chill.
