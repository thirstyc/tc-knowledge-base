Can you drink dry Touriga Nacional?

Short Answer

Yes, and it might be Portugal's most underrated dry red. Touriga Nacional is the backbone grape of Port, prized there for its thick skins, deep color and firm tannin, but those same qualities make it a serious table wine on its own, especially in the Dão and Douro regions where producers have increasingly bottled it unfortified. Dry Touriga Nacional shows dark fruit like blackberry and violet, a floral lift that's almost aromatic for a grape this structured, and firm, grippy tannins that need food or age to soften. Because of that structure, it ages well, often improving over a decade or more in bottle as the tannins resolve and secondary notes of leather and dried herbs emerge. Most people only encounter it fortified and sweet in Port, so the dry versions catch them off guard. Serve it around 16-18°C, similar to a Syrah or Cabernet, with grilled meats or something with real fat to match the tannin.
