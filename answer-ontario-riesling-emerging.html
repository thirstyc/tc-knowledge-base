Why is Ontario Riesling emerging?

Short Answer

Riesling has actually been in Niagara for decades, but for a long time the region's Riesling story was really the ice wine story: grapes left on the vine into winter, frozen, and pressed into small amounts of intensely sweet wine that became Canada's signature export. That overshadowed the fact that the same grape, picked at normal ripeness, does something quietly excellent in this climate. Riesling holds acidity better than almost any other white grape, and Niagara's lake-moderated growing season lets it ripen slowly enough to develop flavour without losing that acid backbone. As local producers have shifted focus toward dry and off-dry table wines rather than dessert wine alone, that combination of ripe stone-fruit flavour and cutting acidity has started getting real attention. It's a similar profile to what makes Mosel or Clare Valley Riesling exciting, just with its own regional accent. Serve it cold, around 8 to 10 degrees, and the acidity will taste precise rather than sharp.
