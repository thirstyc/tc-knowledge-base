What pairs with Chilean Cabernet or Carmenère?

Short Answer

These wines lean on ripe, moderate tannin and often a bit of oak, which means they need fat and protein on the plate to soften and round out that structure, exactly what burgers and roast pork deliver. The fat coats your palate and literally reduces how astringent the tannins feel, while the char from grilling echoes the wine's own toasty, savory notes if it's seen any oak. Carménère in particular has that herbal, slightly peppery edge, which plays nicely against something smoky or seasoned rather than delicate. This is deliberately not a fussy, high-acid food wine, it's built for straightforward weeknight cooking, think grilled meats, tomato-based sauces, or a simple roast, nothing that needs precision matching. Serve it around 15-18°C rather than fully room temperature, since Chilean reds are often picked riper and served too warm will just emphasize alcohol over fruit. It's an unpretentious wine for unpretentious food, and that's exactly its appeal.
