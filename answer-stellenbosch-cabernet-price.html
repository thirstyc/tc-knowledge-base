Why is Stellenbosch Cabernet expensive?

Short Answer

Stellenbosch is South Africa's oldest and most established fine-wine region, planted since the late 1600s, and that history means top estates have decades of vine age and reputation baked into every bottle. The decomposed granite and weathered sandstone soils on the mountain slopes drain well and stress the vines just enough to concentrate flavour, which is the same logic behind pricing in Bordeaux or Napa. Add a cooling influence from False Bay that slows ripening and preserves acidity, and you get structured, age-worthy Cabernet rather than jammy, one-dimensional fruit. Yields on the best sites are kept low on purpose, meaning fewer bottles per hectare and higher cost per bottle to recoup the work. On top of that, a weak rand relative to the euro or Canadian dollar has historically kept South African wine looking cheap internationally, so top producers have been raising prices to close that gap. You're paying for terroir, scarcity and a market correction all at once.

What pairs with Stellenbosch Cabernet?

Stellenbosch Cabernet has firm tannins and a savoury, slightly earthy edge from the granite and decomposed sandstone soils, so it wants fat and char to soften it.
