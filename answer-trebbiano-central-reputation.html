Why does Trebbiano get such a bad reputation?

Short Answer

Because most of it was never made to impress anyone. Trebbiano Toscano has historically been planted for yield, not character, feeding bulk white wine production and distillation in Cognac and Armagnac. High acid, low aromatics and a tendency to produce enormous crops made it an industrial workhorse rather than a prestige grape, so generations of drinkers only ever met the cheap, thin, forgettable version. That reputation is deserved for that category of wine but unfair to the grape as a whole. Producers who cut yields, pick with intent and sometimes use skin contact or extended lees ageing get real texture and savoury depth out of Trebbiano Abruzzese and Trebbiano Spoletino in particular. The acidity that makes bulk Trebbiano taste sour, in a low-yield version, becomes the thing holding the wine together and making it food-friendly. So the bad reputation is really about economics and farming decisions, not about what the grape is structurally capable of.
