Why is Beaujolais so affordable?

Short Answer

Volume, mostly, and a reputation the region still hasn't shaken.

Gamay is generous and straightforward to grow, the area under vine is large, and most of it is farmed for quantity rather than prestige. That sets the floor low before anything else happens.

Then there's Beaujolais Nouveau. From the 1970s the region bet heavily on shipping very young wine around the world every third Thursday in November — a marketing triumph that turned into an albatross. It moved enormous volumes and taught the world that Beaujolais meant cheap, simple, drink it tonight. Serious growers have spent thirty years undoing that lesson.

The upside is that it's currently the best value in serious French red. The crus are made by people doing real work on granite slopes, and they cost a fraction of Burgundy an hour up the road. Same country, same broad idea, a quarter of the price.

If the November bottling is all you know, you don't know the region.
