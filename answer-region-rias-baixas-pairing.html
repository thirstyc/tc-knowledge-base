What pairs with Rías Baixas Albariño?

Short Answer

Albariño's whole personality is built around cutting through fat and echoing salinity, which is why it works so well with Atlantic seafood rather than fighting it. Oysters are the obvious match: the wine's high acidity and faint saline note mirror the brine, while the citrus cuts the mineral, almost metallic finish oysters can leave behind. Grilled octopus works because char and olive oil need acid to reset the palate between bites, and Albariño has enough grip to do that without stripping the smokiness. Seafood paella is the harder test since you've got saffron, stock, and sometimes chorizo fat all going at once, but the wine's texture, usually a little rounder than people expect thanks to lees contact, holds up under that richness instead of disappearing. Avoid pairing it with anything too sweet or heavily creamy, that's where it goes thin. Serve it cold, around 8-10°C, and don't let it sit out warming up at the table, since the acidity is what's doing all the work here.
