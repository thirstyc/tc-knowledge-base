Why is Lambrusco so affordable?

Short Answer

Lambrusco is cheap because almost everything about how it's made is designed for speed and volume, not scarcity. It's grown across the flat, fertile plains of Emilia-Romagna, land that gives high yields rather than the low, concentrated yields wineries chase for premium reds. The second fermentation happens in large sealed tanks (the Charmat method) instead of individual bottles, which is far faster and cheaper than traditional-method sparkling wine, no hand-riddling, no long lees aging, no expensive glass and cork setup. There's no oak barrel aging either, since the whole point is fresh, fruity, bubbly wine drunk young, often within a year of harvest. Local demand is huge, so producers don't need to build export markets or brand prestige to sell what they make, which keeps margins simple and prices low. None of this makes it lesser wine, it just means the cost structure is closer to beer than to Champagne, and that's exactly the role it plays on the table.
