What pairs with Prieto Picudo?

Short Answer

This is a tannic, high-acid, deeply coloured wine, which means it needs fat and protein to soften into something enjoyable rather than austere. León's own lechazo, milk-fed roast lamb, is the classic local match, and it works because the richness of the meat coats the palate and cushions the tannin's grip. Grilled or roasted red meats do the same job, as does morcilla or chorizo, where the fat and smoke round things out. Avoid delicate fish or anything light, the tannin will flatten it completely. Aged cheese with some fat content, like an aged Manchego, is another safe bet. Serve around 16-18°C; too cold and the tannin turns hard and drying, too warm and the alcohol starts to dominate. If the bottle is young, decanting for half an hour helps soften the edges. Basically treat it the way you'd treat a serious Nebbiolo or young Cabernet Sauvignon: hearty, fatty food, and don't rush it out of the glass.
