What pairs with Gewürztraminer?

Short Answer

Gewürztraminer's aromatic intensity, that lychee, rose petal and ginger perfume, plus its usual touch of residual sugar and low acidity, make it one of the few whites that can actually stand up to real spice rather than getting steamrolled by it. The sugar softens capsaicin's burn on the palate, the same mechanism that makes off-dry Riesling work with heat, so Thai curries, Indian dishes with garam masala, and Sichuan food are natural partners rather than accidents of tradition. It's also the classic match for Alsatian Munster cheese, a pungent, washed-rind cheese that would flatten most dry whites; Gewürztraminer's weight and aromatic volume meet it head-on instead of disappearing. Serve it around 10 to 12°C, slightly warmer than a crisp Riesling, so the aromatics open up rather than staying locked down by the cold. Where it struggles is with anything delicate or acid-driven, like a simple green salad, since its low acidity and high perfume will just overwhelm the plate.

Why is Gewürztraminer often off-dry?

Alsatian tradition leans into ripeness, and Gewürztraminer is a grape that ripens with unusually high natural sugar while its acidity drops faster than most whites.
