Why does Moldova matter?

Short Answer

Nowhere else can claim 7,000 years of continuous winemaking and still be a country most people can't place on a map. Moldova was the Soviet Union's wine cellar — literally, in the case of Cricova and Milestii Mici, whose limestone tunnels run for kilometres and once stored bottles by the million for the entire USSR. When Russia banned Moldovan wine imports in 2006 and again in 2013, it wiped out the country's main export market overnight, and that forced a real reckoning: either keep making bulk plonk for a market that no longer existed, or rebuild for Europe, where buyers actually cared about quality. The last decade has been that rebuild — smaller wineries, better clonal selection, less Soviet-era volume thinking. It's still 331 wineries in a country of 2.6 million people making the 20th-most wine on the planet, which is absurd math. The story isn't really about the wine being great yet everywhere — it's that it survived at all, and is now getting good on purpose.
