What pairs with Aglianico?

Short Answer

Aglianico grows on volcanic soils in Campania and Basilicata—Taurasi and Aglianico del Vulture are the names to know—and it's one of Italy's most structured reds: thick skins give heavy tannin, and the grape holds acid even when fully ripe. That combination makes young Aglianico genuinely hard work on its own, tight and a little bitter, which is why the wine and the pairing both need something to soften the edges. Braised short rib or wild boar ragù work because slow-cooked collagen and fat coat the palate, taming the tannin while the wine's acid cuts back through the richness. Aged pecorino does something similar—salt and fat against grip and acid. Char is another good partner: grilled sausage, lamb chops, anything with a charred crust. If you're opening it young, decant it for an hour or two, and serve around 16-18°C, no warmer—heat exaggerates the tannin. Given a decade or so in bottle, the tannins resolve into something closer to leather and dried herbs, and it needs far less coaxing at the table.

Why Is Aglianico So Mineral and Structured?

Aglianico is one of Italy's oldest cultivated grapes, likely brought by Greek settlers over two thousand years ago, and it's grown mainly around Campania and Basilicata on volcanic soils shaped by Vesuvius and Mount Vulture.
