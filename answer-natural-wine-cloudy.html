Why are natural wines cloudy?

Short Answer

Because they aren't fined or filtered. Both processes strip out the tiny particles that make wine hazy, and natural winemakers usually skip them.

Fining uses an agent, like bentonite clay or egg white, that binds to particles so they can be removed. Filtering physically strains them out. Both give a bright, clear wine, and both remove some texture, aroma and flavour along the way.

Skipping them leaves yeast cells, proteins and fine sediment in suspension, so the wine looks hazy, especially when the bottle is shaken or poured to the last drop.

Cloudiness by itself says nothing about quality. It's a style choice, not a fault, and plenty of superb wines are cloudy.

When haziness is a warning sign: if the wine also smells of vinegar or nail-polish remover, tastes unexpectedly fizzy, or has a stale, mousy aftertaste, something has gone wrong.

Practical tips: stand the bottle upright for a day if you'd rather drink it clear, and pour gently, stopping before the sediment reaches the neck.

Serve these wines cool, and expect them to change in the glass more than conventional wines do.
