What pairs with Lebanese wine?

Short Answer

Mezze, definitely, and the reason it works isn't accident. The Bekaa Valley sits at altitude, often over 1000 metres, so despite brutal daytime heat the nights swing cold, and grapes keep real acidity even as sugars climb. That acid-power combination — you get it in Château Musar's Cabernet-Syrah-Cinsault blends and in plenty of newer Bekaa reds — is exactly what a table of grilled kafta, lamb kebabs and pomegranate-glazed meats needs: enough tannin to grip charred fat, enough acidity to cut through it rather than sit on top of it. Where Lebanese wine really earns its keep is with the stuff that kills most reds — tahini, sumac, garlicky labneh, herb-heavy salads. High acid handles that; heavy oak or soft, flabby reds don't. The oxidative, amber-hued whites made from indigenous Obeideh and Merwah work the same way with olive oil and dips. Serve reds around 16°C, slightly cooler than a typical Bordeaux, since a bit of chill sharpens that acid edge against the spice.

Why does Languedoc wine taste so sun-soaked and generous?

Languedoc sits under near-constant Mediterranean sun with scrubby garrigue — thyme, rosemary, juniper — growing wild between the vine rows, and that herbal air genuinely perfumes the grapes as they ripen, which is why the wines smell like the landscape they came from.
