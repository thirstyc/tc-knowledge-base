Why is New Zealand Sauvignon Blanc pricier than most Sauvignon Blanc?

Short Answer

Marlborough essentially invented a flavour profile the world didn't know it wanted, then became the reference point for it—so the region carries brand weight most Sauvignon Blanc zones never earned. But the premium isn't just marketing. Land in Marlborough has gotten expensive as global demand pushed plantings and prices up, and a lot of fruit is grown under contract by independent growers who get paid per tonne, adding a layer of cost that estate-grown wine elsewhere skips. Export logistics matter too: shipping wine from the bottom of the world to Europe or North America costs real money, and currency exchange adds volatility on top. Large producers also spend heavily on maintaining the category's consistency and visibility abroad, and that spend gets baked into the bottle price. So you're paying for a mix of genuine production costs, a stronger currency position for the category, and the fact that Marlborough set the bar everyone else gets compared to.

What pairs with New Zealand Sauvignon Blanc?

This wine's high acidity and aromatic intensity—grassy pyrazines plus tropical thiols—give it real cutting power, which is why it works so well with fat and salt: raw or grilled seafood, oysters, ceviche.
