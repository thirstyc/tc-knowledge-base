What's the best red wine grape?

Short Answer

There's no single best, but three grapes are most often called the world's greatest: Cabernet Sauvignon, Pinot Noir and Nebbiolo. Each makes some of the most celebrated and long-lived wines on earth.

  • Cabernet Sauvignon: structure, blackcurrant and cedar, and huge ageing potential. It's the backbone of Bordeaux's Left Bank and Napa Valley, and grows well almost everywhere.
  • Pinot Noir: elegance, perfume and transparency. It shows the differences between vineyards more clearly than almost any other grape, which is why Burgundy is so revered, but it's difficult to grow.
  • Nebbiolo: pale colour, fierce tannin and acidity, and haunting aromas of roses and tar. It makes Barolo and Barbaresco, and rarely succeeds outside Piedmont.

Other strong candidates:

  • Syrah, for the northern Rhône's savoury, peppery wines.
  • Grenache, for Châteauneuf-du-Pape and old-vine Garnacha.
  • Tempranillo, for Rioja and Ribera del Duero.
  • Sangiovese, for Chianti and Brunello.

The honest answer: the best red grape is the one you enjoy most. A great winemaker and a great site matter more than the variety.
