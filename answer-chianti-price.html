Why is basic Chianti so affordable?

Short Answer

Scale, and a zone drawn generously.

The Chianti DOCG covers a huge sweep of Tuscany, roughly 15,000 hectares across seven sub-zones, and the rules are permissive by Italian standards: higher yields, shorter ageing, more latitude in the blend. That combination makes a great deal of wine, and a great deal of wine is cheap wine.

There's history in it too. Post-war Chianti travelled in the straw-covered fiasco flask and became shorthand for cheap red in a trattoria, sold on volume and charm rather than quality. The reputation outlasted the practice by about thirty years.

What you actually get now at twelve or fifteen dollars is decent everyday Sangiovese — bright, sour-cherry, built to go beside food rather than be contemplated on its own. That isn't a compromise, it's the design brief.

The catch is that the ceiling is low. If you want Chianti that rewards paying attention, that's Classico, and it's priced accordingly.
