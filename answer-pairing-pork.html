Wine with pork?

Short Answer

Pork is one of the most wine-friendly meats. It works with both whites and light reds, so match the wine to the cut and the sauce.

Roast pork loin or tenderloin is lean and mild. Pinot Noir, Beaujolais or a medium-bodied white like Chenin Blanc or dry Riesling are all good choices.

Pork with apples or other fruit calls for a white with some fruit and acidity, such as Riesling, Chenin Blanc or an Alsace Pinot Gris.

Pork belly and other fatty cuts want acidity to cut the richness. Off-dry Riesling, Alsace Pinot Gris or a Grenache-based red all work.

Sausages, especially spicy or herby ones, pair well with Syrah, Côtes du Rhône or Zinfandel.

Pork chops, grilled or pan-fried, suit a juicy red like Pinot Noir, Barbera or Grenache.

Asian pork dishes, like char siu or sweet and sour pork, have sweetness and spice. Off-dry Riesling, Gewürztraminer or a fruity Pinot Noir will handle them.

Barbecued or pulled pork with a sweet, smoky sauce wants a riper red: Zinfandel or Shiraz.

What to avoid: very tannic, heavy reds with lean cuts. They overwhelm the meat's mild flavour.
