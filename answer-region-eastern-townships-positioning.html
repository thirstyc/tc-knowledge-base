Why is Eastern Townships important?

Short Answer

This is where Quebec wine actually started, with the first commercial plantings going in around 1980 near Dunham, when a handful of growers gambled that cold-hardy hybrid grapes could survive winters that would kill classic vinifera outright. Varieties like Frontenac, Vidal and Marquette were bred specifically to tolerate deep freezes without the vine dying back to the roots every year, which is what makes a real wine industry possible instead of a novelty planting. That early bet paid off: the Townships now account for roughly 60% of all wine produced in the province, and the region still sets the tone for style and technique that other Quebec areas follow. It's not the biggest wine region in the world by any stretch, but it's the proof of concept, the place that showed cold-climate viticulture in Quebec could be commercially viable rather than just an agricultural curiosity. Every newer Quebec region owes something to what Dunham figured out first.

What pairs with Eastern Townships Frontenac?
