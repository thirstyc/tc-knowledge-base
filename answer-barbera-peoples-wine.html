Why Is Barbera Considered the People's Wine?

Short Answer

Barbera is naturally low in tannin and high in acid, the opposite structural profile from Nebbiolo, which is why it doesn't need years in a cellar to be enjoyable. It's typically fermented in steel or used oak rather than heavily new barrels, so the fruit stays forward and the wine drinks well within a year or two of release. It's planted all over the Piedmont hills, often on sites considered less prestigious than those reserved for Nebbiolo, which keeps yields decent and prices honest, usually somewhere in the 15-30 dollar range depending on where it's from. None of that makes it lesser, it just means Barbera was bred by circumstance and tradition to be the wine you actually drink on a Tuesday, while Barolo and Barbaresco sit in the cellar for special occasions. It's proof that a region's everyday wine can still have real character and structure without asking for patience or a big budget.
