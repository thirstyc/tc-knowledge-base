How should I serve a Trebbiano and what should I avoid?

Short Answer

Serve it cold, around 8 to 10 degrees, colder than you would a fuller white, because Trebbiano's whole appeal is freshness and a straightforward palate that gets muddled if it warms up and starts tasting flabby. Avoid the trap of pairing it with anything rich or creamy expecting the acid to cut through the way a Chablis would; basic Trebbiano usually lacks the concentration to stand up to that fight, it just gets overwhelmed. It works better with lighter fare, seafood, simple pasta with olive oil, or as an aperitif on its own where its job is just to be clean and refreshing rather than complex. Also avoid ageing it. Most Trebbiano, outside the more serious Abruzzese or Spoletino bottlings, is built to drink within a year or two of release, and there is no hidden depth waiting to emerge with time in the cellar. Buy it for now, drink it cold, and do not overthink it.
