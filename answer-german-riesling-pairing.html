What pairs with German Riesling?

Short Answer

The pairing logic comes down to two things German Riesling has in abundance: acidity and residual sugar, often both at once. That acidity cuts through fat and cleans your palate between bites, which is exactly what you need with fried food, rich seafood like scallops or lobster, or anything butter-heavy. The touch of sweetness, meanwhile, is the actual antidote to chili heat, not beer, not water. Capsaicin dissolves in sugar and fat, so a Kabinett or Spätlese with a bit of residual sugar will tame a green curry or Sichuan dish far better than a bone-dry white, which just amplifies the burn. This is why Riesling is the house white across Vietnamese, Thai and Sichuan restaurants that know their wine list. Serve it cold, around 8 to 10°C, cold enough to keep the sweetness in check and the acid singing. Avoid pairing the sweeter styles with already-sweet desserts, the wine will taste flat by comparison.

Why does German Riesling taste so elegant and mineral?

The elegance comes from restraint, not richness: German Riesling vineyards sit on steep, often slate-heavy slopes far enough north that ripening is slow and marginal, which lets the grape hold onto acidity that would collapse in a hotter climate.
