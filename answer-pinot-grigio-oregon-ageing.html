Does Oregon Pinot Gris age well, or should I drink it young?

Short Answer

Drink it young. This isn't a wine built for the cellar, and treating it like one just means you're drinking a tired, flat version of something that was better three years earlier. Pinot Gris doesn't have the tannin structure or the searing acidity of something like Riesling that can hold up over a decade. What it has is fresh fruit character and aromatics, and those fade. Most bottles are made to be opened within two to four years of the vintage, while the fruit is still bright and the texture still has some grip to it. After that it starts going soft and a bit oxidative, losing definition rather than gaining complexity. There's no secret evolution happening in the bottle that rewards patience here. If you've got one sitting around from five or six years back, it's probably still drinkable, just don't expect magic. The lesson is simple: buy it because you want to drink it soon, not because you're building a collection.
