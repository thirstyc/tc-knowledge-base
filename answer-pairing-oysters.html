Wine with oysters?

Short Answer

Muscadet, Chablis or Champagne. Oysters are briny, cold and delicate, so they need a dry, crisp white with plenty of acidity and a salty, mineral edge.

Muscadet is the classic. It comes from near the Atlantic coast at the mouth of the Loire, it's light and lemony, and it's cheap enough to order by the bottle. Look for "sur lie" on the label, which means extra time on the yeast and a little more texture.

Chablis is the other classic. Many of its vineyards sit on Kimmeridgian soil, a limestone and clay packed with fossilised oyster shells. Whether or not that explains it, the pairing works.

Champagne and other dry sparkling wines add bubbles to cut through the brininess. Blanc de Blancs, made only from Chardonnay, is especially good.

Other great choices: Picpoul de Pinet from the Mediterranean coast, where oysters are farmed nearby, Albariño from Galicia, and Sancerre.

Cooked oysters, like fried oysters or oysters Rockefeller, can take a slightly fuller white or a sparkling wine.

What to skip: oaky whites and red wine, which overwhelm the delicate, salty flavour.
