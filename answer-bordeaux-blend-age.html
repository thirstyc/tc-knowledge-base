Can you age Bordeaux Blend?

Short Answer

Good Bordeaux blends are built to age because of what's in the glass chemically: Cabernet Sauvignon brings thick-skinned, tannin-rich structure and firm acidity, and both compounds act as natural preservatives. Tannins slowly polymerize over years, linking into longer chains that eventually fall out as sediment, which is why an old Bordeaux softens and throws deposit while a young one grips your gums. Oak aging before bottling adds another layer of tannin and slow oxidative exposure that primes the wine for this long evolution. Left Bank wines, heavier on Cabernet, tend to need more time and go further, often 15-30 years for serious classified estates; Right Bank, Merlot-dominant wines soften sooner, closer to 10-20 years. What you're buying with age isn't just longevity, it's transformation: primary black fruit gives way to tobacco, leather, dried flowers, and the texture shifts from structured to silky. Decant older bottles gently, serve around 16-18°C, and don't expect this from a basic supermarket blend without real tannin and acid backbone to begin with.
