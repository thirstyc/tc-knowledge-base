What pairs with Alvarinho?

Short Answer

Alvarinho's whole personality is built around a tight, high-acid backbone with a faint saline, almost briny edge, thanks to the granite soils and river-cooled vineyards of Monção e Melgaço. That acidity is a palate-cleanser, which is why it works so well with anything raw or lightly cooked from the sea: oysters, ceviche, grilled sardines, a simple shellfish pasta. The citrus and stone-fruit notes echo lemon or lime you'd squeeze on fish anyway, so the wine does that job for you. Fresh, unaged cheeses like queijo fresco or a young goat cheese play off the same acidity without a fight. Where it struggles is richness: heavy cream sauces or butter-laden dishes flatten it, and it has neither the tannin nor the weight to handle red meat. Serve it cold, around 8 to 10°C, colder than you'd pour a full-bodied white, so that acid stays crisp and refreshing rather than sharp.

Why does Alvarinho taste so fresh and citrusy?

Alvarinho comes from Portugal's Minho region, in the northwest corner where the Atlantic sits close enough to keep summers mild and nights cool - exactly the conditions that preserve malic acid as the grape ripens.
