What pairs with Alvarinho?

Short Answer

Alvarinho grows in granite soils along the cool, river-fed vineyards of Monção e Melgaço, right where the Minho meets the Atlantic, and that combination keeps malic acid high even as sugars ripen. The result is a wine with real bite and a faint saline edge, less about the soil literally tasting like salt and more about low pH sharpening every other flavour on the palate. That acidity is a built-in palate cleanser, which is why raw or lightly cooked seafood loves it: oysters, ceviche, grilled sardines, a simple shellfish pasta. The citrus and stone-fruit notes do the job of a squeeze of lemon, so you barely need one. Fresh, unaged cheeses like queijo fresco or young goat cheese share the same acid-forward logic without a fight. It falls apart with richness, though: cream sauces and butter mute it, and it has neither tannin nor weight for red meat. Serve it properly cold, 8 to 10°C, colder than a fuller white, so the acid stays crisp instead of turning shrill.
