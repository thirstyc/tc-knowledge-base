Why is aged Tawny Port expensive?

Short Answer

Aged Tawny sits in small oak barrels for years, oxidizing slowly through the wood rather than sealed away from air like Vintage Port in bottle. Each year some wine evaporates (the "angel's share"), concentrating sugar, flavor and alcohol while nutty, caramelized notes build from slow oxidation. A 20 or 40-year-old Tawny is a blend averaging that many years in wood, which means the house tied up barrel space, warehouse room and capital for decades without selling a drop. Evaporation losses compound annually, so what started as enough wine for many bottles shrinks year after year. Add in the blending skill needed to hold a consistent house style across decades and the real risk of spoilage over such long stretches, and the cost starts to make sense. Serve slightly chilled, around 12-14°C, in smaller pours since it's rich and higher in alcohol, near 20%. Don't expect fresh fruit; expect walnut, dried fig, toffee. You're paying for real time and real loss baked into production, not scarcity marketing.

What pairs with Tawny Port?

Tawny's whole flavor profile, dried fruit, caramel, toasted nuts, a touch of orange peel, comes from slow oxidative aging, so the best pairings echo those same flavors rather than fighting them.
