Wine with chocolate?

Short Answer

Something sweet and fortified, like Port or Banyuls. The golden rule: the wine should be at least as sweet as the chocolate, or the wine will taste sour and bitter.

That's why the classic idea of dry red wine with chocolate often disappoints. Chocolate's sugar strips the fruit out of a dry red and leaves the tannin and acidity exposed.

Match the wine to the chocolate.

Dark chocolate has bitterness and intensity, so it wants a rich, sweet red with some power. Ruby or Late Bottled Vintage Port works well, as do Banyuls and Maury, fortified Grenache wines from southern France that taste like dark fruit and cocoa.

Milk chocolate and anything with caramel or nuts suits Tawny Port, with its notes of toffee, dried fruit and hazelnut. A sweet Oloroso or Pedro Ximénez Sherry also works, especially with chocolate and nut desserts.

White chocolate is mostly sugar and fat, so go lighter and fresher: Moscato d'Asti, a late-harvest Riesling or a Sauternes.

Chocolate with berries, like a raspberry tart or chocolate-dipped strawberries, is perfect with Brachetto d'Acqui, a sweet, fizzy red from Piedmont.

If you really want dry red, pick a very ripe, fruity one, such as a Zinfandel, and keep the chocolate dark and not too sweet. It can work, but the sweet wines above are the safer choice.
