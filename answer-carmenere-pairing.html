What pairs with Carménère?

Short Answer

Carménère brings moderate-to-high tannin, decent alcohol, and a distinctive green, peppery streak from pyrazines — the same aromatic compounds behind bell pepper and jalapeño notes — so pairings need to work with that vegetal edge rather than against it. Grilled beef and lamb are the classic match because the char and fat give the tannins something to grip onto, softening the wine's structure while the meat's richness balances its savory, slightly herbal profile. Spicy food is a smarter pairing than people expect: the tannin and alcohol give the wine enough weight to stand up to chili heat without being overwhelmed, and the pepper notes in the wine actually echo mild chili or cumin-driven spice rather than clashing with it. Where it struggles is with very delicate or sweet dishes, which expose the green, slightly bitter edge if the fruit isn't fully ripe. Serve around 16 to 17 degrees — too warm and the alcohol and vegetal notes both get louder than they should be.
