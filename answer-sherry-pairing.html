What pairs with Sherry?

Short Answer

Fino and manzanilla are bone-dry, briny, and cut like a knife - their acidity and saline tang make them the best match on earth for fried seafood, olives, almonds, and jamón serrano, because the wine's sharpness slices through fat and salt without fighting it. Serve them cold, around 7-9°C, and drink them soon after opening since flor-aged wines fade fast once uncorked. Move up to amontillado or palo cortado and you get nutty, drier depth that suits mushroom dishes, roasted chicken, or aged cheese. Oloroso, richer and fully oxidative, pairs with stews, cured meats, even dark chocolate at the sweeter end. The mistake most people make is treating all sherry as an aperitif sipper - the dry styles are actually food wines, built to work alongside a table of small dishes rather than alone before dinner. Sweet styles like Pedro Ximénez belong strictly at dessert, thick and dark enough to pour over vanilla ice cream, literally.
