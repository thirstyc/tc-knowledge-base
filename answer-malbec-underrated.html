Why does everyone think Malbec is only Argentine?

Short Answer

Malbec is French first. It's been grown in Cahors, in the southwest, since the Middle Ages, producing a dense, tannic, high-acid wine locals still call vin noir. Phylloxera and brutal frosts battered French Malbec in the 1800s, right around when French immigrants carried cuttings to Argentina, decades before anyone thought of it as an Argentine specialty. It sat there quietly until growers realized Mendoza's altitude and dry, sunny climate suited the grape perfectly, and by the 2000s Argentina had rebuilt its identity as soft, fruit-forward and approachable. Cahors Malbec is still a different animal — darker, more tannic, higher acid, with herbal and iron-like notes instead of plush fruit. So the Argentina association isn't false, it's just recent history overwriting older history. Want the origin story, look for Cahors. Want the modern crowd-pleaser, Mendoza delivers exactly that, and both are legitimately Malbec.

Why does Malbec taste so dark and velvety?

Malbec has thick skins loaded with anthocyanins, the pigment molecules responsible for its near-opaque color, and long, already-polymerized tannin chains, which is why the tannin feels smooth rather than gritty compared to young Cabernet.
