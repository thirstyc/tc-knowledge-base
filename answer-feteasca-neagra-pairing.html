What pairs with Feteasca Neagra?

Short Answer

Fetească Neagră carries real tannin and dark fruit weight, so it wants food with fat and protein to soften that structure. Tannin binds to fat and protein on the tongue, which is why lamb works so well: the richness of the meat coats the palate and the tannin grips it rather than your gums. Grilled meat does something similar through char and smoke, which echo the grape's own baking-spice and dried-plum notes from oak aging. Spicy food is trickier than the cliché suggests; capsaicin heat amplifies alcohol burn, so this only works if the wine isn't pushing 14.5% or more. Serve it around 16-17°C, slightly cooler than room temperature, so the fruit stays defined and the tannin doesn't turn hot. Skip delicate fish or anything creamy without spice, the tannin will flatten it. Think Balkan and Romanian home cooking, stews, grilled sausage, smoked pork, rather than French bistro fare.

Why does Feteasca Neagra taste so dark and cherry?

Fetească Neagră, literally 'black maiden grape,' is a native Romanian variety with genuinely thick skins, and skin is where tannin and colour pigments live, so thick skins mean more of both get pulled into the wine during fermentation.
