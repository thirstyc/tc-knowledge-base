Why does Mavrud taste so powerful and structured?

Short Answer

Mavrud is a thick-skinned grape, and that's really the whole story. Thick skins mean more tannin and pigment get extracted during fermentation, since that's where both compounds live, so you end up with a wine that's naturally dense in color and structure before the winemaker does anything at all. It ripens late and holds onto acidity even as sugar builds, which keeps the wine from feeling flabby despite all that weight. Bulgaria's warm, sunny growing season pushes the fruit toward dark, brooding flavors - think blackberry, plum, sometimes a savory, leathery edge - rather than the brighter red fruit you'd get from a thinner-skinned grape in a cooler spot. Producers often lean into this with extended maceration or oak aging, which only adds more tannin and structure on top of what the grape already brings. The result is a wine that needs time or food to soften, and rewards patience - young Mavrud can taste almost clenched, but a few years in bottle lets that tannin resolve into something genuinely powerful rather than just tough.
