Can you age dry Furmint?

Short Answer

Yes, and it's one of the more underrated ageable whites out there. Furmint grown on the volcanic soils of Tokaj has serious natural acidity, the same backbone that makes sweet Tokaji Aszú balanced despite huge residual sugar, and in dry form that acid acts as a preservative, letting the wine hold together and evolve for a decade or more instead of just fading. Young dry Furmint tends to be tight, citrusy, sometimes a bit austere with a flinty or waxy edge. Given time in bottle, it opens into honeyed, quince, dried apricot, and beeswax notes without losing that mineral spine, not unlike what good Chenin Blanc or Riesling does with age. The misconception worth correcting: most people only know Furmint through sweet Tokaji, but the dry bottlings (labeled száraz) are increasingly made with real ageing intent. Serve young examples around 10 to 12 degrees, but pull an aged bottle a touch warmer, closer to 12 to 14, to let the honeyed complexity show.
