What kind of wine is Albesse, exactly?

Short Answer

Albesse is a white wine made from Sauvignon Blanc, produced in the Loire Valley in France, though it's declassified from the stricter appellation rules that govern something like Sancerre or Pouilly-Fumé. That's not a knock against it. It just means the grapes might come from vineyards or vinification methods that don't tick every box for the fancier label, so the producer sells it under a simpler regional or brand name instead. The upside for you is a wine that tastes a lot like its more famous cousins from that part of the Loire, all citrus, green apple, and that flinty, almost smoky edge Sauvignon Blanc gets from the limestone and flint soils there, but at a friendlier price. It's built for early drinking, not for cellaring, so don't tuck it away hoping it improves. Grab a bottle, chill it down, and drink it within a year or two of the vintage while that zippy freshness is still front and center.
