Why is Roussanne so silky?

Short Answer

Roussanne's silkiness doesn't come from high acidity, that part's actually backwards. It's a naturally low-to-moderate acid grape, and that's precisely what gives it its soft, rounded texture rather than the crisp edge you'd get from something like Chenin or Riesling. The silkiness comes from phenolic compounds and natural glycerol in the skins and juice, often reinforced by malolactic fermentation and time on lees, both of which soften texture further and add a creamy, almost waxy mouthfeel. Floral and honeyed aromatics (jasmine, honeysuckle, herbal tea) reinforce that impression of richness. In the northern Rhône, it's frequently blended with Marsanne or Viognier precisely because its low acid needs backup to avoid tasting flat or flabby on its own. So the texture is real, but the reason isn't tension from acid, it's the opposite: a grape with just enough structure to carry weight without ever feeling sharp. Serve it a little warmer, around 12 to 14°C, to let that texture show properly.
